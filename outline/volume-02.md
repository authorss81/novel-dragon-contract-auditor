# Volume 02 Outline: The First Hearing

Volume number: 2

Chapter range: 51–100

Central pressure: The Chancery accepts Marek's formal audit request only as a **limited administrative inquiry** into one district's filing practice, and then the **Docket of Powers** opens a classification question against him — because he has read a scale instrument twice, and neither reading is in its register. Meanwhile the Cinder Court's order that no guarantor duty may attach to a person who has not been heard has no force in the one place that most needs it, and a public timber bridge in the lower district of Auremar is the proof.

Starting state: Marek Kest is a clerk of the **Received Room** — a barred door, a stove, and eleven feet of shelf — with no standing, no promotion, and a handover list in his own hand with six items and no heading. His request is alive in the custody of Examinations and unanswered. The Cinder Court's fourth order stands on a list with jurisdiction and **no date**. The Kest property-and-debt record is reopened and not cleared. About **sixty households** in the Mosswake district were written to in the sixth year and joined as guarantors without a hearing; the court that did it has recorded that it cannot get their names. A copyist of the Cinder Court's record is sitting unused on the counter of a counting house on a row by the Kiln sheds. Corvin Dray is in the district house and his trial is the second month of next year.

Starting relationships: Marek and Tamsin Rook are colleagues who can now disagree in front of a registrar and a court, and who have a written rule: either of them may put a choice in writing with a date on it, and the other must answer in a room, in words, with a date. **In Volume 02 that rule is used against them by somebody else.** Nell Kest has come home from four hundred and forty miles with a practice that does not belong to her, a sister who has not answered a four-line letter, and a levy that has been lost in writing. **No romance begins in this volume either.** The romance begins at the end of Volume 4.

Starting power level: **Stage 0, Clerk's Eye, plus the unstable first Seam-Sight** earned on the page in Chapter 8 and spent in Chapter 40. Marek performs **no new reading in this volume**; the volume's power is entirely institutional and the classification question is about the two readings he has already made. He writes no valid exception, no counter-seal, and no mixed warrant anywhere in Chapters 51–100. The word *exception* is a legal term of art in other people's mouths — a court's ruling in a single named case — and it is never his.

Major locations: Auremar's **Roll Hall**, the third gallery and the copying alcove; the **Received Room** and its shelf; the **Docket of Powers**, three rooms off the same corridor; the **Cinder Court at Auremar** and its seven numbered rooms off the Crown Kiln stair; Auremar's **lower district** and **Lowcross Bridge** over the Weir Cut; the Mosswake Roll Court and its clerk's window; the **old records room** at Mosswake, with its press and its press-presser; Emberwatch's weigh-house office on Sillrow; and, in the last third of the volume, **the First Seat's administrative wing**.

Major factions: the **Chancery of the Roll** and its **Notaries' Table** under Pell Vey; the **Docket of Powers** under Oswin Marrow; the **Cinder Court** under Sela Vint, room four, with its unnamed registrar; the **Mosswake Roll Court** under Leda Rell with Ivet Sarn at its window; the **district works of Auremar's lower district**; the **parish of Lowcross**, dissolved in the sixth year; provincial magistrates; guild witnesses; junior clerks; the independent copyists and the four holders of Tamsin's list; a formal intervention from the **First Seat's office**; and a remote appearance by **Vaunt Oris**.

**The one rule this volume must never break, carried from Volume 01:** the **MC/FS circular** and the **First Seat quarterly return** remain two documents, four hundred miles apart, in two buildings, with no instrument anywhere joining them, and **only Marek Kest and Tamsin Rook know both exist.** A foundational compact binds only named ratifying powers to publicly stated duties. The four substituted lines and the public-necessity sentence keep their different ages. The red stitch, the thread in the form's fold, and the working stitch in the Ashfall bundle are three different things and no two of them are ever joined in a sentence.

Midpoint reversal (Chapters 68–73): **A valid scale precedent can be challenged by human law only if the affected parties are named.** Marek goes into the Chancery's own Lowcross file and finds that its schedule of *persons who may be answerable* was compiled out of returns and a parish list, and that the parish was dissolved in the sixth year, and that the only living person named in the file at all is Hester Crane — and she is in the wrong column, under *persons from whom a return has been requested*. **The file names every possible debtor except the one family that can prove the clause was copied onto it.** He had been treating the file as an obstacle. It is a map of everybody the building could not imagine being liable, and the Cranes are the hole in it.

Escalation sequence:

1. **Chapters 51–60, the inquiry and the bridge.** The request is received as a limited administrative inquiry with a name at the foot of it, and Tamsin is made responsible for the public roll copies. The Docket of Powers finds two unregistered scale readings and bars Marek from auditing. The answer to Tamsin's four lines comes back from Glasswater and is about a district that has never had a flood-season continuity filing at all. Ivet Sarn finds the sixty households: not in a register, in a **press** in the old records room, holding fifty-four filed copies and returning marks, with a heating flue booked for the fourteenth of the second month. And **Lowcross Bridge** is heard at a district court, and the hearing resolves it correctly, and the bridge closes, and four hundred people on the far side of a river route find out what a correct ruling is for.

2. **Chapters 61–70, the trial and the witness order.** Corvin Dray is tried in the chamber of the Auremar district court on the second month of next year, on the local forgery charges, his own ninety-one leaves and the thirty-eight lines in three districts. He is convicted on the local counts. His plea is accepted as limited and is not absolution, and his testimony goes against the broker network with nothing given for it. Brammel and the eleven protected people are heard. Tamsin finds, in a **fourth-place** file, that **Pell Vey arranged the altered witness order used in the Venn rehearing** — and that the new evidence is not that the order was changed, which has been on the page since the ninth month, but that a named supervisor caused it. And the Cinder Court's full sitting is given a date, and the date is bad.

3. **Chapters 71–80, the temporary office.** Oren Tal, a retired scale notary with a burn-scarred hand, enters and says out loud that the old office of First Custodian was **supposed to be temporary**, in a room, with a document, and refuses to say what that is for. The classification question is answered by a court that says the readings were of no legal effect **and** that the record they entered stands, which is the worst of both. A limited audit is granted in a form so narrow it cannot be used in Lowcross. And a First Seat office intervenes to protect the Lowcross ruling, over Marek's objection, and the document carries a seal and **a name**: **Seryn Oris, First Seat**, named in the book for the first time, credited with nothing she did not do, and described as the seat that holds the old authority to call a succession convocation.

4. **Chapters 81–90, the intervention and the forged seal.** The First Seat's intervention makes the political authority behind the alteration visible without naming a person who did it in this volume. Vaunt Oris appears remotely, formally, and asks one procedural question that costs the First Seat's own lawyer a day. And a **forged true seal** is put in front of Marek — a certificate impression that would end the Lowcross matter in a week, offered without a ledger, and refused on the record with the reason stated. The batch and the volume's public win is a **finding that the precedent is open to challenge**: not that it is void, and not that the person who holds is not liable, but that a person who is affected may be heard about it in a room, in a place that recognises the room, before it binds anybody.

5. **Chapters 91–100, the certified exception and the watch.** The Venn ruling of the twelfth of the ninth month is certified as a **local exception in a named place** and expressly not as an empire-wide precedent — which is the same words, read the other way round, as everything Volume 01 fought for. Corvin is sentenced on the local counts. Marek is found **eligible for Continuity Auditor training** and placed under **formal watch**, the two in the same paragraph, and the watch is the price and not a qualification. Tamsin is appointed to hold the public roll copies with an office that does not exist and a limit that does. And the volume closes on the fifth term's schedule, still nailed to the rail of the Reed Cut gate, with nobody having come to read it in fourteen months, and on a river whose people depend on it.

Concrete resolution: the Venn ruling is certified as a local exception and not as a general rule; **no court may join a person as guarantor on a printed model of general wording without hearing them and their signature, and that order now binds forty-six district courts and one landing office that have taken a recognition from the Cinder Court**; Corvin Dray is convicted on his local forgery charges and his testimony is used without absolution; **thirty-one of the sixty households of the sixth year are named on a board at a window and twenty-three are not, and the board says both numbers**; Lowcross Bridge reopens on a contract that names a person and a price and a date; Marek is eligible for Continuity Auditor training and under formal watch; and **Seryn Oris is named for the first time as the First Seat that holds the old authority to call a succession convocation.**

Power and relationship change: Marek loses the ability to audit and gains a bar and a watch, which is the correct trade and the wrong direction. His ability to read a seam does not grow and is not used. **What grows is that other people's names are on things: a roll of thirty-one households, a custody form with *None* written in the office box, a register of two unauthorised readings entered in a copyist's hand, and a schedule of persons who may be answerable with a hole in it.** Tamsin keeps an independent copy of the evidence in a book of her own with no office on it, and the private book turns out to be the one thing in the whole of this that no office can reach. Marek and Tamsin use the rule they made with each other against a third party, and it works, and the price of it is that she does it without asking him.

**Relationship lock: no romance begins in Volume 02.** Tamsin is a colleague with independent professional authority who has begun to be named in public registers for other people's acts. Nell and Marek correspond in four lines about the levy and are two letters apart. Bren Kest is in Greyfen and is not in this volume's first three batches.

Next-volume question: If inheritance echo spreads through court acceptance, what happens when the same rule is applied to a river whose people depend on it — and can a cooperative be made to hold a duty that is public without making every child in it a debtor?

Final chapter image: a **gate rail** at Reed Cut with a schedule nailed to it under the water office's own authority, fourteen months old, and somebody finally comes to read it — and the person who comes is a woman of eleven who cannot read it, and is read to.
