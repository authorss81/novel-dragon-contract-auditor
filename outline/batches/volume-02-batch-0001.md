# Volume 02 Batch 0001 Outline: The Inquiry, and the Bridge Nobody Asked For

Batch number: 1 (Volume 02)

Chapter range: 51–60

Batch goal: **Open Volume 02 and turn the volume's central pressure into three concrete things a person has to answer for.** (1) The nine-paragraph request and its supplement are received and consolidated as a **limited administrative inquiry** into the filing practice of one district, to be conducted by the applicant, in his own time, at his own wage, with no power to compel any document, office or witness — and this time there **is a name at the foot of it**, and it is Nevin Trost's, and he says he did not want to write it. (2) The **Docket of Powers**, an office not previously on the page, opens a classification question: Marek has read a scale instrument twice and neither reading is in its register, and a reading by a person not entered is an **unauthorised scale reading**. The charge is not about anything he is going to do. It is about two things he did in Volume 01, and it is the first time in the book that a person is asked to account for a past act on the ground that it was unauthorised rather than false. (3) **Lowcross Bridge** is heard, and the hearing is legally correct, and the bridge closes, and four hundred people find out what that is worth. The batch also pays the first instalment of Volume 01's largest unpaid debt: **about sixty households** were joined as guarantors in the sixth year without a hearing, and they are not in a register, and they are in a **press** in a district's old records room with a heating flue booked for the fourteenth of the second month.

- **Nell** loses the levy for the last time in public and finds the fifth question has an answer in Greyfen and the answer is four pounds of coach fare she has not got.

## Required world reveals

1. **An office can accept your case and shrink it in the same sentence, and put a name under it while it does.** The inquiry is real, it is dated, it is signed by Nevin Trost, and it can do nothing.
2. **A private book of one's own, with no office on it, is outside the reach of every office in this book.** Nothing in the Docket's power, and nothing in the Chancery's, can order a person to produce what they wrote down for themselves.
3. **A substituted word does worse damage to a public duty than to a private one.** A body can be dissolved. A household cannot. That is the whole of the Lowcross case and it is the volume's argument in one object.
4. **The sixty households are not in a register, because a register of them would have been a record of the court doing it.** They are in a press of outgoing copies with a press-marker's returns, and the reason nobody has gone and looked is that no clerk in nine years has ever gone and looked at an outgoing rack.
5. **A correct ruling can cost four hundred people their route to market and be entirely right.** And the sentence that costs more is the one that is true.

### Chapter 0051

POV: Marek

Location/time: Auremar, the **Received Room** on the second floor of the Roll Hall, the first week of the ninth month.

Immediate goal: Start a week in a room with a barred door and find out whether a clerk who cannot be got at is safe, and answer the one question he has been putting off since the twenty-second of the eighth, which is what he does about a bundle on the second shelf of his own office.

Resistance: **The room is the punishment and it is also the promotion nobody noticed.** He is the only person in the Roll Hall who can be got at for a document nobody wants, and the intake clerk tells him so in the first ten minutes, kindly. The bundle is on the second shelf, four feet from his own table, and it has been there twelve years and eleven first clerks have looked at it. There is no rule that says he may not open it and no rule that says he must. **The nine-paragraph decision arrives in the same week** — a single page, dated the ninth of the ninth, and it is the first answer in eleven months and it is an acceptance: the request and its supplement are received and consolidated as a **limited administrative inquiry into the filing practice of the district of Mosswake**, to be conducted by the applicant, in his own time, at his own wage, with no power to compel any document, office, witness or return, and with no power to touch any numbered series, any standing instruction, or any return. **It is signed. Nevin Trost put his name to it and wrote a reason underneath it in his own hand and the reason is that he was told to.**

Information revealed: A request for an audit of a series cannot be granted by an office that has no officer for series, and so it is granted as an inquiry into a district, which is a different thing wearing the same word. The decision is **true**: it answers the request, it dates it, and it puts a name under it. It is also **worth nothing**, because a district's filing practice is not in anybody's hands and a clerk cannot compel a return. And the intake clerk, who has written the time in the margin in ink and initialled it for the same man four times in a year and has never once been thanked, is the first person in eleven months to say something kind to Marek Kest, and it is: *nobody can get at you in here, and that is the first week in four years you will not be asked for anything.*

Action or decision: Marek does two ordinary things. He writes down the bundle again, with the date, in a form that says what it is and what it is not, and does not open it — and this time the reason he writes is different from the reason he wrote in August, and the difference is that a man who is not going to look at a thing every morning has to have a reason every morning. And he answers the decision **in writing, the same day, in one paragraph**, accepting the inquiry and asking for exactly two things, both of them cheap, and neither of them a name: that the inquiry's scope be fixed in writing on the page, and that the person who conducts it be named. He does not thank Trost. He writes, at the foot, in his own hand, that he has read the reason and that the reason is the answer to the question he asked on the eighth of the eleventh of last year, which is whether this office has a person in it.

Emotional change: Marek finds that being unfindable is a relief and that the relief is a symptom, and that the first week in four years in which nobody wants anything from him is the week in which he has to decide what he is for.

Power or resource change: **None.** No reading, no order of his own, and one paragraph accepted rather than granted. He acquires a room, a shelf, and an inquiry he cannot use.

Continuity fact: **The request of the eighteenth of the ninth month and the supplement of the eighth of the eleventh month are received and consolidated as a limited administrative inquiry into the filing practice of the district of Mosswake**, dated the ninth of the ninth month, signed **Nevin Trost, examiner, Examinations**, with his reason written underneath it. It has **no power to compel** and **no power to touch any series, standing instruction or return**. **The Ashfall bundle is not opened** and is not moved. The intake clerk is thanked, once, out loud, and does not know what to do about it.

Ending type: Cost with a small purchase.

### Chapter 0053

POV: Ivet Sarn

Location/time: Mosswake, the clerk's window, the embankment, and the **old records room** with its press, the fourth week of the ninth month and the first week of the tenth.

Immediate goal: Find the names of about sixty households this court joined as guarantors in the sixth year without hearing anybody, when the court has recorded in writing that it cannot get them in time.

Resistance: They are not in a register, and the reason they are not in a register is that a register of them would have been a record of the court doing it. They are in a **press**: a quarterly cycle of outgoing correspondence, four cycles a year, filed copies in a bundle per cycle, press-marked and returned. And the fourth cycle's copies were **never made**, because the clerk who would have made them left in the seventh year. And the records room has a **heating flue booked for the fourteenth of the second month**, and a man from the works is coming on the **second of the tenth** to take the floor up in the corner where the press stands.

Information revealed: The mechanism of the sixty is ordinary and is the volume's argument about quiet. The court sent letters; the letters went out on a cycle; the returns came back marked and were **never opened**, because a docket cannot be closed with returns outstanding and the magistrate of the sixth year refused to close it, and so the returns sat in a press in a room with a window nobody opens. **Fifty-four filed copies** for the three cycles in question: **thirty-one** carry a return with a name and a door against it; **eleven** carry a return with a word on it and no name — *left*, *gone*, *no such*; and **twelve** are copies with no return at all. Six of the minute's sixty are unaccounted for and are the fourth cycle's. And the finding nobody wanted: **a clerk cannot answer a question about a whole district out of a register. The answer to a question about a whole district is in the district's own outgoing rack, and no clerk in nine years has ever gone and looked at an outgoing rack in their life.**

Action or decision: Sarn does not wait for the fourteenth of the second month, and does not write to the works, and does not ask the magistrate. She asks for one day, takes the young clerk from the intake desk who has been writing times in margins for a year, opens the press in a room with a cold floor, and reads the returns. She enters **thirty-one household names with a door against each one** on the board under the register of pending duties, in her own hand, and she writes above them, in chalk, the other number, and the other number is the truth: **thirty-one of about sixty. Nineteen of the returns never came back. Six of the copies were never made.**

Emotional change: Sarn finds that the sixty were never lost. They were in a press eight feet from where she works, and that the reason nobody went and looked is that nobody has ever in nine years gone and looked at an outgoing rack, and that this is the most embarrassing thing she has ever found out about her own court, and she writes it on the wall, in the first person, with the day on it.

Power or resource change: None. A day, a cold floor, thirty-one names, and a flue.

Continuity fact: **Thirty-one of about sixty Mosswake households of the sixth year are named on the Mosswake board with a door against each name, and the board states the other number.** The returns are press-marked correspondence in the old records room; **the fourth cycle's copies were never made and the flue is booked for the fourteenth of the second month.** The minute of the twenty-first of the fifth month is not withdrawn and the restitution of the sixty is not begun.

Ending type: Discovery that pays part of a debt and names the remainder.

### Chapter 0055

POV: Marek

Location/time: Auremar, the **Docket of Powers**, three rooms off the corridor behind the third gallery, the third week of the eleventh month.

Immediate goal: Find out what a man who has read a scale instrument twice, under two permissions that were never discharged, is actually charged with, and whether the charge is about anything he is going to do.

Resistance: The Docket of Powers keeps a register of persons recorded as having performed a scale reading under a recognised authority. It is three rooms, a shelf, a bound register running back about sixty years, and a master, **Oswin Marrow**, who is neither hostile nor helpful and who has a form for everything. The finding is that Marek Kest has twice read a scale instrument, on the twelfth of the ninth month of last year and on the sixth of the fourth month of this year, on each occasion under a limited audit issued by the Mosswake Roll Court; that **neither reading is entered in the register**; and that a reading performed by a person not entered is an **unauthorised scale reading**. The Docket cannot punish him. It can do three things and does all three on the page: enter the finding in a **public register**, **bar him from any audit** until he has been examined, and **refer the question whether those readings were of any legal effect whatever** to the Cinder Court.

Information revealed: The charge is not about falsity. Both readings were accurate, both were entered in a minute book, and one of them decided nothing and cost him a nosebleed. The charge is about **authority**, and the Docket is right on its own terms, and the terms were drafted in a decade when a reading could be forced out of a witness. And the thing Marek did not expect: **the register has one page of names, and a name in it is a public act, and Tamsin Rook has entered both of his readings in it in her own hand without asking him**, which is the fourth time in eleven months that she has done something for the record that he has not agreed to and is the first time she has done it where being wrong would have cost a person other than her.

Action or decision: Marek does not contest the entry. He contests the **referral**, in writing, in one page, and his stated reason is the sentence of the whole of Volume 01 in a new place: **a finding that the two readings were of no legal effect would not touch him, because he is not what they are for. It would touch the only record in which a girl of fifteen was entered as heard and a household was entered as asked, and a person who is erased from a record is a person nobody can call.** He asks instead for the narrowest thing the Docket can give, which is an examination, on a date, before two named persons, and he asks for it in writing and signs the request.

Emotional change: Marek finds out that the thing he has spent eleven months believing was his gift — a way of seeing a clause that had moved to somebody who had not signed — is a thing an office can take away from him with a form and a date, and that the loss is not a wound and is a **bar**, and that the first thing he does with the bar is try to give it away.

Power or resource change: **A bar, a public register entry, and a referral.** He loses the ability to audit. He gains an examination he has asked for by name and date, and no power of any kind.

Continuity fact: **The Docket of Powers finds two unauthorised scale readings by Marek Kest**, enters the finding in a public register, **bars him from any audit until examined**, and refers the question of their legal effect to the Cinder Court. **Oswin Marrow** is master of the Docket. **No reading is performed in this chapter.** Marek contests the referral and not the entry, in writing, and asks for an examination on a date before two named persons.

Ending type: Cost, and a refusal to be comforted.

## Divergences Between These Cards And The Page, Recorded At The End Of The Batch

None of these is a canon change. The page governs and each one is here so a later batch does not write against the card.

1. **Chapter 0054 runs five weeks longer than the card's date band.** The card places it in the second and third weeks of the tenth month. The works return the signed form about nine weeks after the ninth of the ninth, which puts the granddaughter's reading of it and the copyist's visit into the first weeks of the **eleventh** month, and the card's date band has been widened rather than the page compressed. The hearing in Chapter 0057 is the second of the twelfth month and requires the form to have come back before it, so the page chain is the only one that works.
2. **The bridge goes out of use on the twelfth of the twelfth month, not the fourteenth.** The order says the fourteenth. A foreman with an ordinary day's order puts a bar up two days early because there is going to be ice in the next three days, and the magistrate enters the two days afterwards without being asked. **The fourteenth remains the date in the order**; the twelfth is the date on the ground. Chapter 0059 opens on the twelfth and the bridge is carried back on the twenty-fourth.
3. **The chapter 0059 card's list of nine names is a list of the whole eleven days, not of the first two, and it includes a woman with no name in it on purpose.** The card says "about nine names." The page has nine entries, one of which is a woman who would not give her name and is not invented, and one of which is Jonas Cray's Tuesday — now an ordinary day's — order, which she says she will not count twice. The two men off the Reed Cut bank are in it and still will not give their names.
4. **The S. Roke entry on the chandler's-counter page in Chapter 0052 is not the man of sixty-one.** The man of sixty-one said no twice in the sixth month of last year and is unnamed by his own choice. S. Roke is a woman on the dyers' lane who said the other half of the finding out loud, has since been asked twice about a copy for her house, has said no twice, and wants that written on the page. A first initial has been given to a figure Volume 01 left without one, and no later batch may give her a full name without a card.
5. **Chapter 0053's finding is thirty-one of fifty-four, and the minute's sixty is made up by six uncopied fourth-cycle returns.** The card said "about sixty." The page gives the whole arithmetic and the arithmetic is the point. Do not write that the sixty are found, or that thirty-one is all of them, or that the four were not.
6. **Marek's examination is the eleventh of the first month, at the fourth hour, in the Docket's room, before the intake clerk of the Chancery and a copyist of the third gallery.** The card left the two persons to the writer. They are named in Chapter 0055 by Oswin Marrow and not by Marek, and the reason is on the page.
