# Volume 01 Batch 0002 Outline: What a Ruling Costs

Batch number: 2

Chapter range: 11–20

Batch goal: Take the volume's local victory and make it cost something material and personal. The batch must keep three local families alive through a winter, give Orra and Nessa a hearing that is honestly worse than a victory, put Ivet Sarn's four days of delay on the record with its price attached, give Nell a guild answer that costs her foreman something, damage Marek and Tamsin's trust over disclosure rather than over secrecy, and end with the first proof that the same four lines and the same public-necessity sentence have reached two other provinces.

Starting condition: The Venn ruling of the twelfth stands and has been read in twenty-six households. Tavi's personal debt is void, the field is preserved until the second month, when the seven-year term expires, Sivra Oris's gate and navigation duty survives, the fourth term requires a separate signature from anyone who assumes a continuing duty, and the fifth term puts the bank works and the gate reset on the district water office and expressly relieves the household of the field-side upkeep. The public-necessity sentence was read at two o'clock and the previously unfiled Dunn summary was filed on it in the same minute, before the five terms were ruled at twenty past three, and a separate sitting for the Dunn household is listed for the twenty-third. Corvin Dray is in the Auremar district house on a joint order with ninety-one leaves of his ledger copied. Marek's first reading has been paid for in fatigue, an ash taste, a headache, and a temporary inability to lie about one named obligation that is still active. A hand-signed nine-paragraph audit request is in Examinations custody, undecided, with a stamped number and no answer. An old red running stitch sits in the margin of a leaf at the bottom of a tin under a lane-man's bed, on which one of the twenty-six public copies happens to have been written because it was the paper to hand, and nobody knows what it means.

**Calendar:** the whole first batch sits inside one month, the ninth, and ran from the seventh to the eighteenth of it. Chapters 11–18 are the weeks after the ruling and Chapters 19–20 are the seventh and eighth weeks after it, so by the fourth month the flood docket closes. The dated items already fixed are: the Dunn separate sitting on the **twenty-third**, Odile Fenn's examination in the **fourth month**, and the answer to the audit request due inside three months. Do not re-derive the earlier calendar; it is in `state/continuity.md`.

**Two custody facts that are canon and easy to get wrong.** The Venn source leaf lives in the Notaries' Table's cabinet in Auremar, and a comparison cannot be had by a clerk who is not standing in the room when the sleeve comes out, so a junior clerk cannot produce it and Sarn has no Table key. The provincial hold's reference carries no office name; the First Seat's device was named aloud in open court for the first time on the twelfth. Nothing in this batch may quietly relocate the leaf or retro-name the hold.

Starting relationships: Marek and Tamsin are colleagues working under a stated rule — nothing he holds goes back in the bag, everything the same day — rather than under restored trust. Tamsin has begun deciding things and telling him afterwards, and has not asked his permission. Nell has set a term: he shows her anything he writes before he writes it. Pell is a supervisor whose helpfulness has become suspicious and whose name has not yet appeared on anything. Ivet Sarn is a clerk who recorded her own refusal in public and has not yet been asked to explain it. Corvin Dray has offered to give a full statement in a cell with the roll-keeper present.

Batch beginning: Chapters 11–12. The ruling has to be paid for in grain before it can be enjoyed, and the separate sitting has to happen on the date it was promised. Chapter 11 is the bank works the water office now owes, and the discovery that a correct ruling has created a second problem: Tavi's unpaid labour is being read by the lane as an assumption of duty. Chapter 12 is the twenty-third, and what the court can actually give Orra Dunn is a postponement that is worse than the thing they were afraid of.

Batch escalation: Chapters 13–16. The postponement is read to four creditors and does exactly what Orra said it would do, and she chooses exposure to get protection, and a woman from another district arrives at the Mosswake window with a copy of her own file. Ivet Sarn's four days are put on the record with a named household that lost its seed while the file was open, which proves the caution had a real price and did not excuse it. The dye-house guild answers Nell's exit clause with a form whose second box turns the Venn lane's public reading into a danger to the exemption. Tamsin and Marek then disagree, in public, about whether the First Seat form can be circulated before the families it affects can be warned, and Tamsin decides without asking him.

Batch midpoint: Chapters 13–14. The midpoint is not the twenty-third and it is not a discovery about a document. It is Orra Dunn, having been given a hearing, choosing to be named in a public register of pending duties because a duty that exists only in a creditor's hand cannot be contested, and Ivet Sarn, having been put on the record, showing Marek a named household that paid for her caution. Both women turn toward exposure against their own instinct, and the batch's argument becomes clear: in this world the dangerous thing is not being named, it is being named in a drawer.

Batch climax: Chapters 19–20. Two letters arrive that were addressed to the Venn lane rather than to a clerk. The Glasswater and Emberwatch files both carry the same four lines and the same public-necessity sentence, and both were itemised rather than entered in the routine column, which means the form's rule failed in at least one of them. The wording is then traced past the form to a printed administrative circular issued to district courts, and the First Seat form is shown to be the quarterly return that stops anybody counting it. Two documents, one invisible system. Examinations answers Marek's nine paragraphs for the first time with a bounded certification: accept the Venn ruling as an emergency precedent and the audit dies. Chapter 20 ends with the offer on the table and the answer unmade.

Batch aftermath: Chapters 17–18 fall before the climax and are the cost the climax is built on. The eleven Candlewright Lane clients lose their broker, three of them lose income, Brammel wants his own green box treated as his own property, and Corvin's statement, taken properly on the record with the roll-keeper present, names a payment point and no client. Marek refuses a private settlement that would restore the Venn file and end the case, and pays for it by learning that Examinations may narrow his request. Chapter 18 is the only quiet chapter in the batch: the fourth term begins being used in the lane by people Tamsin never trained, the reading table belongs to the lane, the headache has gone, the inability to lie lapses in a small specific moment, and Marek finally tells Nell about Ilyra and gets neither forgiveness nor abandonment.

Pressure rotation across the ten chapters: local survival and harvest (11, 18), courtroom action over a record (12), character and consent work (13, 14, 16, 18), political and guild pressure (15, 17), investigation (19), and political pressure with a decision owed (20). No stretch repeats a pressure, and only one chapter is a hearing.

Support cast bound to this batch, so no chapter needs an invented clerk, foreman, constable, assessor, or witness:

- **Ivet Sarn**, thirty-eight, Mosswake Roll Court clerk, now in her own chapter. In Chapter 3 she said, once, that it took her four days to open a file in the fifth year and that on the fourth day the relief rolls could not be certified; Tamsin wrote it on the board. She has a paper about that winter, kept in a drawer in her own room, and has never told the household it cost. She names the household here, and her caution is tested, not vindicated: she is not a coward and not a conspirator, and she is made to say it aloud.
- **Ostyn Vare**, forty-five, Greyfen dye-house foreman, carrying the guild challenge. He reads the assessor's form, understands exactly what it does to his exemption and his seat, and signs the amendment that costs him.

- **Rennie Sallow**, a Mosswake upper-district household, new to this batch. Their barn term file was the one that was open in the fifth year, and their seed allocation was delayed because of it. Sarn has the paper and has never told them why.
- **Perry Ostrow**, a copyist at a Glasswater river court, new to this batch. She sent a copy of a provincial file to the Mosswake lane on her own initiative, has told nobody at home, and is exposed the moment the letter is answered.
- **Odile Fenn** may appear only as a refusal or a promise of the fourth-month examination. She does not examine Tavi in this batch; that is reserved for Chapters 41–50.
- **Hallis Dren** may appear as a man whose certification and whose mother's canal house are both now at risk, and not as a plot convenience.
- **Ruhl Dunnet** may appear only in connection with Corvin's custody, his joint order, and the protected witnesses.
- **Sivra Oris** is not called as a witness in this batch. She testifies in Chapters 34–40.

Required character changes: Marek moves from a man who has won a case to a man who is the person the lane comes to, and has to stop being that person; he learns that a correct ruling can be the beginning of a family's trouble and that he is not exempt from the consequences of the sentence he helped produce. Tamsin changes from the colleague who discloses by agreement to the colleague who decides and reports, because the alternative was Marek deciding for her; this is progress and a cost. Nell obtains an amended exit clause and pays for it through Vare, and she learns that a public reading she asked for is the thing that endangered her contract. Orra Dunn chooses to be named publicly against every instinct she stated. Sarn's record becomes complete. Corvin remains accountable, constrained, and useful.

Required power changes: None. Marek performs no magic in Chapters 11–20, no Roll-Answer appears, and his first Seam-Sight does not recur. The inability to lie about the guarantor persists and is used at least once against him, then lapses on the page in Chapter 18. The instability of his position — a Stage 0 clerk in Volume 02's jurisdiction fight — is the pressure, not a new power. No valid exception, counter-seal, mixed warrant, true-name reading, Roll Assembly, or Stage 2 ability appears anywhere in this batch, and the word "exception" does not appear in it at all, because the only draft that used it is dead and Tamsin Rook is holding the sheet.

Required world reveals: A court's own order can cost a district its reserve, and the fourth term can be defeated by a family's own unpaid labour being read as acceptance. A duty "pending full review" is enforceable enough to be used by a creditor, which makes a postponement worse than a refusal. A public register of pending duties at a court window is a real, cheap protection and a real exposure. Two documents, not one, are the mechanism: a printed administrative circular carrying the substituted wording and the public-necessity sentence to district courts, and a First Seat quarterly-return form that arranges for such filings never to be itemised. A scale matrix is bought from a furnace-side counting house, and the ledger records that house by a mark rather than a name. Two provinces have already been hit, and one of them ignored the routine-column rule.

Open questions after the batch: Who issues the circular and under what authority; why did one provincial court itemise a filing the form said to leave alone; who is Perry Ostrow going to answer to when the Glasswater letter is traced; what is in the second sealed page; what the red stitch marks; what Examinations will actually do with a request that is now attached to a live offer; and whether a bounded certification can protect the current food supply without turning the Venn sentence into a rule.

## Chapter Cards

### Chapter 0011

POV: Marek

Location/time: Reed Cut, the sixth and seventh days after the ruling, which is the nineteenth and twentieth; the gate, the western ditch, and the district water office. It cannot be earlier: Chapter 9 runs the thirteenth to the sixteenth at the reading table and Chapter 10 has Marek in Auremar from the seventeenth morning.

Immediate goal: Get the bank works and the gate reset actually done under the water office's own authority, and get Tavi Venn out of the ditch, before the water moves the lower field.

Resistance: The works cost seed and labour out of a reserve that is already the district's winter. Hallis Dren's certification and his mother's canal house are both riding on the office doing this properly. The crew will do the work but not without a posted schedule, because a schedule decides whose ditch it is. And the lane has already begun to say that a girl who has cut a ditch unpaid since the fourth month and been praised for it has accepted the duty the fourth term requires her to sign for.

Information revealed: The hold saved the field and the ruling saved the family, and together they have emptied the district's seed reserve, so the district will eat a little worse in the fourth month than it needed to. The unpaid labour that Marek thought the ruling had made harmless is now the strongest argument against the fourth term, and it is being made by people who are grateful.

Action or decision: Marek makes the water office post its schedule at the gate in public, with the office's own authority on it, and stops intervening on Tavi's behalf. He lets her be seen doing the work and lets the lane call it what it likes, because the alternative is that she be the only person in the district who never appears in a record.

Emotional change: Marek discovers that being right about a file does not let him protect a person from the consequences of a correct ruling, and that the gratitude in the lane is harder to stand than the anger in Chapter 5.

Power or resource change: No power change. He loses the illusion that the ruling was an outcome he produced rather than a decision he has to live downstream of.

Continuity fact: The district's winter seed reserve is materially reduced, and the gate reset and bank works are now the water office's obligation under the ruling's **fifth term** alone, with the household expressly not required to perform field-side upkeep for the purposes of the order. Tavi named that ask herself on the tenth, in the knowledge that a girl who has cut the ditch unpaid since the fourth month will be read as having accepted a duty she never signed. That reading is what this chapter must let the lane do.

Ending type: Cost / consequence.

Next-chapter pull: The separate sitting is four days away and Orra Dunn has not been told what she is going to be asked to give up.

### Chapter 0012

POV: Marek

Location/time: Mosswake Roll Court, the twenty-third of the month, the separate sitting in the Dunn matter.

Immediate goal: Get a guarantor duty against the Dunn household postponed on the record rather than argued, and get Nessa's own answer taken down before anything is attached to her.

Resistance: Orra's stated fear that a hearing will expose the whole household is correct and the court cannot prevent it. Rell's timetable is the district's timetable. The registrar has already filed the summary. Creditors and a guild lender are entitled to attend and intend to ask questions. And the sentence being applied was written by this court eleven days ago, so every general word the court uses today will be quoted in the next file.

Information revealed: What the court can give is a postponement pending full review, and a pending duty is still a duty a creditor may act on, hold security against, and name in a demand. The postponement is therefore worse for the family than a clean refusal would have been, and the court knows it and still has to do it.

Action or decision: Marek argues for the postponement because he has no better offer and says so in open court rather than pretending. He asks for Nessa's answer to be taken and read back to her, and for the postponement to carry a date and a bar on enforcement, and he gets the date and the date only.

Emotional change: Orra says, in the room, that Marek was right about what the hearing would cost her and she still went, and Marek understands that being believed is not the same as being forgiven.

Power or resource change: None. No reading, no exception, no order of his own. He learns what a court's own authority looks like when it is doing its best.

Continuity fact: The Dunn guarantor duty against the present holder of the barn is postponed pending full review with a fixed date. Nessa has been heard and her refusal recorded. The public-necessity sentence may not be reused without a separate hearing and a signature, and that principle is now on the record from this court's own mouth.

Ending type: Decision / cost.

Next-chapter pull: A postponement in Orra Dunn's hand is worth nothing to a creditor who needs a number.

### Chapter 0013

POV: Orra Dunn

Location/time: The upper seed barn and the lane above it, the days after the twenty-third.

Immediate goal: Keep the barn, and get the four people Orra owes money to to stop treating a pending duty as a reason to take her floor.

Resistance: Three of the four creditors will hold if the pending status is written a particular way. The fourth will not hold at all and has already told the seed factor. A neighbour is sorry for her and buys seed she does not need. And Orra has already told a court clerk that she did not want her household named anywhere.

Information revealed: A duty pending full review is a number a creditor can put in a demand, and the demand does not have to wait for the full review. The family can be pressed on the strength of a question that has not been answered.

Action or decision: Orra asks, against her own stated preference, for the Dunn household to be named in a public register of pending duties kept at the court window, because a duty that exists only in a creditor's hand cannot be contested by anybody but the creditor. Sarn starts the register because it costs her nothing and she has been wrong once.

Emotional change: Orra moves from a woman who wanted her household kept out of a record to a woman who has understood that an unnamed obligation is the dangerous kind. She is not grateful to the court.

Power or resource change: None. A cheap public register of pending duties exists at the Mosswake court window from this chapter onward, kept in Sarn's hand, with a date, a household, a duty, and the next review.

Continuity fact: The Mosswake court window keeps a public register of pending duties. It protects nobody by itself and it puts every named household in a place creditors, neighbours, and other clerks can read.

Ending type: Cost / turn.

Next-chapter pull: A woman gets off the packet boat at the Mosswake landing with a copy of a file that is not hers, asks for the register, and has the same four lines in it.

Power or resource change: None. Nell's exit clause is amended and accepted; the craft exemption survives; Vare's foreman's seat is exposed to an audit; and Tamsin says out loud, in the room, that a clause that is good for one household and dangerous for another is a form, and forms travel.

### Chapter 0016

POV: Tamsin

Location/time: Auremar, the copying alcove and the yard behind the Chancery, the fourth week after the ruling.

Immediate goal: Decide whether the First Seat form is circulated to the people it affects before those people can be warned, and get the warning to the eleven Candlewright Lane witnesses either way.

Resistance: Publishing a First Seat form that says a district court's flood filings need not be itemised is an attack on a court's competence, and the court in question is the one that gave a widow her field. Not publishing it leaves families in three districts with no warning at all. Pell Vey is unaccountably helpful and asks, twice, in a way that could be innocent, which witnesses already have it.

So the document is not a crime and is worse than a crime, because it is a permission, and the correct thing to do with it is administrative.

Action or decision: Tamsin sends copies to every named protected witness with a plain warning and a date, and does not publish, and does not ask Marek, and tells him afterwards in one sentence that she has decided. Marek, who cannot lie about the guarantor, finds himself unable to argue and is not sure that is the reason he is not arguing.

Emotional change: Tamsin stops being the person who asks permission and becomes the person who decides and reports, which is the growth the batch needed and a loss of standing she has not priced. Marek is not in control of the information for the first time and has to accept that this is correct.

Power or resource change: None. The reading's cost is still active and used once.

Continuity fact: The First Seat form is in the hands of all eleven named witnesses and the wider protected list, with a warning, and it is not public. Magistrate Rell has not been told that a form bearing her court's conduct has been circulated.

Ending type: Cost / relationship turn.

Next-chapter pull: Somebody who is not on the list has already seen it, and has asked a question about it in the wrong place.

### Chapter 0020

POV: Marek

Location/time: Auremar, the Chancery and the exhibit room, the eighth week.

Immediate goal: Identify what produced the two provincial copies, and keep the audit request alive long enough to be answered on the record.

Resistance: Examinations' examiners are not hostile, only tired, and they want the exhibits back. The First Seat form does not name a template and does not mention the wording. The wording is not in the form at all; it is in a separate printed administrative circular issued to district courts, and the form is the quarterly return that stops anybody counting what the circular produces.

Information revealed: The mechanism is two documents. A circular carries the substituted guarantor language and the public-necessity sentence to district courts as an approved model. A First Seat form then classifies filings made under that circular as routine and unitemised. The precedent was issued, not invented, and it is in print, with a number, and it has been in print long enough for a public-necessity sentence written by one river court seven weeks ago to arrive in a file four hundred miles away.

Action or decision: Marek files a supplementary request naming the circular and its numbers. Examinations answers the nine paragraphs for the first time and offers a bounded certification: accept the Venn ruling as an emergency precedent with a review date, and the audit request is closed. The offer is real, it would protect the current food supply, and it is the price of the thing Marek actually wants.

Emotional change: Marek is offered, at twenty-nine, the thing he wanted when he was twenty-four, framed as the price of the thing he wants now, and he understands that the two are the same offer wearing different clothes. He does not answer inside this chapter.

Power or resource change: None. The circular's number and issue are on the record, the request is alive and threatened, and the offer is on the table with a real deadline.
