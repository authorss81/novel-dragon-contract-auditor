# Volume 01 Batch 0004 Outline: The Emberwatch Silence and the Scale Sitting

Batch number: 4
Chapter range: 31–40

Batch goal: Close the coalition arc that Batch 0003 opened and put the case in front of a body that has authority to hear it, without ever producing the motive. Two things happen in these ten chapters that the previous batch could not do. First, the four holders in Emberwatch who never answered are found, and what the silence turns out to be is the volume's argument about quiet: a district that obeyed the form perfectly has produced no document, no number, and no person who can be asked a question, and a trade that has spent forty years not being a problem has had nothing to complain about until it did. Second, the limited scale hearing opens, Sivra Oris testifies, Pell Vey offers Marek a promotion in exchange for calling the correction routine, and Marek refuses it — and the court still cannot make anyone disclose who authorised the sealed instruction.

Starting condition: The reply of the thirteenth is in forty-six districts and the Venn term has expired with nothing decided. The flood-season continuity process is running as a funded six-week trial **with the certification window deliberately shut**, from the first of the twelfth month to the twelfth of the first, with nineteen hearings on the hearing slate by the second of the first month. Its price — a hundred and fifty-six hours, twenty-six days, two hundred and eighty-four relief rolls — is on the public record as a price and not as a bill incurred. The **review is the fourth of the fourth month**; the **rate is struck on the sixth**. The Venn term expired on the nineteenth of the second month. Orra Dunn's early-hearing request is on the slate, **asked, not granted**; Grice's writ was served on the twenty-ninth of the first month and the forty quarters of rye went thirty-one miles up a canal to a mill at Coldsfoot. Ilsabet Kell, sixty-one, has offered to stand with her own note in her hand. A cooper's gauge is gone from Millbrook Reach, three hundred miles down the water, on the fourth of the twelfth, and there is no document. Tamsin Rook has a list of forty-one copy-holders in her own book, of whom **ten did not answer and four of the seven written to are in Emberwatch, in a dyers' trade.** The unsigned ninety-word corridor instruction is in Nevin Trost's hands and unfiled. Pell Vey has asked for it because his name is on the reply of the thirteenth.

**Calendar.** The Batch 0003 day-by-day calendar is canon in `state/continuity.md` and runs from the eleventh of the eleventh month to the twenty-second of the second month. **Chapters 31–33 sit in the second and third months**, after the Venn term has expired and before the fourth month's review. **Chapters 34–40 sit in the fourth month**, and they are the limited scale hearing the volume outline requires for that range. The fourth month is the busiest month in the book: the flood docket closes, the rate is struck, the process is reviewed, the Dunn review is decided, the Caudle levy and the Greyfen assessment both bite, twenty-three houses are to be walked, Odile Fenn examines Tavi, and the scope decision on the nine paragraphs is owed from a named person. **Weekday names are not load-bearing and must not be used to anchor a chain.**

**Two custody facts that are canon and easy to get wrong.** The Venn source leaf lives in the Notaries' Table's cabinet in Auremar; Sarn has no Table key and never had one; a comparison cannot be had by a clerk who is not in the room when the sleeve comes out. The provincial hold's reference carries no office name, and the First Seat's device was named aloud in open court for the first time on the twelfth of the ninth. **Nothing in this batch may relocate the leaf, retro-name the hold, or let the second sealed page be opened.** The scale sitting in Chapters 34–40 is a *limited* sitting about a water-and-field instrument and a filed correction. It is not an audit, it is not a review of the MC/FS series, and it has no power over the nine paragraphs.

**Two things this batch must not do.** It must not produce the motive. Nobody discloses who authorised the sealed instruction, and no document produced in these ten chapters may come close; what may be produced is an order that is *authentic-looking* and correct on its face and traceable to a series rather than a man, which is the same shape as the midpoint's answer and is the reason the volume's question gets harder instead of easier. And it must not let the hearing win. The volume's climax is Chapters 41–46, and this batch's hearing may narrow nothing, compel nothing, and produce exactly one thing of permanent value: a sworn statement from the water keeper, on the record, in scale form, about whose duty the original instrument created.

**The volume outline's own escalation for Chapters 26–33** — "a separate household in another district loses a tool before the notice reaches them" — was discharged by Chapter 30. Batch 0004 therefore opens on the *second* consequence of that district: a district that has lost a tool is a district that will not answer a letter, and the reason it will not answer is not fear.

Starting relationships: Marek and Tamsin work under a written disagreement with a date on it — her objection about the First Seat quarterly return, in his coat pocket — and a rule neither of them will break. Marek has been able to lie again since Chapter 18 and nobody in the building has noticed. Nell has not written to him and is not going to. Pell has asked for the corridor conversation and has not been refused. Sarn is running two registers and a list of people who have agreed to witness. Dren has not told anybody that he answered a quarterly return in October.

Batch beginning: Chapters 31–32. Chapter 31 is the Emberwatch silence, told by Tamsin, and it must not be a detective chapter. Chapter 32 is the hearing's preliminary sitting, in which the parties agree what the limited sitting can and cannot do, and the answer is worse than Marek hoped.

Batch escalation: Chapters 33–35. A trade decides to send someone. A woman's decision to go is the batch's midpoint, not a hearing. The sitting is called and the parties argue about what counts as a party.

Batch midpoint: Chapter 35. The batch's turn is not a discovery. It is a dyers' association in a furnace town discovering that the thing that has protected it for forty years — never being a problem — is the reason that when it finally was a problem, nobody was in a position to be told. The four questions arrive in a place where the person who gave them does not want to be named, and that anonymity, which has been the lane's greatest strength, is the thing that makes the Emberwatch file unusable.

Batch climax: Chapters 36–38. Sivra Oris testifies, and her testimony is the batch's centre: the original instrument created two duties and named the people who held them, Aldren Venn field-side and Sivra Oris gate-and-navigation, and neither of them is an office that can be occupied. Pell offers Marek a promotion in the same week, in the same words he has used for nineteen years, and Marek refuses it and loses the only thing he wanted when he came to Auremar. The court cannot compel disclosure, and says so on the record with a name at the foot of it.

Batch aftermath: Chapters 39–40. The hearing produces one permanent thing and one permanent loss. The permanent thing is the sworn statement. The permanent loss is that the reply of the thirteenth is answered by an office with a seal and no person, in the form the reply asked for, and the scope decision is still owed from a named person. Chapters 39–40 are also where the fourth month's household arithmetic lands on named people: the Venns' gate, Odile Fenn's examination of Tavi, the Dunn review, and the twenty-three houses.

Pressure rotation across the ten chapters: investigation with no document (31, 33), political and procedural pressure with a decision owed (32, 36), character and consent work (35, 39), institutional pressure (34, 37), emotional consequence and material cost (38, 40). No two consecutive chapters run the same way, and only Chapters 36–37 are a hearing, which the volume outline permits as a single sitting.

Support cast bound to this batch, so no chapter needs an invented clerk, foreman, constable, assessor, or witness. All are already on the page: Ivet Sarn, Ostyn Vare, Marl Crenn, Hallis Dren, Ruhl Dunnet, Odile Fenn, Rennie and Wenna Sallow, Orra and Nessa Dunn, Mara and Tavi Venn, Sivra Oris, Nell Kest, Tamsin Rook, Nevin Trost, Bryn Mawer, Ilsabet Kell, Nery Caudle, Coll Fenner, Ivo Harker, the hooded seed-barn copyist, Doctor Perrin Ashe, Wend and Pella Osk, Sabra Ollett, Iselle and Tace Oster. New figures are limited to the four the cards name below.

Required character changes: Marek moves from a man who has refused a bad offer to a man who has refused a good one, and has to find out which refusal cost him more. He learns that a sworn statement from the one counterparty who cannot be mistaken for a clerk is worth more than a promotion and less than a decision, and that the two are not comparable. Tamsin moves from a woman who went to one household to a woman whose method is used without her in a place she has never been, and has to decide whether anonymity is a right she can defend from a distance. Nell moves from a woman whose table ran without her to a woman who is asked for her four questions by a trade, and has to decide whether they travel as hers or as anybody's. Sarn runs the review and has to write on her own board what the trial did and what it cost, in the same hand as everything else. Orra Dunn's early-hearing request is decided in this batch and the answer is no with a date on it.

Required power changes: **Marek may use the unstable Seam-Sight at most once, at material cost, and it may not decide anything.** The first reading was spent in Chapter 8 and has not recurred. The inability to lie lapsed in Chapter 18 and stays lapsed. **No valid exception, counter-seal, mixed warrant, true-name reading, Roll Assembly, or Stage 2 ability appears anywhere in this batch, and Marek writes no order that is his own.** Any order given in the sitting is the court's or the standing office's own authority. The word "exception" must not appear.

Required world reveals: A district that obeyed a form is not safe, it is quiet, and quiet is not a defence a person can use when the harm is theirs. An association of houses that has never had a dispute is the association least able to say one, because it has no practice of it and no person whose job it is. A sworn statement in scale form from a dragon water keeper is worth more than a Chancery circular because it cannot be issued, only sworn, and it names the people. An authentic order with no person behind it is the same object twice: the MC/FS sheet and the order that arrives in Chapter 36 must be recognisably the same kind of thing, and the reader should feel the shape before the text explains it. And who is helped by quiet is answered in the negative, in a furnace town, by a trade.

Open questions after the batch: Who signed the MC/FS sheet of the twenty-second of the ninth month. Who authorised the sealed instruction, which nobody can now be made to say. Why the Emberwatch clerk itemised a filing the form said to leave alone, and whether that clerk and the Glasswater sender are the same person. What Perry Ostrow answers to. What is in the second sealed page. What the red stitch marks. And whether a sworn statement that names two people can survive a season in which one of them has stopped answering letters.

## Chapter Cards

### Chapter 0031

POV: Tamsin

Location/time: Auremar, the copying alcove and the packet stair, the second week of the second month, and a furnace town four hundred and forty miles away.

Immediate goal: Find out why four people in one house in Emberwatch did not answer a letter that asked them nothing, and get a name at the top of an answer for the first time.

Resistance: The list has three columns that matter and one of them is a column of people who have not answered, and a person who has not answered is not a person who has refused, and the difference between the two is the whole chapter. She cannot go: she has a window, a book under a winter coat, and a hearing slate that will not run itself. Emberwatch is four hundred and forty miles away and the coach takes four days each way. Nobody will confirm an address for her because confirming an address is a promise, and the last person who wrote to that house got a form back with a thumb on a line. And a trade does not answer a stranger; a trade answers its own.

Information revealed: The four are one house, not four, and the house has never had a dispute in forty years, which is why nobody in it has ever written to anybody about anything. The Emberwatch clerk who itemised a filing the form said to leave alone is a clerk of that same trade, and the reason the filing is itemised is not that anybody broke the rule but that the rule was never read. An association that has never been a problem has no practice of complaining and no person whose job it is, and when it finally has a problem the problem goes to a room with a bar on the door.

Action or decision: Tamsin does not go and does not send a letter. She writes to the one person in the trade she has already met — the unnamed assessor from the next province, who wrote the fourth question out at the top of a letter to eleven houses without saying where it came from — and asks her the one question she can ask without a document: who is the person in Emberwatch that a person in a dyers' house would go to. She puts the answer on the list as it comes in, in her own hand, and does not tidy the column.

Emotional change: Tamsin finds that the thing she built to stop a person being alone in front of a paper has produced a list in which the loneliest people are the ones nobody can find, and that she cannot fix it by being better at letters.

Power or resource change: None. A letter, an answer, and a column with a name in it.

Continuity fact: The four non-responders in Emberwatch are one house of dyers, not four households, and the house has had no dispute in forty years. The trade's clerk is the one who itemised entry ninety-one's counterpart, and the reason is that nobody read the instruction. Tamsin has not left Auremar and the book is still under the winter coat.

Ending type: Discovery with a cost.

Next-chapter pull: The answer Tamsin gets back is a name and an address and a sentence about what a person in that house is not allowed to do without asking, and it is the only reason the scale sitting can be called at all.

### Chapter 0032

POV: Marek

Location/time: Auremar, the third gallery and the anteroom, the third week of the second month.

Immediate goal: Get a limited scale sitting called on a question he can actually ask, and find out from the court, in advance and in writing, exactly what it will be able to do.

Resistance: A limited sitting is a thing a court grants to make an argument smaller. Every question Marek wants answered — who signed the sheet, on whose authority, what the quarterly return was for — is outside it, and the court will say so in a sentence. Pell, who controls the Table, is the person who would have to certify the parties, and Pell has asked for the corridor conversation and not been given it. Tamsin's written objection is in his coat. And the obstruction is not a villain: the court is protecting itself from a matter that could pull in the MC/FS series and the province's standing instructions, and it is right to.

Information revealed: A limited sitting can hear a sworn statement about one instrument and can record what a party says about its own duty. It cannot order disclosure, cannot strike a filed correction, cannot touch a series, and cannot be described afterwards as an audit. Marek's best available outcome, six weeks ago, was a process somebody else runs. His best available outcome now is one sentence in a form that cannot be reissued.

Action or decision: Marek narrows his own request in writing, in the same gesture he used on the audit request in October, and gives the court the list of things he is not asking for so that it cannot be said afterwards that he asked. He does it before he is asked to, and Tamsin notices that he has done it without telling her, and says so.

Emotional change: Marek discovers that giving things away is now his most fluent move and cannot tell whether that is discipline or a way of staying in control of the shape of a case he is going to lose.

Power or resource change: None. A narrowed request, entered.

Continuity fact: The limited sitting is called for the fourth month on the Venn instrument and the filed correction only. Marek has entered, in his own hand, a list of what he is not asking for. The nine paragraphs are not before the court and the MC/FS series is not before the court.

Ending type: Procedural turn.

Next-chapter pull: The only witness who can speak to whose duty the original instrument created is a dragon, and a dragon is summoned by nobody in this building.

### Chapter 0033

POV: Nell

Location/time: Greyfen, the dyers' lane, the second week of the third month.

Immediate goal: Find out what a levy recalculated onto four houses is actually going to do in the fourth month, in cash, to named people, before she has to walk twenty-three doors with no coach.

Resistance: Marl Crenn said in advance it will be overruled within a year and that Nell should expect to lose it, and Crenn is right. The four signing houses have paid; the four that have not signed have paid nothing and have a claim to say so. The Nine Elms signed on the twenty-ninth of the twelfth and wants something for it. And the assessor from the next province has written a decision in her own association's favour and has not told anyone in Greyfen, which means one trade's improvement is another trade's bill.

Information revealed: The levy does not fall evenly and was never going to. It falls hardest on the house that signed first, because signing first is what made the recalculation possible, and the woman who paid first is now the woman nobody argues with. A trade that takes an amendment is paying for the privilege of having been a problem.

Action or decision: Nell does the arithmetic in public, on the table, in the lane, with the levy book and the four names on it, and puts the four names on the board. It is the first time the lane's table is used to publish a number about the lane's own people rather than read a paper somebody else brought.

Emotional change: Nell learns that being the person who publishes the arithmetic about her own neighbours is a different and lonelier job than reading other people's papers, and that the four questions are no answer to being the one the number is about.

Power or resource change: None. Four names in chalk, and a decision owed by the assessor in the next province that has not been announced.

Continuity fact: The Greyfen levy is recalculated onto the four signing houses on paper and the first-signing house is carrying the most of it. The assessor from the next province has decided in her own association's favour and has told nobody in Greyfen. The ninth of the twelfth Caudle payment and the twenty-ninth of the tenth Caudle signature are both in the levy book and both are readable.

Ending type: Cost, published.

Next-chapter pull: A letter comes back from a furnace town with a name in it, and the name is the person a dyers' house goes to before anybody else.

### Chapter 0034

POV: Sarn

Location/time: The Mosswake Roll Court, the window, the copying room and the board, the third month and into the fourth.

Immediate goal: Get the trial's own accounting done in time for the fourth month's review, in her own hand, on her own board, without making the review into a trial of anybody.

Resistance: Nineteen hearings on the slate, four of them with the words *I do not understand this* in them, and the process has cost her twenty-six days of a window that was shut, which means the arithmetic in the reply of the thirteenth is still an untested claim. The eleven shillings will be spent or will not. Rell wants to know on the seventh what it cost and has not asked in writing. And Sarn is a clerk who has now run two registers and a witness list and has not told anybody, including Rell, that the trial was deliberately run outside the certification window.

Information revealed: The process is safe while somebody stands at the window, and the trial was run in a month when the window was shut, which is the only reason a district can be told a true price. Sarn has to write that down, in a public place, in the first person, as the reason, and not as an excuse. She also has to record the four hearings where a person said they did not understand, because that is the number that decides whether the process worked.

Action or decision: Sarn enters the trial's accounting on the board beside both registers, in her own hand, with the day on it, and rules that the review is not a hearing of any application and that anybody who wants to say something about the process itself must say it in the room, aloud, on the fourth.

Emotional change: Sarn becomes, in her own estimation, the person who runs the thing rather than the person who records it, and does not enjoy it, and cannot hand it to anybody.

Power or resource change: None. A board, a book, and a date.

Continuity fact: The trial's accounting is on the Mosswake board with the day on it. The review is the fourth of the fourth and is not a hearing of any application. The four *I do not understand this* answers are recorded as answers.

Ending type: Practical turn with a public cost.

Next-chapter pull: The witness order goes out to a house four hundred and forty miles away and comes back signed, which is the first time one has.

### Chapter 0035

POV: Iselle Oster

Location/time: Millbrook Reach, a cooper's shop and a landing office, the third week of the third month.

Immediate goal: Get a person in a surety line of sixteen taken off a line she did not sign, in a town with no court that will hear her, before the fourth month, by ordinary means.

Resistance: There is no document. The landing office stamped the creditor's filled-in model into the routine column and cannot produce a number, and Sabra Ollett will say so and has already said so once. The gauge is gone and the son cannot be examined for the trade without it, and the trade is the town's life. A creditor with a stamped instrument is not a monster; he thinks he holds a bond. And the one thing that has worked — a copyist's book with no seal and no office — cannot be produced in front of a creditor without becoming a document and therefore becoming something that can be stamped.

Information revealed: The absence of a document is not a defect in the system; it is the system working as designed, and it works because every person in the chain did exactly what the form asked. That is the chapter's argument and Iselle Oster makes it herself, to a copyist, in her own shop, having lost a gauge. A district that obeyed the form is not safer. It is quieter, and the quiet is the injury, because a person with no document has nothing to show anybody — not the harm, not the fact of it, not that she existed as a person who came.

Action or decision: Iselle Oster asks the question nobody has asked her, which is what the stamp was, and gets an answer from Sabra Ollett in front of two witnesses, and then does the thing that opens the volume's next stretch: she asks Tamsin's register to be read aloud in her shop with the creditor's man in it, knowing that a register read aloud is the only version of this that can move, and that it will move by making an enemy.

Emotional change: Iselle Oster stops being a person who is owed something and becomes a person who has decided what she is owed and is going to say it out loud in a room, which is a different and much more dangerous kind of poor.

Power or resource change: None. A name read into a book that has no authority, and a creditor who now knows where she is.

Continuity fact: There is still no instrument at Millbrook Reach. Sabra Ollett has stated in front of two witnesses what she stamped and when. A register with no seal has been read aloud in a shop with a creditor's man present. Tace Oster has been asked whether it is her fault and has not been told.

Ending type: Consequence, and a new kind of cost.

Next-chapter pull: What happens in a town when a woman with no document says out loud, in front of a creditor's man, that the document does not exist.

### Chapter 0036

POV: Marek

Location/time: Auremar, the third gallery, the end of the third month.

Immediate goal: Refuse the promotion Pell offers him and keep the sitting, and not tell Tamsin first.

Resistance: The offer is real, and it is generous in exactly the ways Marek has wanted for four months: a room, a name on a door, a standing that would make the nine paragraphs answerable, and a signature that would be worth more than a junior clerk's. Pell frames it as ordinary — the correction is routine, the practice is routine, a man who calls a routine thing routine is not obstructing anybody. And it is the same offer in the same words as the yard conversation Tamsin wrote down on the fourth of the tenth, and Pell has never been asked about it, and the ninety words in Trost's hands are still unfiled. Marek cannot ask Pell about the yard without disclosing that Tamsin has it, and disclosing that is the one thing he promised not to do.

Information revealed: Pell is not a villain making a threat. He is a careful man offering a standing to a junior clerk in the ordinary way on a Tuesday, and he has not asked whether the thing is routine because asking is not his office. The offer is a promotion and it is also an instruction, and it is the first time in the volume that a person with power has offered Marek a *reason* rather than a threat.

Action or decision: Marek refuses, in a room, with a witness, and asks for the refusal to be entered in the same form as the refusal of the certification in the eleventh month — offer, terms, person, date, decliner, witness. He asks for it in writing before he is asked for it, which is the habit the last four months have taught him and which Tamsin will later describe, correctly, as his worst and best one.

Emotional change: Marek loses the thing he came to Auremar for and finds that losing it is a relief, and is frightened by the relief, because a man who is relieved to give up a standing has been using the standing as a plan rather than as a want.

Power or resource change: None. A refusal entered, and no promotion, and no standing he did not have.

Continuity fact: The promotion is refused and entered with the offer's terms. Pell's name is on the reply of the thirteenth and is now also on a recorded refusal. **Tamsin is not told before the refusal, and this is the third and last time he does that.**

Ending type: Decision, with a private cost.

### Chapter 0037

POV: Sivra Oris

Location/time: Mosswake, the channel bank, the first week of the fourth month.

Immediate goal: Give a sworn statement about an instrument she signed seven years ago, in a form that cannot be reissued by anybody, about duties that were hers and a man's and nobody else's.

Resistance: A water keeper is not a witness, she is a counterparty, and a counterparty's statement is an admission against interest and can be refused. She has no grievance: the fifth term moved the bank works to the district and the gate is back inside a quarter of an inch, and she is not being harmed by anything Marek wants. She has watched two district courts turn her duty into a general rule and has no way to complain about either, because there is no office she can complain to that is not the office that did it. And she will not speak to what she has not seen, which means her statement will not name who altered the Venn correction, and Marek will have to accept a statement that answers the question he can prove and is silent on the question he wants answered.

Information revealed: The original instrument created two duties and named the people who held them. Aldren Venn owed the field-side upkeep and the winter seed. Sivra Oris owed the Reed Cut gate and the navigation. Neither of them is an office; both are a person's work, and the instrument said so in the only words it had. The alteration did not change the duties. It changed who was held by them, and it did that by substituting a phrase that can be occupied — *the present holder* — for two people who cannot be.

Action or decision: Sivra Oris swears the statement in scale form before the court, in the room, with the channel behind the window, and refuses every question that would require her to say what another office did. She is not helping Marek. She is putting two sentences into a record that will outlast everybody in the room, and she says that out loud, and it is the first thing anyone in this case has said that is not an argument.

Emotional change: None available to her, and that is the point: she has been owed a hearing for seven years and has not wanted one. She leaves having got what she came for and having watched four people want something from her.

Power or resource change: None. A sworn statement, once, in a form that cannot be reissued.

Continuity fact: Sivra Oris swears, on the record, that the Venn instrument created two named personal duties and no office. **She does not name who made the correction and must not be made to.** The statement is the batch's one permanent gain and it is worth less than the promotion Marek refused and more than the reply of the thirteenth.

Ending type: Earned, quiet, and not a victory.

Next-chapter pull: The statement is entered and the court says, in one sentence with a name at the foot of it, what it cannot do.

### Chapter 0038

POV: Tamsin

Location/time: Auremar, the copying alcove, the second week of the fourth month.

Immediate goal: Get the scope decision on the nine paragraphs, and find out whether the office that issued the sheet of the twenty-second of the ninth month can be made to answer for it by anybody with a name.

Resistance: The answer arrives, and it is in exactly the wording Pell Vey dictated in the gallery: the office states that its sheets are issued under standing authority and are not signed by a person, and the applicant is referred to the standing instructions of his own district. It is true, it is unaskable, and it is the answer of a drawer. The reply asked for a decision before the fourth month from a person whose name is written at the foot of the answer, and what comes back has a seal on it. And Tamsin still has the objection in Marek's coat pocket and a date on it.

Information revealed: The machine does not need to be cruel or even careless. It needs only to be issued. An answer that is true, formal, and signed by nobody is unanswerable in a way that an untruth would not be, because the only thing a person can do with an untruth is prove it, and there is nothing here to prove. This is the midpoint's answer turned against them: *issued, not invented* was a finding about the sentence, and it is now a shield.

Action or decision: Tamsin does the thing she has been building toward and writes the reply, which is four lines, and reads it back twice, and does not soften it: the applicant notes that he has received a true answer to a question he did not ask, and asks again, in the same terms, of the district that filed the first of them. She files it on her own signature as a roll-keeper, on the ground that a copyist is the only person in the building with nothing to protect.

Emotional change: Tamsin stops waiting to be the person who is asked. She becomes the person who asks, in her own name, on her own authority, and is entirely unprotected, and finds that she has been waiting four months for permission to do that.

Power or resource change: None. A four-line request in her own hand, filed, costing her nothing and risking everything.

Continuity fact: The scope decision is **not granted** and the answer is true, formal and signed by nobody. **A second request is filed over Tamsin's own signature as a roll-keeper, naming the district rather than the standing office.** The First Seat quarterly return is still not in any reply and still in Marek's coat pocket.

Ending type: Consequence and reversal.

Next-chapter pull: The fourth month's arithmetic arrives as a list of names, and two of them are people who have already been in this book.

### Chapter 0039

POV: Nell

Location/time: The Venn lane, the reading table, the Caudle lane in Greyfen, and a room in Greyfen with an assessor in it, the third week of the fourth month.

Immediate goal: Get through twenty-three doors before the assessment with no coach, her own four hours after the sixth hour, and one honest question to put to an assessor who has decided in her own favour.

Resistance: Twenty-three houses and about forty in two districts and a hundred that have not written, and the arithmetic of a person who can only be in one room at a time, which she has already said out loud to a woman at a kitchen table and cannot now unsay. The assessor from the next province is a competent woman who has done nothing wrong and has a decision that will cost eleven houses something.
