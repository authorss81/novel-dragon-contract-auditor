## THE COUNT OF ASKINGS, SETTLED BEFORE A CARD, AND IT DOES NOT MOVE

**The count is seven at the start of this batch and it is seven at the end of it, and not one of these ten chapters contains an asking, which is a count of things done out loud in a room, one person putting a question to another.** The standing precedent is Chapter 0292 and the whole of Batch 0001. **The extra rule this batch writes into its cards, because the movement is about a form, is stronger than the standing one: no question mark in this batch is put to a person about a person at all.** Every question mark these ten files carry is about a sheet, a figure, a day, a lamp, a stair, a wage, a column or a form. That is the cure, and it is available, and the cost of it is nothing that the page loses.

**One thing in this movement is close enough to the count to be worth naming, and it does not touch it.** A House's border law requires that a thing offered to a border household at no charge be answered, and that the answer be entered, and that the entering of it be a form and not a letter. **That is not one of the seven, and Chapter 0311 says why out loud in a clerk's mouth: no person was in it, nobody in a room put it to anybody, and it is the only successful instrument in this empire that has never once been a person doing a thing to a person. ### 311 — the fourth counter, a man up the stair with a copy, and the only form that ever went to about nine hundred rooms

1. **Day.** Second day of the first week of the third month of the year after the year after the year after next, the second hour to the sixth, and about the fourth hour inside it.
2. **Room.** The fourth counter of a Table in Auremar, at the top of four flights of a stair with no door at the foot of it, a board of four things on the wall of which not one is a time, a received room at the back with four shelves and three of them full, a man of about thirty at the far end doing copies, and about four hundred bought and unfilled blanks in a box under the counter.
3. **Point of view.** Marn Ottery, thirty-four, eleven years at that counter, not a lawyer. Named in the second paragraph.
4. **What happens.** A man of about thirty-four comes up the stair with a copy he has paid fourpence for. She says the shape before she says the thing, the way she has said the shape about nine hundred times in eleven years. The thing: a House's border law on the northern marches requires that a thing offered to a border household at no charge be answered, that the answer be entered, and that the entering of it be a form and not a letter. **It is the only form in this empire ever put in front of about nine hundred rooms at once and it has been standing for about two hundred years and it has been used once and it got an answer in about three weeks.** She says out loud, in the room, why that is not one of the seven: no person was in it, nobody put it to anybody, and a class is the only thing a form can be put in front of without somebody in the room being made a party.
5. **What the reader has to hold.** That the answer arrived in a mouth at the top of a stair, out of a sheet somebody carried, and that nobody goes north, and that the instrument that worked is a form about a class and not about a person, and that she said all of it out loud and it cost her nothing any paper in this empire can hold.
6. **What it may not do.** Not ask him anything, not ask anybody anything, not name the House, not name a seat, not thank anybody, not be thanked, not resolve anything about the box under the counter, and not print the figure of seven.
7. **The `no form` wording it carries.** *There is no form in this empire that has ever been put in front of about nine hundred rooms and asked a person anything.* And in her mouth: *there is no form in this empire that would let a clerk keep a copy of a form about a household and be told what it is for.* And: *there is no form in this empire that has ever been copied out of one House's border law and used in about nine hundred rooms.*
8. **Where the emphasis goes.** Five runs, one clause each, two of them in her mouth and three in the narrator, none holding two sentences: the three weeks; the answer being a form and not a letter; no person being in it; a class being the only thing a form can be put in front of; and the copy in his hand.
9. **`about nine` budget.** Seven raw, of which four are the page figure *about nine hundred* in its three forms and are excluded, leaving three counted. The bound is about eight.
10. **Question marks.** Two, both in a customer's mouth, in bare short lines, and both about a sheet and a day and neither about a person.
11. **The lock it most nearly breaks.** The seven. It does not move, and the chapter says so in her own mouth and gives the reason, and the form it describes was put in front of about nine hundred rooms about two hundred years ago and is not an asking because no person was in it.
12. **Traps.** The House is not named and no seat is named and no notary is named; the two documents are not named; no *may*; the year name is written out in full once; the box is under the counter and is not a post and is not resolved.

### 312 — the same room over the chandler's shop, the lamp not lit, and three weeks set against about eleven

1. **Day.** Second day of the second week of the third month of the year after the year after the year after next, about the fifth hour.
2. **Room.** The room over the chandler's shop taken by the week: a table, a chair stood against the wall the bed is against, a window about a foot wide looking at another house's back, about four feet of bare wall with a nail in it and nothing on the nail, four steps and a landing, and tar and rope and lamp oil under the boards all day. The lamp is not lit until about the seventh hour and is not lit before it.
3. **Point of view.** The man of about thirty-four with a fair hand, who writes out other men's sums so that they come out even, four days a season on somebody's list, a house with a door on it and a wife at the bottom of the steps. Named in the second paragraph in his own voice and not by name.
4. **What happens.** He has the shape of it in his head. A House's own table took about eleven weeks and then said no and lost a hundred and forty years about it, and the border form took about three weeks and said yes to about nine hundred rooms at once, and the difference between the two is not speed. **A person deciding can say no, and this one said no, and it cost a hundred and forty years. A class cannot be frightened, cannot be told what a thing is going to be used for, and cannot write anything down afterwards.** He works out that the only thing that has ever got an answer in about three weeks is a thing that was not asked by a person, and that the whole of the machinery of asking at a scale is a machine for not asking. 7. **The `no form` wording it carries.** *There is no form in this empire that would let a man carry a sheet about four four hundred and thirty miles and have somebody in a room answer him for it.* And: *no form in this empire can keep a man from saying afterwards that he went.* And about his wife: *nothing in this empire is a form for asking a person whether a thing they are about to be told will be used against her.*
8. **Where the emphasis goes.** Five runs, one clause each, all in the narrator: a person deciding being able to say no; a class not being able to be frightened; the hundred and forty years; nobody going north; and the lamp not being lit.
9. **`about nine` budget.** Eight raw, four excluded as *about nine hundred*, four counted.
10. **Question marks.** One, in his own head as remembered speech, in a bare short line, about a figure, and it is not put to anybody.

### 313 — the other end of the same counter, and a law written about two hundred years ago by people with better sense

1. **Day.** Fourth day of the third week of the third month of the year after the year after the year after next, about the fifth hour at the counter.
2. **Room.** The same counter from the other end, and after the counter a room two streets off a street that goes down to the river road, with a table, a lamp, a board on the wall with the rent on it and nothing else, and a book of her own with two things in it and no office on it.
3. **Point of view.** Tamsin Rook, twenty-four, about two years at that counter, not a lawyer. Named in the second paragraph.
4. **What happens.** She says the reason out loud in the room, to nobody in particular, the way she has said the whole of a thing twice in two years. **The border law does not need a person. A border household is not a person either, and the law was written by people who had noticed that about two hundred years ago and had better sense about it than we have.** Then the other half, which is the finding and which she arrives at standing at a counter: it works because it asks a class, and a class is a heading, and a heading is a company, and a company is a thing this empire can hold wages against and give letters to and take to law, and so the one form that ever worked is the one form that makes a company out of about nine hundred rooms, and nobody wanted that, and nobody has copied it since. Her undertaking is live, unretired, unamended and unexercised, and she is not going to make a new reason out of a month in which nobody asked her. The fourth thing in the drawer is untouched, unsent and unopened, with three others. 6. **What it may not do.** Not ask anybody anything, not answer the unanswered question, not open the fourth thing, not rule a third line, not add a heading to anything, not make a romance of anything, and not make the decision about whether she trusts a man's judgment, which is hers and is unmade and is not on the page.

8. **Where the emphasis goes.** Five runs, one clause each, three in her mouth: the law not needing a person; a border household not being a person either; the two hundred years; a class being a heading and a heading being a company; and the form having been used once.
9. **`about nine` budget.** Six raw, three excluded as *about nine hundred*, three counted.
10. **Question marks.** One, in a customer's mouth, in a bare short line, about a day and not about a person.
11. **The lock it most nearly breaks.** The two boxes and the fourth thing. Neither is opened, named, counted, moved or given a heading, and the undertaking is stated as live, unretired, unamended and unexercised and is not exercised.
12. **Traps.** No *may*; her own book is a book in a room and *the book* about itself is not used; the trust decision is not made and is not gestured at; the undertaking is not given a new ordinal number, because the sixth was spent in an earlier month and this batch does not recount it.

### 314 — the room on the ground floor, eleven books, and not one name in any of them

2. **Room.** The room on the ground floor of a building off the old river road, a shelf with eleven books on it, a table and a chair, a window about two feet wide with a pane out of it that nobody has put back in about four years, and at the far end of a passage about four hundred yards long a rail about four feet high and a step worn in front of a door with nobody at it.

4. **What happens.** Border forms have come down her counter for years, about four a year, and every one of them has a heading over a column and a space for an answer and is a form and not a letter, and the answers came back in about three weeks, and the households refused them and the refusals are entered because that law says a refusal is entered. **She says out loud, to a room with nobody else in it, that her eleven books are the cheapest way anybody in this empire has found of putting a question in front of a building, and that they have never once asked anybody anything, and that the whole of the difference between her books and the border form is a name.** A book is a room. ### 315 — four hundred yards up the lane from the bay, a column with no heading over it, and nineteen days of a wage

1. **Day.** Second day of the first week of the fourth month of the year after the year after the year after next, the morning and after it the lane.
2. **Room.** A counting room of the Slade Cut Company four hundred yards up a lane off the Slade from a bay, with a bench, a shutter, and a weigh-house ledger about half a mile off; and the lane itself, with a bay at one end and a hired stone store at the other, and a mark in chalk on a wall at the height of the third link of a chain.
