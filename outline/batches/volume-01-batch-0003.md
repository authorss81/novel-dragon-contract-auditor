# Volume 01 Batch 0003 Outline: The Process and the Copies

Batch number: 3
Chapter range: 21–30

Batch goal: Finish the volume midpoint. Marek must refuse a real offer that would protect the current food supply, and be forced to replace it with a named, reviewable emergency process that does the same practical work without pretending the copied rule is safe. Then open the coalition arc: Tamsin begins building independent copies across Mosswake and Greyfen and discovers what a copy network costs when people are frightened, unhelpful, and owed money.

Starting condition: The offer is on the table, unsigned, and due by the **fourth month**, the month the flood docket closes. A supplementary request naming MC/FS and both print runs is in Examinations custody with a second number. Examinations has refused to return two provincial exhibits, granted a thirty-day extension, recorded the refusal to produce the second sealed page against Marek's name, and left a seventeen-year-old's line in the file at his request. A letter to Magistrate Rell is written, addressed, and in his coat. The two provincial files are in four buildings and no court has ruled on them. The Mosswake flood docket has about six weeks of work left in it and the district's seed reserve is spent.

**Calendar.** The day-by-day calendar for Batch 0002 is canon in `state/continuity.md` and runs from the nineteenth of the ninth month to the ninth of the eleventh. **Chapters 21–25 sit between the ninth and the end of the eleventh month**, inside the fourth month's approach. **Chapters 26–30 fall in the twelfth month and the first month**, so the certification deadline and the flood docket close inside the batch and the refusal has to survive contact with a real docket. Weekday names are not load-bearing and must not be used to anchor a chain.

**Two custody facts that are canon and easy to get wrong.** The Venn source leaf lives in the Notaries' Table's cabinet in Auremar; Sarn has no Table key and never had one; a comparison cannot be had by a clerk who is not in the room when the sleeve comes out. The provincial hold's reference carries no office name, and the First Seat's device was named aloud in open court for the first time on the twelfth. Nothing in this batch may relocate the leaf, retro-name the hold, or let the second sealed page be opened.

Starting relationships: Marek and Tamsin check each other's drafts and no longer overrule each other, but she has not told him what Pell Vey said in the Chancery yard on the fourth day of the tenth, and he has a letter he is not sending. Nell runs the reading table and has been told about their mother and has not forgiven the ninth month. Orra Dunn has a date, a register entry, and a creditor holding to the first week of the fourth month. Sarn has admitted two exhibits on her own authority and told nobody but Rell. Dren has answered a return honestly and does not know what he did.

Batch beginning: Chapters 21–22. The offer has to be examined before it can be refused, and the person who examines it should not be Marek. Chapter 21 is the district's side of the arithmetic — what a certified rule would and would not do to a food supply in the fourth month — told by people who are not the applicant. Chapter 22 is Marek's refusal, made in a room, with the arithmetic in front of him and a named replacement attached to it.

Batch escalation: Chapters 23–25. The replacement process has to survive being built by people who do not trust it. A process with a name, a form, a witness, and a review date is still a form, and Marl Crenn's warning from Chapter 15 is the batch's thesis. The fourth term has to be made usable by a clerk who has never heard of it. And the answer to the request of the eighteenth has to be written in a form that cannot be quietly read as a refusal, because Examinations will write its own version if nobody writes one first.

Batch midpoint: Chapters 23–24. The midpoint is not a discovery about a document. It is a woman with a slate at a court window discovering that a procedure she was told was safe is only safe while somebody is standing at the window, and a clerk discovering that the process he accepted puts him in a room he cannot leave, and that the cost of the process is paid in the hours of people who staff it. The batch's argument turns: the dangerous thing is not a bad rule, it is a procedure with no one named to run it.

Batch climax: Chapters 24–25. The process is named, given a form, a witness, a cost ceiling, and a review date in the fourth month, and a public register of the hearings it produces is established. The reply to the request of the eighteenth is filed and entered, and it states in its own words that the certification was offered and refused, so that the refusal is a public act and not a silence. The volume's midpoint question — is the local precedent issued rather than invented — is answered on the page by the circular, the form, the run, and a signature nobody can produce.

Batch aftermath: Chapters 26–30 fall after the refusal and are the cost the refusal creates. The coalition must now be built by people who are not clerks. A copy network is slower than a ruling and it breaks in ordinary ways: a woman who will not give her name, a foreman who has lost a post, a seed keeper whose seed was delayed once already, a seventeen-year-old who can copy and cannot be examined, a family that refuses to be a precedent, and a household in another district that loses a tool before the notice reaches them.

Pressure rotation across the ten chapters: political pressure with a decision owed (21, 22), local survival and harvest (23, 26), character and consent work (24, 28, 30), institutional and guild pressure (25, 27), emotional consequence (29), and investigation with a witness (30). No two consecutive chapters run the same way, and only one chapter is a hearing.

Support cast bound to this batch, so no chapter needs an invented clerk, foreman, constable, assessor, or witness. All are already on the page: Ivet Sarn, Ostyn Vare, Marl Crenn, Hallis Dren, Ruhl Dunnet, Odile Fenn, Rennie and Wenna Sallow, Orra and Nessa Dunn, Mara and Tavi Venn, Nell Kest, Tamsin Rook, Nevin Trost, Bryn Mawer, Ilsabet Roon, Nery Caudle, Coll Fenner, Ivo Harker, the hooded seed-barn copyist, Doctor Perrin Ashe, the customs clerk on the quay, Brammel, Wend and Pella Osk. New figures are limited to the two the cards name below and to one unnamed district works foreman.

Required character changes: Marek moves from a man who answers an offer to a man who is responsible for a procedure, which is worse and is the point. He learns that a named process with a witness and a review date is still something a frightened clerk can sign wrongly, and that the honest thing to build is a process whose failure is visible. Tamsin moves from deciding alone to building something with other people in it, and discovers that a copy network has an owner she does not. Nell discovers that the thing she built at the lane works without her and that this is not a slight. Sarn stops being a woman who records other people's decisions and becomes a woman who runs a register and a rota. Orra Dunn gets a hearing she did not expect, in her own favour, and has to argue for it.

Required power changes: **None.** Marek performs no magic in Chapters 21–30. No Roll-Answer appears and the first Seam-Sight does not recur. The inability to lie lapsed in Chapter 18 and stays lapsed; a later batch may use the fact that he can lie again as a pressure on him, but no chapter may restore the inability. No valid exception, counter-seal, mixed warrant, true-name reading, Roll Assembly, or Stage 2 ability appears anywhere in this batch, and the word "exception" does not appear in it at all.

Required world reveals: A certified rule and an uncertified sentence are two different kinds of danger and the second is worse, because it has nobody under it. A procedure needs a named person to run it, a form to record it, a witness who is not the applicant, a cost ceiling, and a review date, and a procedure without a name has no failure anybody can report. A clerk who cannot examine a person for a craft can be asked to read a paper to them, and that is a smaller thing that is actually available now. A public register of hearings is more useful than a register of duties, because it shows who came. A copy network fails on money before it fails on courage.

Open questions after the batch: Who signed the MC/FS sheet of the twenty-second of the ninth month, and on whose authority does the Standing Office of Provincial Continuities issue anything. Why did a provincial court itemise a filing the form said to leave alone, and was that one court or two. Who owns the counting house on the Kiln row. What Perry Ostrow answers to. What is in the second sealed page. What the red stitch marks. And what the fourth month actually does to a district that has been living off a court for two months.

## Chapter Cards

### Chapter 0021

POV: Hallis Dren

Location/time: The Reed Cut district water office and the district works, the second week of the eleventh month.

Immediate goal: Get the fourth month's supply and works picture in front of somebody who has to decide, before the decision is made without it, and refuse to let a court sentence be the thing that plans a seed allocation.

Resistance: Dren is a clerk whose certification is on a nail at a gate and who has already answered one return he did not understand. The district works is a foreman and nine men and a boy and a wall of forty lines with no dates on it. Nobody in the district wants to hear about a certification, because the certification is the thing that would let the office stop asking. And Dren cannot present the arithmetic alone, because a water-office clerk presenting a legal argument is a man choosing a side.

Information revealed: What a certified rule would do to the current supply is measurable, and the measurement is not reassuring. Forty-six clerks deciding individually will produce forty-six different answers and four hundred and sixty relief rolls that go out late in the worst month in nine years; a certified rule would produce one answer and get the rolls out on time, and the cost of that one answer is that a person who has not been heard is treated as though she had been. Dren is the first person in the story to say the certification out loud as a working convenience rather than a threat.

Action or decision: Dren compiles the district's own arithmetic and sends it to the court, the water office's two lines, and to Examinations, on his own letterhead, with his name and his mother's canal house on it, because a district that says nothing is a district that has agreed. He does not say what should happen. He says what would happen either way, in numbers a foreman can check.

Emotional change: Dren stops being the man who signed a general paper to clear a backlog and becomes the man who put the next one in writing, knowing what it is, having chosen it.

Power or resource change: None. A district office has no authority over any of this and Dren says so in the same document.

Continuity fact: The district's fourth-month supply position is now a written figure that exists in three places, and it is the arithmetic both sides of the certification question have to argue from. The Reed Cut return of the eleventh of the tenth is not mentioned by Dren and nobody in the story knows what it contained.

Ending type: Discovery / cost.

Next-chapter pull: The arithmetic arrives at the Roll Hall on the same day as the offer, and the two documents are put on the same table by accident.

### Chapter 0022

POV: Marek

Location/time: Auremar, the copying alcove and the Received Room, the second and third weeks of the eleventh month.

Immediate goal: Refuse the certification in a room with a witness, on the record, and attach to the refusal the thing he will accept instead, so that a refusal is not a vacuum.

Resistance: The offer is genuinely good and Trost has said so in writing. Refusing costs the current food supply a season of certainty that forty-six clerks cannot supply. The fourth term cannot be made into a general rule, because a rule is a form and forms travel, and Marl Crenn has said that out loud about a different form in a different trade. And Marek cannot offer a process on his own authority, because a Stage 0 clerk with an unstable standing is the last person who should be naming a procedure other people will have to staff.

Information revealed: The difference between the certification and what is actually needed is not the sentence, it is the *review*. A certified rule with a review date and a person named to run the hearings is safe enough to feed people for six months. An uncertified sentence with nobody under it is not safe at all, because forty-six clerks will each decide and none of them will be answerable. The process Marek accepts must therefore do one thing the certification would have done: make somebody answerable, in writing, by the fourth month.

Action or decision: Marek refuses the certification in the Received Room with Nevin Trost in it, has the refusal entered, and asks for — and gets — a named, reviewable emergency process instead: flood-season continuity filings that are not categorised away, heard individually on request, recorded in a public register, with a witness who is not the applicant, a stated cost ceiling from the district works, and a review in the fourth month. He does not write the process himself. He writes down what it must contain and hands it to the court, the water office, and a copyist.

Emotional change: Marek understands that being responsible for a procedure is a larger thing than being right about a file, and that the only part of it he can keep is the part that can be read by somebody he has never met.

Power or resource change: None. The process is the court's, the water office's, and a register's, not his, and no burden is placed on his body.

Continuity fact: The certification is refused on the record, the offer's terms are in the file, and a named, reviewable emergency process is accepted in its place with a review in the fourth month. The process is not a rule, is not a precedent, and does not apply to any file but the flood-season filings in the districts that adopt it.

Ending type: Decision / earned turn.

Next-chapter pull: The process is agreed and nobody has been told, and Tamsin points out that a process with no name in a room has no failure anybody can report.

### Chapter 0023

POV: Tamsin

Location/time: The Mosswake Roll Court, the clerk's window and the copying room, the fourth week of the eleventh month.

Immediate goal: Get the process into a form a district can actually run, and find out who is going to staff it, because a form with no person in it is the thing she has spent six weeks explaining.

Resistance: Sarn is a cautious clerk who has been wrong once and has just started a register, and starting a second register is a thing she has to be talked into by somebody who owes her a debt she cannot name. The court has four hundred and sixty rolls to certify in six weeks and the process adds hearings, not removes them. The witness cannot be the applicant, and there are very few people in a flood district who are not either an applicant or a creditor.

Information revealed: The process costs people hours, and the hours come out of the same district that has already lost nineteen sacks of seed reserve. A hearing that is not staffed is a hearing that does not happen for the people who need it most, which is a second inheritance echo in the shape of administration. Tamsin works out that the process is safe only while somebody is standing at the window, and that the person at the window has to be somebody whose living does not depend on the answer.

Action or decision: Tamsin writes the process into a form with a box for the hearing, a box for the witness, a box for the cost, and a box for the next review, and gets Sarn to keep the register of hearings on the same slate as the register of duties, so that a person can see in one place both what is owed and who came about it. She names the cost ceiling from Dren's figures and writes in that exceeding it stops the process rather than continuing it unpaid.

Emotional change: Tamsin learns she is building something she will have to hand to other people, and that the handover is the point.

Power or resource change: None. A form, a register, and a cost ceiling.

Continuity fact: The Mosswake court keeps a **public register of hearings** alongside the register of pending duties, in Sarn's hand, at the same window, at a shilling a copy. The process's form has four boxes and a named witness who is not the applicant. Exceeding the stated cost stops the process.

Ending type: Practical turn.

Next-chapter pull: The first person to use the form is a woman who has been told by a broker that she does not have to come, and who comes anyway, and who brings a paper nobody at the court has seen.

### Chapter 0024

POV: Nell

Location/time: The Venn lane, the reading table, and the Greyfen dye-house, the end of the eleventh month and the first of the twelfth.

Immediate goal: Find out whether the fourth term can be used by a woman who cannot read it, and whether the lane can answer a person who has no clerk to send.

Resistance: The fourth term is written in the language of a court and assumes a person can read a paragraph and understand a clause, and about half the lane cannot do the first thing. The rota reads papers aloud but cannot advise, and advising is what people will ask for. A seven-year-old can ask them. A child can carry a message. A woman who has refused to give her name can read a paper aloud in a schoolroom hand and say nothing else and that is enough. Action or decision: Nell writes the four questions with Tamsin, keeps them on the edge of the trestle table, and hands the table to the lane for good — no rota of four, no rota at all, a chalk board and a list of names people can add to themselves. Emotional change: Nell discovers the reading table works without her and that this is not a slight, and that the part of it she misses is the part where somebody arrives frightened and she is the one who is standing there.

Power or resource change: None. Four questions, a chalk board, and a paid job.

Continuity fact: The fourth term is in use in the lane as four plain questions, on a chalk board, in nobody's name, and the reading table belongs to the lane outright. Ending type: Earned victory with a cost.

### Chapter 0025

POV: Marek

Location/time: Auremar, the Received Room and the copying alcove, the second and third weeks of the twelfth month.

Immediate goal: Get the answer to the request of the eighteenth written and entered before Examinations writes its own, and make the refusal of the certification a public act rather than a silence.

Resistance: Examinations will draft a reply whether or not one is drafted for it, and a reply drafted by the office is a reply that reads as a refusal by a clerk who declined. The reply has to state the offer, the refusal, the process, and the cost, and every one of those four facts is somebody else's decision being reported by a man with no standing. Pell is the supervisor Marek must hand the whole thing to, and Pell's name has not yet been on anything. And the signature for the MC/FS sheet of the twenty-second of the ninth month cannot be produced, and a request that names a document nobody can sign is a request about stationery.

Information revealed: A reply can be made to carry a refusal as a fact rather than as an absence, and the form of the words is the whole of the difference. The Standing Office of Provincial Continuities is not, on anything in the file, an office with a person at the top of it; it is a seal, a series, and a print run, and nobody in Auremar can say who authorised the sheet. That is the volume's midpoint answer: the local precedent was **issued, not invented**, and the issuing is traceable to a series and a date and not to a man.

Action or decision: Marek hands the reply to Pell Vey to sign, because a reply about a district court is not a junior clerk's to sign, and then finds that Pell asks to see the corridor conversation he is not supposed to know about, and Marek has to decide whether to tell Tamsin first. Tamsin tells him herself, in the alcove, in one sentence, having decided that the letter to Rell going out is the thing that makes it safe to say.

Emotional change: The secrecy that has cost Marek everything in this volume costs him one last time, and Tamsin ends it without asking, which is exactly what she did in Chapter 16 and is the opposite of what he did in Chapter 5.

Power or resource change: None. The reply is entered; the offer and the refusal are both in the public record; the process has a name, a form, a register, a cost ceiling, and a review in the fourth month; and the request survives because a refused offer and a live process are two facts, not one silence.

Continuity fact: The reply to the request of the eighteenth is entered and states in its own words that a bounded certification was offered and refused and what was accepted instead. Pell Vey's name is on the reply. Tamsin has told Marek about the yard. The letter to Magistrate Rell is sent.

Ending type: Decision / resolution of the midpoint question.

Next-chapter pull: Two copies of the reply leave Auremar for provinces that have never heard of any of this, and one of them is going to a place where a woman's name is in a surety line and nobody has warned her.

### Chapter 0026

POV: Tamsin

Location/time: Mosswake, the Greyfen lane, and the Venn reading table, the fourth and fifth weeks of the twelfth month.

Immediate goal: Start the independent copies in earnest and find out how many people will actually make one, and who pays for the paper.

Resistance: A copy costs paper, and paper costs money, and the people with money are the people with trades to protect and the people without money are the ones being evicted. The hooded woman will not give her name and cannot hold a register. The professional copyist in Greyfen charged nothing once and says that was because she was angry and she is not angry now. Sarn's copying room is a public room with a bar on the door. And a copy network is only as good as its worst keeper, and one keeper is a foreman who has just been audited and has stopped answering letters.

Information revealed: Independent copies are not a movement. They are about forty people with paper, and the reason they work is not courage and is not the law: it is that a person who has a copy does not have to go and ask anybody for anything. The cost is that somebody has to pay for the paper, and the only people who can pay are the ones with a reason to.

Action or decision: Tamsin builds a small, honest, countable thing: a list of who holds a copy, in whose hand, of what, and dated. No network, no name, no seal, no promise. She asks each holder for one thing and one thing only — that the copy be read to anybody who comes to the house with a paper — and pays for the paper out of her own wages and refuses help from the Chancery, which would make it an office and would end it.

Emotional change: Tamsin understands that she is the person who will be asked when a copy turns out to be wrong, and that this is the price of being the one who made it, and she had expected to be asked for something else.

Power or resource change: None. A list, about forty copies, and a wage.

Continuity fact: The independent copy list exists, is dated, and is kept in Tamsin's own hand in her own book. The copies are of the Venn ruling's five terms, the reply of the thirteenth, and the four questions. Nobody in the Chancery knows it exists.

Ending type: Cost / construction.

Next-chapter pull: One of the forty does not answer, and the one who does not answer is in Emberwatch.

Immediate goal: Get the last house to sign the amended clause before the fourth month's assessment, and keep the levy from landing on a second wage somewhere she has not looked.

### Chapter 0028

Ending type: Decision / consequence.

Next-chapter pull: A woman at the window in the ninth week has been told by a broker that she does not have to come to the court at all, and she has come, and the paper in her hand is a form with a run on it.

### Chapter 0029

### Chapter 0030

POV: Tamsin

Location/time: Auremar and a district three hundred miles downriver, the eleventh and twelfth weeks of the twelfth month.

Immediate goal: Get one household in a district nobody has written to heard before the fourth month, and find out what is actually happening in a place where the form was obeyed.

Resistance: The household has lost a tool, and the loss is not a debt and cannot be put on a file and is not a guarantor duty, and a person who has lost a tool is not a person the legal system has a box for. The district obeyed the routine-column rule, which means there is no itemised filing, which means there is no document, which means the case is a woman's account against a district's memory. And Tamsin is a copyist who cannot cross-examine a district, and Perry Ostrow is in the building, and her husband cannot read.

Information revealed: A district that obeyed the form is not safer; it is simply quieter, and the absence of a document is itself the injury, because a person with no document has nothing to show anybody.
