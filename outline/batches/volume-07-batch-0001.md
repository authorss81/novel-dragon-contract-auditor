### 301 — the man of about thirty-four, his ordinary week, and the count

1. **Day.** Fourth day of the third week of the twelfth month of the year after the year after next, about the fourth hour, and the lamp is not lit until about the seventh hour and is not lit before it.
2. **Room.** The room over a chandler's shop, taken by the week: a table, a chair stood against the wall the bed is against, a window about a foot wide looking at another house's back, about four feet of bare wall with a nail in it and nothing ever hung on it, four steps and a landing, and tar and rope and lamp oil under the boards all day.
3. **Point of view.** A man of about thirty-four with a fair hand, who writes out other men's sums so that they come out even, four days a season on somebody's list, a house with a door on it and a wife at the bottom of the steps. Named on the page in the second paragraph in his own voice and not by name.
4. **What happens.** He is on nobody's list this week. He goes through the rest of it, which he does about once in four weeks, and this is the once: the two in their boxes, the question in the second of the eleven books, the bank and the guarantee and about sixty children, the Lowcross bill, the four men in a bay, the two of seventeen, nobody thanked anywhere, and the two of the practice about four hundred and thirty miles apart. He writes out one small sum for a man who cannot pay him. He does not go and look at anything.
5. **What the reader has to hold.** That a practice is a count of things done and not of people, and that a count counting people would be six, and that the two men in it are about four hundred and thirty miles apart and have never met and are not going to be put in a room by anything in this empire, and that the bill for his being findable is not his and stands on somebody else's account and he found that out at second hand and did not go.
6. **What it may not do.** Not ask anybody anything, not light the lamp before about the seventh hour, not go and look, not be thanked, and print no figure for the count other than the one figure of seven.
7. **The `no form` wording it carries.** *There is nothing in this empire that can be sent to a room, and a room is the only place this matter has ever been anything, and there is no form in this empire for putting two men in one of them.* And in a mouth, second one: *there is no form in this empire that would let him ask whether she minds being told.*
8. **Where the emphasis goes.** Four runs, one clause each, all in the narrator, all single sentences: the figure of seven; the once in four weeks; the two of them about four hundred and thirty miles apart; and the bill for being findable not being his. No run in a mouth.
9. **`about nine` budget.** Eight raw, and of those about four are the page figures *about nine hundred*, *about nine hundred rooms* and *about nine of about nine hundred*, which are excluded, leaving about four counted.
10. **Question marks.** None. The chapter's whole claim is that nothing was asked.
11. **The lock it most nearly breaks.** *This year* is not used at all and the fourth year name is written in full once, in the third paragraph, and not abbreviated.
12. **Traps.** No *the book* about itself; the wall, the nail and the lamp are the room and are not a symbol and are not said to be; no swept word; no *may*; no card language; the count of seven appears once in the file and in no other file of the batch.

### 302 — the woman of twenty in a counting room, four hundred and thirty miles up this river

## The standing lesson, and what this batch's own repair taught it

**A bound is measured after the last edit, with the method stated. A sweep that has not been run is not a result. A sweep that returns the same number twice has not measured anything. A test that returns zero is evidence of nothing until you have checked what shape of damage it is blind to, and a check that is blind is written down as blind in the same record that claims it clean. A repair record must name the phase that made the repair. A corrected figure must be named as corrected beside the wrong one rather than overwritten. And a record that prints a figure under a heading that measures something else is a record that has not said which set of files it measured.**

**Two additions this batch earned. One: a scripted paragraph-splitter that cuts at a sentence boundary will cut inside a bold run, because the pinned sentence reading treats a terminator immediately followed by an emphasis marker as a sentence end; eight of the ten files were damaged that way and every damaged file was caught by the parity test and the line-break test and by reading, and not one of them was caught by the sentence bound, which is the sharpest available statement of the lesson and of why a bound is not a check.** Two: **a case-sensitive search is a search with a blind spot, and the blind spot is the start of a sentence.** Both are the standing lesson in the two forms this batch actually met it in, and both are carried to Batch 0002 in its prompt.

**A self-audit is not a review. A second pass by the writing hand is not a second reading. No phase in this repository has ever been reviewed by anybody but the agent that wrote it, `reviews/` holds two files and both were produced by the writing agent and its README records the fallback, and the two batch review logs are not in a tree a run can open because `logs/` is gitignored, so no log is quoted here and the contents of `logs/` are not printed as a fact. Never call a self-audit, a second pass or a repair a review.**
