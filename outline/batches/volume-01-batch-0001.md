# Volume 01 Batch 0001 Outline: The First Correction

Batch number: 1

Chapter range: 1–10

Batch goal: Establish Marek's ordinary legal work, make the Venn alteration a human problem with a visible winter deadline, prove that the same substituted language is spreading through a second Mosswake household, and force Marek to choose public accountability over a private fix. The batch ends with Corvin's local network exposed but the larger authority still hidden.

Starting condition: No novel prose exists yet. Marek is a Stage 0 Clerk's Eye in the Auremar Roll Hall. He has a disputed Kest property-and-debt record, no independent legal standing, and a missing mother's name. Tamsin Rook is a junior roll-keeper assigned to his evidence; Nell Kest is preparing a dye-house contract; Pell Vey controls the Notaries' Table.

Starting relationships: Marek and Tamsin are professionally curious but not yet trusting. Tamsin respects exact copying and challenges Marek's habit of treating suspicion as authority. Nell wants an independent trade and resents being shielded from information. Pell is a useful supervisor whose preference for administrative continuity is beginning to hide deliberate alteration.

Batch midpoint: Chapters 3–5. Tamsin finds the unfiled Dunn summary that would make Nessa a guarantor, Pell asks Marek to treat the Venn correction as routine, and Nell's contract plus Marek's concealment begin the trust break. The batch's real midpoint is the discovery that the second household is already in the machinery, not the later ruling that activates it.

Batch climax: Chapters 7–10. At a public evidentiary rehearing, Marek and Tamsin establish the original text, the witness order, and Corvin's seal-broker trail. Marek obtains a ruling that clears Tavi's personal debt, preserves the field and Sivra Oris's gate and navigation duty, and requires a separate signed obligation for any successor. The court activates the Dunn consequence. Corvin is arrested after the copies and broker records are secured, but the sealed First Seat instruction sends the larger question beyond Mosswake.

Required character changes: Marek moves from quiet private investigation to a public request for audit, while recognizing that his protective secrecy harms Nell and Tamsin. Tamsin chooses to preserve and publish a copy against Marek's first instruction, establishing her as an independent colleague. Nell keeps her own agency and obtains a visible exit clause. Pell's clean authority becomes visibly compromised; Corvin becomes an accountable local criminal rather than an abstract danger.

Required power changes: Marek begins at Stage 0 and does not perform magic in the first seven chapters. The authorized Venn audit in Chapter 8 produces unstable Seam-Sight with counterpressure, fatigue, and a temporary inability to lie about one named obligation, plus exactly one Roll-Answer that reports the operative Venn record and its inherited effect. No valid exception, counter-seal, mixed warrant, true-name reading, or Stage 2 ability appears in this batch.

Required world reveals: The Venn instrument is a fixed-term scale contract whose later correction substitutes “the present holder” for a named signer; a dated flood memorandum is the administrative paper used to justify the later correction; inheritance echo travels through copied precedent and institutional acceptance; Orra and Nessa Dunn are the second affected household; Corvin sells predictable corrections and seal access; a First Seat instruction reaches the local court; an unexplained red-thread-like mark appears in the copied Venn file.

Open questions after the batch: Who ordered Corvin's corrections, why does the sealed instruction use First Seat authority, what did Ilyra copy before the Ashfall fire, and why does the Dunn precedent resemble the Kest record? The Kest claim remains legally disputed and Marek's mother remains officially erased.

Required continuity updates: Record Tamsin's preserved copies, the witnessed audit authorization, the transport chain for the original Venn leaf, the flood memorandum, the unsettled audit request in Chancery custody, Corvin's arrest and custody status, Marek's shallow-reading cost, and the unexplained red-thread-like mark. Keep the First Seat instruction as one administrative form and do not identify its author yet.

Expected batch word range: Approximately 22,000–32,000 words for ten chapters, or roughly 2,200–3,200 words per chapter. Treat this as a flexible guide; complete every scene, aftermath beat, and emotional turn even if a chapter runs longer.

## Chapter Cards

### Chapter 0001

POV: Marek

Location/time: Auremar Roll Hall, late afternoon during the ordinary inheritance docket.

Immediate goal: Complete the Venn inheritance packet accurately enough to send it to the Mosswake Roll Court before the evening courier leaves.

Resistance: Pell expects routine certification, the file has been compressed into a summary, and the witness marks on the summary do not match the small source image Tamsin finds while copying it.

Information revealed: The Venn packet cites a later court correction that is not attached to the original source leaf. The correction changes who owes a winter-seed debt and uses language broad enough to be copied into another household's file.

Action or decision: Marek refuses to certify from the summary, requests the original file, and asks Tamsin to make a private comparison copy before the packet closes. He does not yet know who altered it or whether the change is fraud rather than careless filing.

Emotional change: Marek's ordinary competence shifts from pride to unease when a clean page disagrees with the record beneath it. Tamsin becomes curious enough to keep looking, but neither treats the discrepancy as proof.

Power or resource change: Stage 0 only. Marek gains a preserved copy and a witness-list question, not magical perception or independent authority.

Continuity fact: Aldren Venn signed the original seven-year water-and-field instrument; Mara witnessed it but did not sign a later continuation or correction.

Ending type: Discovery.

Next-chapter pull: Marek travels to Mosswake to inspect the field and hear Mara before the debt notice is served.

### Chapter 0002

POV: Marek

Location/time: The Venn farm and Reed Cut channel, the following morning. Marek travels to Mosswake with Tamsin carrying the comparison packet as the assigned copyist.

Immediate goal: Establish which physical work and obligations belong to the Venn family before the court treats Tavi as a debtor.

Resistance: Mara distrusts a city clerk, Aldren's absence makes every practical answer incomplete, and flood water has begun backing up against the lower field. Tavi wants to work the channel, not discuss inheritance in front of neighbors.

Information revealed: Aldren's original text with Sivra Oris names Aldren as the winter-seed debtor and field-side maintainer, while Sivra holds the Reed Cut gate and navigation duty. Mara witnessed that text and never signed a later inheritance stipulation. Tavi has signed nothing.

Action or decision: Marek separates Aldren's named field-side duty and Sivra's separate gate duty from Tavi's alleged debt and records Mara's account as testimony rather than treating grief as consent. He refuses to promise that the court will rule in their favor.

Emotional change: Mara moves from guarded refusal to cautious cooperation when Marek admits the danger to the winter. Tavi's relief is mixed with anger that adults have made her debt feel inevitable.

Power or resource change: Marek gains direct field evidence and a witness relationship, but no legal authority. The first concrete deadline is established: the winter-seed notice will issue before the flood gates are inspected.

Continuity fact: The field may remain with Mara and Tavi for the term; Sivra Oris's gate and navigation duty survives independently of Tavi's alleged personal debt.

Ending type: Pressure / consequence.

Next-chapter pull: Tamsin discovers that the same “the present holder” wording appears in a neighboring seed-barn file.

### Chapter 0003

POV: Tamsin

Location/time: The Mosswake Roll Court copying room, midday, during Marek's assigned field visit.

Immediate goal: Find the source of the Venn correction and determine whether the wording is unique to the Venn family.

Resistance: The local clerk says the correction is a settled precedent and refuses to reopen a file during flood season. Orra Dunn is waiting outside with her fifteen-year-old daughter Nessa, who has been told to expect a debt she never signed.

Information revealed: The Dunn seed-barn file repeats the same phrase, but the original Dunn instrument names Orra as the only signer. A copied, unfiled court summary has added Nessa as guarantor without a witnessed hearing. The court has not yet made that summary operative, but Nessa has already been told to expect a debt.

Action or decision: Tamsin copies the Dunn file, records the clerk's refusal, and asks Nessa directly what she signed. Nessa has signed nothing. Tamsin brings the discrepancy to Marek as evidence that the problem is not confined to one widow, not as a ruling that has already taken Nessa's field.

Emotional change: Tamsin stops treating the Venn case as an assignment and starts treating the copied wording as a public danger. She feels the first professional pride of having preserved a record that an institution wanted left alone.

Power or resource change: Tamsin gains an independent copy, a second witness, and a clearer picture of the network. She gains no magical ability or official standing.

Continuity fact: Orra Dunn and Nessa Dunn are the second affected household; Nessa has not signed the copied, unfiled court summary that names her as guarantor.

Ending type: Revelation / escalation.

Next-chapter pull: Marek must compare the two files in the Chancery before Pell closes the Venn packet for routine processing.

### Chapter 0004

POV: Marek

Location/time: Pell's Notaries' Table in Auremar, the next day after the Mosswake field visit.

Immediate goal: Obtain a formal comparison of the original Venn leaf, the filed correction, and the Dunn precedent before the matter is certified as settled.

Resistance: Pell treats the discrepancy as a filing error and warns that reopening a flood-era precedent will delay winter relief. Tamsin has already preserved a copy, and the source leaf is stored in a locked cabinet that requires Pell's authorization.

Information revealed: The correction's ink, seal pressure, and witness mark do not match the original. A payment notation points toward Corvin Dray's seal-broker office, but the notation is not enough to identify the person who ordered the alteration.

Action or decision: Marek requests a written comparison and records Pell's refusal to explain the source. Tamsin keeps the copied Dunn page visible rather than hiding it in a private drawer. Marek chooses a slower, reviewable path over a quiet correction that would protect his job.

Emotional change: Marek's respect for Pell becomes distrust, while Tamsin's trust in him becomes conditional on whether he will let the record challenge him. Pell remains calm and helpful enough that the conflict is not yet a public break.

Power or resource change: Marek gains an official comparison request and a documented chain of custody. He still has no power to compel a hearing.

Continuity fact: Corvin is a broker who profits from predictable filing errors; his network is a criminal and political infrastructure, not proof of a single mastermind.

Ending type: Institutional pressure.

Next-chapter pull: A sealed administrative hold threatens to freeze the Venn field before the comparison can be heard.

### Chapter 0005

POV: Nell

Location/time: The dye-house workroom near Greyfen, evening. Tamsin accompanies Marek because Nell asked for the contract language to be read in front of two witnesses.

Immediate goal: Secure a better wage and a contract that does not turn a craft year into an inherited obligation.

Resistance: Nell cannot afford to lose the position, the foreman has already promised work, and the contract's continuation language is buried in a dense trade schedule. Marek arrives intending to remove the document before she can see it.

Information revealed: The dye-house contract lets a guild recover tool debts from “the next household worker” if the present worker leaves. Nell has signed the wage schedule but not the continuation. A copied evidence packet in Marek's satchel contains the same language as the Venn correction.

Action or decision: Nell finds the packet, refuses Marek's attempt to manage her reaction, and forces him to state what he knows and what he fears. She asks for the contract to be read aloud before signing. Marek finally admits that he withheld the packet because he thought ignorance would protect her. Tamsin notices that Marek also kept an unauthorized draft exception from her along with the Nell packet; the draft is an invalid Stage 2 attempt and cannot be used. She refuses to work beside him until he discloses both.

Emotional change: Nell's anger is practical and specific: Marek's protection still gives him the power to decide when she becomes afraid. Tamsin's professional trust in Marek cools because he has made the same decision for her and hidden an invalid draft as if secrecy were a valid legal move. She refuses to let him turn that concealment into protection. She does not hide the copied Dunn page from Nell.

Power or resource change: Nell gains a voice in the contract process and the beginning of a visible exit clause. Marek loses the illusion that secrecy is a harmless substitute for consent.

Continuity fact: No counter-seal exists yet. The copied packet is ordinary evidence, not a magical exception. Marek's concealment of the Nell packet from Tamsin is the first concrete trust break; they do not repair it instantly.

Ending type: Cost / relationship turn.

Next-chapter pull: The flood deadline forces Marek to choose a public rehearing that may delay Tavi's winter grain.

### Chapter 0006

POV: Marek

Location/time: Mosswake flood terrace and the river court's emergency stair, two days later.

Immediate goal: Get the Venn matter into a public evidentiary rehearing before the winter-seed notice and flood inspection converge.

Resistance: A public rehearing will pause the field's seizure until the court rules, and the flood may destroy the lower harvest. A private exception could preserve the grain immediately but would exclude Tavi and Mara from the decision and violate the procedure Marek is trying to protect.

Information revealed: Magistrate Leda Rell's court has accepted the corrected precedent for routine flood cases. The local court can hear witnesses and material disputes, but it cannot be crossed by Marek's personal authority.

Action or decision: Marek chooses the public rehearing despite the winter risk. He gives Tamsin the full comparison packet, asks Mara and Tavi to decide whether they will testify, and prepares a practical channel plan rather than a magical shortcut.

Emotional change: Marek feels the cost of choosing a process he cannot control. Tamsin sees him share the danger instead of deciding for the family, but she makes him promise not to turn the hearing into a private investigation.

Power or resource change: Marek obtains a scheduled public forum and a material flood plan. He does not gain authority, an exception, or protection from the consequences of delay.

Continuity fact: The court proceeding is an ordinary local evidentiary rehearing, not Marek's later First-Hearing Auditor ability.

Ending type: Decision / earned pressure.

Next-chapter pull: The court's published witness order has been altered before the hearing begins.

### Chapter 0007

POV: Tamsin

Location/time: Mosswake Roll Court's river courtroom, the morning of the rehearing. Tamsin is present as the assigned copyist who accompanied Marek's field visit and has brought the comparison packet.

Immediate goal: Ensure the rehearing hears the actual source witness and the original Venn leaf in a usable order.

Resistance: Pell objects to the comparison copy, the clerk has placed a broker witness first, and Magistrate Rell warns that a broad ruling may be the only way to keep the flood channels functioning. Corvin's witness has arrived with a certificate that looks valid until its seal pressure is checked.

Information revealed: The witness order was changed after the parties arrived. The certificate bearing a copied scale seal repeats the copied third witness notch. Tamsin's memory of who entered the room and the sequence of copied pages provides ordinary evidence that challenges the certificate.

Action or decision: Tamsin asks the court to pause, identifies the altered order from memory and paper sequence, and uses the comparison copy plus the filing dates to obtain a witnessed preservation order and a limited audit authorization for the original leaf. The Mosswake Roll Court clerk releases the source, produces the dated flood memorandum issued by the district water office, and seals both in a marked document sleeve for transfer to the court under a recorded chain of custody. She does not claim that Corvin's witness's presence proves his guilt; she shows why his document cannot be accepted unexamined.

Emotional change: Tamsin feels the fear of being the clerk who made the official record inconvenient. She chooses public competence over quiet obedience, and Marek follows her decision rather than taking over.

Power or resource change: The comparison packet becomes admissible evidence and the broker trail widens. Tamsin gains professional standing; Marek gains no new magical stage.

Continuity fact: Corvin's network falsifies witness lists and sells copied scale seals, but his larger political authority remains unidentified.

Ending type: Reversal.

Next-chapter pull: The court accepts that the Venn correction was altered, but demands a ruling that can also address immediate flood necessity.

### Chapter 0008

POV: Marek

Location/time: Mosswake Roll Court's river courtroom, the afternoon of the ruling.

Immediate goal: Secure a judgment that clears Tavi without pretending the channel has no maintenance duty.

Resistance: Rell needs language that lets the court act quickly during future floods. Pell proposes wording that makes the present holder a guarantor. The Dunn file is already waiting as proof that a broad sentence will travel.

Information revealed: The original Venn text supports a fixed-term field duty for Aldren and a gate and navigation duty for Sivra Oris, but not a debt for Tavi. The proposed public-necessity sentence would turn occupancy into consent.

Action or decision: Marek argues from the original material record and accepts a narrow interim channel arrangement rather than a blanket exception. The court voids Tavi's personal debt, preserves the field during the term, keeps Sivra Oris's gate and navigation duty, and requires any future successor to sign separately. The court records the broad public-necessity sentence, and the consequence reaches the Dunn file.

The limited audit authorized in Chapter 7 is exercised at the rehearing; after the ruling and its physical cost settle, one Roll-Answer appears against the operative filed record, including the court's public-necessity gloss. The reading is accurate about what the court has just done. Tavi's personal debt is gone, and the file now reaches whoever occupies the field, so the reading names that occupant rather than the woman the ruling cleared:

> Named: the present holder of the Venn field.
> Promised: the present holder may be joined as guarantor until full review.
> Carried after death: the record carries a guarantor duty to whoever occupies the field unless a new hearing ends it.

Emotional change: Marek feels the first genuine relief and immediately understands that his victory has created another vulnerable person. He has saved Tavi by proving a seam, not by becoming safe to use.

Power or resource change: Marek reaches unstable Seam-Sight. He can see one active chain and its immediate beneficiary, but the first reading is shallow: fatigue and counterpressure leave a temporary inability to lie about one named obligation. He still cannot write a valid exception or use a counter-seal.

Continuity fact: The Roll-Answer reports the current legal effect, not whether the record is fair; the harmful precedent names a present holder as guarantor until full review, which is the mechanism that reaches Orra and Nessa Dunn.

Ending type: Earned victory with reversal.

Next-chapter pull: The Dunn clerk arrives with the ruling's operative copy: the previously unfiled summary has now been used to name Nessa as guarantor, and she asks whether the court has just made her a debtor by another name.

### Chapter 0009

Location/time: The Venn farm, Dunn seed barn, and a temporary public reading table, the following days.

Immediate goal: Deliver the ruling in a form each household can use and make sure the new duty is heard rather than copied as a slogan.

Resistance: Mara wants the field secured immediately and distrusts any promise that depends on the capital. Orra is afraid that a hearing will expose the whole household to creditors. Nell has not forgiven Marek and refuses to let Tamsin turn the reading into a campaign speech.

Nell signs a new wage schedule with a visible exit clause after hearing the language read aloud; the dye-house guild still has to accept the clause. Marek admits that he cannot guarantee the larger chain is safe and stops pretending that his narrow result is a complete victory.

Emotional change: Tamsin feels useful but not in control; she has learned that public access does not erase unequal consequences. Mara receives relief without being promised security. Nell's trust returns only as a willingness to keep working with Marek under stated limits.

Power or resource change: The ruling becomes a physical public copy rather than a private favor. Marek's Seam-Sight remains unstable and cannot identify who first introduced the chain. No Roll Assembly or mixed warrant exists yet.

Ending type: Aftermath / discovery.

Next-chapter pull: Corvin's payment ledger is scheduled for inspection, and a sealed First Seat instruction appears in the custody chain.

### Chapter 0010

POV: Marek

Location/time: Corvin's seal-broker office and a roadside hearing room in Auremar, one week later.

Immediate goal: Secure the broker ledger, protect the witnesses who agreed to testify, and move the Venn case into a formal audit rather than a private settlement.

Resistance: Corvin claims the corrections were ordinary disaster administration and offers to restore the Venn file if Marek closes the comparison. Pell's name appears on an authorization that is genuine but incomplete. Auremar's Chancery constable can arrest only under a joint order from the Mosswake Roll Court, and the office is full of clients whose livelihoods depend on the broker; a direct seizure could destroy the records before they are copied.

Information revealed: Corvin's ledger contains payments tied to copied witness marks in Venn, Dunn, and two provincial files. A sealed First Seat instruction is an administrative form requiring the Mosswake correction to remain categorized as routine; its relationship to the other files is not yet known, and it does not identify Seryn or explain who authorized its use.

Action or decision: Marek works with Tamsin and the Mosswake Roll Court to copy the ledger under a public inventory. Under a joint order of the Mosswake Roll Court and Auremar's Chancery, Auremar's constable arrests Corvin after the copy is complete. Marek accepts Corvin's cooperation only after witnesses are protected and refuses a private settlement that would erase the chain. The formal audit request names the First Seat instruction and asks the Chancery to preserve the original source files. Marek expects the request may be accepted only in a limited form, but he cannot control its scope.

Emotional change: Marek feels the satisfaction of catching a criminal and the larger fear that the arrest changes nothing. Tamsin chooses to stand beside the request without pretending their evidence will win easily. Corvin is arrested, but the seal on that instruction points beyond his office.

Power or resource change: Marek retains Stage 0 authority plus unstable Seam-Sight, gains a public audit request and a copied ledger, and loses the option of resolving the case quietly. No new warrant or counter-seal is granted.

Continuity fact: Corvin is the first concrete enemy and a valuable witness, not the final antagonist. The First Seat's instruction is an institutional reach, not proof that Seryn personally ordered every local fraud.

Ending type: Decision / earned hook.

Next-chapter pull: The Chancery takes custody of the audit request and assigns the case to a higher office without deciding its scope; the red-thread clue and the First Seat instruction now point toward Volume 2's First Hearing.
