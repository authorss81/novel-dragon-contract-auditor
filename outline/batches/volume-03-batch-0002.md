# Volume 03 Batch 0002 Outline: A Person Who May Be Made a Party

Batch number: 2 (Volume 03)

Chapter range: 111–120

Batch goal: **Open the road and pay for opening it.** The first ten chapters found the shape of the problem and put a person in a box and a body nowhere. This batch takes the one thing that was found and not followed — **a name on a ninety-one-year page that is in four houses at the north of the ford** — and the one door that was found and found shut, and it puts them in the same matter and makes the district pay for both. The road runs through a person the instrument touches and the only way to be touched is to be harmed, and on the second day of the third month **nobody on that bank has been harmed yet**. Over ten chapters the batch finds out what a notary's table actually asks a person for; finds that a person *is* findable in this batch and by an ordinary record nobody made on purpose; **puts a cart into a public ford and hurts a man, and finds out that a person harmed by a work is not a person an instrument touches and that this does not open the door**; and ends with a decision taken in advance of the water: **four households at the north of the ford are to be named as persons to be heard, on purpose, by people who have found out what a party is, and the price of that is that they will be findable for ever.** The batch's climax is not a fight. It is a sentence said in a shed by a man who is in a box and has said in nine words that he will not be asked twice.

**The batch's power is institutional and it is still not his.** Marek Kest is four hundred and thirty miles away, eligible, untrained, watched, with no certification and no office and no fund, and he performs no reading of a scale instrument and writes no instrument. **Burden-Bearer does not appear on the page in this batch and the word *exception* does not appear in it.** What he brings back from Auremar is worse than nothing: the fourth line of a four-line form, and the discovery that a register of persons cannot be asked what a form does to a person.

## Required world reveals

1. **A notary's table asks a person four lines, and the fourth line is *in what way the instrument touches you*, and that line is the whole of the door.** The form is not the difficulty. The form is right to want a person.
2. **The form can be put in before the instrument takes effect upon a person, and there is a line for that, and it is used about four times a year in the empire, and the reason is a finding of a district court of Auremar's lower district of the ninth of the eighth and not a rule of the Table, and the Table's form does not contain it and never will.** It is said out loud in a room and is not put in writing, and it stays a finding of a court four hundred and thirty miles from a counter.
3. **A register of persons cannot be asked what a form does to a person.** The Docket of Powers keeps persons and not records; that is why it is safe, and it is also the reason it cannot help, and a name in that register is a door at which a person may be served for ever and for anything.
4. **A record kept openly for customers is a record anybody can read, and it is the first person made findable in this matter by a record nobody made on purpose.** A landing's book, on a nail, in the open, because a customer wants to know whether his stone has gone.
5. **A person harmed by a work is not a person an instrument touches, and being harmed does not open the notary's door.** A man hurt in a ford cannot fill in the fourth line, because the fourth line asks about an instrument and nobody on that bank has read the instrument and the certified copy is in a run that is a number of a book.
6. **What opens a door is being heard, and being heard is a fact about a person and not about a document** — because an instrument of which no person is named has no operation upon any person until that person has been heard about it in a room in a place that recognises the room. **That is the one step the road has, and it is available to one person at a time and never to ninety-one households at once, and the cost of it is that the person is findable for ever.**
7. **A household made a party on purpose, in advance, by people who know what a party is, is a household a man with a tray can ask about for ever — and the batch says the price out loud and pays it.**
8. **A rule a body writes for itself, with a date on it, is a certificate of a kind that certifies itself: issued by nobody, binding nobody, deniable by anybody, and the only paper a body with no office behind it has ever had.**
9. **A duty held by a person for a term with a date is the shape this volume has arrived at, and holding it a second time costs more than holding it the first time, and the reason is that the second time somebody has been found.**
10. **The only door in this empire that opens before an instrument binds anybody is a court, and a court binds nobody who was not in the room, and about sixty people will not be in the room, and a list of names is the price of the room.**
