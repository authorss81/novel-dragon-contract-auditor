## What the volume is, in one paragraph, and what the title means

**A volume in which a piece of paper gets a second holder.** That is the whole of the change, and it is not a small one, because the machinery of this matter has now been built for nine volumes to hold one person to one thing. A record is stationary. A form is stationary. A heading is stationary. A class is stationary. A box is stationary. **Volume 09 moved one blank four miles and found out that a practice works by being cheap and finds the cheapest person available every time without being told to. Volume 10 finds out what happens in a matter built that way when a thing stops having one person attached to it, and the answer is that a second holder is not a witness, a second holder is not safer, and a second holder makes a thing faster.**

**The page has already said every sentence this volume is built on, in mouths, in rooms, across two volumes, and the writer who does not go and read them will write a mystery instead of this volume.** Chapter 0351 gives the whole of it in about eight words, at a counter in another matter, between a man who wanted a thing and a clerk: *I will copy them as they are* — *That is not what I came for* — **No, said the clerk. It is what you get for the fourpence.** Chapter 0184 gives the consequence of it, in about forty words: **there is no form in this empire for stopping a copy; fourpence is not his to withhold, and the words are not his to withhold once they are set in type, and the sheet is a thing anybody may have, and a thing anybody may have is a thing that cannot be recalled, and a thing that cannot be recalled is the only instrument any of them have ever had.** Chapter 0407 gives the size of the thing in a woman's mouth at that counter: **it is the exact width of a name and nothing else has ever been that size**, and the reason it is the only paper that will take a name without a heading over it is that the heading is what makes it a column, and a column cannot say no and cannot be hurt by what comes back. Chapter 0379 gives the price and the queue and the fact that a copy of a thing which is not on the counter is a different object: **there is no form for buying a copy of a thing that is not on it, and fourpence buys me a second piece of paper with the same four things printed along the top of it.** And Chapters 0447 and 0450 give the state of the man this series is about, in the same clause in both: **there is no second copy of anything in that building.**

**The title names the smallest object in the volume, and a title is a heading, and a title that names a body, an institution, an event or a finding is false before a chapter is written.** *The Second Sheet* names a piece of paper and it is the second of two, and there is a first. ## The question, and the half of it that is not given

*What is a second sheet for, in a matter where the only paper in this empire that cannot be laid against anybody is the paper that comes one piece at a time, and where a copy is what a person gets for the fourpence instead of the thing they came for, and where about four hundred people a week buy a piece of paper with nothing printed on it and put it inside a coat and go down four flights, and a man of thirty-eight has been carrying two of them for about two years and has told nobody — and what does the second one cost the person who ends up holding it?*

**That is the volume's question, it is answerable, and this volume answers it, and the answer is not comfortable.**

**The half of it that is not given, and is not given here.** The second half of this matter's question is unasked in all four hundred and fifty finished chapters and is not asked in the fifty of this volume, and it is **not restated in this outline in any form a writer could put it in a mouth, and the name itself is printed nowhere in this file.** The hand that struck a line out of a leaf is unnamed on the page across four hundred and fifty chapters and is unnamed here, and **unanswered is not the same as closed, and it belongs to Volume 12 and not to this volume.** The name that belongs at the end of that struck line is not printed here and the disputed family claim is not printed here and no part of either is answered here. **The decision about whether a woman of twenty-four takes her colleague's judgment on a thing like this is unmade, is not on the page, and may not be made by an outline, a card or a state file.** And the next asking — the eighth asking, which is nobody's yet — may not be made anybody's here either, and no card written from this file may make it anybody's, and the man of about thirty-four with the fair hand is the only person anybody could put a question to and this volume does not put one to him. **A writer who feels the pull to answer any of these is doing the one thing this volume forbids, and the pull is the correct sign that the volume is working.**

## The question the last volume asked, which is inherited as a question and not as an answer

**Volume 09 closed on a question and not a plan, and the question is how much of a thing a person can carry before the carrying is what the person is, and where the size of a thing stops being about the paper and starts being about the person who has it. That question names no person. It does not make the next asking anybody's. And it is not answered in this file, which is a plan and not a book.**

**It is also not answered by this volume, and a volume that answered it would undo the last three volumes of work.** What a volume does with a question nobody may ask is the whole of this volume's craft problem, and the answer this outline gives is structural and not thematic: **the question is put into about nine hundred rooms instead of into a mouth, and about four hundred times a week somebody buys a piece of paper that is the exact width of a name, and every one of them is a person carrying a thing of a known size, and the size of the thing is known and the size of the person is not, and that is the whole of the matter and it has been the whole of the matter for nine volumes.**

**And Volume 10 comes at the question from the one side Volume 09 could not reach.** Volume 09's answer was that a practice works by being cheap and finds the cheapest person available. **Volume 10's answer is that a practice that works by being cheap stops working the moment a thing has a second holder, and does not stop for the reason anybody would want: it stops because a second holder is a person who can be shown holding a thing, and a person who can be shown holding a thing can be shown not holding it, and then the thing is in the second pair of hands and the first pair is free.** A thing that can be taken off one person without being lost can be carried by anybody at all. **That is the volume's danger and the ending of record's mechanism, and this volume states both and pays neither.**
