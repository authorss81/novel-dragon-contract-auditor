## What the volume is, in one paragraph, and what the title means

**A volume in which a refusal has to be put somewhere it can be seen, and there is nowhere.** That is the whole of the change, and it is not a small one, because the machinery of this matter has now been built for eleven volumes out of two things only: a piece of paper that anybody may have, and a sentence said out loud in a room. Neither of them can be entered against a person. **Volume 09 found out that a practice works by being cheap and finds the cheapest person available every time without being told to. Volume 10 found out that a practice which works by being cheap stops working the moment a thing has a second holder, and does not stop for the reason anybody would want. Volume 11 found out that a practice which works by being cheap has a second ear as well as a second hand, and that the second ear costs the person who is there the only protection they had, which is the ability to be shown to have been nowhere near it. Volume 12 finds out that the second ear is also the only instrument this empire has for putting a refusal into the world without asking anybody anything, and that the same protection is lost the same way, and that nobody in nine hundred buildings can pay for the getting of a person to where a thing is heard.**

**The page has already said every sentence this volume is built on, in mouths, in rooms, across two volumes that have been closed for years, and a writer who does not go and read them will write a hearing instead of this volume.** Five of them carry the whole thing and all five are on the closed page and none of them is printed in the plan. ## The seven movements, what each one is for, and the volume's smallest thing

**The volume is seven movements of ten chapters and one ordinary week to a chapter.
