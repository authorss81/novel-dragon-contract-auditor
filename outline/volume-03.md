# Volume 03 Outline: Water Debt

Volume number: 3

Chapter range: 101–150

Central pressure: Volume 02's answer **binds nobody who has not been heard** — a sentence with no supply of hearings in it — so a duty that belongs to the public has no holder anybody can name, and the next thing that comes down a river hands it to whoever is nearest, and the nearest is a household, and a household that holds a duty produces a child that holds the duty. This volume takes one district of the river, one public work, one cooperative of ninety-one households, and asks the question Tavi Venn asked out loud on a bank in the twelfth month of the twelfth year and that nobody answered: **whether a cooperative can be made to hold a duty that is public without making every child in it a debtor.**

Starting state: **Volume 02 is closed at Chapter 100.** The sitting of the eighteenth of the seventh month has been spent in room four and produced an order in five entries that names no person, finds no record altered, finds no person at fault, makes nobody liable, and **binds nobody who has not been heard.** The Venn ruling of the twelfth of the ninth month of the ninth year is certified as operating in one named place and in no other place whatever, and the words *exception* and *precedent* are not used of it. **The two halves of the winning sentence are nine days out of order with each other and neither court has read the other**, and a later volume may join them only in a room, with a witness, and not on a page. **Marek Kest's bar is discharged** and he is **found eligible for Continuity Auditor training and placed under formal watch, in one sentence, deliberately**, and the watch is the price: a person from an office, in this matter unmentioned, may read anything he writes, and he may not be in a room without a person in it who is not from that office, and **the office is not named and cannot be named.** **Tamsin Rook holds an office that does not exist with a limit that does.** The crossing at Lowcross is open, nobody is liable, the bill is **nineteen pounds three and fourpence** and is unpaid. **The thirty-one Mosswake households of the sixth year are thirty-one**, the fourth column of the register of pending duties reads *next review: none named* thirty-one times, and the fourth cycle of the sixth year was never written and cannot now be written. **Nell Kest and Bren Kest have not been in Volume 02 at all** and are owed a lane and a shop.

Starting relationships: Marek and Tamsin Rook are colleagues with a written rule between them — either may put a choice in writing with a date on it and the other must answer in a room, in words, with a date — and **a court order now requires her in any room he is in**, which she has said out loud she does not like and has decided to let stand rather than decide. **Nell Kest's letter of the sixteenth of the eighth is unanswered and she is entitled to that.** **No romance begins in this volume either.** The romance begins at the end of Volume 4.

Starting power level: **Stage 0, Clerk's Eye**, plus the unstable first Seam-Sight earned on the page in Chapter 8 and spent in Chapter 40. **Marek performs no reading in the first ten chapters and the volume's first batch is institutional.** The **training** that his eligibility carries is a reading of a file, in a room, with a person in it who is not the person being trained, on a day fixed, out of an afternoon, and **there is no fund**, and a training is not a certification and no certification is granted anywhere in this volume. **The word *exception* is not used anywhere in Chapters 101–110** and is earned in the volume's third movement, where the volume's midpoint makes it. **No valid exception, counter-seal, counter-mark, mixed warrant, true-name reading, Roll Assembly or Covenant Architect ability appears in Batch 0001.**

Major locations: the **district of Mosswake**, the Reed Cut **gate**, **water office** and **district works**; the **Slade Cut** up-channel, its bank, its tool shed, its **grain store** and the **mill yard** off it; a **lane of channel-side houses** and a **weigh-house**; the **Mosswake Roll Court** on the flood terrace, with **Ivet Sarn's** clerk's window and the embankment corridor behind it; **a landing**; the **Received Room** in Auremar and the **Docket of Powers**' three rooms; and, later in the volume, the **old bridge** and the road up to the Salt Teeth.

Major factions: the **Slade Cut Company**, a cooperative of about ninety-one households that has worked a public cut for ninety-one years out of a book kept in a kitchen; the **Reed Cut district water office** under its keeper; the **Reed Cut district works** under its foreman; the **Mosswake Roll Court** under Magistrate Leda Rell; the **Chancery of the Roll** in Auremar and the **Docket of Powers**; the **Cinder Court** at the Crown Kiln, whose registrar keeps a register of persons who came and whose judge has granted four things in nine years; channel crews, seed farmers, millers and copyists; and, held off the page of the first batch and named later, **House Oris water notaries** and the trade assessors who stand between a house and a guild.

**The one rule this volume must never break, carried from Volumes 01 and 02:** the **MC/FS circular** and the **First Seat quarterly return** remain two documents, four hundred miles apart, in two buildings, with no instrument anywhere joining them, and **only Marek Kest and Tamsin Rook know both exist.** The **First Seat quarterly return does not appear on the page in Volume 03 at all**, is still in no reply, and **Hallis Dren's return of the eleventh of the tenth is still dormant, armed, and known to nobody in the story.** The four appearances of the substituted wording keep their different ages and are never written as a chain, in one sentence, in date order, and **no chapter adds a fifth**; nobody wrote the substituted wording and nobody wrote the public-necessity sentence. The red stitch in Ivo Harker's tin, the thread in the fold of the sealed page, and the working stitch in the Ashfall bundle are three different things and **no two of them may ever be joined in a sentence.** The two sealed pages stay shut and **there is no third refusal of either.** The counting-house mark is a mark and no chapter converts it; the year in the run is not entered anywhere; forty-four thousand eight hundred and nineteen is a number of a book that nobody reads; and a gap in a copy is a fact about the copying and **a court has directed that nobody be asked about the ninety-second leaf.** **Sivra Oris, Seryn Oris and Vaunt Oris are three people and are never merged**, Seryn is described as a seat and is credited with nothing she did not do, and **Vaunt Oris does not explain himself again.** The **Venn ruling has five operative terms and the numbering is canon.**

**A second rule this volume establishes and must then keep:** **a duty needs a holder that can stop.** An office can be instructed and can therefore be pushed; a person can stop; **a body can do neither.** A cooperative, a company, a guild, a parish, a household, a district and a household are all bodies, and a duty put on a body is put on every person in it and on every child born into it, and it reaches further than any person in it will live. **The word that goes in a box in Volume 03 is not the word that walks about, and it is expressly not entered as an instance of anything**: the substituted phrase remains *the present holder*, it stands in four files of four ages, and the new box on the river says **a body**, which is a different failure with the same result.

Midpoint reversal (Chapters 121–130): **the first attempt to move the duty works, and it works too well.** Marek writes the one instrument he is eligible to write, it is technically good, it saves a named household, **and it makes the Company the guarantor of every future flood on that cut** — and the Company cannot refuse, because a Company is a body and a body cannot refuse, so the thing that saves one family is the same thing that puts about sixty children inside a duty they were not born into. The reversal is not that the instrument fails. It is that **it is the correct instrument and the wrong shape**, and that a burden moved onto a body is not lighter and is merely distributed, and that the finding is one a person can only make by watching a household that has just been helped.

Escalation sequence:

1. **Chapters 101–110, the Slade Cut and a duty a person can stop.** A spring bank slip on a cut the water office can work for a fortnight in the year and cannot for the other eleven months. A sheet comes down the river with a box in it and a body in the box. **The water office's keeper refuses to enter a body and enters two people's names instead, and disclaims the office's own flood authority in writing to do it.** A book with nine hands in it turns out to be the only record of ninety-one years of a duty nobody ever papered, and a copy of a copy turns out to be the only paper the Company has. A foreman signs a list of works with her own name at the foot of it and says the volume's question out loud before any lawyer arrives. **A cooperative is not a Roll Assembly and is not described as one.**
2. **Chapters 111–120, a person who may be made a party.** The certified copy of the Slade Cut instrument is in Auremar, in a notary's table, by number, and **a person who is not a party cannot ask for it, and the Company cannot ask for it because a Company is not a person.** The volume's own instrument turns inside out: **a document about nobody alive cannot be answered, refused, held to be a forgery, or produced against a person, and so it cannot be asked anything by anybody either.** The route runs through being a person the instrument touches, and it touches nobody, and so somebody has to be touched. **And the four hundred and thirty miles are crossed again for a hearing that is not a hearing.**
3. **Chapters 121–130, the flood season and the transfer.** The route closes. The works cannot stand the cost and says so in writing for the second time in nineteen years. The one instrument Marek is eligible to write is written, and it is the volume's technical win and its moral defeat, and **the cooperative becomes the guarantor of every future flood.** About sixty children are inside a duty inside a body that cannot refuse.
4. **Chapters 131–140, the house and the concession.** **Vaunt Oris** is asked to certify something that is not there and **refuses a false reading**, and a concession is lost — **his House's, and he is not a villain and is not converted**, and the loss is entered in a form with his name at the foot of it, which is the only way a dragon can be seen to pay for anything in this book. Seryn's wing writes again and a notice of a seat is declined in a form with a reason, and **there is no why.**
5. **Chapters 141–150, the rotating duty and a public expiry.** The water is survived. **The cooperative accepts a rotating duty with a stated term, a review date and a right of refusal in it, and a body is made to be able to stop, which is the whole of the work of this volume.** The chain reaches **a guild and a dragon House** rather than stopping at one village. **Marek carries a counter-mark tied to the cooperative's channel duty**, and the named consequence is labour and liability and not an invisible geographic tether. **A magistrate of the district court nine days out of order with a high court is still not joined**, and the door to Auremar is still a door.

Concrete resolution: the Slade Cut holds a public duty for a season on a list of works with a person's name at the foot of it and a date against it, and **the Company is not made a guarantor of anything.** The thirty-one of Mosswake gain a seventh column nobody has been able to give them. The Lowcross bill is still unpaid and nobody is liable for it. **What Ilyra Kest found in the Ashfall archive is still not on the page**, and the road to it is now a **run number in a notary's table in Auremar and a person who may be made a party to an instrument that touches nobody alive**, and that is a route and not an answer.

Power and relationship change: **Marek performs one reading of a file, on a day fixed, out of an afternoon, with no fund and with Tamsin Rook in the room because a court order requires it, and it is a human clerk's competence and not magic.** The counter-mark that the volume ends on is bought with named labour. **Tamsin is present in a room in a district four hundred and thirty miles away because a court order she does not like made her the mechanism of a watch**, and she begins to be the person who says the method out loud in somebody else's district, and **the second of the two sentences this volume is about — a person may be heard, and a person may be heard *before* — is dealt with in a room with a witness in the volume's third movement and not on a page.** Nell Kest is owed a lane and a shop and gets them in this volume's later movement, and Bren Kest is owed an honest sentence about what the work costs their family.

**Relationship lock: no romance begins in Volume 03.** Tamsin and Marek are colleagues who can now be in the same room in a district because a paper says so, and **the paper is not the reason**, and both of them know it, and neither of them says so.

Next-volume question: **What did Ilyra Kest find in the Ashfall archive that was worth dying for, and why does the original document name a blank heir.** Volume 03 opens that road and does not walk down it. The road is a certified copy in a notary's table and a category of person who may be made a party, and the answer is a document about nobody.

Final chapter image: **a list of works on a bank with one person's name at the foot of it and a date against that name, and a book in a kitchen with a new hand in it, and a girl of eleven who cannot read the list being read to by somebody who is not paid** — and the volume's last page is the fourth such list in ninety-one years and the first one anybody on that bank will be able to point at.
