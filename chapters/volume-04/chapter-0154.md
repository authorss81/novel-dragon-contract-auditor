# Chapter 154: The Refusal, and What It Cost

He filled the third box at about the fourth hour of the third week of the fifth month of next year, in the same room, at the same counter, with the same woman on the other side of the oak.

It took him about nine seconds to write a name. He had written it four hundred times in fourteen months on the backs of sheets in books with no office on them, and he had got it very good, and he did not use the good one.

The woman of twenty-four was four feet behind him because a court order required her to be in any room he was in, and that order is one instrument, and the watch on him is a different instrument and requires only that a person who is not from the office that cannot be named be in the room, and the two have never been the same piece of paper and are not going to be joined in this matter by anybody, including me.

He put the pen down and squared the sheet and pushed it across the slab.

Marn Ottery read it. She read it twice, the second reading slower, which is a thing she does and has never explained to anybody and did not explain then.

Then she went and got a blank, and she ruled nothing on it, because a form for refusing a question is not a form you print.

---

"**This office cannot certify the third box, and here is why, and I am going to write it in my own hand and in more words than the box holds, and there is no box on this paper for a why, and you are not getting one, and that is not spite.**

"The board over this counter has four things on it. What an instrument is. What a book is, and what is written in it. What a party is. What a document does. The first, the third and the fourth are the same question asked three ways, and all three of them are about documents. The second one is the one that is not.

"Your third box asks this office to say that a person is a party to an instrument. That is item three, and item three is a thing this table does, and I can certify what a party is in about nine seconds and put four shillings against it.

"Your third box also asks this office to say that the person named in it is not touched by the instrument, because you have written in your own mouth on a piece of paper at this counter that you are not touched by anything alive, and you have signed it, and a signature on a thing you wrote yourself is the only kind of signature in this empire that anybody may rely on and you cannot take it back.

"**A person who says out loud, in writing, that nothing touches them is not a party. A party is a person an instrument does something to. If nothing does anything to you then there is no party, and there is no third box, and there is no application, and I cannot enter a form that certifies a person who has certified himself out of the room.**

"That is not a fault in your application. That is your application being honest, and I have watched about four hundred people decide in about nine seconds that it was a fault in the form, and I have never once been able to make one of them take the four seconds they needed."

She wrote it out longhand on the back of the blank, in her own hand, in about eleven lines, and dated it, and signed it at the foot, and turned it round.

"**Appealable by nobody. There is nobody to appeal to. I am a clerk and this is not a court and it is not in front of a court, and I have written down exactly what this office cannot do and why, and the day, and my name at the foot of it, and I have not written down one word of why I cannot do the other thing, because a why is a thing you put in a book for other people to argue with and there is nobody in this building to argue with me.**"

He read it twice. The second reading was slower, because she was watching him and he did not notice.

"Is it right," he said.

"It is entirely right," said Marn Ottery. "I have been entirely right twice this month and one of them cost a man a third of a sheet of good paper, and the other one is about to cost him more than that, and I am going to tell him how much, because he has not worked it out and I have watched about nine hundred people work it out at about four seconds each and the four seconds is the part that stays with me."

---

She took fourpence for the copy and put it in the drawer and wrote it in her own column on the back of the counter.

"You have paid for a record," she said. "A record is not a receipt. **A receipt says you paid. A record says you came.** And this one is dated, and signed, and has a name on it, and it will go in the day-book of this counter because a refusal has to go somewhere, and the somewhere is here, and the day-book is a book anybody with fourpence and a reason can have a page of.

"So here is what it has cost you and I am not going to do it in a way that sounds like a story. **In about four years, anybody in this empire who has fourpence can read that a man of thirty-eight with a discharged bar came to the fourth counter of the Notaries' Table in Auremar in the third week of the fifth month of next year, asked a question about a run of impressions, asked for the person the instrument touches, wrote his own name in the box, and was refused, correctly, by a clerk with a reason and no why.**

"**And there is no way in this empire to un-ask.** I am aware that I have just made being right into a thing that can be served on a person, and I did that on purpose, and I have done it before, and it is on the back of a blank in your book, and I would do it again, and I would like somebody to find out whether I am allowed to."

"You are allowed to," said Marek Kest.

"I have been allowed to for eleven years," she said. "Nobody has ever asked me whether I am, and I have assumed it, and assuming a thing for eleven years is not the same as being allowed to do it and I know the difference and I have not been able to make the difference do any work."

---

She entered it in the day-book at about the fifth hour and she entered the question and the day and the fee and the name of the person it was refused to, and she did not enter the reason.

"Where is the reason," said Marek Kest.

"Where you are going to leave it," said Marn Ottery. "A reason entered in a day-book of this office becomes a rule of this office. And I have had that counter eleven years and there is one refusal on the page before this one, and that one is nineteen years old and it was put there by a man who is dead, and I know what it says and I am not going to tell you, and I have read it about four hundred times.

"And if I put mine in, then in about nine years there will be two, and there will be a clerk after me who reads the two of them and works out a pattern, and the pattern will be right, and the pattern will be a rule, and this counter does not get asked. This counter does not get asked. I have said that sentence three times today and it is the sentence my whole trade is built on and I have never once been able to make anybody hear it as anything except an excuse."

"Do you want the counter to be asked," said Tamsin Rook.

"I want the counter to be asked about a number and not about a person," said Marn Ottery. "Those are two different questions and only one of them is about the trade."

---

He did not argue with it.

He had come up that counter with an argument ready and it was a good one and it had been ready since the second week of the fifth month, and it went about nine seconds unused, and she saw it go and did not enjoy it.

He did not put the same question again in a different form either, and that is the harder of the two nots, because a table may refuse a question and be entirely right and a party may put the same question differently and be served inside four days, and the only defence anybody in this empire has against that is a person who is in the room when the second version is written, and there is no form for that, there is a person for that, and the person for it is standing four feet behind the man who is not using the argument he came with.

"What are you going to do," she said.

"I'm going to go down the road four hundred and thirty miles and be liable for a bank," he said. "And I am going to have this in my coat."

"Why."

He thought about it for longer than she expected, and she let him, and there is no form of words at that counter for how long a person may think.

"Because I have been looking for it since the fourth month of last year," he said at last. "Not this one. A no with a day on it and a name at the foot of it from somebody who has to write a reason and has to say what the office cannot do. And I have not been able to get one, and I have not been able to say out loud that I have been looking for one, and ten months ago a woman of sixty-four who cannot read a paragraph said it in a bay in about nine sentences, that a person who cannot say no in a room is not the same as a person who cannot say no in a book, and I have been carrying that about since, and it is the only reason I could tell you what I did with this the moment I read it."

"And now you have it."

"Now I have it and I cannot do anything with it at all," said Marek Kest. "**That is what I want out loud. I have wanted a refusal for four months and I have got one and it is the only piece of paper in this matter I cannot use, and the reason I could not say what I was doing until just now is that wanting to be told no is preparation, and preparation is for me, and the only person it protects is me.**"

Marn Ottery put the pen down and looked at him for about four seconds and then said, "That is the first time in eleven years anybody has said that sentence at this counter, and it is not mine, and I am not going to have it written down anywhere near me."

"You will have it written down in about four years," said Tamsin Rook. "It is in his coat."

"Then it is in his coat and not in a press with an address on the outside of it," said Marn Ottery, "and that is a different arrangement and it is the only one of the two I am willing to be part of."

---

At about the ninth hour she said the other half of the cost, which was hers, and she said it to the empty counter again and it went into her own column and not into the day-book, and it was this:

**Two refusals on a counter in eleven years is a counter that has had something.** The first one is nineteen years old and the man who wrote it is dead. She had been proud of that page the way you are proud of a thing you have never had tested, and now there is a second line under it in a different hand, and there is going to be a clerk after her who will count them, and she has known the clerk she trained for about six years and he is a good clerk and he is not a careful one.

And she could have refused without a reason. There is a version of a refusal with no reason on the face of it, and it is the ordinary one, and it costs the person asking nothing at all except a shilling they did not get an answer for.

She wrote the reason because the alternative was a form with nothing in it, and a form with nothing in it is a form in which nobody is told anything, and she had spent four days that week with a form on her counter with three ruled boxes and a strip of paper gummed over the only line in it that mattered, and she was not going to hand anybody out a blank refusal on top of it.

That is the cost and it is named and it is hers and it is not recoverable and she is not thanked for it and nobody offered to.

---

And what was left standing at the end of the third week of the fifth month of next year, in the plainest words there are:

An application in three ruled boxes with two of them filled in, dated, and refused in a form with a reason in it and no why and a day on it and a name at the foot of it, and a copy of it at fourpence in the inside coat of a man of thirty-eight who holds no certification and is entered in no register of persons who read and has a bar discharged on the twelfth of the ninth month of last year.

About eleven weeks was running from the question bought in the second week, and the answer to it is due in the first week of the eighth month of next year.

**And the application was refused, and a refused application is not an application, and the third box of it was in his own coat in a third of a sheet of good paper that no table in this empire may certify, and the instrument touches nobody alive, and the person the instrument touched is dead, and the man who filled that box had put his own name in it and been told that a person who says out loud, in writing, that nothing touches him is not a party to anything.**

There is no form in this empire for putting a person back into a box they have been taken out of, and there is no form for taking them out of one either, and the man is not going to be able to fill it in a second time without being asked, and there is nobody to ask him.
