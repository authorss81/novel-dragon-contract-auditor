# Chapter 194: The Roll Gets Checked

The roll of households of that district got checked in the fourth week of the fifth month of the year after, and a check is not a thing anybody decides to do. A district keeps a roll because a district has to know who is in it, and a roll that is never checked is a list and not a record, and there is a day in the year when the person who keeps it goes down the lanes and asks, and the asking is about the roll and not about anything else, and it has been done this way for longer than anybody on that lane has been alive.

Nell Kest is twenty. She is a dye-house worker, which is what she is paid for, and she keeps the books of the eleven houses on that lane and of four other houses in three other districts, which is what she is not paid for and has done since she was seventeen. Her wage stopped in the second week of the third month and it is stopped now, and the word for it is *held*, and nobody has said the figure and she has not given it.

The counting room is about eleven feet by fourteen. It has a bench, a window with a broken light, a stove she does not light until the ninth month, and shelves of about forty books that somebody made for taller books than these. It is not hers.

---

The woman who keeps the roll came down the lane on the second day of the fourth week with a sheet on a board and a basket, and she is about sixty and she has kept that roll for nineteen years, and she has never once been asked what a heading on a form means, and she is not asked anything today either and did not expect to be.

She came to the eleventh door first, because the eleventh door is first on the list and the lane is walked in the order it is written, and then she came to the counting room, and she stood in the doorway with the board against her hip.

"Two things," she said. "I am asking about a place of custom or of work at the fourth and about a mill out of the seventh, and that is the whole of it, and if you have moved I want the new one and not the old one."

"I have not moved," said Nell Kest. "The mill is the mill and the room is a room over a shop and I have been in it since I was seventeen and there is one person in it."

"That is the whole of what I wanted."

Then she turned the board round, which is what a person keeping a roll does, and there were two lines of the fourth column visible and one of them was the description of a household and one of them was something else.

**And the something else said *representative in a matter*, and it was against her name, and there was a day against it in a hand she did not know.**

---

She read it standing up, and the woman who keeps the roll did not say anything, because a woman who keeps a roll for nineteen years knows what it is to stand in front of a lane and say nothing while somebody reads.

The line said: *Representative in a matter. Entered in the office of a seat on the fourth day of the third week of the fifth month of the year after. No further consent of the person required.*

"No further consent of the person required," said Nell Kest, out loud, in a counting room, to a woman of about sixty holding a board. "**And that is on my name on a roll, and it is the answer to a question nobody asked me.** It is written on a roll of households, which is the one book in this empire that nobody can be kept out of, because being in a district is not a right and you cannot give it up."

"I did not put it there."

"I know you did not put it there. I want to say the next thing and I want it in the room. **I am not blaming you and you did not do it and you are the reason I can stand here and say what I am about to say out loud.** If you had not come down this lane on the second day of the fourth week I would not have found out for a season, and a season is about the length a thing can be held for without anybody deciding it is a season."

---

Then she said no, and she said it in a room, out loud, with the day on it, which is the only place in this empire a no has ever worked.

"No," said Nell Kest.

The woman of about sixty said, "You are standing in your own counting room and I have got a sheet and I can put it in the fourth column of the roll and it is a column and it is mine and I will do it and it will be there for nineteen years."

"Write it down."

"I will write it down."

"**Write the day. Write that I said it out loud in this room with you in it. Write that I did not consent.** Do not write why, and do not write a reason, because there is no box for a why on a roll of households and I have looked at the roll in your hand and there is nothing anywhere on it for one."

The woman of about sixty wrote three things and no fourth and read them back once.

---

And it did not work, and she had known it would not before she said it, and she said that too.

"I am going to tell you the rest of it and I am going to tell you now, while you are standing here, because you have been decent enough to walk down a lane with a board." She put the pencil down. "**I am refusing to be served. That is the instrument.** It is the only one there is in this empire and I have used it once and I am not a lawyer and I am not proud of it and I am using it now, and I would like you to write that I used it."

"I will write it."

"It is not going to work, and here is the reason, and the reason is four words long and I have read them." The woman at the eleventh door had come to the door of the counting room by then and was standing in it. "**No further consent required. A room that writes a no down is not the thing that has the no in it.** I have said no out loud in a room, with a day on it, using the only instrument there is, and the instrument is a form and a form is a thing that happens, and what has happened is that a clerk in an office about nine miles from a counter filled in a box the way the box is printed, and the box said the person, institution or thing engaged in the matter, and she was not a villain and she was not asked and she did it correctly, and I cannot get at her and I cannot get at the office and there is no form in this empire that lets a person produce a thing that is not in a book with a name on the spine."

"So there is nothing to be done," said the woman who keeps the roll.

"There is something to be done and it is not done by me in this room and I have been about nine minutes working out what it is and it is a hearing about four hundred and thirty miles up this river on the third day of the third week of this month, and I am going to it, and I am not paid to go and nobody is paying for me to go and I am not going to add it up."

---

Then the woman of about sixty at the eleventh door said the thing she came to say, which is that she had been standing at the door for a while and had heard the word *hold* used twice.

"That is my word," she said. "That is the word for it when a thing cannot be paid."

"Yes."

"And you have not got a column for me anywhere."

"No."

"Then I have been left out of it twice," said the woman of about sixty, "and I told you in the second week of the third month that I would rather be asked than not, and you told me you would not be able to do that for me and that it was much smaller, and it was, and here it is again, and I have not been asked either." She was not angry and she was not asking for anything and she went home.

Nobody thanked her. She is not thanked on that lane and has never been and there is no form anywhere that requires it.

---

What Nell Kest did for the rest of that week was the only thing she is any good at, which is a count.

She counted it. Not the wage, which is held and is not given, and not the figure, which is not in anything. She counted the entries.

**The entry was made on the fourth day of the third week of the fifth month. That is a day.** A form that cannot be asked can still be filled in, and the day it was filled in is a day, and the day is the only part of the whole thing that anybody can find. Everything else about it is a description and a heading and a thing with no name on it, and a description is not findable, and a heading is not findable, and the only thing in that entry with a thing in it that can be compared against another thing is the day, and there is exactly one day, and she wrote it on the second clean sheet with the heading at the top of it.

Then she stopped, and she looked at the sheet for about a minute, and she put the pencil down and left it.

**The sheet says what a person is owed, and by whom, and on what day, about the eleven houses on this lane and the four other houses in three districts and about nobody else.** She started it in the second month of the year after because a column with a heading over it is a machine for turning one thing into another kind of thing and she wanted to see whether she could aim one on purpose. It is hers, nobody asked her for it, there is no form for it, no fee for it, and no column in this town that will take it.

A day is a fact. A day on a sheet of her own with her own heading on it is a record of a person, and a record of a person is the easiest thing in this empire to make, and she has spent three years refusing to make one.

"I am not going to put myself on it," she said, out loud, to the counting room, on the second day of the fourth week of the fifth month of the year after. "**That sheet is for the eleven houses and the four other houses.** I have written down what nine people on this lane are owed about nine hundred times and I have never once written down what I am owed, and it is not modesty. It is that a number about a person can be taken away in about four years, and this one is not a number about a person. It is a day, and a day is worse, because anybody with a copy can go and stand at that office on that day and ask who filled in the box."

She did not tear it out. She left the day on the sheet in her own hand, under her own heading, and it is the first entry on it, and it is the only one that is about anybody in her family.

---

---

On the fifth day she did the count, because she has done it every week since she was seventeen and she is not going to stop in a week when a thing has happened to her.

Pieces out on the frames on the last day of the week, by house. Vat-hours. What the mill takes and what the mill does not take. It takes her about four minutes and she does it out of her head and has never once written it down and there is nobody on that lane who knows she can do it.

She got to the eighth house and stopped, and then she went on, and then she stopped again at the eleventh, which is the one room over a shop with one person in it, and she stood at the bench with her hands flat on it for about a minute and a half.

**The count has one line in it per house and it has had one line in it per house for three years, and there is a second thing it could have now, which is a line for what that house is owed, and she has the figure for nine houses on this lane and she does not have it for the eleventh, because the eleventh is hers and she has never written down what she is owed.**

She could have made it two columns in about a minute. It would have cost her nothing and it would have been a machine and a good one and she would have been able to point at it.

**A column is a machine and a second column is two machines, and a person who has one machine and can see the whole of it knows what it is for, and a person with two of them has to choose which one she is looking at, and choosing is a thing she does not do in a book.** She left it one column and she did not write the eleventh house at all in the part where she writes the houses, and the omission took her about nine seconds and it is the third time since the third month.

"You are not writing the eleventh," said nobody, because there was nobody there.

She said, out loud, to a counting room about eleven feet by fourteen, "**I am not writing the eleventh, and the eleventh is a household, and a household of one is a person, and I have said out loud in a room this week that a room which writes a no down is not the thing that has the no in it, and a book is a room, and I am not going to start filling one in on myself.**"

Then she did the rest of it and shut the book that was already shut and put the clean sheet back where it was with the one day on it under her own heading.

---

On the sixth day of the fourth week she put the fourth book of the association of dyers off the shelf and opened it at the fourth column, where it has been open more or less since the eleventh of the eighth of the year before last, and she wrote a fifth thing in it.

> *Told on the second day of the fourth week of the fifth month, in this room, by the person who keeps the roll of households of this district, in the ordinary course of checking it, that there is an entry against my name in the fourth column of that roll which says I am a representative in a matter, and that it was entered in the office of a seat on the fourth day of the third week, and that the entry says no further consent of the person is required.*
>
> *I said no, out loud, in this room, with the day on it, and the person keeping the roll wrote it down, and my name is not in the line of it, and the reason the name is not in it is the reason it is not in the other lines and it is four words and they are the same four words as last time. A person, not a column. I said it out loud to my brother in a street about four hundred and thirty miles from this room in the second month of the year after and I have never seen him write them down anywhere. I have got them myself. I have not been asked why and there is no box for a why and I have looked.*
>
> *It did not work and I want that written down as well, because a person reads a book after four years and not before, and in four years this page will say that a woman said no and will not say what the no did. I am refusing to be served and the instrument did what an instrument does, which is it exists. — and I am not going to be thanked, and there is no name in this column because there is no name in the column.*

She read it back once, which is what a person does with a thing they have written, and she did not read it back twice, and the reason is that there is nobody in the room and she is not doing it for anybody.

Then she shut the book and put it back on the shelf in about forty books on shelves made for taller books, and the stove was cold and she did not light it, and her wage is held, and her name is in a column in an office about nine miles away that was filled in by a person doing her job properly, and a district clerk of about sixty walked down a lane with a board and asked her about a place of custom or of work, in the ordinary way, and got the ordinary answer, and wrote it down, and will do it again at the same time next year. **There is nothing in that which anybody can be angry with and there is everything in it.**
