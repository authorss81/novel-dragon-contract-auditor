# Chapter 183: One Person Fits It

A man came down the embankment corridor about four hundred yards from a clerk's window with a copy of a schedule in a hand that is not his own, and he could not read it, and he asked her what it said, in the ordinary way, and she read it out, and it took her about nine minutes to work out what it does.

Ivet Sarn is thirty-eight. She has been at that window for nine years and she came to it because nobody else applied, and she is the clerk of this court by a key on a string that she has shortened twice herself, and there is no form in this empire that makes a person be in charge of a thing.

The window is about four feet off the ground. That is not a figure anybody chose. The court was built for a counter and the counter was left where it was.

---

The man was about forty and he had the schedule folded twice in his coat, and he took it out and put it on the sill and did not give it to her, and she did not ask him to, and the two of them have never worked out which of them decided that.

"I have been asked to look at this," he said. "I do not know what looking at it means. That is not a complaint, I have never been asked to look at anything before."

"I will read it out," said Ivet Sarn, "and then I am going to tell you two things, and one of the two things is going to be about you and I would rather you heard it from me than from a form."

---

She read the whole of it. It took her about four minutes to read and about nine to have finished thinking about it, and the second nine minutes were longer than the four.

She read the words as they are on the sheet and she did not improve them and she said so before she started, because he was going to have to say them to somebody and the somebody was going to look them up.

> *Of age and resident: having reached the age of eighteen years, and having a place of custom or of work entered in the roll of households of the district in which the person is resident. That roll is kept by the district. It lists the households in the district. It does not list the rights, holdings or claims of any person in it.*
>
> *And it is provided that where a sum payable to a person of that family is entered as held, the wages of a person of that family are held at the same rate and for the same period, and a person whose wages are so held is not to be paid that held sum by any person, on account of the person of that family.*

"That is the whole of what it is," she said, "and I am going to say the whole of it in one sentence and then I am going to say why it took me nine minutes, and I am going to say both of those things out loud because there is nobody in this corridor at the moment except a man going by with a barrow, and he was gone before I had finished, and I have not asked him whether he wanted to hear any of it and that is deliberate and I want it known."

"**A form with a heading over it and no person under it can be executed against a person who has not been told it exists.**"

---

"What does executed mean," said the man from the district works.

"It means it happens to you without you being asked," said Ivet Sarn. "**You do not have to do anything.** That is the finding and it took me nine minutes because it is such an ordinary sentence and I have spent nine years in a building where people are asked to sign things.

"A notice of a seat that names a person is served on that person. Served means a person has been told, and being told means the person can do something about it, and doing something about it is refusing. **The only instrument in this empire for refusing a thing is a refusal to be served.** It is the only one, and there is no second one, and there is no box for a why anywhere in the form of it. And this one does not name anybody. It names a class, and a class cannot be served, and so the person it is about is not told, and a person who is not told cannot refuse, and there is nothing left to do that is refusing.

"And being entered as a representative is being served. That is the part I want you to hold on to, because it is the part everybody gets backwards. Entering a person as somebody's representative is a service on that person. It puts them in a matter. It makes them findable. And the whole engine of the thing is that the only way to be put in a matter in this empire is to be served with something, and the schedule attached to this has four definitions in it and one of them says in its own words that nothing done under the notice requires the agreement of the person it is done to, and that is not a gap in it. It is the fourth definition. They wrote it down."

---

The man asked her what a roll of households is, and she told him, and it took about a minute and a half, and it is the ordinaryest answer anybody in that building has given in nine years.

"It is a list of who is living in the district," she said. "A district has to know who is in it. It is how a district knows who to send a notice to, who to send a levy to, who to count for the water, and who to take off the roll of households when they go. **It is a book of addresses that behaves like a book of people.** It is not a list of rights. It does not say what anybody owns. It does not say what anybody can be given."

"Then a person with nothing is still in it."

"A person with nothing is in it, and a person with nothing cannot be taken off it, and that is what I want to say next, and I have not said it out loud in nine years because nobody ever asked me."

---

"Then here is the first thing," she said, "and I am saying it to you and not to a book, and I am not going to write it down, and I will tell you why in a moment.

"**A family that holds nothing enforceable is an eligible family and not a poor one**, and about a month ago a man of thirty-eight worked that out in a room four hundred and thirty miles up this river and said it out loud and there were three people in the room and none of them had written it down, and I have known it since the third week of the first month because he wrote to me about something else and mentioned it.

"And the whole of what a blanking buys a family is that nothing can be given to them. No duty, no shilling, no house, no guild, no term. Nothing. That is what his mother did and it worked and nobody is going to argue with her about it.

"And the whole of what it costs is a roll. **There is a roll of households in the district of a furnace town most of the way down this river, and that district has a lane in it with eleven houses in it, and there is a name in that roll at a house of one room over a shop.** Behind that name there is nothing at all. The whole of what the blanking did was remove a family from what can be given to it, and it did not remove them from where they sleep, and it could not have, because being in a district is not a right and you cannot give it up.

"Nobody put her there on purpose. A district keeps a roll of households because a district has to know who is in it. That is the whole of it and there is no villain in it and there is no conspiracy and there is no office at it. **It is a book that a district keeps because a district is a place where people live.**"

---

The man from the district works said, "Then that is a bad book."

"It is an ordinary book," said Ivet Sarn, "**and that is the whole of what is wrong with it and it is why it will still be doing this in nine years.** That is not a comfort and it is the reason I have stopped worrying about it. I have read every form in this building at some point in nine years and I have never once read one of them against another, and every one of them was filled in by somebody doing their job properly on an ordinary day of the week. I am not a lawyer and I hold nothing and I have been at this window for nine years and the whole of what I know is what comes up it, and what comes up it this month is a form that does not need anybody's agreement, sitting on a definition that everybody in the building that wrote it would have said was a book of addresses."

Then she stopped, and she put both hands flat on the sill, and the second thing came out of her in the flat way she says everything.

"The second thing is about you and I said I would say it, so here it is. **A household is held, not a person.** The form says wages are held, and the wages belong to a person, and there is a name somewhere in the office that fills it in, and that person is going to fill in the name of a thing, because that is what a form with a heading over it and no person under it asks a clerk to write. If it says a household then it holds a household. If the household is one then it holds one woman and there is nobody in the room to say no to it. And I cannot do anything about any part of that and I have been told nothing about it and I found out about it at about the fourth hour this morning from a man who cannot read."

---

The corridor is about four hundred yards and it is stone and it is the coldest part of the building without any argument about it. The water came into it in the fourth month and stood about four inches off the paving for nine days, and it is still about four inches off the paving, and nobody has washed it off, and about a hundred and forty files went up onto the shelves in a press behind her in her own hand and she has never read them as a set.

She has never been asked to close the corridor either, and there is no form for it, and she would not know who to ask.

The reason she says things in the corridor and not at the window is nine years old and it is this: a person who is at a window is a person asking something, and a person who is walking through a corridor is a person going somewhere, and there is no form in this empire that makes a person going somewhere a party to whatever the room at the end of it is about. **She has given facts in this corridor four or five times in nine years to people who were not parties, and not one of them has ever been served with a syllable of it, and that is the only reason she has been able to do it at all.**

The man from the district works said, "Do people here just say things in a corridor."

"People here say things in a corridor," said Ivet Sarn, "**because it is the only room in this building where nobody is anybody.**"

---

Then the barrow came down the corridor.

It is about fifty foot of corridor between the window and the door the barrow came in by, and he came down it with a barrow of mortar under a wet cloth, and he stopped, because there was a clerk talking to a man at a sill and there is nothing in this empire that obliges a person with a barrow to walk through that.

Ivet Sarn read him one sentence of it out loud without asking him whether he wanted it.

"**A wage can be held against a household and there is no column anywhere for the person whose wage it is.**"

The man with the barrow said, "My wage is my wage," and went on up the corridor, and did not stop again, and was not thanked, and it is not known whether he heard the half of it that was under the cloth.

---

She said the price out loud afterwards, in the corridor, with about four feet of stone on either side of her, which is the way she says things.

"I am a party as of the first week of the twelfth month of next year because a direction named me, and being named is being served, and there is no form anywhere for taking a party back out of a box, and I have said that out loud in this building four times and I am saying it a fifth time now for a man who was not here.

"And I want somebody to notice the order of those two facts. **A person who has already accepted a price is the only person in a building who can tell anybody what the price was.** A person who has not paid it will tell you it is nothing, and it is not nothing, and I have paid mine, and it is about four years of being findable and a key on a string that two people have now written about in a document. And it is cheap. It is much cheaper than the other side of this."

---

She wrote the whole of the schedule out in her own book that afternoon, in ink, with the day on it, all four definitions and the provision and the head of it, about a page and a half.

A hard-backed book with no office on it, and her name nowhere in it, and that is the whole of what it is for.

"**A description on a schedule is not findable.**" she said, to nobody, at about the sixth hour, when the corridor had emptied. "A schedule goes into an office and a schedule is not kept, it is filed, and a filed thing is looked at when somebody has a reason to look at it and the reason will be that somebody wants it. **A description in a book with a day on it in one hand is findable, and I am the one who is going to be asked to fill in a box, and I would like there to be a hand of mine that somebody can compare against my hand in about four years, and there is one now.**"

---

Two things came out of that corridor that had not been said in it.

**The man of thirty-eight does not fit the description, and he has thought about why, and the reason is that the family is entered in the record as a house, and the house is empty, and a man with nothing enforceable behind him has no place of custom or of work anywhere in this empire to be entered, and that is the protection working, and it is not a coincidence, and it is the same nine lines.**

And nobody has been entered. That is the second thing, and it is the one that is going to matter, and there is a person in this empire who fits the description exactly and she is on a roll because a district cannot help it, and she has not been told that there is a document, and nobody has asked her to be anything, and there is no form in this empire for asking her, because asking is being served and she has not been served and she cannot be told and so she cannot be asked.

None of it has been answered by anybody.
