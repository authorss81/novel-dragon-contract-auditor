# Chapter 180: The Plainest Words, and the River

Here is what the third week of the first month of the year after leaves standing, in the plainest words there are, and none of it is a question and none of it has been answered by anybody.

Tamsin Rook wrote it out at about the ninth hour on the fourth day of the third week, at a table in a room in a city about four hundred and thirty miles up this river, with about nine hundred blanks in a box under a counter on the other side of the floor and a woman of thirty-four asleep on a bench for four hours because she walked six days.

She read it back twice, the second reading slower.

There was nobody in the room to hear either reading.

---

A room that was to be cleared on the fourth of the eleventh month of next year, and was not cleared.

A district's notice on a nail in a corridor, with jobs against days and a district at the foot of it and **a day at the end of it in chalk**, and the same day in ink in a hard-backed book with no office on it. The chalk can be washed and the ink cannot. A clerk of thirty-eight wrote both of them and has not washed one of them and would not have washed the other if she could.

A cart that went out of a yard at about the seventh hour with a book in it and **no person in charge of it**, for the length of a cart road of about nine days, because a notice is a list of works and the men on a list of works are working, and a person who is working is not a person in charge of a thing.

A form with four lines on it, filled in two of them, the fourth line empty and no way on earth to fill it, because the person who takes a thing is a person who is then in charge of it, and there is no form that makes a person agree to that.

---

A court direction, applied for twice.

Refused in four days, in a form with a name at the foot of it and a day on it and no box for a why, and the word *bound* in the middle of a sentence.

Granted in a second form, in four days, with a day on it, and **a person in it**. And the person in it is a clerk of thirty-eight who was the only person in this matter who was not a party, and she is one now. Being named in a grant is being served, and there is no form anywhere for taking a party back out of a box.

**A grant is findable, and there is no way in this empire to un-ask, and this is the first time in this matter that being right has cost somebody other than the man who asked.**

A schedule of a term of an office, bound in a vellum gone the colour of a thumb, in the fourth press, in a wall, in an embankment corridor behind a court on a flood terrace.

Nine completed terms with nine holders and nine days at the end of nine terms. **The tenth has a holder and no day, and the holder is the office, in its own name, as its own holder**, and the line where the day would go is empty, and there is no column in that book for the person who emptied it.

A copy of ten entries on four sheets of copyist's paper, in a copyist's hand, dated, signed, findable, in the inside of a coat, and a second copy at a counter in a city. **A copy of a thing is a fact about the copying, and the copy can be read and it cannot be leaned on, and no court on earth can be made to change it.**

---

And a house about four miles off this road, with a door in it and mortar in the door and a name in the mortar.

A mason opened it, because a door in a wall a man is paid to work on is not a seal, it is an obstruction. He was not asked. He is not a body. He is the only one holding a trowel, and he has a boy of nine and is not going to have that boy be a person that somebody comes about.

The district pays three shillings for a day on a wall that is not on its list and a shilling for a day on a wall that is. He has argued that with a man in a shed four times and lost four times and is not going to argue a fifth time for free.

Inside it: a press of undelivered sheets on a shelf, a bench with a groove in it about the length of a forearm, a floor of lime two inches deep with about nine heel prints in it and none of them a size anybody would order shoes in, and a bound register on the bench with five columns.

---

**A form with five lines on it, printed, in nine hundred buildings, for about a hundred and forty years.**

A person says a thing.

A second person is in the room and is not a party to the thing.

The second person writes it down, and the day down, in their own hand.

The second person does not read it back to the person, and does not show it to them.

**And the second person asks the person, before the thing is said, whether she is a party to it, and the answer is written here.**

The fifth of those has never once been done.

Not because it is secret, and not because it is hidden, and not because anybody cut it off. **Because the person it is written for cannot be told what to ask.** A person who is not a party is not served. A person who is not served is not told. And a person who is not told cannot be asked whether she is a party.

---

A register with five columns, in which the fifth column is full, in every line, from the first page to the last, with the name of the office that holds the sheets.

Because the heading at the foot of the sheet is the name of the office, and the fifth line sits under that heading, and a line under a heading looks like a name, and a second person copying a sheet copies the heading and the line under it because a line under a heading is part of the heading as far as the eye is concerned.

**So every register in this empire says every witness was told, and the register is the copy, and nobody has compared a register to a form in a hundred and forty years because comparing is work and the work is not anybody's job.**

A woman of thirty-four in a gallery in a city four hundred and thirty miles up this river turned her head and found a form four feet away from a register she has been keeping for eleven years on the same table.

The answer to the question in the fifth line of it is sitting in about four hundred lines of that book, and there is no column in any form in that building that will take it.

---

**The fifth line was done once**, at about the second hour of the second day of the first week of the first month of the year after, in a kitchen, by a clerk of thirty-four who is a party and could therefore be told, and a woman of sixty-three who is not, and a girl of nineteen who came for a bundle of kindling and gave her name to nobody and is not thanked and does not expect to be.

It took about nine minutes.

It is written in pencil on a sheet in a house about four miles off a road, and it is findable, and a hand can be compared against it for the next forty years.

Nothing whatever happened. The register in the other room still says what it has said for a hundred and forty years, in every line, in the office's name. **A person being findable is worth more than a machine stopping, and it is a completely different thing.**

---

A woman of sixty-three wanted to know what she had written down.

She has had it read to her twice, the second reading slower, by a person she had met four days earlier. It was not what she remembers, and that is the correct answer, because a person remembering a thing and a person reading a thing back are two different acts and only one of them is evidence.

She wanted to know whether she did it wrong, and was told that nobody knows, and that there is no right way and no wrong way, and that she is not to be shown it afterwards, which is the fourth line, and is correct, and is the cruellest sentence anybody in this empire has ever printed.

She is owed an answer to one question and nobody in this matter can give it to her, and it is the only thing in the whole of this that anybody is actually waiting for.

She asked whether anybody is going to go and say to a woman who is dead that it was written down and it is still there. **A person in a house that is shut has no address.** A person who is not told has not been erased, and those are two different things, and the first one is the harder one, and the second one is what this matter is about.

---

A man of thirty-eight who has known for a matter of minutes that he has been eligible, since the day a name came out of a file, for an office in this empire that a human being may hold for at most seven years, and that requires no enforceable inherited asset or right at the moment of appointment.

**That is not a test about being poor. A family holding nothing enforceable is an eligible family and not a poor one.**

What his mother did was real, and it was a child, and it was done on a box, in nine minutes, by a person who was not asked where the idea came from and did not say.

The protection and the concealment are the same act. A family that can be given nothing cannot be served. A person who cannot be served cannot be asked. A person who cannot be asked cannot say no.

A disputed claim is a recorded claim, and a recorded claim has a name on it, and a name on a claim is a road, and a road does not care whether the claim can be enforced. The whole of what she bought is that nothing can be given to him. The whole of what it costs is that he is the cheapest person in this empire for anybody who wants a name in a column and nothing behind the name.

**And nobody forgave anybody.** He did not say his mother was right. She was not said to have been kind. There is no forgiveness in this and there is not going to be any in the next two batches either, and the only thing anybody said about a dead woman was a fact, and the fact is that she was a person who could be made to answer for nothing and she did it to herself, on purpose, with a pen, and that is a thing a record can hold and a person cannot forgive.

---

A man of thirty-four has found out that she is in a column herself, in a hand that is not her hand, in a roll about four bays from a register she has been reading for eleven years.

She has said out loud in a room that she is not going to correct it, and has given three reasons, and the second of them is a man on a bank four hundred and thirty miles down this river who refused to rule a fourth column in about nine seconds and whom she has never met.

A woman of twenty-four has said the price of a party out loud four times and once of them was about somebody else.

She has used the rule of the twelfth month of last year once in this stretch of it and been refused it in a room, in words, with a date. Her third line forbids advice and her fourth line says a fact is not advice, and she has said the words more times than she can count in four months.

There is a drawer in a room in this city with four envelopes in it, one of them there since the first month of the year before last, unsent, unopened, and three others. It is the fifth time in two years she has not thought about it and she is not going to start now.

---

A woman came to a counter with a paper and a seal on it that is not that building's, and bought a copy of a record of property and debt for the Kest house, at fourpence, in full.

She was not asked her name and she was not thanked, and she was told in the ordinary way that a copy is a fact about the copying and will tell her nothing at all about whether any of it can be enforced, and she paid anyway, and went, and has not been at that counter again.

Nobody knows who sent her. Nobody can stop her. A man of thirty-eight stood in the room for four minutes with his hands in his pockets, because fourpence is not his to withhold, and because **a person in this empire gets to be private by nobody being able to say in a room what she is after**, and that is the only privacy there is.

And the finding of the whole of this, said by a clerk of thirty-four in a gallery and repeated in a kitchen and repeated in a room: it is not a villain, and it is not a conspiracy, and it is not an office, and it is not anybody at all. **It takes a second person at about the ninth hour who is copying a sheet, and who does not know that there is a difference.**

---

And a man of thirty-eight who is not going to tell his sister, and has not sent her a word since the last week of the seventh month of next year, and did not send her a word at the end of the last week of the first month of the year after when he had four pages in a book that were about her.

The reason is four words long and it is *a family finds out from itself*. A person finds out about a family from the family and not from a document, and he is the document, and he has been one for about four months, and nobody has told him that either.

She is twenty. She has a lane and a shop and a refused column and a brother's letter of the sixteenth of the eighth of last year that is still unanswered and is still hers to leave unanswered. She has not been told any of this and is not going to be told any of this by anybody in this stretch of it, and there is no form in this empire for telling a person a thing about their own family and not writing it down.

---

**A guarantee under the fire-and-water device stands offered and unanswered at no charge on a bank about four hundred and forty foot long, answerable at any time afterwards by a person answering in its terms, and there is no such person and there is not going to be one, and about sixty children under sixteen are inside it, and inside a guild's blank list, and inside a rotation they have not been asked about, and have been told about none of it.**

**A bill at Lowcross of nineteen pounds three and fourpence, unpaid, nobody liable, and about a penny a day the real cost of it, and no line for a bridge in that fund and there has not been one in nineteen years, and four hundred and eleven people live on the far side and the district's return for four weeks says three hundred and eighty-eight crossings, and those are two figures and they are never the same.**

A second season, in its second month, agreed to by nobody and asked of nobody, running.

About nineteen people who said no with a reason are still behind that bank and have not been asked a second time and are not going to be by this stretch of it. About four people who cannot read a paragraph were never asked once and have now not been asked twice.

A reader of seventeen was asked once and did not knock the second time and gave the reason out loud. She was right. Nobody has followed the answer.

A reader of seventeen, on a written engagement, at forty-five pence a day, four days a week, not thanked, not going to start, and this is the last season in which anybody on that bank can ask her something and be answered for nothing. **She is about four hundred and thirty miles from every person in this paragraph, and none of them has sent anybody to her, and none of them is going to.**

The thirty-one of Mosswake are thirty-one.

---

About four hundred and thirty miles of road between a person and a door, in each direction, at about six days, and a cart road of nine days with a book in it and no person in charge of it, and a lane about four miles off it with a door in it that a mason opened for a wall, and a woman's kitchen about four hundred yards further on with a sheet of paper on the table in it in pencil.

**There is no fund and there is no form for a fund, and a river is running and does not know any of this.**
