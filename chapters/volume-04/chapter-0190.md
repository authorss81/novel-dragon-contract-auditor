# Chapter 190: The Last Week of the Fourth Month, and a Woman Who Was Not Sent

Here is what the last week of the fourth month of the year after leaves standing, in the plainest words there are, and none of it is a question and none of it has been answered by anybody.

A certification with a clerk of thirty-four's hand at the foot of it, written in about nine seconds, after she said the price out loud in a room with three people in it. **The price was nine seconds of her own time and about four years of being findable.** It is the second thing in this matter with her hand on it and the first one she chose.

A notice of a seat in six numbered lines and a foot. The same paper as the last one, the same hand, the same device, and the device is a seat's own and is not the fire-and-water mark. **A seal at the foot of it and no name under the seal**, and the second line asks that no inference of any kind be drawn from the absence of any name.

A schedule in a second hand, not signed, with four definitions in it and a provision. One of the definitions turns on a roll of households, and a roll of households is a list of households and not a list of what anybody owns, and it is a book a district keeps because a district has to know who is in it, and nobody has ever been asked whether the family on that book holds anything at all.

**And one person in this empire fits that definition, and she is on a roll because a district cannot help it.** A roll of households is a roll of households and it says who sleeps where and nothing else, and she has not been entered into anything and nobody has asked her to be.

A wage stopped in a household of one person, by a form with a heading over it and no person under it, and the name that went into the box was the name of a thing and not the name of a person, and there is nothing anywhere on the hold that says which person the thing is.

A refusal entered in the fourth book of an association of dyers in a furnace town about four hundred and thirty miles down this river, in a woman's own hand, with the day on it, and **no name in it, and the reason the name is not in it is in the book in her own hand, four words long.** It is the second time that column has held a refusal with no name under it. The first of those two is six years old and it is not hers, and in six years nobody had thought to ask a woman of twenty for her name, because in six years nobody had thought there was a question to ask it for.

About seventy copies gone out of a counter in a passage at fourpence each in about nine days, and no form anywhere for asking who bought them, and nobody asked and nobody is going to, and the reason is that strangers buy copies and strangers are the only people in this empire who pay for facts.

---

And a printed sheet on a board in that passage with nine lines in a dead woman's hand in it and a man's name at the foot of it and a heading that says what it is and where it came from, and the heading is the whole of why anybody can now tell there was a part.

**What is not in it: a third column with no heading over it, and a woman of sixty-three**, and a man of thirty-eight who did not put her there and will not be thanked for it and has not called it a kindness.

---

Nobody sent her.

That is the whole of it and it is the last thing that happened in the last week of the fourth month of the year after and it is the only thing in the four months of this that nobody arranged.

A printer in the town where the lane is has a window and a price painted in it, and in the third week into the fourth week of the second month he set that sheet in type, because a copy of a printed sheet is worth exactly what a form is worth, which is that anybody may have it for nothing, and a printer who does not set a sheet that is going round four hundred yards of a town is a printer who has decided to keep it, and there is no form anywhere that says he may.

A man who cuts kindling and sells it at every door on that side of the water bought one at fourpence.

He can read. He is the only person in that lane who can, and he has never said so and nobody has ever asked him, and he read the whole of it standing in a doorway, and then he read the nine lines out loud in that doorway to a woman of sixty-three who cannot read a paragraph, **because they are in her own hand and he did not know that and worked it out from the middle of it, and he read them out because there was a person in front of him who could not read them.** He did not have a form for it and neither did the printer's man and there was nobody to ask.

He has told nobody else since, and not the printer and not the woman and not a soul in that lane, and nobody has asked him. He is not going to be asked and he does not want to be, and his name is not in this and he is not in this, and fourpence is about four days of what a man makes selling bundles at doors, and nobody has told him that and nobody is going to.

And she got on a cart.

---

He took a room for four days by the day above a shop in a street in this city, out of about forty-four pounds a year with no fund and no form for a fund, because he has a rented room with a bed in it and he is not having a woman of sixty-three find where he sleeps.

She was at the door at about the sixth hour on the second day and she came up four flights because there is no other way up, and she stood in the room she had not been asked into, with her shawl on, and she did not sit down, and she looked at the board's fair copy on the table where he had left it out, which was a mistake, and he did not take it in.

"There is my hand," she said.

"Yes."

"Read it out."

"No."

"You read it out to me in a house in a lane in the second month of the year after, twice, the second time slower."

"That was a different thing and I told you at the time that it was a different thing and you are not going to get it again, and there is nobody in this room who is going to read it to you a third time and not one of them is going to be good at it."

---

Then she asked the question she came for, and she asked it standing up.

"**Is there more of me than nine lines.**"

He had about nine seconds and he used them and the answer he gave was the truth and the truth does not pay.

"Yes," he said. "There is more of you. There is more of you in about four hundred lines in a bound book in a house about four miles off a road, and there is more of you in about nine hundred buildings in about nine hundred books that have never been compared with anything, and in every one of them there is a column with no heading over it and a set of hands in it that is yours, and nobody has ever read one of those columns against another and nobody would think to."

"Then why can you not tell me."

"Because the only instrument in this empire that can hand a person the rest of what they wrote is a question, and the question is on the fifth line of a printed form, and the fifth line has been done once, in pencil, in a kitchen, in about nine minutes, in the first month of the year after, by a clerk of thirty-four who is a party and therefore can be told and therefore is the only person in the room entitled to ask it, **and she said the price out loud before she asked, and the price was that she can be served anything said in that room for the rest of her life.** And she asked you, in your own kitchen, and you answered, and the girl of nineteen wrote it in pencil, and there is a sheet of paper in that kitchen with your name on it that is not the office's name.

"And that was the once. **Nobody is going to ask you a second time.** You are sixty-three and you are owed one question and you have had it, and there is no form in this empire for asking a person the same thing twice, and if there were one then the second asking would be about the first, and you would be asked in a room whether you remember, and a person remembering a thing and a person reading a thing back are two different acts and only one of them is evidence and I said that to you in the second month and I am not going to soften it now."

**Nobody asked her the fifth line again.** Nobody has sent anybody and nobody is going to.

---

And then she asked the second one, which is the one she is owed and has been owed since the second month of the year after, and she asked it in the same words she asked it in a doorway in the rain with a lamp behind her.

"Will somebody tell her. That I wrote it down. That it is there."

And Marek Kest gave her the same answer, in the same words, because there is only one answer and he had about four days to think of a second one and did not find one.

"**A person in a house that is shut has no address. A person who is not told has not been erased.** Those are two different things, and I have known the second one for about four months and I have never once been able to say it out loud without the first one coming with it. I am saying both of them now, and the first one is the harder, and I am not going to leave it out to make the second one easier."

"That is a very cruel thing to say to a woman of sixty-three in a room," said Bess Tarrant.

"Yes," he said. "It is."

And she said, "Well, I have come four hundred and thirty miles to be told it in a room, and it is worse in a room, and I would have had it in a doorway for nothing, and there is a bed in it and you have not offered it me and I am sixty-three and I am not going to ask you twice."

"I am sorry."

"Do not be," said Bess Tarrant. "**I did not come to be told anything by a man who has no career to lose and no office to be struck out of.** I came because a man who cuts kindling stood in my doorway and read my own hand out loud to me for nothing, and that is the only thing that has happened to me in this that was not done to me, and I have had four months of things being done to me and one afternoon of a man reading, and I know the difference, and I have put it in a book in my own hand with the day against it, and there is no name in it."

"You have?"

"There is no name in it and there is not going to be," said Bess Tarrant, "and I have never once been asked whether I am a party to anything in my life and I am sixty-three and I have kept a house and I have never once been in one of them by name, and that is the only thing I have got and it is not nothing."

---

And then she asked about the girl, and she asked it the way people ask about things they have decided to be all right with.

"The one who wrote it out. The young one."

"She was nineteen and she came to your door for a bundle of kindling and she was in the room because there was nowhere to put the kindling, and she was not asked her name and she did not give it."

"Will she be asked."

"No."

"By anybody."

"**By anybody, ever, and I made sure of that by not writing her down.** There is no form anywhere that says she was ever in that room, and she has a bundle of kindling and a story about a kitchen and that is the whole of what she has and it is not nothing."

"Well," said Bess Tarrant. "Then it is not only me."

"No," he said. "**It is not only you.** That is the half of the fifth line the clerk of thirty-four said out loud in that room and did not make a speech about, and I have not said it in a room to anybody in about four months. You are the wrong person to be told it and the only person in this empire who is owed anything and I could not think of a better one."

She said, "That is a very odd sentence and I do not know what to do with it," and he said, "Neither do I," and that was the end of that, and neither of them said anything else about the girl of nineteen and there is nobody in this room to say it to.

---

**A guarantee under the fire-and-water device stands offered and unanswered at no charge on a bank about four hundred and forty foot long, answerable at any time afterwards by a person answering in its terms, and there is no such person and there is not going to be one, and about sixty children under sixteen are inside it, and inside a guild's blank list, and inside a rotation they have not been asked about, and have been told about none of it.**

**A bill at Lowcross of nineteen pounds three and fourpence, unpaid, nobody liable, and about a penny a day the real cost of it, and no line for a bridge in that fund and there has not been one in nineteen years, and four hundred and eleven people live on the far side and the district's return for four weeks says three hundred and eighty-eight crossings, and those are two figures and they are never the same.**

A second season, in its fourth month, agreed to by nobody, asked of nobody, running, and the wages of about forty-one people on it held against a company whose whole money is four shillings a year and all of it is the rent of a roof.

About nineteen refusals with a reason written in them are still standing behind that bank, unasked a second time, and nobody in this matter is going to ask them again before the season comes round, and when it comes round somebody is going to have to.

About four people who cannot read a paragraph were never asked once and have now not been asked twice, and there is no column for them and there is not going to be one.

The thirty-one of Mosswake are thirty-one, and there is a name and a door against every one of them in a returning officer's own hand, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for asking a returning officer what he wrote down after he had walked it.

---

And here is the last of it, and it is a name.

A form is printed in about nine hundred buildings and it has five lines on it and the fifth is a question that has been asked once, in pencil, in a kitchen, by somebody who said the price out loud first, and nobody is going to ask it again.

A hearing is a room. A room is where records are asked for, and a record is what a person asks for when a person is going to do something with it, and a clerk of thirty-four said that out loud at a counter in the second week of the first month of the year after, and about four years later there is going to be a room in which a woman of sixty-three is asked whether there is more of her than nine lines.

That room has a name. The name is a hearing. **Nobody has written the hearing down yet.**

And in a passage in this city there is a thing on a board at fourpence a copy, and it says, in a dead woman's hand, that it is to be done on a box and not on a paper, because a paper goes to a house and a box goes to a roll.

**This is the first time in about four months that the paper has gone to a house, and nobody asked her to send it.**
