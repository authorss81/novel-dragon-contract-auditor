# Chapter 197: In One Breath, And In That Order

The last sitting was on the fifth day of the fourth week of the sixth month of the year after and it went on until about the ninth hour, and the two sentences were said in one breath at about the seventh hour by a person with nine years of minute books behind her, and the order of them is the whole of this chapter and nobody in the room was permitted to reverse them afterwards.

Orla Denning is about fifty-five and she has kept the minute books of that office for nineteen years and she is not a lawyer and is not a party and is not a judge. She entered a date four years off in the first week of the fifth month of the year after and did not know what she was entering. She has never once been asked what any of it meant.

She was asked on the second day of the third week, in four words, by a person with no office in this matter, and she said yes, and then she was asked again on the third day, and she said yes again, and on the fourth day a person asked her whether she understood what she was writing down and she said that she was going to find out and report.

---

The finding is two sentences and they are in this order and there is no third sentence between them.

**The witness status of a person is restored, the record having been wrongly kept, and the restoration is entered with a day on it and a name at the foot of it, and the name goes on a roll, and there is no way in this empire to put a name back a second time.**

**An act done by a person who was not heard cannot be undone by a court that has no power over her and no standing to make one.**

That is the whole of it. The first is a thing a court can do because the court found the record wrong. The second is a thing no court in this empire can do, and the reason is not that the court is unwilling and it is not that the court lacks the power, and it is that the person is not alive to be heard, and **there is no form anywhere in this empire for a person to be heard after, and the not-hearing is the finding** and it cannot be got round by anybody in this room by any instrument anybody has.

The first sentence is not a consolation for the second. Orla Denning said so, out loud, before she read either of them, in the plain voice of a person who has read a thing out nineteen years' worth of.

"I am going to read two sentences and I am going to read them in this order and I am not going to stop between them," she said, "and if anybody in this room thinks the first one makes the second one better then they should leave now, because they will be here for the rest of it and there is nothing in the rest of it either."

Nobody left.

---

Then the act itself, and the finding describes it without a word for it, because there is no word in this matter.

It is a box. It is a box on a form, and the form is printed in nine hundred buildings, and the box was filled in by a person with authority to fill boxes, in a room, with a second person in it who was not a party and could not ask anything.

It is nine lines in a woman's hand. It is on the page whole in a public document at fourpence a copy in about nine hundred buildings, with a heading that says what it is and where it came from, and a man's name at the foot of it as the person who gave it up, and no date and four lines arguing why there is no date. The last of the nine lines says it is to be done on a box and not on a paper, because a paper goes to a house and a box goes to a roll.

**The box went to a roll.** That is what happened to it and it is the only thing that has happened to it, and there is no instrument in this empire that can be asked what it was for by anybody who was in the room, because the only person who was in that room and was not the person being written about is in nine hundred books under no heading at all, and nobody is going to ask her again.

The finding records that the box went to a roll, and that the person who wrote the nine lines has never been asked what any of them was for by anybody entitled to ask, and that there is no form for asking her, and that the reason there is no form is that the question is on the fifth line of a printed form and the fifth line asks a person who by definition cannot be told.

---

---

Then a roll was brought in, and it is a bound book about four inches thick with a marbled board and a clasp, and it has been in a press in this building for nineteen years, and it is a roll of persons, and a name goes onto it when a record about that person has been found to be wrongly kept, and about nine hundred names have gone onto it in about a hundred and forty years, and this is the first one in nineteen years.

The woman who took the minute turned to a page and put the book on the table in front of Orla Denning and gave her a pen, and neither of them said anything, and Orla Denning read the top of the page down about a quarter of the way and then stopped and gave the pen back and asked for a different one, and the reason she gave was that the first one had been in a pocket.

Then she wrote the name, in her own hand, in the ordinary place, and the day against it, and she did not hurry and she did not go back over it.

**Nobody in that room had seen a name go onto that roll in about nineteen years, and a name only goes onto it when a court has found that a record about that person was wrongly kept, and there is no other door into that book, and a person who was never entered as a person in any record has nothing to be restored to.**

She read the entry back once. She did not read it back twice, and somebody at the back of the room said *the rule is twice* and she said, without turning round, "That is a rule of a bay about four hundred and thirty miles down this river that a girl of seventeen made in the second week of the fourth month and nobody decided it and I am not going to decide it in a room with a rail in it, and the day is in it twice anyway, once in my hand and once in the margin, and there is nothing in this book that can be read back twice because there is no second reading of a roll."

"Is it findable," said Tamsin Rook, from the back.

"It is on a roll in a press in a building in a city and anybody with a copy of the page and about four years can put a hand of mine side by side with about four hundred others and find it," said Orla Denning. "**Findable is not a consolation. Findable is what a name on a roll is, and there is no other way to put one back, and a court that has found that a record was wrongly kept has got one instrument and it is a pen, and I have used it, and I would like the room to understand that I have used it and that nobody in this room is going to be thanked for it and I am not going to be either.**"

---

Then the part of the finding that is a refusal, and Orla Denning said it in her own words and the court entered it in her own words.

"There is a second thing in this room and I am not going to enter it and I am going to tell you why in a sentence so that the not-entering is on the record with a reason on it and is not a silence."

Nobody said anything.

"There is a question in front of me about whether a seat of this empire gave an order in this matter. **I cannot answer it. Nobody in this room can answer it, and it is not going to be answered in about four years by anybody, and the reason is not modesty and it is not caution.** It is that the only document that would bear on it is a document that cannot be produced by anybody."

"What document," somebody said.

"**A notice of a seat in six numbered lines with a device cut into the head of it and a seal at the foot of it, and under the seal there was a name once.**" That document is in this building. That is all I am going to say about it and I am not going to say the name and I am not going to describe the hand and I am not going to say which of the two notices of a seat in this matter it is, and I would like it entered that I declined to and that I was asked and that I was not pressed."

It went in with the day on it.

"And I will give the room the reason for the whole of it, and it is on the table in front of you and it has been on the table since the second week of the second month of this year and nobody has read it aloud." She put her hand flat on a sheet without touching it. "**A document arrived in this building about four hours before a second one did, and the second one had a seal and nothing under the seal, and it asked in its own second line that no inference be drawn from the absence of any name.** The arrival of the second document is the proof. That is the whole of what the name at the foot of the first one was for, and it took one year and a printer and a piece of paper at fourpence a copy to find out, and nobody did anything wrong in either of them."

Nobody thanked her. She is a clerk and clerks are not thanked in this building and she had said in advance that nobody was to.

---

And then she said the thing that is hers, and it is nineteen years long, and she had never said it out loud.

"**A seat is a thing in a room without being a person.**"

She said it and then she stopped, because a room waits.

"I have written nineteen years of minutes about a seat. I have entered things a seat has asked for and things a seat has refused and things a seat has done and things a seat has not done, and I could not tell you one single thing about what a seat is, and I have never been in the same room as one, and there is no post in this empire whose business it is to be in the same room as one. I am the only person in this room who cannot be examined, and I am the only person in this room who is paid nothing, **and those are the same reason and nobody has ever put them together.** An office can be instructed, a person can stop, a body can do neither of those. A seat is not any of the three and that is the whole difficulty and it has been the whole difficulty for about a hundred and forty years and it is not going to be settled this month."

Then somebody asked her, in the ordinary way, whether the finding bore on it.

"No," said Orla Denning. "**The finding is about a record and a roll and a name. The seat is not in the finding.** Naming a thing in a room does not put the thing in the room, and there is no form in this empire that makes a document a person, and a document that names an office is a document and not an office. I have been in this building nineteen years and I have written out a great many documents and not one of them has ever been a person."

---

Then a name was given to a question, and the room did not answer it, and the not-answering is on the record.

A question had been put to a court in this city about nine years ago, and it was put in a room with a number on the door, and the court is a court of this empire and it still exists and it sits on the same ground, and the question was about a clause, and the clause has a name, and the name is written in about nine hundred documents in this empire and is not on any of the boards in this city.

"The Cinder Clause," said Orla Denning, and she said it in a room with a rail in it, once, correctly, and then she stopped, and she did not say anything else about it, and the court recorded that the question exists.

"That is all," she said. "**The question exists. It was asked nine years ago by the court that asked it. It has not been answered in nine years by the court that asked it**, and it is not going to be answered this month, and nobody in this room has been asked it and nobody in this room is going to answer it. She is not going to say one word about what it means, because she does not know, and she has looked, and there is nothing in the eleven books in this building that says."

"Enter that it is named," said somebody, "and that naming it is not answering it."

"Enter that it is named," said Orla Denning, "and that it has not been answered in nine years by the court that asked it, and that naming a question is not the same as answering it, and that the recording of a question is a document. **A document is findable, and I am going to put it in a minute book with the day on it and my name at the foot of it, and that is the only protection a question has ever had in this empire.**"

She wrote it. It took her about nine seconds. She read it back twice and the second reading was slower.

---

And there was a woman in the room who was not a witness and could not be examined and is not on any roll of anybody in this matter, and she said two sentences at about the seventh hour, and nobody asked her for them and nobody stopped her.

Tamsin Rook is twenty-four. She is a party to an instrument about a dead woman's name and a court order requires her in any room he is in and a watch on him requires a person who is not from the office that cannot be named to be in any room he is in, and the two are separate instruments and have never been merged, and **both of them require her.**

"I am a party to an instrument about a dead woman's name," she said. "**From the day I was served I can be served anything about it for the rest of my life, and there is no form anywhere for taking a party back out of a box, and that is the tenth time I have said it out loud.**" Nine of the ten were about a person and this one is about a name on a roll, and a name on a roll is the only thing she has ever been in a room about that she cannot get out of by leaving the room. She said the number with the sentence, because a number is findable and a person who says a number can be held to it, and the number is four years and a hand of hers and about eleven weeks of standing in a corridor four hundred and thirty miles from where she was sitting."

"You are not on the roll," said somebody.

"I am not on the roll," said Tamsin Rook. "**And the woman who is not on the roll is sixty-three and she is in this city, and she cannot be put on that roll by this court or by any other, because the roll is a roll of persons and she was never entered as one.**"

---

The court rose at about the ninth hour of the fifth day of the fourth week of the sixth month of the year after.

A name was restored to a roll with a day on it and a name at the foot of it, and it is findable, and there is no way in this empire to put a name back a second time.

An act done by a person who was not heard was not undone, and cannot be, and the not-undoing is entered with a day on it and a name at the foot of it, and it is findable too.

**And there is no way in this empire to un-undo one either, and that is the half of it that nobody is going to write about**, and a reader is going to be left with two facts and no way of telling which of them this is about, and that is not an accident of the finding and it is not a defect in the record. It is what a room is.

Nobody thanked anybody. There is a form nowhere that requires a person to be thanked and there never has been, and about four hundred and thirty miles down this river a second season is in its fourth month and has not been asked of anybody, and the wages of about forty-one people on it are held against a company whose whole money is the rent of a roof.
