# Chapter 182: Six Lines and No Name

A notice of a seat is the only document in this empire that is entered by a mark and can be asked nothing, and a seat can send one down a river with nothing at the foot of it and it will not be any weaker for that, and the reason is four words long and the four words are in the second line.

Tamsin Rook is twenty-four. She is at a roll-keeper's counter four bays from the fourth counter of the Notaries' Table in Auremar because a paper says so. She has a third line that forbids her advice and a fourth line that says a fact is not advice, and she has been here for about four months, and the paper is not the reason and she has not said so to anybody yet.

The wrapper came on the fourth day of the second week of the second month of the year after and it had no office on it.

---

It came by a cart that came up the road and a boy who came off it with a tray, and the boy put it on the sill of the fourth counter and was gone before anybody had looked up, and the tray had six other things on it and none of the six had an office on them either, and that is how a thing gets to a counter in this city and the reason is that a counter is the one place a person can walk into without saying who they are.

The outer wrapper was opened at the counter because that is what a counter does.

The girl of about twenty-five at the intake desk opened it. She is not a party to anything in this matter and she is not going to become one, and she is the only person in the room who is not, and the reason she is the only person in the room who is not is that she had not been told what was in the wrapper before she opened it, and a person who has not been told what is in a wrapper cannot be made to be a party to what comes out of it.

She opened it, and she read the head of the sheet to herself, and then she carried it the four bays and put it down in front of a party, because a party is served and a person who is not a party is not served and cannot be told what she is holding.

That is the whole of what happened at the sill and it took about nine seconds and nobody has thanked her for it and she does not expect to be thanked for it and there is no form anywhere that says she was ever in the room.

---

The paper is good. It is one side of a sheet of good paper and it is not printed, because everything that has come up that stair for years has been printed.

The device at the head of it is a seat's own device and it is cut into the sheet and not stamped, and it is not the fire-and-water mark, and anybody in this room who has seen the fire-and-water mark on three things would know the difference in a second and none of the three people in it is going to say so, because there is a form about a device and about three in one building four hundred yards from here and it was read out in full in open court and applied once, and the registrar said then that he was not going to read it again and he was going to apply it.

The foot is a seal.

There is nothing under the seal.

---

Tamsin Rook went out of the room and came back in about nine minutes with the other one.

The other one is in a press in her own bays and it is in a wrapper with her own hand on the outside of it and the day she got it on the outside of that, and she has never told anybody in this building that she keeps it, and she did not tell anybody now, and she put it face up on the table beside the new one and squared the two edges with two fingers.

Both sheets are the same paper. Both are the same hand. Both are set out in six numbered lines and a foot. The first came up that stair on the tenth of the fourth month of last year and it has been in a file above a stair ever since and treated exactly as the file treats it, which is as a seal with a person written under it and nothing else.

She put her thumb on the bottom of the old one.

"There is a name under that one," she said, "and I am going to point at it and I am not going to say it out loud in this room, and Marek is going to look at me and I am going to let him."

He looked at her.

"Not because I have not got it in my head," said Tamsin Rook. "**Because this document arrived about four hours ago and it has just proved that the absence of a name is a thing a seat can use, and there are now two notices of a seat in the world and one of them has a person's name at the foot of it, and I am not going to say that name aloud in a room about the other one.**

"A person put a name at the foot of a document and a seal above it, and the sixth line of that document says that no person of or belonging to that seat is party, cited or examinable, **and she wrote her own name underneath a line that says she cannot be examined, and everybody in this building has spent about a year working out why, and the whole of what that name was for is the removal of one inference.** It was not a signature. It was a person declining to be able to hide, in her own hand, at the bottom of the page, once, and then about four hours ago a seat of the same office sent the same kind of paper up a river with a seal and nothing under it and a second line that asks that no inference be drawn from the absence of any name.

"And the difference between those two notices is the whole of what has changed in this empire in about a year and it is fourpence and a printer and a second person copying a heading over a woman, and nobody has done anything wrong in either of them."

---

"There are three differences," she said, "and I am going to take about four minutes over them and I am not going to advise anybody about any of them, and if I am asked a question that is not a fact I will not answer it and my third line is why."

"The first is the second line, and it is four words different in the middle and it is the whole of what the document is."

She put her finger on the old one and then on the new one without touching either of them, which is a thing a person does in a room with two documents in it.

"This one says: *No inference of any kind may be drawn from the fact that a person has not been named.* That one says: *No inference of any kind may be drawn from the absence of any name.*"

"That is four words," said Marek Kest.

"That is four words and it is the entire difference between a seat that has put a person at the foot of a document and a seat that has not, and the first of those two is the only one anybody in this city has ever been able to point at."

---

"The second is the sixth line. The old sixth line named the office, and said the First Seat held an old authority, and stated it so that no person should be in doubt that the notice was made by a seat and not by an individual, and stated it without a claim and without a threat.

"The new sixth line does the opposite. It declines to name the holder of the office of the seat. It asks that the office be treated as the seat in all things touching the matter. It says that no person is to be cited for the office and no office is to be cited for a person. And it says, in its own words, that nothing in it is a claim and nothing in it is a complaint.

"So the old one had a person in it and the new one has an office in it, and an office cannot be sued and an office cannot be examined and an office does not have to be told anything, and there is no form in this empire for making an office answer a question, and the reason there is no form for it is the same reason there is no form for making a mark answer one."

---

"The third difference is the third line, and the third line is new, and the third line is the whole of the document."

She read it out. She read it out because a person who cannot read it must have it read to them, and there is one in the room who has not been asked whether she wants it, and the not-asking is deliberate and is the practice of about a hundred and forty years and nobody has ever been asked to confirm it.

> *Three. Where a recorded claim is entered and marked disputed, and the family of the claimant holds no enforceable asset, the person of that family who is of age and resident is the family's representative in this matter, is to be entered as such in the office of the seat, and no further consent of that person is required.*

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

"**Say the last part of it again,"** said Marek Kest.

She said it again.

---

"There is a schedule with it," she said. "It came in the same wrapper and it is in a different hand and it is not the hand of the notice, and the office sends it and does not sign it, and there is no seal on it and nothing at the foot of it either."

She turned it round so that the two people at the table could see it and did not hand it round, because a document handed round in a room is a document four people have touched.

> *Of age and resident: having reached the age of eighteen years, and having a place of custom or of work entered in the roll of households of the district in which the person is resident. That roll is kept by the district. It lists the households in the district. It does not list the rights, holdings or claims of any person in it.*
>
> *Family: any person entered in a register of claimants, or in a roll of holdings, or in an entry of a dispute, and any person who may be described out of one of those as a member of a household so entered.*
>
> *A sum payable to a person of that family: any sum, however arising, entered in the office of the seat as payable to a person of that family, whether or not the sum is capable of being enforced, and whether or not anybody has claimed it.*
>
> *No further consent: nothing done under the notice requires the agreement of the person it is done to, and no person may refuse to be entered by reason only of not having agreed to be entered.*
>
> *And it is provided that where a sum payable to a person of that family is entered as held, the wages of a person of that family are held at the same rate and for the same period, and a person whose wages are so held is not to be paid that held sum by any person, on account of the person of that family.*

---

"That is four definitions and a provision," said Tamsin Rook, "and I am going to give you four facts about them and I am not going to tell you what to do with any of them, and the reason is my third line and the other reason is that I do not know."

She put her hands flat on the table on either side of the schedule and did not touch the paper.

"**Fact one. Nothing in that notice is served on anybody.** A notice is a document and a document goes to people. This one has a seal and no name, and a seal is not an address, and a document with no address is not a late document, it is a document that is nowhere. I have been in four bays for about four months and I can tell you where every document that has come into this building went, and I have never in four months had one that I could not.

"**Fact two. The second line asks that no inference be drawn from the absence of a name, and a line that asks for that is doing the work of a name and not the work of a protection.** The old second line was a shield. This one is a hood, and it is fitted by the same hand that fitted the shield, and the fitting is the ordinary way.

"**Fact three. The third line does not require anybody's agreement, and the schedule says so in terms, and the schedule is the part that actually holds a person.** The notice requires consent to be unnecessary. That is not the same as not mentioning consent. It is a thing you write down on purpose and it takes about four words and it is on the fourth line of the schedule.

"**Fact four, and it is the one I would put in a book if I were going to put anything in a book.** The definition of *of age and resident* turns on a roll of households. A roll of households is a roll of households. A district keeps one because a district has to know who is in it, and it is the ordinaryest book a district has, and there is nothing sinister in it and nothing has been done to it. It does not list rights, and the schedule says in its own words that it does not list rights, and that sentence is in the schedule on purpose and it is the most honest sentence in the whole of it."

---

Marek Kest had not touched either document and had not spoken for a while.

"And the hold," he said.

"The hold is the last line of it and it is about wages and it names no person either, and the thing that is held is whatever the thing is, and if the thing is a household then the household is held, and if the thing is a household of one then it is one person who is held, and the form will not say who, because the form does not have to." She looked at the girl of about twenty-five. "**A person is held by a description. That is the whole of the mechanism and it is not a secret and it is not hidden and nobody cut it off.**"

---

Then she said the thing she says, and she said it for the fifth time, and this time it was not about herself.

"I am a party to an instrument about a dead woman's name. From the day I was served I can be served anything about it for the rest of my life, and there is no form anywhere for taking a party back out of a box, and I have said that out loud in four rooms and I am saying it now for the fifth time and it is not softer than the other four.

"And I want the room to notice that this time it is about somebody who is not in it. **The sentence works in both directions and it has always worked in both directions and the second half of it is the half nobody prices.** A person in a matter is findable, and a person who is findable can be asked, and a person who cannot be served cannot be told, and a person who is not told cannot be told what is being done to her, and a person who cannot be told what is being done to her cannot stop it. I am the first half. There is a person somewhere in this empire this month who is the second half and she has not been told and she is not in this room and there is no way for me to warn her, because warning a person is serving her."

The girl of about twenty-five said, "Should I not have opened it then."

"No," said Tamsin Rook. "**You should have opened it, because you were going to open it anyway and the only difference is that now I can say out loud in a room that you did.**

---

"And the last thing about the document, and then I will stop," she said, "and it is a fact and not advice and it is the reason I have been sitting here for about nine minutes.

"Naming a question is not the same as answering it. I know that better than anybody in this city and I have said it in rooms. **The trouble with that is that a question can be refused out loud.** A person can look at a question and say no. A form cannot be refused out loud, because a form is not asking. And the thing that came in that wrapper this morning is not a question. It is a form with a seal on it, and there are about nine hundred buildings in this empire with a form in them that nobody can be refused by, and this one has a seat's device at the head of it and no name at the foot, and it is going into rooms for the next four years and it is not going to be the biggest thing in any of them, because a form is not a thing anybody argues with. It is a thing that happens."

---

Then she said a thing that was not about the document and that she has not said in this building before.

"A person who reads things out loud to people who cannot read them is a person who can be told to stop. I have said that sentence out loud in this city about a girl of seventeen four hundred and thirty miles down this river, and nobody has followed it, and it is still true and I still say it when it is the right thing to say.

"**And it is not only her.** A clerk can be told to stop copying. A copyist can be told to stop reading. A man with a coat full of paper can be told to stop bringing it up a road. Every one of those is a person and every one of those is somebody's. The only person in this room who cannot be told to stop by anybody is the one who is paid nothing and answers to nobody, and there is no form in this empire for that, and it is not a thing anybody would think to want."

"Who is that," said Marek Kest.

"You are," said Tamsin Rook. "You have no office and no fund and no register and no certification and a bar discharged on the twelfth of the ninth month of last year by a one-line application you wrote yourself. **Nobody can instruct you and nobody can pay you and nobody can thank you, and that is why you are the most dangerous person in this matter and it is also why there is nobody in this room who can protect you.**"

Nobody thanked her. She had not asked to be thanked and she had said in advance at a counter four bays from here that nobody was to, and it is the fifth time she has said it and she has never once been thanked and has never once expected to be.

---

The notice went into a press in her own bays, in a wrapper, with the day on the outside of it, and the schedule went in beside it unsigned and undated, and neither of them can be produced in a room by anybody, because there is no form in this empire that lets a person produce a thing that is not in a book with a name on the spine.

And about four hundred and thirty miles down this river there is a furnace town with a dyers' lane in it of eleven houses, and the district that lane sits in keeps a roll of households, because a district has to know who is in it, and the roll does not list rights, and nobody has ever been asked whether the family on that lane holds anything at all, and there is a name in that roll at a house of one room over a shop with a woman of twenty against it, and she has never been told that she is in anything, and there is no form in this empire for telling a person a thing about themselves and not writing it down.
