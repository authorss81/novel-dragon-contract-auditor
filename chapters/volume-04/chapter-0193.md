# Chapter 193: A Stub, And Three Times In One Form

She was served on the second day of the third week of the fifth month of the year after, at the window, about four feet off the ground, by a man who carries notices for the office of a seat and who put four things on the sill and said the word served out loud, which he is required to do and which he said properly, and she was standing at the window when he said it and had a rag in her hand. **All four of them are about a man called Pell Vey**, and she is the only person in that building who has ever had to write any of them down, and she wrote them all down. About four days afterwards a girl of about twenty-five at an intake desk four hundred and thirty miles up this river asked her a question about a board in a passage and did not get an answer. That is a different thing and it is not in this.

Ivet Sarn is thirty-eight. She has worked at that window for nine years. She is a party as of the first week of the twelfth month of next year and being told has stopped being a cost and started being a fact. She is in charge of four presses in the embankment corridor behind her and nobody ever asked her what the presses were for. She has a key on a string that she shortened twice herself and there is no form in this empire that makes a person be in charge of a thing.

Being served has stopped being a cost. **Being documented is a different thing and it is not cheaper**, and she priced it out loud in the corridor about four minutes after he had gone down it, and there was nobody there to hear it, and she said it anyway, because a fact said to nobody is still a fact.

---

The four things.

The first is a delivery stub. It is a slip about the size of a playing card and it has a day on it and two numbers on it and a line at the foot with a hand on it, and a delivery stub is the one document in this empire that is designed to be handed to a person who is not the person it is about, which is why it is on four of the six presses in every building that keeps one.

The hand on the foot of it is not her hand and it is not the hand of the man who carried it, and she put it beside four other hands within the hour and it took her about nine minutes.

"It is Pell Vey," she said, to the corridor. "He is the head of the Notaries' Table and he has held the cabinet key in this city for years, and there is not one document in this building with his hand on it that I have ever been able to read, and there is one now. It is his own hand and there is no office on the stub and there is nothing else on it, and a man who puts his own hand at the foot of a thing that is going to be carried to a building four hundred yards from his own is a man who has decided to be findable, and I have been at this window nine years and in nine years I have not once seen an office do it."

**A hand identified by comparison is a hand nobody can deny, and that is the first time in this matter that anything has been a document against a person who is alive and in a building.**

---

The second is a file, and the file is in four places.

That is not a secret. A file that is in one place is a file that can be lost, and a file that is in four places is a file that cannot be lost, and anybody who has kept files for as long as that man has kept them keeps them in four places, and the four places are on paper and the paper is a list and the list is in his own hand.

It came to her on the fourth day of the third week with the same man and the same word at the sill, and the list of the four places has four lines on it, and three of the four lines are buildings she can put a name to, and the fourth line is a back room.

**The fourth place is a back room off a corridor she has walked every working day for nine years, and she has never once been in it, and there is no list anywhere in this building that says who is allowed in it.**

"I have got a room," she said.

"You have got a room," said the man who carries notices, "and it is not mine and I do not know what is in it and I am not going to tell you what is in it, and I am telling you that I am not going to tell you, because I have said the word served and that is the whole of what I came to do."

---

The third of the four things took the rest of that week and it is the reason the other three are a document and not a grievance.

On three days, on a form with a question on it, the same person was asked the same question, and on all three days he refused, and he refused in the same words each time, and the words were fourteen words long and they were his own and they did not vary by a word.

She has the three. They are in a wrapper with a day on it in her own hand. **A refusal in one form on three days is a person being consistent, and a person being consistent is a document.** She said that out loud in the corridor on the last of the three days and then she said the other half of it, which is the half that took her nine minutes to get to and which is the reason she wrote all three of them down.

"**A man in this building could have refused in four different forms and been unfindable, and he did not.** There is no form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be consistent, and the fact that he was is the most exposed thing about him that anybody has found this year, and nobody made him do it and he cannot now unsay it."

Nobody asked her for that. She said so, twice, in two rooms, to two different people, and wrote it down, because the whole of what she is worth in this matter is that she is the only person in this building who can be told things and is therefore the only person in this building who can be examined about them, and being examinable is not the same as being asked.

---

The fourth thing is the back room, and she went to it on the fifth day, and she went because a file in four places is a file that can be produced, and a file that can be produced is a file somebody will ask about, and it is better that the first person to ask about it is the clerk who has been in charge of the other three.

The room is off a corridor she has walked every working day for nine years. There is a table in it and a bench and a chair with a broken front leg and a window about two feet wide with a pane out of it.

There was nothing in it.

**That is the finding and it took her about four seconds and it is the reason this chapter is not about a man who hid something.** In nine years she has been able to say that she did not know what the presses were for. She can now say that she knows what the fourth place is for, and that it is for a person with a pen to sit at a table with, and that a person with a pen at a table can put a thing into a column and take a thing out of a column, and that both of those are the same hand and the same table, and that there is no instrument anywhere that stops it.

The thing that had been done in the room was in a margin, in a copy of a roll, in a hand that was not his, with a day against it, and the day was the fourth day of the fourth week of the ninth month of next year, and it corrected a line in which a body stood in a column headed for the person who did a work, and put a person's name there instead, and wrote underneath why, and signed it.

It is nine minutes of work. It is the only thing in the whole of this matter that is a record on purpose and not a record by accident, and it was given away.

**A provincial archivist, on the ordinary round, with a box on her hip and a list of eleven stops, came to this building for four days and corrected a line and gave it away.** She was not paid for it because nobody asked her to be paid and there is no form for it, and she was not thanked and she has not been thanked and nobody is going to thank her. She is not the person who did the wrong and she is not the person who did the right. She is the person who wrote down what the wrong was so that it could be looked at, and she said in that back room in the ninth month of next year, in words, that it is not a crime and does not make her a good person, and that she would like both of those understood before anybody says anything kind about it.

She does not know. That is the sentence that belongs here and it is the hardest one in the chapter.

**She does not know that her hand is in a fourth place of a file in a building about four hundred and thirty miles from her own office, and she does not know that a clerk of thirty-eight has spent about nine minutes in a corridor matching her writing to three other hands.** She does not know that a man is on a form three times in three days refusing in the same fourteen words because of a line in a margin. She is a provincial archivist and she is on the round and she has nine counties in her and she has done it nine times in about nine years and it is the only thing she has ever been proud of and she said in a back room in the ninth month of next year, in words, that it is not a crime and does not make her a good person and she would like both of those understood before anybody says anything kind about it.

The same surname is not a door and has never been one. It is a fact about her and not a fact about the room she sat in, and the man who carries the notices does not know it and has not been told and is not going to be told, and Ivet Sarn is not going to be the person who tells him, because telling him is a service and being served is a party.

---

On the sixth day a man of about thirty-four came up to the sill with a paper in his hand and a question, and the question was the one a person asks at a window, and she answered it in about four minutes and then could not answer the second one at all.

He had been at the district works since he was seventeen and he had a fortnight's wage held and he wanted to know whether the thing holding it had a name on it.

"It has a description," said Ivet Sarn. "**It is a form with a heading over it and nothing under the heading, and what went in the box is a thing and not a person, and the box says the person, institution or thing engaged in the matter, and a person fills a box the way the box is printed.** I have read it. I did not fill it in and I have never been asked what the words mean and I am not going to be."

"Then is it me."

"No," she said, "and I want you to hear the reason, because the reason is worse than the wage. **You are not on it, and if you were on it you would be a person in a box with a heading over it, and a person in a box can be asked about for the rest of their life.** There is no form anywhere for taking a person back out of a box. So the thing that is holding your wage has protected you from being findable and cost you fourteen days of a man's work, and both of those are true, and neither of them is anything to do with me, and I am the clerk and I have been here nine years."

He wanted it written down. He said so twice.

"I cannot," said Ivet Sarn, "**and I am not going to be able to, and I have looked for a form for it this morning and there is not one, and I have been the person at this window for nine years and that is the first time I have wanted a form and not found one and it is the worst morning I have had here since I came.** I can put it in my own book with the day on it and my own hand at the foot of it, and that is a record of me saying it to you and not a record of you, and it is worth exactly what it is worth, which is that there is one person in this district who can say it happened and her name is mine and she is a party."

"Then put it in that."

She put it in that, and she read it back to him, and she read it back a second time slower because he said please, and it is in a book with no office on it, and nobody thanked her and she is not thanked, **and he went off down the terrace with fourteen days of a man's work still held against a thing with no name on it and did not know that a clerk had put his face in a book, and that is the correct arrangement and it is not a comfort.**

---

She wrote all of it down that evening, in her own book with no office on it, in the corridor, with the day on it, and she read it back twice, and the second reading was slower and was for her and not for anybody, and she did not read it a third time.

Then she did the thing she has started doing and has not told anybody about, which is that she wrote at the bottom of the page, in the same hand, the reason she wrote it.

> *Written down because a hand of mine is the only hand anybody is going to be able to put side by side in about four years. I have now done this three times since the third month and every time I have given the same reason and the reason has not got any better and I have not stopped.*

Nobody thanked her. She had said in advance in the corridor that nobody was to, and nobody did, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be thanked and there never has been and she is not the first clerk in a building to find that out and she will not be the last.

And a hand of hers is now in a fourth place, and a man who is alive and in a building is on a form three times, and a woman about four hundred and thirty miles away on a fourth stop of a round has a pen in a box on her hip and does not know any of it, **The finding of this is not a man and it is not a woman and it is a hand.** Two of the four places in that file have a name on the foot of them and one of them does not, and the one that does not is the one that is going to cost somebody something, and the somebody is not in this matter and does not know they are in it.
