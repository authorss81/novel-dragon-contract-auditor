# Chapter 191: A Date In A Minute Book, and a Name At The Foot Of It

The first week of the fifth month of the year after there were two dates entered into one minute book in a building off the old river road, and both of them had a name at the foot of them, and neither of the two names was the name of the person the date is about.

Marek Kest is thirty-eight. He has no office and no fund and no register and nothing in this empire he could certify anything with, and a bar of his own that came off him on the twelfth of the ninth month of last year on an application of one line which he wrote himself. He has a rented room with a table in it and a book with no office on it and about four feet of a wall he does not use. He is the person who gave a dead woman's nine lines to a printer in the second month of the year after and he is not thanked and has not asked to be.

He went because a clerk of thirty-four said out loud at a counter in the second week of the first month of the year after that a record is what a person asks for when a person is going to do something with it, and in about four years there is a hearing, and a hearing is a room, and a room is where records are asked for. That was in the second week of the first month. The four years has not gone anywhere and nobody has produced it and it is the only figure in this matter that anybody ever wrote down in a room and then did not enter.

---

The minute books are on a shelf in a room on the ground floor with a window about two feet wide and a pane out of it, and there are eleven of them, and they are on a shelf because a shelf is what a book that is looked at twice a year lives on.

The woman at the table is Orla Denning. She is about fifty-five and she has kept the books for nineteen years and she did the first four of them in a hand that is not the hand she is using now, and she is not a lawyer and she is not a party and she is not a judge, and she has never been asked what any of it meant.

"You are not on the list," she said, before he had got his coat off.

"I know what I am not on," said Marek Kest. "I want to know whether there is a day."

"Then you want a thing that is not in this room, and the thing that is not in this room is about four hundred yards down the road and a shilling a question, and I would tell you to go and pay it and you would not go."

"I have paid fourpence for things that told me less."

"Fourpence is a copy," said Orla Denning. "**A shilling is a person. There is no price in this building for a day and if there were one I would not know what to do with it.**"

---

She turned the volume round to face him and did not hand it to him, which is what a clerk does with a book.

The entries are in a column. The column is not ruled. Each entry has a thing entered, a place, a day, and a name at the foot, and the name at the foot is the name of the person who entered it, and it is in the clerk's own hand, and it is the reason the entry is a document and not a fact about the world.

**A date with nobody's name on it is not a summons to a person.** He said that out loud, standing up, in the way he says things he has been carrying since the first month, and she looked at him for longer than a clerk at a table usually does.

"You are the second person in this matter who has said that sentence to me in four weeks," she said, "and the first one was a woman who was a party to a thing and told me the price of it out loud before she said it, and you are not a party to anything and you have not told me anything, and I would like it noted that I noticed."

"Noted by you and by nobody else."

"Yes."

---

The first entry is the one he came for.

The hearing is a restitution hearing into which a recorded claim of property and debt is entered and marked disputed, and it has been entered and marked disputed for longer than either of them has been in a room, and it has had no day on it until this week because a seat does not put a day on a thing until the day is needed by somebody.

It has a day now. It is the fourth day of the second week of the fifth month of the year after, at the second hour, in a room with a rail in it, and the notice of it went out in the first week and there are six numbered lines on it and a foot, and the device at the head of the notice is a seat's own and is cut into the sheet and not stamped, and it is not the fire-and-water mark.

**The notice is dated for the day the thing happens on.** That is what a notice is, and it is the reason it can be produced in a room by anybody holding it, and it is the whole difference between a notice and the entry it was entered from, which is in this book on a shelf and is of no use to anybody standing in a room eight days later.

"There is a seal at the foot of that notice," said Marek Kest, "and there is nothing under the seal."

"There is."

"And the sixth line will not name whoever holds the office."

"**The sixth line will not name the office's holder and the second line asks that no inference be drawn from the absence of any name**, and both of those are in there, and I have entered the day of a thing whose paper says that, and I am not going to say the name at the foot of the other notice, and you did not ask me for it."

"I did not ask you for it."

"You did not," said Orla Denning, "and I want that said in the book, and I am going to write a line in the margin saying that nobody asked me for it, and it is the first line I have written in nineteen years that is not a date."

She wrote it. It took her about nine seconds and he counted them, and he did not tell her he counted them.

---

The second entry is the one he did not come for, and he did not know it was in the book until she had turned two leaves.

It is four years off.

It is a room, and the room is named the way rooms are named in that book, which is a floor and a stair and a door, and the thing entered against it is a question, and the question is whether there is more of a person than nine lines.

There is no name at the foot of the question. There is a day at the foot of the question, four years off, and the day is the figure a clerk of thirty-four said out loud at a counter in the second week of the first month, and the figure was entered on the day it was said and it was not in a minute book, and it has not been in one since until now.

"Read it again," said Marek Kest.

Orla Denning read it again.

"Who gave you that."

"A sheet. With a day on it and a reference at the top of it and no office on it that I could see."

"**And the person in the question.**"

"There is a person described in it and not named. About sixty-three. It says the person is the second person in a room and it says the person is to be asked it before anything else is said, and there is a line under that which says the person has been asked once."

"She has been asked once."

"It says so."

"And it is four years off."

"It is four years off the day a copy was bought at that counter and I have entered it as I was given it, and I did not know what I was entering, and I am telling you that in the room with you standing there. **A clerk of nineteen years who says afterwards that she did not know is a clerk of nineteen years who was asked, and I would rather be asked.**"

"I am asking."

"Then I did not know," said Orla Denning, "and I entered it anyway, and there is nobody in this building who could have stopped me and there is nobody in this building who would have, and I would like both of those facts in the book and I am writing them."

---

He read the entry again himself, standing up, with his hand on the edge of the table and not on the page, because a hand on a page in that room is a thing that gets written down.

**A person who is not told cannot be told, and a person who cannot be told cannot be warned, and a person who cannot be warned cannot be asked whether she wants to be asked.** That is the ordinary way and it has been the ordinary way for about nine years and it is not going to stop, and the date is in a book on a shelf in a room with a pane out of the window, and a date is findable, and a person cannot be stopped from a date.

"Where is she," he said.

"I do not know and it is not written and it was not given to me."

"She is in this city. She came up the road on a cart about ten weeks ago and stood in a room above a shop that I hired by the day for four days and asked me a question I could not answer, and she has not gone home, or she has gone home and come back, and either way she is on this side of about four hundred and thirty miles of road and nobody sent her and nobody can tell her."

"Then somebody is going to tell her."

"Somebody is going to try," said Orla Denning, "and there is no form in this empire for telling a person about a date, and a notice can be served on nobody and a date can be stood in by anybody, and **the only protection a person has against a day is to be told about it**, and being told is a service and being served is a party and there is no way back out of a party and I have known that since about the seventh month of next year and I did not learn it in this room."

---

Then he did the thing he had come to do, which was to look at the entry, and the entry was correct.

A day. A place. A reference. A name at the foot, in a clerk's own hand, with a day on it in the margin beside it saying that nobody asked her.

**That is the whole of the protection.** It is fourpence of ink and about nine seconds of a woman's time and it is the only protection anybody in this matter has ever had, and it works in one direction: it makes the clerk answerable and it does nothing whatever for the person the date is about. He said that out loud and she wrote it down because he asked her to and he said why, and the reason is that a person who writes a thing down in a book in a room in front of a witness is a person who cannot be asked in about four years what she meant by it.

"You understand that you have just made a document about my own reasons," he said.

"I have made a document about what you said in a room," said Orla Denning. "That is the trade and it is the only trade there is. **The thing I do not have is the part where somebody tells a woman of sixty-three in a city four hundred and thirty miles from her own lane that there is a room with a day on it and she is in it.** I have entered the day. I cannot enter the telling, and there is no column for it in that book and there is no column for it in any book in this building, and I have looked, and I have been looking for nineteen years."

---

He left it at that and went down four hundred yards and stopped on the step, because the next part was the part he had come for and it had not been in the book and it was in a street.

The hearing has a day. That is the whole of what a day does to a piece of paper.

A third line has had nothing to do since the second week of the second month, when a wrapper came up a stair in a city on a tray with six other things on it and none of the six had an office on it either. The third line provides that where a recorded claim is entered and marked disputed and the family of the claimant holds no enforceable asset, the person of that family who is of age and resident is the family's representative, is to be entered as such in the office of the seat, and no further consent of that person is required.

**A third line with no day under it is a third line.** A third line with a day under it is a thing to be done, and what is to be done is a box, and the box is a box that has been printed and is in about nine hundred buildings, and the box says the person, institution or thing engaged in the matter, and a person fills a box the way the box is printed.

It went into a box on the fourth day of the third week of the fifth month of the year after, in an office about nine miles from a counter, by a clerk who was not a villain and who did not know and who was not asked.

The name at the foot of it is a clerk's and it is on a form and not on a roll, and the name of the person in the box is a name, and the person in the box is about four hundred and thirty miles away on a lane with eleven houses on it in a furnace town, and she is one of the people the line catches and not one of the people it was built for, and there is a reason for that and the reason is the ninth line and the fourth line in a dead woman's hand and nobody chose it.

Nobody was asked. Nobody was told. Nobody knew.

**And it was not done by anybody who wanted it done, and it was not done in a hurry, and it was done in about nine seconds by a person doing her job properly, which is the whole of what has gone wrong in this matter for about a hundred and forty years and is the reason nothing can be put right by anybody being found.**

---

He did not go to the counter and ask to be entered, and there is a reason for that which he wrote down that night and it is in the book with no office on it and no day on it, and it is this: **a man with nothing enforceable behind him has no place of custom or of work anywhere in this empire to be entered, and a man who is entered as a representative of a family that holds nothing is a name in a box with a heading over it, and the whole of what the third line wanted was a person in a column, and he is the cheapest person in this empire for it, and the reason he is the cheapest is that his mother made him that on purpose, and she was right and he is not going to say so and nobody has told him she was kind.**

He did not tell his sister. She came up the road in the third week into the fourth week of the second month and found out on a step in a passage that he had put nine lines of hers in her mother's hand on a board, and she has not been in this city since and she has not written and he has not written, and there is a letter of his she has not answered since the sixteenth of the eighth of last year and it is hers to leave unanswered and he is not going to ask her about it.

What he did instead was write the two dates down.

Both of them, in his own hand, in a book with no office on it, with the days under them, and under the first one the words *asked for by me and entered by a clerk who was not asked what it meant*, and under the second one the words *not asked for by anybody and entered anyway*, and the day on both of them in the same hand and the two entries four days apart.

**A book with no office on it is a thing a form cannot be answered with, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that makes a person produce a thing that is not in a book with a name on the spine, and that is the only kind of record a person in this matter has ever had, and he has had one since the first month of next year and it has cost him nothing and it is worth nothing and he is going to keep it.**

Nobody thanked him. He said in advance, in the room, out loud, that nobody was to, and nobody did, and there is a form nowhere that requires a person to be thanked and there never has been.

And about four hundred and thirty miles down this river there is a foreman of fifty-one who has never once in nine years been asked to read anything by anybody at the district works, and a seat's paper arrived with her name at the top of it about two weeks ago, and she has not opened it, and there is no form anywhere that says she has to.

**Nobody sent her, the same as nobody sent the woman of sixty-three, and there is no form in this empire for a person who comes on her own account, and the two of them are not compared and are not going to be.**
