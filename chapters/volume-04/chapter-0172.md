# Chapter 172: The Fourth

There are two ways for a thing to travel in this empire without anybody being responsible for it, and both of them are on a list of works.

Marek Kest worked that out at about the fourth hour on the fourth day of the eleventh month of next year, standing at the west end of an embankment corridor about thirty feet long, with a copy of ten entries on four sheets in the inside of his coat and a form with four lines on it in the same pocket, watching four men lift a book off a shelf.

The book is about the size of a hymn book and it is heavier than a hymn book. It is bound in a vellum that has gone the colour of a thumb and the stitching is visible at the top of the spine.

**The press stays in the wall. The thing in it goes into a cart. A wall is not a thing anybody moves, and that is the only reason the press is not being carried down the corridor on four men's shoulders, and the men with the shoulders are not here.**

The man who was here last week, with the barrow and the toolbag, was not here today. There were four other men and one of them had the barrow from the district and one of them had a coil of rope on his shoulder.

---

"You are the man who was in here last week," said the man with the rope.

"I am."

"Then I am not going to tell you what is in it and you are not going to ask me, and if you ask me I will say it is district property and that is a true answer and it is also the whole of what I am able to give you."

"I am not going to ask you. I have a copy of it in my coat and I made the copy myself standing up at about the ninth hour in the second week of the tenth month of next year, in a corridor, with a woman holding a door with her hip because both her hands were needed for the page, and I did not take the leaf out of the binding and I said out loud twice why."

"Then you have done more with it than the district has," said the man, "and I am not going to say that to anybody."

They lifted it into the cart.

There is no form in this empire for a person being in charge of a thing in a cart for nine days. There is a form for a thing being consigned and a form for a thing being received and a form for a thing being weighed at a landing, and not one of the three has a line on it for a person, and a person is the only thing in the four of them that can be instructed and the only thing that can stop.

**A notice is a list of works, and the men on a list of works are working, and a person who is working is not a person in charge of a thing.**

He said that out loud in the corridor at about the fifth hour and the man with the rope said, "That is a very clever thing to say and it is about four in the afternoon and we are loading."

"I know what time it is."

"Then say the rest of it, if there is a rest of it."

"There is not a rest of it," said Marek Kest. "That is the whole of it. **For about nine days of cart travel, that book is in nobody's charge, and a thing in nobody's charge can be opened by anybody in a lane.**"

---

The man with the barrow came back up the corridor with his hands in his pockets.

"Nobody is going to open it."

"That is true and it is not a reason."

"It is the only reason there is."

"**Nobody is going to open it, and the reason nobody is going to open it is that it has not happened yet.**" Marek Kest did not raise his voice and there was no reason to. "In about nine days this book is on a bench in a building I have never been in, and there is a man there who has been told to put it on a shelf, and nobody has told him what it is, because nobody above him has ever been told what it is either. And he will not open it. And then, in about four years, somebody will need to know whether it was opened, and there is no line in this empire that anybody can write that in, and I have looked, and the looking took me a court and four days and a word in a sentence that means more than it says."

The man with the barrow looked at the cart for a while.

"I have four men," he said, "and they are on it. And one of them has a wife and one of them has a boy and I do not know anything about the other two, and that is how a list of works works, and I would like you to understand that I am not a villain in this and I am not a hero in it either. I am a man who has been given a job on a day and a place to take it and I have been in this trade since I was about their age and I have never once been told what I was carrying. I did not ask either. I want that said. **I did not ask.**"

"You did not ask," said Marek Kest, "and that is the correct answer and I am not going to improve it."

---

The form was in the inside pocket on the left, folded twice, and the folds had gone soft, and it had been folded twice before that.

It is a return of an impression and of the keeping of it. It is four lines and it has been the same shape for a hundred and forty years and it has never once been printed.

> *The thing:*
> *The person in charge of it:*
> *The day it was taken:*
> *The signature of the person who took it:*

He wrote the first two lines in the corridor, standing up, with the cart in front of him and about nine minutes' light left.

The first line is the book, described the way a person describes a thing they have read: a bound schedule, in one volume, the schedule of a term of an office, one volume, four feet back from a door in a wall.

The second line is a person. It is a clerk at a window about four feet off the ground at the back of this building, by a key on a string and not by any appointment, nine years.

And the third line was a day and the fourth line was a signature, and the fourth line wanted a person who had taken it, and there was no way on this earth to get it.

---

**The person who takes a thing is a person who is then in charge of it.**

He said that to the man with the barrow at about the sixth hour, and the man said, "I know," and Marek Kest said, "You do not, but I am going to say it anyway because you are the man who has to write a name in the fourth line or not write it, and I would like the reason to have been said in front of you."

He said it properly.

Being in charge of a thing for nine days of cart travel in a district you do not live in is not a duty. **It is being made a party to it.** A party is a person a book can be true about, and it is served, and it can be made to answer for the thing, and there is no form in this empire that makes a person agree to be a party, and there is no form anywhere for taking a party back out of a box once one is in it.

"So the fourth line cannot be filled," he said, "by me, because I am a party already and I cannot be the person in charge of a thing in this matter as well as be served for it. It cannot be filled by the man who wrote the second line, because she is not in this corridor and I have not asked her to be and there is no notice in this empire that goes to a person in charge of a press. It cannot be filled by the man with the barrow, because a man with a barrow on a list of works is working and a person who is working is not a person in charge of a thing, and if he signs that line he has stopped being a man on a list of works and become a man in charge of one, and in about four years there will be a question about a book and there will be exactly one name in this empire that can be asked about it and he put it there himself with a pen in about nine seconds in a corridor."

The man with the barrow said, "Then it does not get filled."

"**Then it does not get filled.** And that is not a defeat, and I want you to hear why, because I have been standing in this corridor for a day and a half working it out and you are the first person who has been in the room while I did it." He put the pen away. "**A duty that stops is not a duty that survives stopping. And a person can stop. And the fourth of this month is a day at the end of a notice that a clerk of thirty-eight wrote on in chalk four days ago on her own account, and she wrote the same day in ink in a book of her own, and a day at the end of a notice means there is somebody who is supposed to be in this corridor today, and today there is not, and that is the whole of how this stops.**"

---

He tried three times to get her to say it, and he got it wrong three times, and the third one is the one worth writing down.

The first time was at the west end of the corridor, in front of the cart and the four men, and he said, "Are you going to be here on the fourth," and she was not in the corridor and the question went into the air.

The second time was at about the seventh hour at the end of the corridor with one man left in it, and he said, "You said the fourth on the wall, so you know the fourth is coming, and I am asking you as a person who has been in a room with a document in it whether you will stand at that door on the fourth."

And the man with the barrow, who had not gone, said, "She is not going to say that to me."

"Why not."

"Because she does not know you." He shouldered the barrow. "**I have been a man in a corridor for about two hours and I have not said my name and she has not asked me it, and that is the arrangement, and you walked in here with a coat with paper in it and you are the man who was in here last week, and she has not asked you anything either, and that is a different arrangement, and you want her to say a thing in a corridor in front of four porters that she has said to nobody.**"

---

So he walked back round the building to the window.

It is about four feet off the ground and about two feet wide and there is a board above it with about nine names on it in chalk, and she was at the sill with a rag in her hand, and she was not surprised to see him, and she had known about the fourth since the second week of this month, and she had known about the cart since the last week of the tenth month of next year.

"Two things," he said, "and I am going to say them in the wrong order on purpose so that you can stop me at the one you would rather not have."

"Go on."

"The book is not going anywhere. It is in the cart and the cart is going and there is nobody in charge of it, and I have said that in a corridor to four men and a man with a barrow and nobody has written it down. The reason it is not going anywhere is that a person can stop, and you stopped it, and you stopped it by not being in a room you were not asked into, and I want to say that out loud to you before I ask you the other thing, because the other thing is a favour and the first one is not."

She put the rag down.

"And the other thing is that I need a person in a room on a day with a witness in it, and a party is served and a witness is asked and a person cannot be both about the same thing, and I am a party, and so is a woman of twenty-four who is not in this district, and so the witness has to be somebody who has come about something else." He said it in about nine seconds. "**Will you stand at that door on the second day of the twelfth month of next year with a man of about fifty who patches the terrace wall in the corridor, and let the two of us read ten entries out loud in a room with the two of you in it.**"

She did not say yes for about four seconds.

"You have asked me for a day in about six weeks' time," she said. "I cannot tell you now whether I will be in this building on that day. **Not because I will not promise. Because a promise with a day on it is a thing anybody can be shown, and I have been in charge of four presses for nine years and I have never once been asked to promise anything, and if I promise a day now then in about four years there is a person with a piece of paper who can say I did not come, and I would rather be asked on the morning.**"

"That is a no."

"That is a yes with about six weeks on the front of it," said Ivet Sarn, "and you asked a person at a window a question in the ordinary way, and I am going to answer it in the ordinary way, and I am going to ask you for the one thing that would make it a yes, and if you have not got it then I will not be here on the day and you will not be able to say I was."

"What is it."

"**Tell me how long I have.** How long the room is. How long a person is going to be standing at that door with a man of about fifty in it. Not how long you need the book read out. How long *I* am stood there." She took the rag up again. "Nobody in this matter has told anybody how long they have. Not one person. Not once. I have been saying that in a corridor for a month and I am saying it to you now because you are the only one of the three of us who has ever asked me a question and waited."

He stood at a window about four feet off the ground and worked out that he did not know.

"Then I will tell you what I am able to tell you," he said, "and it is not enough and I am going to say so. About twenty minutes. A lamp, a bench, ten entries, read out. And the twenty minutes is out of my own day, and a day of my wage is a little over two shillings and fivepence, and I have been in this matter for five journeys and about thirty days of my own time and there is no fund and there is no form for a fund, and I am not going to work out a new total for a woman at a window because a total is a thing a man does once."

"Twenty minutes," said Ivet Sarn. "That is a length. I can think about a length. **I cannot think about a day on a wall with somebody else's name at the foot of it and I am not going to try.**"

She wrote the day on the wall. It was already on the wall. She had put it there four days ago in chalk and it was still there and nobody had washed it, and she looked at it and then she wrote it again on the same line in the same chalk, on top of itself, which is not a correction and is not a second entry and is the only thing in this matter anybody has ever done twice.

---

He walked down to the water and up the bank after that, and the second season was in its second month, and there were about nine men on the ditch on the south side because a scour is on the list and nobody has agreed to the list.

Nobody thanked anybody for the fourth of that month, and nobody had thanked anybody on the third, and the chalk on the wall is still on the wall, and a day at the end of a notice is a day at the end of a notice whether or not anybody was stood at the end of it, and the cart went out of the yard at about the seventh hour with a book in it and no person in charge of it and a list of works with a district at the foot of it and four men on it who were working.
