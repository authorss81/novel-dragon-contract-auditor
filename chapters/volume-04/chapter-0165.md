# Chapter 165: Three Pounds and Twelve Shillings

It took him six days and a bit over, which is what it takes, and there is nobody in this empire who can shorten it and three people in three districts have said in a room that they can.

Three people in three districts have offered Marek Kest a way to take nine hours off that road. He has heard all three offers and he has taken none of them, and the reason is not that the road is six days. The road is six days. The reason is that a cart is a day of somebody else's wage, and a day of a wage of seventeen shillings and a penny the week is a little over two shillings and fivepence, and he has about one day of his own in a week and has had it for as long as he has been doing this, and the things he can afford are benches, towpaths, locks, and the inside of a hired stone store, and all four of those are free.

He wrote the money down on the fourth day at a lock, on the back of a leaf in a book with no office on it, in his own hand, and then he said it out loud to a carrier of about fifty who was sitting on the same bench and had not asked.

---

"Twenty-one days is two pounds and eleven shillings and threepence," he said. "That is the three journeys. Twenty-four days is about three pounds, and that is the four, and the fourth is finished. Thirty days is three pounds and thirteen shillings and threepence and that is this one, and a year of that wage is about forty-four pounds."

The carrier did not look up. He was about fifty and he is not named and he stays unnamed, and he had a line of somebody else's writing in his inside coat and ninepence for it, and he said, "I have read the heading."

"Which heading."

"By whose authority. "He said it the way a man says the name of a town he has been through. "I have read the heading and that is as far as I have got, and I have been carrying a line of a register down a towpath since the third week of the fifth month of next year and I have read the heading and nothing else, and I would rather be a man who has read the heading than a man who has had a clerk explain it to me on a bench."

"That is the correct answer and I am not going to improve it."

"I know it is. Nobody improves it. "He got up. "Ninepence is what you owe me and it is the same as last time and I have said every time that if anybody ever wants me to carry a document I am not doing it, and you are not carrying a document, you are carrying a line, and a line is a thing I can read and a document is a thing I cannot and I have been wrong about the difference twice in nine years of carrying for other people and I have not been wrong since."

---

Here is the whole of the money, and it is not a complaint, and he has never once said it out loud in a room where anybody could do anything about it.

Three journeys: up in the first week of the fifth month of next year, down in the last week of it, up in the third week of the seventh. That is twenty-one days on a towpath and in a cart and on a bench, and the second of those three was not paid for and the other two were not either.

The fourth journey was up in the third week of the seventh and the descent in the first week of the sixth, and four journeys is twenty-four days, and twenty-four days is about three pounds.

**And this one is the fifth, and it is thirty days, and it is three pounds and thirteen shillings and threepence, out of about forty-four pounds a year, and there is no fund for it and there is no form for a fund and there never has been.**

Six days is a little over fourteen shillings, and a bench on a towpath is nothing, and a lock is nothing, and a night's floor in a lock hut is a thing a man of about fifty did not charge him for and would deny having done.

"A man of forty-six has a contract at one shilling and sixpence a day," he said. "A barrow of stone, a man and a boy, ninety days being a hundred and thirty-five shillings, which is six pounds fifteen shillings. Nine men in three gangs. He has a question and it is the right one and it has not been answered in writing."

"What question."

"The name of the man who instructs them. "The man of thirty-eight said it to a bench. "Nine men in three gangs have asked in writing for the name of the man who instructs them, and one of them has said out loud that asking may have cost him the contract, and the other eight have said nothing since, and that is the correct thing for eight men to do."

"So what."

"So the answer to that question is worth more than the whole of my five journeys, and I have not got it, and I am not going to get it on a road. "He closed the book. "**That is the whole of what I have learned walking about four hundred and thirty miles in each direction with no fund, and it is that the only thing I have ever been able to do with money is to be somewhere a person can be asked a question in, and there is one such place in this river district and I am not paid to be in it.**"

---

**And here is the thing he worked out on the fourth day at a lock, and it took seconds, and he did not say it out loud and has not said it out loud since.**

Two dates in one week. Four hundred and thirty miles apart. No form in this empire that puts two dates in one box, and a clerk of thirty-four said so out loud in a city at the end of the seventh month of next year and put it in a room with two people in it and did not put it in a file.

The collision cost him nothing.

He is not on the list of works. The list of works has never had him on it. The keeper entered him in the seat on the seventh of the first month of next year without asking him and wrote on the face of her own sheet that she had done it without asking, and he refused it in front of about forty people in nine words, and she was right to enter him and he was right to refuse it, and the sheet went on the wall and neither of them has to be there. The first review was a day a person comes to. He is not a person who comes to it.

So the week cost nothing, and the only person the week cost anything to was a girl of seventeen who is paid forty-five pence a day, four days a week, on a written engagement, to read things out loud to people who cannot read a paragraph, and who walked a day up the length of a bank to every one of those doors in two days, and was not paid for her feet, and has been looked at by about nine hundred people since the second of the fifth month and has minded being thanked about four times.

**There is no form anywhere that puts two dates in one box, and a person cannot be in two places, and the reason a person cannot be in two places on a road like this one is that the road takes six days and a bench is free and a day is a little over two shillings and fivepence, and a girl of seventeen does not have six days, and a girl of seventeen is not going to be asked whether she has six days.**

He wrote that down too, on the back of the same leaf, with the day against it, and he put it in the book, and the book has no office on it, and that is what a book with no office on it is for.

---

The two of them had asked a court and been sent a form back.

That is what happened on the sixth day, on a bench at a landing a hundred and forty yards below the ford, at about the fourth hour, and it took four minutes and it is the reason the fifth journey cost what it cost.

A district court and a high court nine days out of order with each other. Nobody has joined them. There is no instrument joining them and a magistrate has tried four times in nine years to write a form for asking another court what it has found and none of the four was ever sent, and three of them have *why not* written against them in her own hand. A direction of a court is a thing a court gives, and there are two of them, and they are nine days out of order, and the door to Auremar is still a door at the end of a corridor, and **a chapter may not join two courts except in a room with a witness in it, and there was no witness on a landing a hundred and forty yards below a ford.**

So they did not join them. They asked one, and got a form back with a reason in it, and he read the reason twice and it did not say what the thing was in, and he put it in the inside pocket of a coat where the other form is, and there are two forms in that pocket and one of them is older.

Being right is what makes a document. A document is findable. There is no way in this empire to un-ask, and about four years from now anybody with fourpence can read that a man of thirty-eight asked a court for something on the sixth day of the sixth week of a journey and was told no in a sentence that says nothing about the thing itself.

---

They came down onto the Slade on the second day of the first week of the ninth month of next year.

The bay is a hired stone store off the Slade, north bay, about thirty-one foot by nineteen, about thirty people, a bench against the north wall that came with the building, a bench in the middle, and a door in the west end that shuts, because a woman of twenty-eight packed its lintel with a wedge off a sleeper in about nine minutes in the fourth week of the sixth month of next year, on her own account, and entered one day in the bay's own ledger and did not read it out.

The door shut. He noticed it in the first second and it took him the rest of the afternoon to work out why he noticed it in the first second.

Nobody was in the bay. It was about the second hour and the second fortnight of a season that nobody had agreed to.

On the nail over the bench in the middle there was a slate.

It had a day on it at the top and a number under the day and no names anywhere, and it had been washed at some point since the review and washed badly, and half the chalk was gone and the number was still there.

The bay's own ledger was on the bench against the north wall where it is kept, and it is a hard-backed book the company has kept since before the war, and it has three columns, and the third one has not had a heading over it since the sixth year.

He opened it at the back and did not go far.

> *First week of the eighth. Price of a review, itemised. Four shillings a year, all rent. One reader, two days, forty-five pence a day; the walking is not that wage. Four not asked. Nine cannot read. Nine of about forty-one did not come, and nobody is paid to find out which. One cart, and it belongs to a man on the list. One bench, and it will not take a woman of sixty-four. — S. Ild.*

And under it, in a different hand, and she had not read that one out either, and it is four lines long and it says that the second season is running and that nobody agreed to it and that nobody has been asked, and then it says the sentence, and the sentence is that a duty is found to have stopped when a person stands in a room and does not do it, and the name at the foot of it is H. Wray, foreman.

Two women. Two pens. Two days about a week apart.

**Neither of them knew he was going to be late, and neither of them wrote his name anywhere, and there was no form that asked them to, and about four hundred and thirty miles of road is what it costs to be a week late to a bay that holds about thirty people and a bench that will not take a woman of sixty-four.**

---

Sefa Ild came in at about the half hour with a lamp and a bucket and did not startle, and she put the lamp on the bench in the middle and looked at the slate on the nail for a while.

"That has been washed," she said.

"Somebody washed it."

"I washed it. On the fourth day, and I did it badly on purpose, and I want that written down somewhere before anybody puts it down that it was done badly by accident. "She set the bucket down. "A record that a person can destroy has to be destroyed by somebody, on purpose, on a day, or it is not a record, it is a piece of chalk. **I washed a slate and I am the person who washed it and the number is still on it because the chalk on this slate is the bad kind and I would like somebody to know that the bad kind is why.**"

"You should have written a reason on it."

"There is nowhere to write a reason on it. That is the whole of what a slate is. "She wiped her hands on her skirt. "And I have been in a room with a woman of twenty-eight's whole life in it for four months and I have learned one thing in it and it is this: **every record on this bank is a person, and a person is not a record, and a person can be told to stop, and a slate can be washed, and a book in a hired store can lose its building, and a satchel can go into a house and stay there.** Those are four objects on a bench and there is no fifth one in this empire that any of us can hold."

"And the third column."

"The third column is in the only book on this bank that will still be here when the Company is not. "She did not look round the bay. "That is not sentiment. That is a hard-backed book the company has kept since before the war and it costs four shillings a year to keep the roof on and not one penny to keep the book, and in nine years the roof will go and the book will be in a house somewhere with a person who does not know what it is, and that is a better fate than a slate and a worse one than nothing, and I have thought about it for two days and that is the whole of my thinking."

"You are not thanked for any of it."

"I said in the fourth week of the sixth month of next year that I am not going to be thanked and I have not been, and I would like it noticed that the reason I am not thanked now is a different reason from the one I gave then, and I do not entirely know what it is, and I have decided not to find out. "She picked the bucket up. "The door shut on your way in."

"It did."

"**It shut because I packed the lintel with a wedge off the top sleeper in nine minutes on my own account, and it shuts now, and you are the first person to walk in and have it shut on you since it was packed, and I have not said anything about that and I am not going to, and I would like the record to show that I noticed it and said nothing.**"
