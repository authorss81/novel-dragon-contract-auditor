# Chapter 189: Protection Is Not Secrecy, and It Is the Same Hand

On the fourth day of the fourth week of the fourth month of the year after there was a conversation in a room four bays from a counter, and it had a day on it, and nothing else in the world did, and neither of the two people in it was going to be told anything afterwards.

Tamsin Rook is twenty-four. The room is hers. There is a table in it, and a press with a rail across the front of it, and about six hundred printed slips in that press that say the fourth line is not to be completed by that office, and a hard-backed book with no office on it that is on the table, and a lamp, and about nine feet of wall she does not use.

Marek Kest came in at about the seventh hour and had been up two nights and had a sheet of paper in his hand that was the third fair copy of a thing that was already on a board in a passage, and she looked at it and did not ask him about it, because he had brought it to show her and not to be asked about.

---

"You are going to say the price of being a party out loud," he said.

"I am going to say it out loud and then I am going to say the number, because a number is findable and a person who says a number can be held to it." She said it standing up, in the flat way she says things. "**I am a party to an instrument about a dead woman's name. From the day I was served I can be served anything about it for the rest of my life, and there is no form anywhere for taking a party back out of a box, and that is the seventh time I have said it out loud.** Four of the seven were about me. One was about a person who is not in this room and cannot be told and cannot be warned, and that was the week the notice came, and the sentence works in both directions and the second half is the half nobody prices. And the seventh is this, and the seventh is not about either of us, and I have thought about why that is and I have not worked it out and I am not going to guess at it in this room."

---

"And the other thing you are going to say."

"I am going to say the thing about the paper and I am going to say it now, and I have never said it to you, and I have said it to myself about nine hundred times, and saying it to yourself is not the same and I have known that for about four months and I did nothing about it until today." She put the book down flat. "I am in these four bays because a paper says so. **The paper is not the reason.** I have been a roll-keeper for about four months and I have a counter and a press and about six hundred printed slips in it that say the fourth line is not to be completed by this office, and there is not a single one of those six hundred slips that I put there. I have come in on a day of the week and I have gone out on a day of the week and I have done more things in a week than I can name, and every one of them was decided for me by somebody else's instructions, and I have been good at it, and I have been the door in this building for about four months, and it is not the reason."

"Why are you telling me this on this day."

"Because you handed a dead woman's nine lines to a printer this month and told the whole of a city that there was a part, and you are going to be asked by about nine people in the next two years why you did not tell your sister, and you have four words in a margin, and the four words are a good answer and they are not an answer to the question that will actually be asked." She did not raise her voice. "**If I ever say a true thing out loud about why I am in a room, then that is who I am, and there is no form in this empire for a person to be who they are.** I have been hiding behind a paper for four months because the paper is a machine and a machine cannot be asked a question about itself. That is the whole of it. That is the thing I have not said."

---

"Say the other thing then," he said, "and I have been waiting four months for it and I would like it said out loud in a room."

"I am not going to comfort you."

"Fine."

"It is not coldness and I want that entered in whatever you keep." She sat down. "**A person who is being comforted has stopped being in the room.** That is the whole of the reason and it is a thing I learned the hard way about four months ago and I have not been unkind to anybody since. You have spent nine days deciding to hand a dead woman's method to a printer, and four weeks finding out you had been eligible for an office since you were a child, and about four months since finding out that your mother made you unservable so that she could not tell you, and you have not eaten properly since the second week of the second month and you have a third fair copy of a thing that is already in type in your hand as though the fair copy were the part that mattered.

"**And I am not going to be the person who takes you out of this room tonight.** If I do that then in about four years there will be one version of the last four months and it will be the one where a clerk of twenty-four was kind to a man of thirty-eight, and that version has no sheet in it and no board and no woman of sixty-three in it and no third column."

---

He put the fair copy down on the table.

"Protection is not secrecy," he said. "I have not been able to say it out loud for about nine days."

"Then say it out loud, and say it in the middle, and I will tell you when the middle has been and not before, because a person who is talking about a third of a thing is doing a thing I do not like."

"Say it in the middle."

And she said it in the middle, and she counted the sentences under the table where he could not see her, and she has never admitted to anybody that she does that.

"One of them is a thing you do to a person and the other is a thing you do about them. There is no form in this empire for the first one and everybody does it anyway. The reason everybody does it is that it works, and it worked. **Your mother did it to you, and it worked, and you are not alive anywhere anybody can serve.** You did it to a woman of sixty-three in the second month of the year after and it worked, and she is not on a board in a passage and her name is not in any book anybody can buy.

"And here is the part that nobody has said to you and I am going to say it now. **She was in the book the whole time, and she is in about nine hundred of them, and there is no heading over the third column of any of them, and what is in that column is her hand and not her name.** So keeping her off a board did not take her out of a book and I want you to hear that before I say the other half. **A hand is findable, and it is the most findable thing there is. Anybody with a copy and about four years can put about four hundred hands side by side and find the one that is hers, and nobody is served anything and no name is said out loud and there is no form for it and no fee and no day at the end of it.** And a register of witnesses is not a copy that anybody has, because the register does not travel and the leaf does not travel and the three things a leaf needs are all three required, and none of the three has been had, and the reason none of them has been had is a party and a form and a corridor and about nine minutes on a bench, and that is a different month and a different road and not a thing that can be said in one line in a room. and so the finding is not free and it is not available to anybody who wants it and it is not refused either. It is simply out of reach, which is the same thing as the whole of this matter and is not the same as a refusal, and there is no form anywhere that turns out of reach into a refusal.

So your act of protection did not move her out of a book. **It wrote down the only sentence in the world that says where the books are, and put it on a board at fourpence a copy, and told every buyer that there are five columns.** And you did not print the third one, and not printing the third one is the kindness, and telling the world there is a third one is the part that is not. And that is not a thing I have caught you doing. It is a thing the doing is, and it has been the same for about four months and it is the same in your mother and it is the same in the register, and I am not going to say that it was wrong.

"I am going to say that it was the same act, and I am going to say it about your mother, and about your sister, and about you, and the same hand is not a figure of speech. **The same hand is the point: one hand in one family writing the same sentence in one register and one margin and one form, and the sentence is always that a person is better off not being asked.** And I do not believe any of you three decided to do it and I do not believe the woman of sixty-three decided to do it either, and I have watched about four hundred people in this city decide to do things and almost none of them decided."

---

"I am not going to say she was right," he said.

"No. And nobody is going to tell you she was kind, and if somebody does tell you that in the next year, you will want to know what they get for it."

"Nobody is going to forgive anybody."

"**There is nothing in this room to forgive.** I have said the word out loud twice this month in two rooms and I have not got a softer one for it and I am not going to look for one. I have not done anything to you that needs forgiving. I have been a party, and I have been useful, and I have said the price of both out loud every time, and that is the whole of what I have done and I would do it again on the same day."

---

The room was quiet for a while, and the press with the six hundred printed slips in it was against the wall behind her and was not making a sound, and outside there was a bay with people in it about four hundred yards away who were not part of any of this.

---

And then the girl of about twenty-five at the intake desk put her head in at about the eighth hour and she did not knock, because the door is four bays from a counter and everybody in this building has been in and out of this room for about four months without knocking.

"I have got a question about the board in the passage."

"You have," said Tamsin Rook, "and you have had it since the second week and you have brought it to me because I am the door and you did not think about the second half of that. Say it."

"They have sold about seventy of them." The girl had the counter's day-book against her chest and she was not asking to be helped, she was asking to be answered, and the difference between those two is the whole of her trade. "Nine days, seventy copies, fourpence each, and the printer's man says he does not keep them and he is right not to. And the man who wrote it is standing in this room, and I have been three times this week to that counter with about fourpence of my own and I have not been able to ask him, because you are in here."

"I am in here," said Tamsin Rook.

"I can go and ask him."

"You can go and ask him and he will tell you nothing, and I am telling you that now so that you do not stand in a passage being lied to, and I am telling you why, and I am telling you because you are right that the question is reasonable and I am not going to pretend that it is not."

She said it the way she says facts.

"**Four facts. One: there is no form anywhere that says a counter takes the name of a person for a copy, and that is not a rule anybody made, it is a trade, and it cannot be asked for without inventing it. Two: he has not asked you to buy one and he cannot ask you and he would not want to. Three: a printer's man at fourpence a copy is the only person in that passage who is nobody's clerk, and if he kept a list it would be a list of strangers, and he will not keep it. Four: if you want to know who bought a fourpence copy of a printed sheet, there is nothing in this empire that will tell you, and the reason there is nothing is that about four hundred people a year pay fourpence for a fact, and a great many of them would not care to be one.**"

"So that is four facts and none of them helps you, and I am not going to help you, and I am not going to be unkind about it either."

The girl of about twenty-five looked at her for about a second.

"That is what I thought," she said, "and I wanted it said by somebody who knew."

"**Then say it to the next person who asks you, and do not say it to him, and do not ask me again, because I have just given you the whole of the answer and the second time I will have nothing to add and you will know that I have nothing to add and it will be worse.**"

She went.

And Marek Kest had been standing at the table through the whole of it, four feet away, with a third fair copy of his own sheet in his hand, and he had not said one word.

"You were in the room," said Tamsin Rook.

"I was in the room."

"She came to me about your act and I would not help her with it, and she came to me because I am the door, and you did not say a word, and I want to know what that was, because I have watched you for about four months and you have never once in that time been in a room and failed to say a word."

"I did not say a word because she is a party to this room and not to anything of mine."

"That is the correct reason," said Tamsin Rook, "**and it is the reason I am going to say the next thing at all.**"

---

"There is one thing in this room you have not asked me about," he said.

"There is. And you are not going to ask me, and if you do then I will not answer, and it will not be a refusal to be served."

"That is a different instrument."

"**It is a refusal to be asked, and I have never had to use that one and I would rather not learn tonight what it costs.** There are four in it and one of them has been there since the first month of the year before last and it has not been sent and it has not been opened, and this is the seventh time in two years I have not thought about it, and I am not going to start now, and that is the whole of it and I have said more tonight about a thing I have not touched in about two years than I have said about anything else in about four months."

---

Then she said the last thing, and she said it standing up with her hand on the back of her own chair, and she said it as a fact and not as advice, and she has never in her life been able to say it any other way and she is not going to learn a new way tonight.

"**I am not going to decide anything in a room on a day when one of us has been up two nights.**" She said it and then she stopped and started again, because the first time was not the sentence and she wanted the sentence. "I am not going to be told things. I have not asked anybody for anything since the day I was refused in a room and nobody has asked me for anything either, and those are two facts about the same week and only one of them is in a book, and I have made my peace with which one.

"And there is a form in this empire that makes a person a party, and there is no form in this empire that makes a person safe, and I have been the first of those since the last week of the seventh month of next year and I have said the price of it out loud seven times and nobody has thanked me once and I did not expect to be thanked once and I would like it written down that I did not, because in about four years somebody is going to read that I said the price seven times and they are going to assume somebody heard it."

Nobody had heard it.

And at about the ninth hour he put the third fair copy inside his coat, where four sheets of copyist's paper had been since the second week of the twelfth month of next year, and he went out of the door and down four bays and along a corridor, and she shut the room and put the lamp out and left the one thing in it alone, and the day was on both of them and neither of them was going to be told anything.
