# Chapter 188: Forty-Five Pence a Day, and a Hold Against a Thing

The form came up the road to the bank on the second day of the second week of the fourth month of the year after with a heading over it and nothing under the heading, and the whole of what it took him to see it was about four seconds, and the whole of what it took him to say about it was about nine minutes.

Rennick Adley is thirty-eight. He is a book-keeper. He has been keeping the books of the Slade Cut Company in a hired stone store off the Slade since the second month of next year, and the Company's whole money is four shillings a year and all of it is the rent of the building.

In the second week of the eighth month of next year he refused, in about nine seconds, to rule a fourth column in that ledger, and the reason he gave was that a column ruled in a man's own hand is a heading over a page in about four years put there by a man who did not stand in the room. A clerk of thirty-four four hundred and thirty miles up this river has been carrying that reason for about a month and has never met him.

This is what his reason was for.

---

The box on the form says the person, institution or thing engaged in the matter.

There is nothing under it.

Nold Prawle's is on a flood terrace a long way from here and says *the district works*. The mason who put a wall in that one wrote that a heading is a machine that runs on whatever is nearest, and a mason put a wall in it because there was a wall to hand and he had a boy of nine.

**Rennick Adley put the name of a building in it, in his own hand, in about four seconds, because there was nothing else to hand.**

> *The Slade Cut Company, of the hired stone store off the Slade, in the matter of a recorded claim entered and marked disputed, and in the matter of the works engaged in it. Engaged from the first day of the fifth month of next year and continuing. — R. Adley, book-keeper, and no other person has signed this line and there is no other person in this empire who could.*

---

"What have you done," said Doreth Ferrow, who was sixty-four and had kept that bay since the second month of next year, and who had told a foreman of fifty-one in front of half a bay that she did not want to be one, and had meant it.

"I have put our name in it," said Rennick Adley.

"That is the worst thing anybody in this store has done since the spring bank."

"It is." He did not look up. "**And it is the only thing I can do, and it is what a mason does about a form with a heading on it, and I found that out four hundred and thirty miles from here from a woman of thirty-four who has never met me and who has been carrying my reason for a month.** I have got a choice of two things. I leave the box empty, and then a clerk in an office fills it in, and she will fill in a thing, and it will not be a good thing and it will not be this thing. Or I fill it in myself, and then it is this, and the roof is in a matter and I know exactly whose roof it is because I have paid the rent out of the whole of it."

"It is four shillings a year."

"It is four shillings a year and it is the roof over about forty books with no office on them and the bench the people stand at," said Rennick Adley, "and I have put it in a box in about four seconds and I would do it again tomorrow and I would rather it was me than her."

---

Then he stood in the bay and read it out, and that is the second thing that happened that week and it is the thing that has a name.

There were about thirty people in the bay when he started and about four of them cannot read a paragraph, and one of the four is a woman of sixty-four who has been in that bay since the second month of next year and who had a thing read to her three times about a word she did not want in a book, and she is not thanked and has never asked to be.

**He read the names first.**

Not the form. The names, out of the third column, in the order they are in, in the order they came on, one after the other, and he read every one of them and he did not hurry the ones he knew were slow and he did not skip the ones he had wanted to skip for about four months.

The third column is the one with no heading over it. It has had no heading over it since the sixth year and every one of the four hundred and something people in that book has gone into it, and about nine hundred and forty names are in that book and about four hundred of them are dead.

**It is the only column on this bank he is allowed to read a name out of, and the reason he is allowed to is that nobody has ever asked him for it, and a column with no heading over it is not a heading, and a person in a column that is not a heading has not been entered under anything by anybody.**

---

Then he read the form, in the ordinary way, once, and it is a sheet of foolscap with a heading and four boxes and his own name at the foot of one line of it.

> *Held against the Slade Cut Company, being a thing, in the matter of a recorded claim entered and marked disputed, and in the matter of the works engaged in it.*
>
> *The wages of the persons engaged are held at the same rate and for the same period as the sum held, and a person whose wages are so held is not to be paid that held sum by any person, on account of the person of that family.*

And then he read it a second time, slower.

Nobody in that bay has ever asked him where he got that from, and he has been asked twice about the double reading by a woman of thirty-four four hundred and thirty miles up this river and has not told her, and he is not going to be asked, and the first reading is for the words and the second reading is for the people who did not have them the first time and he has never said that out loud to anybody either and it is written now and it is not going to be in his mouth.

---

There was a letter on the bench in the store, and it had been on the bench since the third week of the third month, and Rennick Adley had not answered it and had not thrown it away, and Doreth Ferrow had asked him twice about it.

"Read it out," she said. "You have read everything else in this bay out loud."

"It is from a factor and it is four lines and it is not for the bay."

"Read it out."

He read it out, because she asked and because he reads things out.

> *Sir, I am directed to require from you the name, place of custom and description of a person in the above works who may be served, together with the consent of that person to be served, and I am to say that without a name and a consent this office cannot proceed and will not proceed, and the matter will stand against the works until one is given. — Your obedient, factor of the second district.*

"Nineteen days," said Doreth Ferrow.

"Nineteen days."

"You have had it nineteen days and you have not sent it and you have not sent nothing."

"**I have sent nothing and that is the answer and I have been sitting on it for nineteen days because every day I do not send it is a day nobody has been entered.**"

"And when you do send it."

"Then there is a name in a box in an office about nine miles away with a heading over it, and the name belongs to a person who works about four hundred yards from this store, and the person will be served, and served is party, and party is a person a book can be true about for the rest of that person's life, and there is no form in this empire for taking a party back out of a box. I refused to rule a fourth column in this ledger in the second week of the eighth month for exactly this reason and I have not changed my mind in about eight months and I am not going to change it in a letter to a man I have never met."

The bay did the arithmetic on that in about four seconds, which is what bays do, and about nine of them understood it and the rest of them did not and neither of those facts is anybody's business.

"Then we are all of us sitting on it," said Doreth Ferrow, "waiting for a letter neither of us is going to send."

"Yes," said Rennick Adley. "**And I would like it minuted that I said the name of it out loud, and that about four people in this room cannot read the minute when it is written, and that I have read it out loud instead, and that the reason I have read it out loud is not kindness. It is that a fact said in a room with about thirty people in it is the only kind of fact in this empire that a person who cannot read a paragraph is also a party to.**"

---

The hold is against the Company and not against the people, and that is the finding and it took him four seconds and it took nobody else in that bay any length of time at all, because it is the sort of thing a bay is good at.

**A hold on a rotation of about forty-one people holds every one of them and it has to name nobody. It has to name a thing, and the thing is a company, and a company is a roof, and the roof is four shillings a year.**

And this is the part that ought to be frightening and is not, and he said it out loud at about the fifth hour with about thirty people in the bay and it is the sentence he is going to have to live with.

"**The second season was never agreed to by anybody. It was never asked of anybody. It began in the first month of the year after and it is about the length a season is, and forty-one people are on it and nobody signed anything and nobody refused anything, and that is exactly why this is the cheapest hold in this empire.** There is nothing to break. There is nobody to complain, because complaining needs a thing you were refused. There is no form anywhere that says a person may ask to be paid, and I have looked, and I have been the book-keeper here for about four months and I know where the forms are kept and they are not there.

"**If they had refused it, we would have had nine reasons in a book. Because they did not refuse it, we have nothing, and the nothing is what is being held.**"

---

About four people in that bay cannot read a paragraph. They have never been asked once about a hold or a season or a wage, and this week they were not asked a second time, and there is no column for them and there is not going to be one, and the reason there is not going to be one is that a column for them would be a column somebody would have to fill.

A girl of seventeen was in the room, at the back, on a written engagement, at forty-five pence a day and four days a week, doing the thing she is paid for, which is reading out loud to people who cannot read.

She was not spoken to about the hold. She did not ask about it. **A person who is paid to read is a person who can be told to stop**, and Rennick Adley has said that sentence out loud in this bay twice and has not sent anybody to anybody and is not going to, and the girl was not named, and she was not thanked, and she is not going to start, and this is the last season in which anybody on this bank can ask her something and be answered for nothing.

The chair of sixty-four in the bay said one thing about it and she said it once, and she said it to Rennick Adley and not to the bay.

"**A carriage is not a wage.** A thing that pays for a person to come and does not pay them for the day is not an offer, it is a way of counting how many rooms a person is in. I said that in this bay in the sixth month of last year to about thirty people and it was the truest thing anybody said that month and it has not been useful to anybody since, because being right about a thing is not the same as a thing being done about."

"She has another one," said Rennick Adley. "She has one she has not given me whole and I have wanted it for about four months."

"Ask her."

"**I am not going to ask her.** The half I have is about a foreman throwing a paper with a heading on it away twice, and the half I do not have is on a slate in a counting room in a town about four hundred and thirty miles up this river, and a girl of twenty carried it up there on a cart and read it out in a lane, and I have never been in that lane and nobody on this bank has ever heard that half, and I am not going to be the man who says to a woman of sixty-four in a bay, please, what is the other half, because the day I ask her that is the day she stops saying the half she has got."

Nobody said anything. About nine men in three gangs had come in from the second gang by then and were standing at the back and had not understood one word of the last part of it and had not asked, and that is correct, because a bay is a room and about four people in it cannot read a paragraph and the rest of them had the sense.

---

He wrote it in the bay's own ledger that evening, in the third column, with the day on it, in his own hand, and read it back twice, once for the people who could see the words and once for the people who could not, and the second reading is the one he did not skip.

He wrote the names, and the day, and one line under the names, and the line is this.

> *The form came with a heading and nothing under it. I put the name of this Company under it, in my own hand, once, and the day. That is the whole of what I could do about it. It is the worst thing anybody in this store has done since the spring bank and I would rather have done it than had a clerk in an office do it, and those are the only two things anybody had, and one of them was me and the other one was not a person.*

Nothing was thanked. Nobody thanked him, and he had said in advance, in the bay, out loud, that nobody was to, and nobody did.

The hold is still in force. It is against a roof, at four shillings a year, and the wages of about forty-one people are stopped against it, and about nine men in three gangs are on a list of works with a day at the end of it, and a factor's written request for a name went out in the second week of the third month and has not been answered and is not going to be, and about nineteen people who said no with a reason in the first season are still behind that bank and are not going to be asked again by anybody in this stretch of it.

And about four hundred and thirty miles up this river there is a board in a passage with nine lines in a dead woman's hand on it at fourpence a copy, and a heading that says what it is and where it came from, and about seventy people had bought one by the end of that week, and there is no form for asking who bought them, and nobody asked.
