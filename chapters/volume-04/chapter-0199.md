# Chapter 199: Two Boxes Filled, And A Third Thing With No Box

Nothing had happened for three weeks, and that is the whole reason there is a scene.

The finding was entered in the fourth week of the sixth month and it is on a page and it is findable and nobody has thanked anybody. The day a man of thirty-eight lost is three weeks old and it is not coming back and nobody has come to see whether it is still gone. The paper with a seat's device on it went to a shed on a bank four hundred and thirty miles down this river in the first week of this month and a foreman of fifty-one read it and put it down on a bench and shut a door, and there has been no letter about it, because there is no form anywhere that requires one.

Tamsin Rook is twenty-four. She is in four bays because a paper says so, and she said out loud in the fourth week of the fourth month of this year that the paper is not the reason, and she is a party to an instrument about a dead woman's name, and there are four of a certain kind of thing in her own rooms, one of them there since the first month of the year before last, and she has not thought about them in about two years and did not think about them tonight.

She said in advance, and she said it in a room, and she gave the reason: **I am not going to decide anything in a room on a day when one of us has been up two nights.** It is the third week of the seventh month. Neither of them has been up two nights. It is the day, and the sentence is the reason there is a scene here and not an apology.

---

He came at about the seventh hour with about four feet of a wall he does not use in his rented room behind him, in the sense that he had not been sleeping much, and she made him sit down, and he sat down, and neither of them said anything for about a minute and a half.

Then she put a book on the table.

It is a hard-backed book with no office on it and it is hers and it has her own hand in it in about nine places, and she opened it at a page near the back and turned it round so it was facing him, and there were two boxes ruled on the page in her own hand with a straight edge and a pen, and neither of them had ever been filled in.

"That is not a form," she said. "**It is not printed and it has never been sent anywhere and it is not a copy of anything, and I ruled it about eleven weeks ago because I could not get the sentence I wanted out of my head onto a page.** I have not shown it to you and I have not thought about it in about nine days."

"Show me now."

"I am showing you now." She turned the page back. "**Both of them are you. Not you the person. You the two instruments.**"

And then she said the two instruments, and she said them apart, and she said them in the order she has been keeping them apart in for about a year, and the reason she keeps them apart is that a form gets merged by being inconvenient and she has watched about four hundred people in this city not do that on purpose.

"The first one is a paper. A court order, made in a form, in four days, with a day on it, requiring me in any room you are in. It is a thing that can be produced by anybody holding it, and it has been produced four times, and every one of the four times it produced me in a room with a rail in it in front of about thirty people, and the last time was in the fourth week of the sixth month and I was in it and I could not be examined and I was not examinable and I was in it anyway."

"The second one is a watch. It is a separate instrument and it does not name you and it does not name me. It requires that a person who is not from the office that cannot be named be in any room you are in. It does not say who. **There are about four hundred people in this city who could satisfy it, and it has been satisfied four times, and three of those four times it was me**, and the fourth time it was a girl of about twenty-five at an intake desk who opened a wrapper because she was going to open it anyway."

"So it is not about me."

"It is not about you and it is not about me and it is about a room," said Tamsin Rook, "**and that is the part that has taken me about eleven weeks to write down, and here it is, and I have not improved it: a person who is safe because nobody can instruct her and useful because somebody needs her standing in a room is a person with two boxes filled in on the same form.**"

She put the pen down beside the book.

"That is not a complaint. It is not a thank you. It is a description of a person and I have been that person since the second month and I have not got anybody to be it with, because everybody I know is somebody's clerk. **It has taken me eleven weeks to work out that the sentence is about me and not about the arrangement, and about nine days to work out that I do not mind.** I am saying both of those out loud because my third line forbids advice and my fourth line says a fact is not advice, and I have said the words of both of those lines in this room tonight and I have never said them out loud in a room before."

---

He looked at the page for a while.

"There is a third thing," he said.

"There is."

"It has no box."

"**It has no box and there is no form anywhere in this empire that makes one, and I have looked.**"

"Then say what it is and I will write it down."

"No," said Tamsin Rook.

"That is the first time anybody has refused me anything."

He tried four of them, out loud, standing up, in the way a man who has spent this spring learning to say a hard thing in a room tries a sentence and finds out whether it is true.

The first was that he was not going to be able to do this without her, and she said no in four words. The second was that he would be careful, and she said that *careful* is a word for a person who is going to do a thing and it is not a thing and it goes in a book. The third was that he had thought about it for about eleven weeks and could not make it into a sentence that was about the two of them, and she said that was the first of the four that was true and that it was not enough.

The fourth one he did not finish.

"You have stopped," she said.

"I have stopped because I have got to the end of what I prepared and I have not prepared any more of it, and I am a man who prepares things, and there is nobody in this empire I would rather have stopped at the end of a sentence than in."

"Then that is the sentence," said Tamsin Rook. "**You have got to the end of what you prepared. That is what the third thing is, and it has no box because there is no form in this empire that will hold a sentence a man has not finished, and I am not going to help you finish it and I am not going to ask you to.**"

"That is not a thing anybody can be a party to."

"No," she said, "and that is the first good thing anybody has said in this matter since the first week of the fifth month, and I have written it on nothing."

"It is not a refusal to be served and it is not a refusal to be asked and it is not a refusal of any kind you could put in a book, and I am going to tell you exactly what the third thing is so that you do not go looking for the instrument. **It is that a thing this empire can put in a book is a thing this empire can ask about, and there is one thing in this room that is not going in a book.** It is not going in a book because neither of us is going to write it down, and we are not going to write it down because if it is written down then in about four years a clerk can hold it up and read it out and ask us both the same question, and a person remembering a thing and a person reading a thing back are two different acts and only one of them is evidence."

Nobody said anything.

"The rule of the twelfth month of last year is there," she said. "On the table, in both our heads, since that month. Either of us may put a choice in writing with a day on it and the other must answer in a room, in words, with a date. It has been used six times. It could be used tonight in about nine seconds and it would be the correct instrument and it would be answered in a room in words with a date, and it would be a document, and there would be a day on it, and a name at the foot of it, and about four years."

"Are you not going to use it."

"**We are not going to use it and a correct non-use is as good as a use and it is entered as one, and I have said that out loud in a room, and if you want it in a book you can have it in a book.**"

---

Then she filled in the two boxes, in her own hand, with the day on it, and she read them back twice and the second reading was slower, and he did not read it and did not ask to and did not touch the page.

The first box said: *Person required to be present by a court order made in a form in four days, with a day on it. — T. Rook.*

The second box said: *Person present, not being of the office that cannot be named. — T. Rook.*

"Two names that are the same name," he said.

"Two boxes and one name in each, and they are the same name, and that is the honest fact. **It is why I ruled them by hand instead of writing one out and drawing a line under it**: a line under one name would have made it look like a single thing, and it is two things that happen to land on the same person four times since the second month."

---

Then she said the other two sentences, because they are standing there and the page would be dishonest if she did not.

"The undertaking of the fourth of the first month is live and has not been retired and has not been amended and has not been exercised tonight, and I am not going to exercise it tonight, and it stands."

"I know. I have not put it in front of you once since the fourth month and that is not an oversight and I have thought about it every week and I have not put it there, and you know why I have not put it there."

"I do know why."

"**Say it.**

"Because there is nothing in it that I have got a reason against," said Marek Kest, "and if I put it in front of you and you gave me a reason, then I would have to act on the reason, and the reason would be in a room, and I have watched about four hundred people in this city be careful in about four hundred rooms and I am not going to be the four hundred and first, and that is the whole of why I have not put it there and it is a bad reason and I know it is a bad reason."

"It is a bad reason," said Tamsin Rook, "and it is the reason I have not asked you for it, and we are both hiding behind the same bad reason and neither of us is going to say so out loud, and that is the third thing and it has no box."

---

And the room was quiet for a while, and the press with about six hundred printed slips in it was against the wall and was not making a sound.

"You are not going to say the price of it," he said.

"I have said it ten times in a room and I am not going to say it an eleventh time in this one, **and I am not saying it because it would put a number in a room and the number is the only part of me that anybody can be held to and I have had enough of being held to.**"

"You have never once asked me for anything."

"I have not."

"And you are not going to."

"I am going to ask you for one thing," said Tamsin Rook, "**and it is not advice and it is not a fact and it is not on any form, and I am asking you for it out loud in a room because I have noticed that the things we say in rooms are the only ones that work and the things we write down are the only ones that can be taken off us.**

"I am asking you not to thank me."

He did not say anything for about nine seconds.

"I was not going to," he said.

"I know. **I want it written down that I asked and that you said you were not going to, and that there is no form anywhere that requires it**, and there are four of them in my rooms and one has been there since the first month of the year before last and it has not been sent and it has not been opened, and I have not thought about it tonight and I am not going to start."

---

Then the lamp, and the door, and the two of them standing, and the room not being shut.

In the fourth week of the fourth month of this year he left at about the ninth hour and she shut the room, and that is written down, and it is not going to be unsaid, and he has not asked her about it and she has not offered.

On the third day of the third week of the seventh month of the year after there is a lamp that is still lit at about the ninth hour and a door that is open and nobody shuts it, and the difference between those two facts is the whole of what the last three weeks were for, and **there is no form anywhere in this empire that requires a door to be shut, and there is no column anywhere in this empire for the two boxes being filled, and the whole of what either of them has done tonight is the first thing either of them has done that no form in this empire requires.**

It is not entered.

**It is not entered.** About four hundred and thirty miles of road is what it would cost anybody who wanted to come and see it, and there is no fund, and nobody is going to be told, and the reason nobody is going to be told is not privacy. It is that a fact said in a room with two people in it is the only kind of fact in this empire that is not evidence of anything, and they would rather have that.

---

And the thing that is left standing at the end of it, in her mouth, in a room, is not a comfort and it is not a reward and it is not about the hearing and it is not about the mark on his wrist and it is not about the day he lost:

"**The third thing has no box.** That is what the whole of it since the second month has been for. I have been the woman with two boxes filled in on one form, and I have been the most dangerous person in this matter, and I have been the person nobody in it can protect, and those three things have been true at once since the second month of this year, and I did not know it and he did not know it, and it has now been said out loud in a room, and it is not going in either of our mouths again."

Nobody was thanked. Nothing was forgiven. There is nothing in the room to forgive and she has said that in a room before and said it again tonight in different words, and he did not say his mother was right, and nobody told him she was kind, and the day is a day and the door is open and nothing has come through it yet.
