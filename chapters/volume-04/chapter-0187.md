# Chapter 187: A Shilling a Day, and No Person Under the Heading

A form came down to a flood terrace in the third week into the fourth week of the third month of the year after with a heading over it and nothing under the heading, and the thing under the heading was not a person, it was a works, and every man on the list of works was inside it.

Nold Prawle is fifty. He is a mason. He has been patching the terrace wall for about eleven years and he is the only one holding the trowel on this stretch of it, and there is no form in this empire that makes a person be in charge of a thing, and he has never once thought about that and it is the same as a clerk's key on a string.

He has a boy of nine at home, and there is nobody in this matter who knows the boy exists, and he has said out loud, twice, in two rooms, that he is not going to be one of those men who makes his boy a person somebody comes about.

---

The man from the office in the second district read the form out in the yard, standing up, to about nine men, and he read it well. That is the first thing to say about him and it is the thing that made it work.

"**Held against the district works, being a household of the district, in the matter of a recorded claim entered and marked disputed, and in the matter of the works engaged in it.**"

"Which bit of that is us," said Nold Prawle.

"The last bit."

"And who filled that in."

"A clerk," said the man from the office. "She filled in *the district works* because that is what the box says to fill in. **The box says the person, institution or thing engaged in the matter, and a person fills a box the way the box is printed.** I will say that much for it and no more than that, because I have read it the way I read everything and I am not a clerk and I do not have anything to do with the filling in."

---

The yard did the rest of it, because that is what a yard is, and it took about eleven minutes and nobody had to be told to start.

Nold Prawle went and got the list of works off the nail in the tool shed, which is where it lives, and it is a sheet of foolscap with jobs against days and a name at the foot of it, and there is a day at the end of it in chalk, and the same day in ink in a clerk's own book with no office on it.

He brought it out and put it on the bench by the mortar, which is what you do with a sheet you want read in a yard, and about nine men came over it.

"That is the list," he said. "That is the works. And the rate on a wall on that list is a shilling a day."

"Then the form has got the works in it," said a man of about thirty from the second gang, who had not said anything until then. "**Not a wall. The works. And every man on that list is in the works.**"

Nobody said anything for a while and then about six of them said it at once, which is the only arithmetic a yard has ever needed.

"And it does not matter which wall," said Nold Prawle. "It was never the wall. The wall is why I am here and I am here every day of it and the wall is not in dispute and nobody has ever once said the wall was in dispute. **The form is not about the wall. The form is about the works, and a works is a thing, and the thing is what the box asked for.**"

The man from the office in the second district looked at the list of works for a while, and he did it properly, and he went over it twice.

"Nobody told me that," he said. "I want that said in this yard and I want it said by whoever needs it said, because I was sent with a form and I have read it out and I have not lied about one word of it and I did not know what it did to a rate."

"Then say it again to your office," said Nold Prawle, "and if they ask you what the words mean, do not tell them you asked us. **Tell them you were told by a mason. That is worth more than anything you could have found out in an office and it will last about four years.**"

A boy of sixteen on the list asked, "Does that mean I am finished."

"No," said the man from the office, and he was right, and he said, "**You are on a list of works and the list has got a day at the end of it and you are on the list on that day, and this form has got no name under it and no day at the end of it, and there is no way on earth for a form with no name under it to finish a boy who is on a list with a day against it.** And I have to tell you that I cannot tell you that for certain, because I have been in an office for nineteen years and every single thing I have ever read there that had no name on it finished somebody."

---

Here is what a mason says about that, and he said it in the shed and not in the yard, and he said it about eleven days after, and he has not said it in a room since and does not intend to.

"A heading is a machine. A column with a heading over it is a machine. A form with a heading over it and no person under it is a machine that runs on whatever is nearest, and if there is nothing else near it then it runs on the works, and the works is a thing, and a thing is a thing you can be held against without anybody being in it at all.

"**I did not put my name in it. I put a wall in it.** That is what I have got instead of my name, and I have got about four days of a man's work in a fortnight sitting in a box in an office about nine miles away, and the rate on a wall that is on the district's list is a shilling a day and the rate on a wall that is not on the district's list is three shillings a day, and I have argued that with a man in a shed four times and lost four times and I am not going to argue it a fifth time for nothing."

---

**He gives the cost of it in the yard, in as few sentences as it will go, because he is not a man who says a hard thing quietly.**

"About four days of a shilling a day is four shillings, and a shilling a day is a shilling a day whether the wall is on the list or not, and four shillings is a fortnight of a boy's food and a fire and a barrow's wear, and the barrow's wear is the part that does not come back at the end of a fortnight because the barrow is the same barrow and it has been up a wall four more days.

"I have got a boy of nine. He does not know what this is and I am not going to tell him what this is, and I am not going to be one of those men who tells his boy about the state of the district works. He will find out at some point the way everybody finds out, from somebody who is not me, and that will be a different afternoon and it is not going to be this one.

"And the other half, and I am saying it because nobody in this yard is going to say it. **I stood in a room for about twenty minutes on the second day of the twelfth month of next year with a lamp and a bench and ten entries read out loud, and I was not asked whether I would mind, and I said yes in five words.** She told me beforehand how long I would be standing there, and that is the only time in about eleven years anybody has told me how long anything was going to take.

"I have not been thanked for that and I am not going to be thanked for it and I would like it recorded that I would rather not be, because the day somebody thanks me for standing in a room is the day a person comes about the boy. And nobody has thanked me and I would leave if anybody did, and I have said that in two rooms now and I would say it in a third."

---

And then he said the thing that he is not going to say was worth it, and he said it on purpose, and he had it ready.

"**I am not going to tell anybody that it was worth it.** It was not worth four days of a shilling and it was not worth four days of anything. A man who says a thing was worth it after it has cost him four days of a shilling is a man who has stopped counting, and I have counted every day of my working life in this trade and that is the only thing I have that my father did not have, and I am not going to lose it in a sentence that makes me sound well thought of.

"It stood in a room. It is a fact. It is findable. That is the whole of the value and the whole of the value has a price and I know the price and I have said it out loud and half a yard heard me say it and none of them said anything, and that is correct, because there is nothing anybody can do with it."

---

Then he went and found the clerk.

There is one on this terrace and she is at a window about four feet off the ground, and he has been down this corridor twice before and not for this, and he went because there is a woman at that window who was the only person in the whole of this who was not a party and then was, and he is a man who notices that, and he does not expect to get anything and he is not going to ask.

He did not ask her to fix it. He said one sentence and he said it in the ordinary way, the way a man says a thing he has said four times in a shed.

"The rate is a shilling and the district owes me for a wall. The form is not the wall."

Ivet Sarn did not say anything for about nine seconds, which is longer than a clerk at a window usually takes, and then she went and got a copy of the schedule out of a press and read a definition out of it to him standing up, in a corridor, with about four feet of stone on either side of them both.

> *Engaged in the matter: having been asked to do a work of or about the matter, or being paid by a person who has been so asked, or being under the direction of a person who has been so asked. There is no column under this heading and this heading is not entered in any roll, and a person may be engaged in a matter without knowing that the matter is a matter.*

---

"Read that last one again," said Nold Prawle.

She read it again.

"Right," he said. "**Then I have not been engaged. I have been standing where a wall is.** I have never been asked to do anything about anybody's claim, I have never been paid by anybody who has, and I have never taken a direction from a man who has. I am a wall with a man on it and the man on it is fifty."

"That is correct," said Ivet Sarn, "and I am not going to write it down anywhere, and I want you to hear why I am not."

"Because you are in it."

"**Because I am in it and because a thing written down at this window about a form is a thing a form can be answered with.** There is no box for a why in any of this and I am not going to supply one in ink and find out in four years what it does."

---

They worked it out together in about nine minutes, standing in a corridor, and neither of them wanted to.

"A form can hold anybody it likes," said Nold Prawle, "as long as the person administering it is not asked what the words mean."

"And the person administering it is a clerk in an office about nine miles away," said Ivet Sarn, "who is not a villain, and I want that said because it is the whole of what I have got to say and I have been working on it for two days. **She is a clerk. She opened a form, she read a box, she put in the name of a thing because the box asks for a thing, and she did it correctly, and she is not going to be asked what the words mean, because nobody in this empire has ever asked anybody what the words mean, and that is not a failure of her it is the condition of her.**"

"What is it, then."

"It is that there are nine hundred buildings with a form in them and in every one of them there is a box and in every box there is a heading, and there is no post in this empire whose job it is to stand in front of a heading and ask. **I have been at a window for nine years and nobody has ever come to this window and asked me what a word on a form meant, and I have been waiting for it, and it has not come, and I have gone on serving them out at the rail.**"

Nold Prawle said, "Then you are waiting for the wrong thing and it is not coming, and I have lost four arguments in a shed to men who are not villains either."

"So you have."

---

Nobody thanked him. He said in advance, in the corridor, that they were not to, and nobody did, and he wants it written down that he was not thanked more than anybody else in that building was, because a woman in that building had said the price of being a party out loud five times and a man of thirty-eight in a city four hundred and thirty miles up this river had put nine lines in a dead woman's hand on a board at fourpence a copy, and a mason of fifty who has lost four days of a shilling is not in the same market as either of them and would be insulted to be told he was.

He went back up the terrace to the wall and set out his tools at about the fourth hour and worked a shilling's worth of a day on a wall that is on a district's list, which is a third of what the same wall is worth to anybody who is not on the list, and he has said that in a shed four times and lost four times and he said it this fifth time to nobody at all, in a yard, on his own, and nobody answered him because there was nobody there.

And the four days are still held, in a box, under a heading, in an office about nine miles away, and he is not going to ask for them back, and the reason he is not going to ask for them back is that asking for them back means asking a clerk what the words on her own form mean, and he has been in this trade for about eleven years and he has never once seen anybody do that and survive the doing of it.
