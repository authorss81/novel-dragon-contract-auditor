# Chapter 184: What Goes on a Public Board

Marek Kest had no office and no fund and no register and nothing in this empire he could certify anything with, and a bar of his own that came off him on the twelfth of the ninth month of last year on an application of one line which he wrote himself, and that is the whole of the reason he could hand a dead woman's method to a printer in the third week of the second month of the year after, and the whole of the reason it cost him nothing that anybody could put a price on.

He had thought about it for about nine days. He had tried it four ways and the fourth way was the one that worked, and the fourth way was that there was nothing to protect.

He had a rented room with a table in it and about four feet of a wall he did not use, and a book with no office on it that had four pages in it headed *What the blankness is for*, and a line about two inches long on the inside of his left wrist that read nothing that week.

---

He set the sheet out on the table in his own hand and then he copied it out fair, because a thing that is going on a board has to look like a thing that somebody meant, and then he read the fair copy back to himself twice, the second reading slower, and there was nobody in the room to hear either of them.

This is the whole of it.

---

> *These are nine lines written in the hand of the second person in a room.*
>
> *They are the fourth column of one line of a register of witnesses kept in a house in a lane about four miles off the old river road. A register of witnesses has five columns.*
>
> *The nine lines are set out below entire and without alteration. The second person is not named on this sheet. The house is not named on this sheet. Neither of those has been left off because it is inconvenient. The reason is in the fourth paragraph below and the reason is that the only piece of evidence in this matter that cannot be published without publishing a person is the column the second person is in, and that column is not on this sheet and is not going to be.*
>
> *The day is not given. The day is not given because the line these nine lines stand in is the only line in about four hundred that carries no day, and nobody has established whose hand put a day there or whose hand took it out, and a date printed beside them would be a number of years on the only line anybody in this matter has, and it would be a number of years with nobody at the end of it.*
>
> *The first column of that line is the witness and the name in it is Ilyra Kest.*
>
> *The finding, in the words of the person giving this up: a family that holds nothing enforceable is an eligible family and not a poor one, and a recorded claim with a name on it is a road, and a road does not care whether the claim can be enforced.*
>
> *The nine lines:*
>
> *The house is to hold nothing.*
> *Nothing is to be entered against it and nothing is to be taken out of it.*
> *The boy is to be put down as holding nothing and to be left so.*
> *A person who holds nothing cannot be given anything and that is the whole of it and it is the best of it.*
> *She was not asked where it came from and she did not say and I have written that down because I did not think of it at the time and I have thought of it since and it is a long time to have thought of a thing and not to have said it.*
> *The woman is to be put down as having said it and not as having asked it.*
> *It is to be done on a box and not on a paper, because a paper goes to a house and a box goes to a roll.*
> *Nobody is to be asked the family anything.*
> *That is all of it. I have not put anything else down and I was not told to leave anything else off.*
>
> *Given up by me. M. Kest. I hold no office and no fund and I am not a lawyer. The second week of the second month of the year after.*

---

The nine lines are on there word for word as they are in a bound book in a house with mortar in the door, and the whole of what he did with them was move them, and the moving is the act, and he has thought about that for about nine days and has not found a version of it in which the moving is not the act.

**A copy of a thing is a fact about the copying. Nine lines that have been in a house in a lane are about to be on a board in a city, and the only difference between the two is that about nine hundred buildings hold the form they were written against and not one of them holds the register.**

He did not cross anything out. There was nothing to cross out.

---

Tamsin Rook came in at about the eighth hour and read the whole of it standing up, and she read it the way she reads things, which is once, and she gave him the count and the date back to him at the end of it without being asked, which is a thing she does and has never once explained.

Then she said the thing that the sheet had done and that he had known it would do.

"That is the best sentence on it and it is the sentence that ends you."

"Which one."

"**A register of witnesses has five columns.**" She put her finger on it without touching it. "You wrote that because a heading that does not say what a thing is is a lie, and you are not going to print a lie, and you are right, and it is correct, and it is the sentence that tells about seventy people by the end of next month that there was a part."

"I know."

"Say it out loud, because in about four years there is going to be a person with a pen who is going to ask you why you told the world that a thing existed and hid which thing, and you are going to want the answer to be in your own mouth and not in mine."

Marek Kest put both hands flat on the table.

"I am publishing one column of one line of a book of about four hundred lines, in a city where a copy of the form it was written against is printed in about nine hundred buildings, and I told the room how many columns the register has, and the day is not on it, and the day not being on it is the first thing anybody will notice and I have explained it in four lines and I believe the explanation. And every one of those four things I did on purpose and I would do them again tomorrow and I am not going to stand here and tell you it was a slip, because it was the only way to print it at all. **A copy of a form is worth exactly what a form is worth, which is that anybody may have it for nothing, and the only thing that makes a copy of a register worth having is a reader who knows there is one.**"

---

"And the part that is not on it," said Tamsin Rook.

"No."

"Tell me why, in one sentence, because you have had nine days and I have had about a minute."

"The third column has no heading over it and it is where the second person is, and the second person is a woman of sixty-three at the end of a lane about four hundred yards further on, and the one piece of evidence in this matter that cannot be published without publishing a person is that line, and I am not going to publish her."

"**That is protection and it is a machine, and you have just used the machine, and I want you to hear the sentence.**"

"I hear it."

"I am not saying it to be unkind. I am saying it because in about four years somebody is going to notice that the man who published nine lines about a person being left out of a book left one out himself, and they are going to decide that he did it kindly or that he did it for himself, and neither of those is going to be what happened." She said it the way she says facts. "What happened is that you could not do it the other way and you did it anyway and you have written four words about it. That is the whole of it and there is no virtue in either direction."

"I have not called it a kindness and I am not going to be thanked for it."

"Then do not be thanked for it," said Tamsin Rook, "**and do not be quietly pleased with yourself about it in a rented room either, and I say that as a person who has watched a man in a good coat do the second thing and I have watched it twice.**"

---

He said the price out loud then, in the corridor, and he said the number with it, because a number is findable and a person who says a number can be held to it, and neither of them counted it out loud in that corridor.

"I am a party to an instrument about a dead woman's name. I have been eligible for an office in this empire that a human being may hold for at most seven years since the day her name came out of a file, and nobody was ever going to tell me, and the reason nobody was going to tell me is the fourth line of what I am printing at fourpence a copy. **I have no office, so there is nothing to be struck off me. There is no career to damage. There is a bar that is discharged and a book with no office on it and a line on my wrist that reads nothing, and a person has to be damaged in what they are, and the only thing I am is a shape, and I have just handed the shape to a printer.** And I would like somebody to write down that I said it in that order, because I tried the other order first and in the other order it is a speech about courage and it is not that."

"It is not," said Tamsin Rook. "It is a woman who did nine minutes of work on a box and is dead, and a method that is still sitting in a shelf, and a man who decided that the second of those is the dangerous one. And I am not going to be thanked for reading your sheet and I am not going to be thanked for this, and this is the sixth time I have said the price of a party out loud and it is not about either of us, and I have noticed that it stopped being about us the moment it stopped being about me and I have not decided what to do about noticing that."

---

He paid for the printing.

Three hundred sheets, and a board at fourpence a copy, and a stall in a passage off the same building as the fourth counter, where a printer's man sells anything anybody cares to set in type at fourpence a copy, and there was nothing else on the boards in that passage that had anything to do with anything, and the fourpence is the ordinary rate and it is the rate at which four hundred people a year buy a fact about how a book was copied.

He gave the printer the sheet and the printer asked him for a heading for the board and he gave him the first line of it, and the printer cut a strip of card and copied the first line onto it in a fair hand, and the strip went up over a counter about four feet wide in a passage where there was nothing else on any of the boards that had anything to do with anything.

The printer did not ask who he was. The printer does not ask anybody who they are, because at fourpence a copy the seller of a thing and the buyer of it are the same kind of person, and that is the only privacy there is in this trade and it is not a thing anybody built.

**And there is no form in this empire for stopping a copy.** Fourpence is not his to withhold, and the words are not his to withhold once they are set in type, and the sheet is a thing anybody may have, and a thing anybody may have is a thing that cannot be recalled, and a thing that cannot be recalled is the only instrument any of them have ever had.

---

He stood in the passage on the day it went up, at about the second hour, and he stayed there for about forty minutes, and he had not planned to and he did not say why.

A woman of about thirty came and stood where he was standing, because that is what a person does at a board, and read the heading, and read the first four lines of the nine, and then said, "Is this a play or is it a thing."

"It's a thing," said the printer's man, who was about thirty-five and had been doing this for nine years in a passage off the same building as the fourth counter and who had not asked who wrote it and was not going to.

"A thing about what."

"It's about a woman wrote nine lines in a room," said the printer's man, "and here's the heading, and it says where it came from, and that's the trade, and it's fourpence."

The woman of about thirty read the whole of the nine lines at the board, standing up, in a passage, at about the second hour of a working day, and she did not buy one.

Marek Kest stood four feet from her and listened to a stranger read his mother's arrangement for a family that holds nothing, and he could not hear a sound of it, because a sheet of paper in the hand at a board makes no sound at all, and that is the first thing a person discovers about a public document and it is not a thing anybody warns you about.

Then a man of about fifty came, and asked for a copy, and paid fourpence, and did not give his name because the counter does not take a name for a copy, and folded it into four and put it inside his coat, and went up the passage and out.

Marek Kest did not follow him and did not try to see the face and did not work out whether he knew him. He stood in the passage and thought, in the ordinary way, that he had just watched a man of fifty pay fourpence to find out something about a family he had never heard of, and that fourpence had come out of that man's own money into nobody's, and that this had been his decision, and that it was made.

He has written down in his own book, with the day on it, that he stood there for about forty minutes, and that the reason he stood there was that he wanted to see somebody buy one, and that a man who has decided to publish a thing and then goes and watches a stranger pay for it is a man who has decided to find out whether it was worth it, and that what he found out was that it is worth fourpence to a man of fifty and that is a different question from the one he asked himself in a rented room and he has not worked out which of the two is the real one.

---

He wrote four words in the margin of his own book that night, at about the eleventh hour, under the four pages, and the four words are not the only four words in a margin of that book, and he is not going to put the two of them next to each other, and in about four years there is going to be a person holding both pages who has to work out which of the two of us is talking about a family.

*a person, not a column.*

And he did not write them for anybody. He wrote them because a man who has spent four months watching a form put a name in a place so that the name can never be asked anything ought to have something of his own in the book, and because a person is going to read this book in about four years and is going to want to know what he thought on the night he handed a dead woman's nine lines to a printer for fourpence a copy.

Nobody thanked him. He said in advance, in the room, out loud, that nobody was to, and nobody did, and there is a form nowhere that requires a person to be thanked and there never has been.

And the board went up in the third week into the fourth week of the second month of the year after, with nine lines in a dead woman's hand on it and a man's name at the foot of it and a heading that says exactly what it is and where it came from, and the heading is the reason that from that day there was a part, and there was a part before that day as well, and it was in a book in a house, and now it is on a board, and a woman of sixty-three who is in the third column of it is not on the board, and nobody has been told that she is anywhere at all.
