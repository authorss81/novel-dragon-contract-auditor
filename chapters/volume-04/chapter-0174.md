# Chapter 174: A Door in a Wall a Man Is Paid to Work On

A door in a wall a man is paid to work on is not a seal. It is an obstruction.

Nold Prawle said that at about the fourth hour on the third day of the second week of the twelfth month of next year, to a man of thirty-eight he had met once, standing in a lane about four miles off the old river road with a barrow of mortar in it, and he said it the way a man says a thing he has been saying to himself for years and has never had anybody to say it to.

He is fifty. He has a boy of nine at home who was not in the lane and is not named. He has been in this trade since he was about twenty and he has patched, by his own count and roughly, every wall in this district that anybody will let him near, and the reason he has patched them is that he is the only one holding the trowel, and **there is no form in this empire that makes a person be in charge of a thing and there never has been, and the way he came by a wall is the way a clerk of thirty-eight came by a press, and neither of them applied and neither of them was sent, and he has never in his life thought of that as the same shape and it is.**

---

The house is at the end of the lane and it is a ruin, and what is left of it is about two rooms and a wall and a roof on three sides, and the fourth side has no roof and never had, and there is a door in it and the door is a door and not a gate and the hinges are on and the hasp is on and the hasp has a lump of mortar in it about the width of a hand.

There is a name in the mortar.

Marek Kest read it and did not say it out loud, and the mason watched him not say it, and neither of them said anything about it for about four seconds.

"That is a seal," said Marek Kest.

"That is a lump of mortar with a word in it that I could not read when I first came and can read now and have not told anybody what it says for about eleven years," said Nold Prawle. "**I have been patching round that door for about eleven years and I have never had to go through it, and if I had not had to go through it I would not have gone through it, and that is the whole of my interest in it, which is that it is in the way.**"

---

He put the barrow down and wiped his hands on his coat and did not sit down, because there is nowhere in that lane to sit down.

"The district has got a list of things it will not put a roof on," he said. "I do not know what the list is called. It is on a board in a shed and it has got nine things on it and one of them is this and the writing on it says *structure unsafe* in a hand that has not been near this house in a hundred years."

"And nobody has challenged it."

"Nobody has challenged it because challenging it is a job and the job is not anybody's, and because the house is not anybody's, and because there is a lump of mortar in a door." He nodded at the lane behind him. "**There is a woman at the end of this lane who came to me about this house once. She wanted to know what was in it. I told her I did not know and she did not ask me again and she did not go and look, and she has been in that house about once every four days for about nine years buying a bundle of kindling off a man who cuts it, and she has not gone in and she has not asked me again.**"

Marek Kest said, "She wanted to know what was in it."

"That is what I said. I did not think much of it at the time and I have thought of it about four times since and I do not have a better account of it than that."

---

"You are going to ask me to open it," said Nold Prawle, "and I am going to tell you three things and then you can ask me or not ask me, and I want them said in this order because the second one is the only one that matters and the other two are just so that you know I have thought about it."

"Go on."

"One. **I am not going to do it for you.**" He said it flatly. "You have a coat with paper in it and you have been in this matter about four months and everybody in it has been extremely careful, and careful is what I would want, and I am not going to do a thing I cannot undo because a man I met once asked me nicely in a lane."

"Then what are you going to do."

"Nothing for you. That is what I am going to do. I am going to do it for the wall, on my own account, at about the fourth hour, with my own barrow, and I am going to do it because I have a job on this lane and the door is in the way of the job and the district will not pay me for a wall with a lump of mortar in it, and I have a boy of nine and he is nine."

"And the second thing."

Nold Prawle took his time about it.

"**Nobody asked me.** And I am not going to say I was asked, and if I say I was asked then in about four years there is a person who can come down this lane and say a mason opened a door about four miles off a road because a man he had met once asked him to, and that person would be telling the truth, and it would be the only true thing anybody in this house has ever been able to say about it."

"And the third."

"**You cannot make me stand in a room and I cannot be made to be in a room and there is no form in this empire for it and I have never wanted one.**" He looked at the mortar. "So I am not going to be frightened of you and I am not going to be grateful to you and I am not going to be waiting for you to come back and tell me what was in there, and if you want to know what was in there you will have to ask somebody who was in it, and I am not going to be that somebody and I am telling you so now while there is still a lump of mortar in the door."

---

He worked at it for about nine minutes with a chisel he had brought for the wall and not for the door, and he did not hurry, and Marek Kest stood about four feet away and did not help and was not asked to.

The mortar came off in three pieces and went on the ground and the hasp came away with the third of them, and the door came open about four inches and stopped, because there is a board across the inside of it about the height of a man's knee, and the board is nailed and it has been nailed a long time and the nails are the old kind.

The smell came out into the lane. Lime, and paper, and cold.

"**That is a hundred and forty years of paper in a room with no fire in it**," said Nold Prawle. "I have smelled that in three other buildings and in all three of them somebody was being paid to be frightened of it."

---

There is a bench against the wall inside, and a press of sheets in the corner with about a hundred and forty... no.

He did not count them. He counted the top layer and stopped and did not finish, and he has never said the number out loud to anybody including himself, and **the reason he did not finish is not a reason anybody would accept and it is the correct one: a person who counts a thing in a room in order to say the number later is a person who has put a number in a book in his head, and a number in a book in a man's head is the only kind of number that cannot be corrected by anybody who comes after him.**

There is a floor of lime about two inches deep and it has been walked into and it has gone hard and it holds a heel print in nine places and none of them is a size you would order shoes in.

There is a press of undelivered sheets. They are printed, and they are stacked, and the top of the stack has a crease in it about the width of a thumb.

And there is a second thing on the bench, and it is the only thing in the room that is a book, and it is a bound register, and Marek Kest did not open it in this lane and did not open it in the lane after that and did not open it for about four days, and the reason he gives is in his own book in his own hand with the day on it and it is four words long.

*I did not ask.*

---

They stood in the lane for a while.

"Offices that keep a house like this shut it," said Marek Kest. "That is what it is. It is not a house anybody lives in. It is a house a thing gets shut inside until somebody with a particular kind of standing asks for it, and the standing is not a rank, and it is the only standing in this empire that cannot be given to a person by anybody, and I have not got it and I have not any idea what it is called."

"Can it be shut again?"

"It can be shut again with a lump of mortar and no form at all, in ten minutes, by a man with a chisel, and he would not be doing anything wrong, and I have just watched him do about a third of it for free."

Nold Prawle picked up his barrow.

"Then I am going to take my barrow up to the wall and I am going to be about thirty feet from this door all day tomorrow and the day after, and if anybody in this lane says the door was shut, it was shut by a man who was not asked and is not waiting for anybody, and that is a true sentence and I would like it said before anybody comes down here with a paper about it." He got the barrow up. "I am not going to be thanked. I am not going to be told what was in there. And I am not going to be asked twice, because I will say the same thing the second time and it will not be any riper."

He went up the lane, and he was not gone four minutes.

He came back with a tallow candle in a tin with a handle, and a piece of nine inches of board with a nail in one end of it, which is a thing a man who works on walls carries so that he can stand a candle on a shelf, and he stood it on the shelf and lit it and gave the tin to Marek Kest without saying anything about it.

"There is no light in there," he said. "I have been in about four feet of it twice in eleven years and both times with a lamp and both times I could not see the far wall, and that is not because it is far, it is ten feet. It is because you have to hold the thing up and the thing is the light."

---

So the man of thirty-eight went four feet into a house that nobody asked him to go into, holding a candle in a tin, with a mason who was not asked either standing behind him and not coming further in.

You cannot get a person into a room in this empire. You can ask. You can pay. You can put a name at the foot of a list of works and nail it to a rail, and there is one of those four miles up this river and about nine of the other three, and the one that works is the one that costs somebody a day of their own.

He had not asked the mason and the mason had not offered and neither of them said anything about it, and the arrangement was made entirely out of a candle.

---

At four feet, on a shelf, the top of the stack of sheets had a heading over it and the heading was about a foot long.

Marek Kest could not read all of it. He read the first two words, and then he read them again, and then he went back to the first two words, and he could not make the third one go with the first two, and after about nine seconds he stopped and stood there with a candle in a tin and did not read any more of it.

"That is a form," he said.

"That is a form," said the man behind him. "I have looked at that stack from about four feet in about two hours of my life over eleven years and that is all I have ever been able to say about it, and I have said it to about four people and two of them were not interested."

"It is addressed to somebody," said Marek Kest.

"They are all addressed to somebody. They are on a shelf in a building in a box of a hundred and forty things and the ones I have not looked at are on the ones I have looked at and the whole of what I know about that room is that it is about four feet deep and nine feet across and about eleven feet to the roof and I have never had a reason to be in it." He said that last part without any weight at all. "**I am a man who patches walls. In eleven years I have been given two things to look at in this house and one of them was a roof and the other one was that stack, and I have told you what I know about both of them and I have not been asked for more and I am not going to be.**"

---

"Three shillings," he said, at the door, on his way out. "That is a day on a wall that is not on the district's list. A shilling, that is a day on a wall that is on it. That is the whole of what they have done about this house in eleven years and they have done it in one line of a hand, and I have argued about that line with a man in a shed four times and I have lost four times, and I am not going to argue about it a fifth time for free."

"You could be paid to argue about it a fifth time," said Marek Kest.

"I could," said Nold Prawle, "and I am not, and I would like you to notice that I have told you the number instead of asking you for it, and that is because you are not the district and I have not got a form from you and there is nothing I could do with a form from you."

He looked back at the door.

"And I have a boy of nine. And I am not going to have that boy be a person that somebody comes about, and I have worked that out in a few seconds and I have said it out loud so that you know I have worked it out."

---

And a woman of about sixty-three came down the lane at about the fifth hour with a bundle of kindling under her arm, and she went past the open door without stopping, in the way of a person who has been going past an open door for most of a decade and has not once gone through it, and she looked at the man of thirty-eight standing in the gap and she did not stop and she did not say anything and she went on to the end of the lane and put the kindling down outside a door of her own and went in.

Marek Kest looked at the door she had gone through and then at the door of the house and did not say the sentence he was going to say.

He has written down in his own book, in his own hand, with the day on it, that the woman at the end of that lane came down it at about the fifth hour and went past an open door without stopping, and that is all he wrote, and it is a fact and not a question, and he has read it back twice, the second reading slower.
