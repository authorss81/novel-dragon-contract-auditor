# Chapter 155: The Third Box

She did it at the second hour of the fourth week of the fifth month of next year, while he was at the estates counter four feet away asking a clerk about the cost of a certified copy of a schedule, and it took her about eleven minutes and she did not sign the first box.

The sheet was on the slab where he had left it, because she had told him to leave it at the end of the day before, and she had told him in exactly those words, and he had left it.

Box one: who asks. His name, his room, in his own hand, four hundred and thirty miles down a river.

Box two: the thing asked about. The run, the row, the fourpence, the eleven weeks, the fact that there is no provision for anybody else and there never has been.

Box three: the person the instrument touches.

She wrote her own name in it and the day, and she wrote them in the hand she had been writing in for four years, which is a copyist's hand and a small one and it slopes about nine degrees to the right and everybody in three galleries can tell it from anybody else's, and she put her hand flat over it for a second as if to be sure the ink was down and then took the hand away.

Then she folded the sheet once and put it in her own bag and did not sign box one, and did not countersign anything, and nobody saw her do it, and that is exactly how it happened.

---

She waited about four minutes.

He came back along the passage and stopped four feet short of the counter, which is the distance, and she watched him read the slab where the sheet had been and then look up.

"You left it," he said.

"I asked you to leave it."

"You said leave it on the counter, not leave it with you."

"I said leave it," said Tamsin Rook, "and I did not say where."

---

He put both hands flat on the oak on either side of the empty space and did not say anything for about nine seconds, and she had watched a man do that before in about four rooms in fourteen months and it had always meant one thing, and this time it meant two things at once and the one on the top was not the one he was thinking about.

"I am not going to say anything about it for about a minute," he said, "and I want that minute entered somewhere, because I have watched myself not do that for four months."

"Entered where."

"In the book with no heading, by a person who is not a lawyer, in words, and I am asking you to do it as a fact and not as advice, because I have a line in a room four hundred and thirty miles from here that forbids me advice by proximity and I am not going to be the man who broke it by accident and meant to."

"That is a fact and not advice," said Tamsin Rook, "and I am going to do it because it is my hand and not yours, and I have never once done it for you."

---

Then he did the thing she had not expected, and it took him about four seconds to decide and she has thought about the four seconds since.

He got a sheet of foolscap out of his coat and wrote on it in his own hand, and she could see the shape of it from four feet away and she did not read it and she would have turned away if she could have.

"A choice," he said. "In writing. With a day on it. You have to answer me in a room, in words, with a date, and the rule is the twelfth month of last year and it is the rule and not a form, and I am using it for the seventh time and I would like you to notice that it is a stupid rule and I have never once been glad of it until this afternoon."

"Go on."

> *Either: do not put yourself in the third box.*
> *Or: put yourself in the third box.*
> *The sixth of the fifth month of next year.*

She read it twice, the second reading slower, and she did not need the second reading and took it anyway.

"Answered in the room," she said. "In words. With a date. **I am already in it, and I have been in it since about a quarter past the second hour this morning, and the question you have put to me does not have a third answer, so the rule does not apply, and a rule that does not apply is a rule that has been used correctly, and I would like that written down as well because it is the first time since that rule was written that one of us has got a non-use out of it and I have heard of exactly one other.**"

"Who was the other."

"A man of thirty-eight in a room with a stove in it in the fourth week of the fourth month," she said, "and I am not going to tell you what he was not allowed to do, because you are him, and you will find it when you find it, and I am not going to be the person who spends a thing on you that you have not earned."

---

Here is the price of it, and she said it out loud, and she said it twice, and she did not soften it the first time and she did not soften it the second.

"I can be served anything about this instrument for the rest of my life. Not this month. For the rest of my life. **A party is a person a book can be true about, and a book can be true about me from this morning, and a thing that can be said to be true about me can be served on me, and it can be served on me at the address in the first box, and I have put that address there, and it is the address I have been making findable on purpose for fourteen months, and I did that on purpose too, and I did not know until about eleven minutes ago that the two of those are the same decision.**"

"And there is no form for taking a party back out of a box."

"**There is no form in this empire for taking a party back out of a box.** There is no form, and there is no office with power to do it, and there is no clause, and I have looked, and I looked in about four minutes, standing at a counter, while you were at the estates counter, and I looked properly. I did not find one. I did not look in every place. I am telling you the truth about the size of the looking."

He said, "What is the second thing you are not softening."

**"That you are cheaper than I am, and that I knew it, and that I did it anyway, and that this is not a rescue and I am not going to let anybody in four hundred and thirty miles call it one.**

"A body cannot be made a party. A body cannot be instructed and a body cannot stop. That is not my sentence, it is a keeper's, in a room, in three parts, in the first month of last year, and about nine hundred rooms have heard the three parts and not understood them. A person with a wage can be made to answer for a thing. **A person with nothing behind them can be made to answer for nothing at all, because there is nothing to be liable for, and because nobody can serve a person who has nothing to serve.** That is not a thing I worked out at this counter. That is a thing that was worked out about a hundred and forty years ago in a building where a strip of paper was gummed over the only line on a form that mattered, and nobody ever said it out loud because there was no reason to.

"So the cheapest party in this matter is a man of thirty-eight with a discharged bar and a wage of seventeen shillings and a penny the week and three pounds eleven and threepence of it gone nineteen days at a time out of a weigh-house ledger, and he is standing in front of me, and you can have him for nothing at all."

"And you put yourself in it."

"**I put myself in it because there were two of us and one of the two of us had a wage and one of the two of us had a sister, and I have not said the second half out loud in a room and I am not going to now.**"

---

"Will you tell me why," he said. "Not for the form. The form is written."

"No."

"Because of the rule?"

"**Because of the rule.** You have a right to put a choice in writing and a date on it and I have to answer in words with a date, and I have answered, and the answer was that the rule does not apply. **If I tell you why now, then in four years there is a form in a press in a building in this city with a reason on it and no why on it, and the why is in a room, and the room is not a room anybody can be summoned out of.** And there is already a woman of thirty-four in this building who wrote down a thing about a strip of paper with gum on it and had to put it in a press with no address on the outside of it, and she has done that twice now and she is one person, and I am not going to be the second one in the same month."

"You have never once been a person anything happened to," said Marek Kest.

"No.

"Not a party. Not a person an instrument touched. Not a person a thing was done to. You said that at this counter in the fourth month of last year and asked for it to be believed by a clerk and it was believed in about eleven seconds."

"And I have been the person on the inside of a box for fourteen months," said Tamsin Rook, "in a column with my hand against every line of it, and I have never once been in it. And I have never been able to work out what the difference is, and I have decided this afternoon that **it is not a difference, and there is no inside, and I have been standing in the doorway of my own column for fourteen months congratulating myself on the view.**

"So I would like to know what it is like for about eleven weeks. **That is the whole of it. That is a want and not a reason, and I am telling you it is a want, because a want is the only honest name for it and because a want entered in a form at this table would be in a book for a hundred and forty years, and I am not going to do that, and I have thought about it for about nine seconds and I have decided.**"

---

He did not thank her.

He wanted to. She watched him not do it and she knew what it cost him and she has never said so to anybody and she is not going to say so out loud either.

He said, "I am not going to thank you for it, because that would make it a thing you did for me."

"Yes," said Tamsin Rook.

"And I am not going to be the man who says *the paper is not the reason* either, because you have not said it and I have not said it and both of us have been in four bays this year on account of a piece of paper, and if either of us says it first then it is a sentence and a sentence can be put in a book."

"That is the correct use of a rule," she said, "and you have now made two non-uses in about nine minutes and I am going to write both of them in a book with no office on it and one of them is mine and one is yours and neither of us is going to be thanked for it."

"Write mine down wrong, then."

"I will not."

---

And here is what she did not say at that counter, and has not said anywhere, and this is the record of it because somebody has to write it down once.

She is two boxes on one form. She said so herself, in the second month of this year, in a room, to about nine people: a person who is safe because nobody can instruct her and useful because somebody needs her standing in a room is a person with two boxes filled in on the same form, and there is a limit that does and an office that does not, and the whole of what she is for is the limit.

And there are two instruments and they are not one, and she has noticed, and she said so out loud, to a man at a counter: "I am in this room because a court order says I am in any room you are in. There is a second paper and it is not that one, and it does not require you and it requires somebody else in the room, and I know which of us it wants. And I am not going to tell you which of the two papers is the reason I am here, because they are two papers and they do not go in one box, and I have watched a hundred and forty years of people put two papers in one box and the box is where the gum is."

And then she paid fourpence for a copy of the application, out of a copyist's wage that is on no page anybody in this matter has, and did not enter it in the fee book of that counter, because there is no head on that board for a thing like that and the only honest place for it is the column of a clerk's own spending that is kept on the back of a counter in a city and shown to nobody.

And there is a drawer in a city four hundred and thirty miles down a river with four envelopes in it, and one of them has been there since the first month of the year before last, and it has not been sent and it has not been opened, and she did not look at it, and it is four lines long, and that is the whole of what it is and nobody touches it.

---

And what was left standing at the end of the fourth week of the fifth month of next year:

An application in three ruled boxes, two of them filled on the fourth day by a man of thirty-eight with a bar discharged and a watch on him, and the third filled at about a quarter past the second hour on the fifth day by a woman of twenty-four with a copyist's hand that slopes about nine degrees to the right, and neither of them has signed the first box, and there is a copy at fourpence in a bag in both their coats.

A question bought at a shilling with the day at the top of it and about eleven weeks on it, and the answer due in the first week of the eighth month of next year.

A refusal in a form with a reason in it and no why, and a day on it, and a name at the foot of it, and a copy of it at fourpence in the inside coat of a man who asked for one for four months and cannot use it.

**And a strip of printed paper about two inches by one, gummed to the foot of about nine hundred forms a year, with the only line on the sheet that changes what a person is printed on it, and no knife on the counter, and a pair of scissors in the second drawer for estates ribbon.**
