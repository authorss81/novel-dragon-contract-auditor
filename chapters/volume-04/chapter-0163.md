# Chapter 163: A Row and Not a Leaf

The answer came to the fourth counter of the Notaries' Table in Auremar in the second hour of the fourth day of the first week of the eighth month of next year, and it was three weeks in a press and it was the answer to a question that had cost a shilling in the second week of the fifth month, and Marn Ottery read it out twice, the second reading slower.

Four hundred and thirty miles down this river, in the same week, about forty-one people on a bank about four hundred and forty foot long had held a review.

She has said out loud that there is no form in this empire that puts two dates in one box. She said it in the last week of the seventh month, at that counter, to two people, and she meant it, and it is eleven days old, and it is the finding she would like somebody to do something about and would like it not to be her.

Both of those things came true in one morning and neither of them was arranged by anybody.

---

She was thirty-four. She had been at the fourth counter eleven years and had come to it from the second, where estates are, and she had never once been a lawyer and had never wanted to be one, and she knew more about scale instruments than anybody who has ever been a lawyer at that counter and none of that was on the board.

She took the slip off the wire where it had come up and looked at it for a while before she said anything, which is not what she usually does, and what she usually does is read it out.

"This is an answer," she said. "It is about four lines. It is the answer and it is correct and it is not what either of you came for and I am going to read it and then I am going to say four things that are not on it, and the four things are the reason you paid a shilling and the four things are not on anybody's paper."

"Read it."

---

> *Query: run of the device described by the mark at the number given, the register of runs of this office, book of the ninth year.*
>
> *Answer: there is a row. The row records a striking-out. The striking-out was made with an impression of the device and the impression took a number in the run. The row is not an entry of an instrument and no instrument of this office answers to that number.*
>
> *The column in which the name stands is headed *by whose authority*. A name under that heading is the discharge of an office. It is not an admission of authorship and it is not an accusation.*
>
> *No document corresponding to the run number is held at this table.*

"That is the whole of it," said Marn Ottery. "**And it is four lines and it is correct, and there is no document corresponding to the run number held at this table, and the second half of that is the only part of it that anybody could have done anything about.**"

She read it again, the same words, slower.

---

She has read a thing back twice, the second reading slower, about nine hundred times in eleven years at that counter and she has never once said where she got it. A girl of seventeen four hundred and thirty miles down this river has been doing it in a bay for about a season, out loud, in the cold, and Marn Ottery has been asked twice in four days where she got it and has not told anybody twice.

Neither of those is going to change because of this morning.

"That is where I got it," she said, when she had read it the second time, to nobody, and then she put the slip down on the oak and squared it, which is a thing a certain copyist does, and which she has never once said she learned it from and is not going to.

---

"Four things," she said. "The four things are not on the paper and I am putting them in a room with two people in it and not in a file, because a file is a thing that goes to whoever comes next, and what I am about to say is of no use to whoever comes next."

"Say them."

"**One.** What was struck out was not a scale instrument. A striking-out of a name out of a roll is done with an impression, because an impression is the only thing in this building that will take a number, and the number is a run number, and a run number is how this office accounts for a thing it did not make. That has been in this room for a hundred and forty years. About thirty rows in that one run are not instruments at all and no column in that book says which is which and I have told you that once and I am telling you again because the answer does not say it either."

"Two."

"**Two.** The thing struck out was a leaf. "She turned the slip over and there was nothing on the back of it, which is itself a fact. "A leaf of a bound schedule. And I am going to say the next part in a plain voice because I have had it in my head since the fourth hour of the fourth day and I cannot make it smaller: **a bound schedule does not travel.**"

Nobody said anything for a while.

"A leaf can be copied. A leaf can be certified at fourpence in person by the person it is about. A leaf cannot be taken out of a binding and carried four hundred and thirty miles and be the same document afterwards, and the reason is not law and not a punishment and there is no clause about it anywhere. **The reason is that the binding is what holds the schedule whole, and a schedule is held whole by the fact that the leaves are in it, and a leaf out of a bound schedule is not a copy of the schedule and is not a leaf of it either.** It is a removal."

"And where is it."

"**Fourth press.** In a wall. In a corridor behind a court on a flood terrace, about four hundred and thirty miles down the water. "Marn Ottery put two fingers on the oak. "It has been in that corridor longer than anybody in that building has been alive, and nobody in that building has ever been told it is there, and I have read about the corridor and not been in it, and that is the size of what I know."

"Three."

"**Three.** There are three things required and one of them is had. A written application, a court direction, and a recorded custody entry. You have the application and it is in a bag in a coat and there are two names in it and the answer-space at the foot of it is empty and it is going to stay empty for a while."

"And the other two."

"A court direction is a direction of a court. **This table is not a court.** I have no power to direct a court and no form on which to ask one, and I looked for about nine minutes in the press behind me and I did not look everywhere and I am telling you the size of the looking. The other of the two is a recorded custody entry, and I am not going to say a word about that this morning except that it is not a thing a table grants and it is not a thing a form produces, and that whoever told you there were three things told you the truth and told you nothing about which of the three is the hard one."

"Which is the hard one."

"That is the third one and I have not said and I am not going to say today."

"Four," said Marek Kest.

"**Four.** A copy of a thing is a fact about the copying. "She said it the way a person says a thing they have been carrying a long way. "You can have this slip for fourpence whenever you like for the rest of your life. A copy of a row is fourpence. A copy of a row is a fact about a row and a fact about the person who wrote it down and not about what was struck out. And I told you at the end of the seventh month that if you were not here I would put it in the press and you could have it for fourpence, and I said that as though it were the whole of what was on offer, and it is not, and I found that out on the fourth day of the first week of the eighth month of next year and I have not slept particularly well since."

---

"You are not in the wrong building," he said.

"No."

"I am four hundred and thirty miles from the place the thing is in, on the same week that forty-one people held a review I did not go to, because I am not on the list and the list has never had me on it."

"No."

"So the collision cost nothing."

"**It cost you nothing at all,**" said Marn Ottery. "And I want to say that in this room, at about the fifth hour, in front of the two of you, because I put the two dates next to each other in the last week of the seventh month as though it were a wall, and it is not a wall, it is a hole. You were never on the list. You are not on the list. There is about thirty-one people in a bay and a girl of seventeen carrying a day to about nine of them for nothing and that is what the week cost, and the only person in this matter it cost anything to is a person of seventeen who is paid forty-five pence a day and is not paid for her feet."

"You know that."

"I know it because a sheet came up a road on the back of a carrier's answer and the answer says what a person wrote on the back of a form four hundred and thirty miles away, and people write on the back of forms."

---

And then she said the price, because she was going to say it before either of them could and she had learned that in the last week of the seventh month.

"The price is that the two of you are now findable about a row. Not about the bank, not about the water, about a row in a register in a building in this city, with a day on it and my name at the foot of it, and a copy at fourpence, and in about four years anybody with fourpence can read it. **And now you are findable about a press in a corridor four hundred and thirty miles away, which is a different door, and the price of a second door is the second door.**"

"That is the second time you have said that."

"It is worse this time and I am not going to pretend it is not. "She put both hands flat on the oak. "I have been the door in this room for eleven years and I always thought it meant people came in. **It means people go out as well, and I have been sending a man down a road for three weeks in somebody else's coat and it has not cost me one thing, and that is because the door does not know which way it is facing, and a door that does not know which way it is facing is not a door anybody can shut.**"

Somebody in that room said the word *door* and then said nothing else, and she let it stand.

"Do not thank me," she said. "I did not do it for you and I have not done it yet."

---

Tamsin Rook had the application out of the bag by then and was holding it the way she holds everything, which is square.

"The third box has my name in it," she said. "And the answer-space at the foot is empty."

"It is."

"And neither of us signed the first box, and I filled the third one, and a party cannot be released, and I said the price of that out loud twice in this room in the last week of the seventh month and I said I would say it again if it came up."

"It has come up."

"**It has not, actually, and I want that noticed,**" she said, "because I have been sitting here for about four minutes waiting for it to come up and it is not about that, and the thing it is about is a press in a wall, and I have been in a box and the press is not in a box, and there is a difference and I have not worked out what it is and I am going to work it out on a road. "She squared the sheet. "What I am going to say is a fact and not advice and the third line says I am not to advise anybody and a fact is not advice and I have said that in this room four times now."

"Say the fact."

"**A person who has been put in a box can be served anything about that box for the rest of her life, and there is no form anywhere for taking a party back out of a box, and the size of the looking is about four minutes at a counter and not in every place.** And a press in a wall cannot be served anybody, because nobody can be made a party to a press, and **that is not a mercy.** That is a wall. And I have been in a box for four months and I have never once wanted to be in a wall and I want somebody in this room to notice that I have never once wanted to be in a wall, and to write it down, and I am not going to ask anybody to write it down."

---

They left at about the sixth hour, down the passage past the estates counter and out into the evening, and she watched them as far as the propped door with the quill in it, and then she did not go and do anything else, which is not how she works.

A row in a register in a book on a high shelf, and a number in a run, and about thirty rows in that run that are not instruments, and a column headed *by whose authority* with one name in it, and **no day, because a clerk of thirty-four would not read a day aloud in a public room and said out loud why in the second week of the fifth month of next year and has not moved on it since.**

A slip of four lines with a day on it at the top of it, in a hand that has gone over the row more than once, and **going over is not writing, and there is no column in that register that says how many times a row has been gone over, and there never has been.**

A press in a wall in a corridor behind a court on a flood terrace, four hundred and thirty miles down the water, fourth press, and a key in a person's hand, and a copy of a row in a bag in a city four hundred and thirty miles from the wall.

**And one of three things had, one of three refused before anybody asked, and one of three not yet understood, and about four hundred and thirty miles of road between a person and a door, at about six days each way, out of a wage of seventeen shillings and a penny the week, with no fund and no form for a fund.**
