# Chapter 164: The First Fortnight of a Season Nobody Agreed To

On the eighth day of the second fortnight the work went on.

That is how Sefa Ild put it and it is the plainest sentence anybody has used about that bank in four months, and there were people on the bank when she said it and there were more on it in the afternoon, and the water was two feet down on a bank eleven feet high with about four hundred feet of new facing on the outer face, and nobody had agreed to be there.

The first season of the Slade rotation began on the second of the fifth month of next year and a season is about ninety days, and ninety days from the second of the fifth month of next year is a day in the first week of the eighth month of next year, and **no person put that in a sentence.** It arrived by arithmetic.

Arithmetic is not a notice.

---

Tavi Venn is seventeen. She has been on the Reed Cut bank since she was old enough to stand on a platform. She is on a written engagement that begins on the first of the fourth month of next year, four days a week, forty-five pence a day, with a copy in a satchel and the same notice as everybody else, and she is not thanked and she is not going to start.

She had asked, in a bay, at the seventh hour on the review day, *and who is going to ask us again*, and nobody in that room had answered it, and that is what a room does with a question like that, and she had written it on the back of a leaf in her own book with the day against it.

On the fourth day of the second week of the eighth month she asked it again, on the platform at the Reed Cut gate, at about the sixth hour, with the whole of the evening's crowd on it.

She asked it the way she reads a list of works, which is to say without a word of anything around it, and she did not say that anybody could go home, and she was not going to.

---

The rail at the Reed Cut gate is a piece of iron with a curve in it that somebody's grandfather set into the platform so that a boat has something to hold against while it is being worked.

The schedule is on it and the list of works is beside it, and the schedule is on good paper and about eleven inches by nine and folded once across the middle, and it carries the water office's device, which is not a name, and a memorandum number, which is a number of a book, and a foreman's name at the foot of it, which is the only person's name on the whole sheet. **It is on four nails and there are the holes of an earlier set in the rail, and the four old nails are still in the rail, and nobody has pulled anything down.**

It is the fifth term of a ruling that is posted in two places, and a list of works is a list of works because the district works are a list of works, and a list of works is the only kind of sentence in this empire that a person can act on the same day.

It does not say a season. It does not say a year. It does not say when anybody has to be anywhere.

"Read that part again," said a man of about forty off the second boat.

"I did not say a part. That is the whole of it and it is the whole of it every week and I read it every week. "Tavi Venn turned round. "You want it slower?"

"I want to know what happens at the end of it."

"**Nothing happens at the end of it.** It is a list of works. That is the whole of what it is for. "She had the slate in her other hand and there were people in front of her who could not read a paragraph and others who could and had stopped pretending they could not. "That is the fifth term of a ruling and the fourth term is a signature and I am not going to say anything about the fourth term because I have been asked not to, by a person who has been right about it. **A list of works has a day against a name and a name at the foot of it and that is the whole of the machine, and the machine is the only thing on this bank that anybody can act on the same day, and it is about four hundred and forty foot long and it has a nail in it.**"

Nobody took it off the rail.

---

Here is what the fortnight found out, and it took four days and half a bay and the finding is not dramatic and is the whole of the second movement of this season.

A duty is found to have stopped when somebody stands in a room and does not do it. That is how about nineteen people are off a rotation: they said no at a door, in a room, in front of a person, and the person said no on their own behalf and the duty stopped.

**There is no other way.** There is no column, and there is no form, and there is no notice that ends a duty, and the only instrument in this empire that can stop a duty is a person standing in a room not doing it.

Which means three things, and the bay worked all three out in a fortnight and got the first one right.

**One.** About four people who cannot read a paragraph have never been in that room. Not once. Not in the first asking and not in the fortnight. So their duties have not been shown to have stopped and have not been shown to have started, and there is no column for either, and about nine people know it, and that is nine people and not a list.

**Two.** A no with a reason in it is in a book, and a no with no reason in it is in a doorway. The nineteen said no with reasons and the reasons were written down at the asking, in a book, with the day. **The reason they said no with a reason is that somebody held a book and said *say the reason and I will write it*, and the reason the four were never asked is that nobody stood in front of them with a sheet.**

**Three.** And this is the one that nobody had thought of and it took a girl of seventeen four days to think of, sitting on a platform in the cold: the notice on her engagement is the same notice as everybody else's, and it does not contain a season.

She took it out of the satchel at about the seventh hour and read it out on the platform, all of it, twice, the second reading slower, to a boat and a crowd.

It said four days a week. It said forty-five pence a day. It said that either side could give a week's notice.

It did not say a season. It did not say when it ended. **It did not say a day at the end of anything at all, and the only date on the whole of it is the date it begins, and a date at the beginning of a thing is not a date at the end of it.**

"So the season ended on the review day," said the man of about forty, "and not before."

"The season ended on the review day because ninety days is ninety days. "She folded the notice and put it away. "**Nobody told us.** The water office did not tell us. The Company did not tell us. A sheet of paper told us by being the wrong shape, and half of us worked that out and half of us did not, and there is no form anywhere for saying that a duty has finished and here is the form."

---

On the seventh day she took the slate and a stub of chalk and walked up the lane at the north of the ford with the intention of doing the one thing that fortnight had left anybody able to do, which was to go to four doors.

She got to the second one.

She stood on the step with the chalk in her hand for about as long as it takes to read a paragraph out loud twice and she did not knock, and she came back down the lane and put the slate on the bench in the middle of the bay and said so.

"You went up there to ask them."

"I went up there to ask them."

"You got to the second door."

"I got to the second door."

"Why not."

Tavi Venn looked at the chalk.

"**Because they have not been asked once,**" she said. "Not in the first season and not in this one. And if I go up there in the second week of a season nobody agreed to and I ask them whether they will do a thing they have never been asked whether they will do, then what has happened is that they have been asked. And somebody will write the day against it. And in about four years there will be a book somewhere with a day against a name in it, and the day will be the second week of a season that nobody agreed to, and the person who wrote it will be a girl of seventeen who is paid forty-five pence a day to read and was not paid to come up that lane."

"And that is bad."

"**That is not bad.** That is not allowed. "She put the chalk down on the bench. "A person can be asked and can say no, and the no is worth something and it goes in a book and it stops a duty, and that is the only machine in this empire that stops anything. And the machine works by somebody standing in front of another person with a sheet. **It does not work by a person being written down after the fact, and if I go up there now then in four months a person will look at that day and think somebody asked them, and nobody asked them, and the whole of this matter is about a machine that puts names in books so that the names can never be asked anything, and I am seventeen and I am about to do it with a piece of chalk.**"

Halla Wray said, "Then how do they get asked."

"They get asked when there is a season they have agreed to. That is the whole of it and it is not fair and I cannot make it fair with a slate. "Tavi Venn put her hand flat on the bench, which she has done once before, in a bay, four months ago. "And if there is not going to be a second season that anybody agreed to, then they are not asked, and that is the answer, and the answer is bad, and I am the person who has to say it out loud because I am the one who is not going to do it quietly."

Nobody thanked her for that either, and she had said in advance that nobody was to.

The slate went back on the nail over the bench in the middle with a number on it and no names under it, and it is not a record and it is not a book and in about four years it will be washed.

---

The boy of fifteen who cannot stand on the leg was on that platform on the fifth day and was not asked to do anything he could not do and has not been asked and nobody is going to ask him, and the whole of what happened to him is that the ground was flat and somebody noticed.

A girl of eleven stands on that platform every evening with a pail. She cannot read a paragraph, and nobody is going to give her a reason for it in a second season either, and nobody ever has.

She was read to. That is the whole of it.

A woman of about seventy-one at the fourth house at the north of the ford was asked at her own door four times a year, exactly as she asked in writing, and she has said the same reason twice and has said she will not say it a third time, and nobody asked her a third time and is not going to.

Raff Ordway, sixteen, is on the list of works at eight pence a day and five for the boy, and somebody put him on it who did not put him in a box, and a foreman is a person and can be asked questions, and that is the whole of the protection anybody has ever been able to give him.

---

At the end of the second week of the eighth month of next year, four people wrote the finding down.

Sefa Ild wrote the price of a review in the third column of the bay's own ledger and put her own name at the foot of it. Rennick Adley wrote the names in a book with no heading, and the third column of a hard-backed ledger with no heading over it, and about a hundred and forty feet of ditch on the south side and a scour at the third-of-the-way mark that is on the rotation's list now.

Tavi Venn wrote the question and the answer to the question in her own hand on the back of a leaf, with the day against each line, and the leaf went into the book and the book went into the satchel, and it is findable, and about four years from now a person with fourpence can have a copy of it out of a book with no office on it.

And Halla Wray, fifty-one, foreman, second on the list, wrote one line at the foot of the sheet in the bay, with the day on it, in her own hand, and did not read it out, and it says:

> *Second season, eighth month of next year, running. Nobody agreed to it. Nobody has been asked. Four not asked, first season or this. Nine of about forty-one did not come to the review. A duty is found to have stopped when a person stands in a room and does not do it. — H. Wray, foreman.*

**A list of works is a list of jobs with days against them and a name at the foot of it, and that is the only kind of sentence in this empire that a person can act on the same day, and about four people in ninety-one households cannot read the one sentence that is on the rail in front of them.**

**And a guarantee under the fire-and-water device stands offered and unanswered at no charge on that bank, answerable at any time afterwards by a person answering in its terms, and there is no such person and there is not going to be one, and about sixty children under sixteen are inside it, and inside a guild's blank list, and inside a rotation they have not been asked about, and have been told about none of it.**

**And a reader of seventeen is on a written engagement and is paid forty-five pence a day and is not thanked and is not going to start, and this is the last season in which anybody on that bank can ask her something and be answered for nothing, and she has worked that out herself and has not told anybody.**
