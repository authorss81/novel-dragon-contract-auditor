# Chapter 200: A List Of Works, and Nineteen Who Say No Again

The second season was asked for on the fourth day of the last week of the seventh month of the year after, in a bay, standing up, by a man who is a book-keeper and who said out loud before he started that he had been asked to ask and had not volunteered and that if anybody wanted to know who had asked him they could ask him after and not before.

Rennick Adley is thirty-eight. He has been keeping the books of the Slade Cut Company in a hired stone store off the Slade since the second month of next year, and the Company's whole money is four shillings a year and all of it is the rent of the building. He is the man who put the name of that company into a form in about four seconds in the second day of the second week of the fourth month of the year after, because the box says the person, institution or thing engaged in the matter and there was nothing else to hand.

He has a factor's letter of four lines on a bench in that store which has not been answered and is not going to be answered, and **About nineteen people who said no with a reason in the first season are still behind that bank, and this is the first week since the first month in which any of them has been asked anything at all.**

---

The list of works was read out and a man was first on it.

He had asked, on the fifteenth of the seventh month of a year that is not this one, to be asked last, and he was written first, and he has never corrected it, and the reason he has never corrected it is that a list of works is read out and a name at the top of it is the one everybody hears, and he wanted to hear it.

**Kem Ostry is a carter.** He works the Slade to the mill wall and back with a cart of no particular description and he has no employer of record, and he has been paid at a guild rate of one shilling and sixpence a day for a barrow of stone, a man and a boy, since the fourth month, and his name has been in nobody's column anywhere.

He said no at the gate in the first season, in the same words as about sixty other people, and he gave a reason, and the reason is that a name at the foot of a list of works means somebody comes and finds out whether it is working every fortnight for a season, and that he had been the one watching since the fourth month and would like it stopped.

He was not argued with. He was not asked a second time. The reason was entered because a person is entitled to have the reason they gave written down in the place where the no is, and not in a letter.

He was not asked a second time either. That is the finding and it took him about four seconds to arrive at it, standing at the front of a bay at about the sixth hour, and the finding is that nobody had asked him, so nobody had to ask him again, and the reason nobody had asked him is that a person who is owed a wage cannot be asked about a season, because asking is a conversation and a conversation with a person whose wage is held is a conversation with a person who is losing money by having it.

**"You have not asked me once since the first month," he said, "and I have been waiting for you to, and I want it noted that I have been waiting, and that is not a complaint and I am not going to make it one."**

"Noted," said Rennick Adley, "and I did not ask you because I am not allowed to, and I am not allowed to because I am the one who put a roof in a box on the second day of the second week of the fourth month, and a man in an office read a definition out to a mason in a corridor in the third week into the fourth week of the third month, and both of those are the same sentence said by two people in four hundred and thirty miles, and I have carried mine since the second day of the second week of the fourth month without anybody to hand."

---

He said it again, and he said it in the same words, and about nine men in three gangs heard it, and the reason went in the book in the place where the no is.

Then the rest of them, one after the other, out loud, in a bay, in about four minutes, and **about nineteen people said no again.**

Every one of them gave the reason they gave the first time. None of the nineteen reasons was the same as any other of the nineteen, and not one of the nineteen was a reason about the weather or about the mill or about money, and Rennick Adley wrote all nineteen down in the third column of the bay's own ledger with the day on each one, and read the whole page back twice, and the second reading was slower and was the one he did not skip.

Nobody in that bay has ever asked him where he got the double reading from, **and he has been asked twice about it by a woman of thirty-four four hundred and thirty miles up this river and has not told her and is not going to be asked.** The first reading is for the words and the second reading is for the people who did not have them the first time.

Then it was read out, all nineteen, out loud, in a bay, and it took about four minutes, and Rennick Adley read the reasons and not the names, because a name in a room is a thing that can be carried out of a room.

The reasons were these, in the order the people had given them, and every one of them is in a book about eleven feet by fourteen off a dyers' lane in a furnace town about four hundred and thirty miles up this river as a heading over a column.

*My boy is on a list of works with a day against it and somebody comes and looks at it every fortnight and I have had that twice.*

*I am the one who holds the book and I will not hold a book that has a season in it.*

*I have not been asked anything in nine years and I am not going to be asked in the first week.*

*I said no once and the reason went in a book and nobody argued with me, and I would like that to happen twice.*

*I am paid by a man who has not got four shillings a year and he would have to find it out of me.*

*The last time there was water in it I was the one who went out on the low side with a rope and I am not doing it on a paper.*

And the nineteenth, which was a woman with two children and had come down the lane in the rain, and she gave it in four words and it is the shortest of the nineteen and it is the reason the other eighteen are believable.

"I have to be home."

Somebody at the back said, "That is not a reason."

"That is a reason," said Rennick Adley. "**A person is entitled to have the reason they gave written down in the place where the no is, and not a second question, and not an opinion from the back of a bay. I have been keeping books since the second month of next year and I have read every form in this store and there is not one page in any of them that says a person has to give you a better reason than that one.**"

Nobody gave her a second question. She went home at about the seventh hour and her name is in the third column of a bay's own ledger with a day on it and no reason attached to it beyond the four words, because the four words were the reason.

---

Nobody argued with any of the nineteen. Nobody sent anybody to anybody. And a refusal is a thing a person is entitled to make once and have it kept and not be troubled about it again until the season comes round, **and the season came round and about nineteen people said no again and they are still behind the bank.**

About four people in that bay cannot read a paragraph. They have never been asked once. They were not asked on the fourth day of the last week of the seventh month. There is no column for them and there is not going to be one, and the reason there is not going to be one is that a column for them would be a column somebody would have to fill.

A girl of seventeen was at the back, on a written engagement, at forty-five pence a day and four days a week, doing the thing she is paid for.

She was not spoken to about the season. She did not ask. **A person who is paid to read is a person who can be told to stop**, and Rennick Adley has said that sentence out loud in this bay three times and has not sent anybody to anybody and is not going to. She was not thanked. She is not going to start. And nobody asked her to read anything that carries a rule, because there is no rule in that bay to carry and there has not been one since a man of fifty refused to rule a fourth column in a ledger in the second week of the eighth month of next year in about nine seconds.

---

Then the hold was read out, and then the other three things, and none of them has moved since the fourth month and none of them is going to be moved by a bay.

The hold is against the Slade Cut Company, being a thing, in the matter of a recorded claim entered and marked disputed, and in the matter of the works engaged in it. The wages of the persons engaged are held at the same rate and for the same period as the sum held, and a person whose wages are so held is not to be paid that held sum by any person, on account of the person of that family. The Company's whole money is four shillings a year and all of it is the rent of a roof.

**A guarantee under the fire-and-water device stands offered and unanswered at no charge on a bank about four hundred and forty foot long, answerable at any time afterwards by a person answering in its terms, and there is no such person and there is not going to be one, and about sixty children under sixteen are inside it, and inside a guild's blank list, and inside a rotation they have not been asked about, and have been told about none of it.**

A bill at Lowcross of nineteen pounds three and fourpence, unpaid, nobody liable, about a penny a day the real cost of it, and no line for a bridge in that fund and there has not been one in nineteen years, and four hundred and eleven people live on the far side and the district's return for four weeks says three hundred and eighty-eight crossings, and those are two figures and they are never the same.

The thirty-one of Mosswake are thirty-one, and there is a name and a door against every one of them in a returning officer's own hand, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for asking a returning officer what he wrote down after he had walked it.

A factor's letter of four lines, sitting on a bench in a hired stone store since the second week of the third month, requiring the name, place of custom and description of a person who may be served together with that person's consent, and saying that without both it cannot proceed and will not proceed, and the matter will stand against the works until one is given. It is not going to be answered. Every day it is not answered is a day nobody has been entered.

Nobody thanked anybody. Nothing was forgiven and nothing was paid back and nothing in that bay was reversed.

---

And here is the last of it, and it is four hundred and thirty miles up this river, and none of it is a question and none of it has been answered by anybody.

**The fourth counter of a notary's table in a city about four hundred and thirty miles from that bay, on a sheet of printed matter with three ruled boxes on it**, and the counter is a counter with an oak slab and a rail and a fee board of four things on it and none of them time.

The first box has a name in it. The name is the name of a person whose witness status was restored in the fourth week of the sixth month of the year after, entered with a day on it and a name at the foot of the entry, and the name is on a roll, and it is findable, and there is no way in this empire to put a name back a second time.

The second box has a name in it. The name is the name of a person who was entered as the representative of a family on the fourth day of the third week of the fifth month of the year after by a clerk filling a box the way the box is printed, and that person said no out loud in a room, with the day on it, and it did not work.

**Two of them are filled with the names of two people who are not the clerk.**

The third box is filled with a description, and the description says that there is a person in this matter who has not been asked and has not been found.

That is the whole of what is in the third box. It does not say a name. It does not say a hand. It does not say a lane, and it does not say a county, and it says the two facts that are true and both of them are findable and neither of them is a person.

**There is a fourth space on that sheet with no heading over it, where a heading would go, and nobody has ever put one there, and about two hundred and forty people have never known it is there.**

On the fourth shelf of a received room in that city there is a bundle marked *damaged, four items, uncollated*. It has been on that shelf for about twelve years. It is not opened. It is not named. **It is a location and it is not a discovery**, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to go and look at a bundle because they have found out that it is there.

And the door of that room is a door, and it is open, and nothing has come through it yet, and a person may now ask for a thing in a room, and about four hundred and thirty miles of road is what that costs, and there is no fund.

---

The entry was read back twice and the second reading was slower.

A girl of seventeen about four hundred and thirty miles down a river made that the rule of a bay in the second week of the fourth month, and it is now the rule of a counter, and nobody decided to make it the rule of a counter, and that is what a rule is.

There was one person in that room. She is a clerk of thirty-four and she has been at that counter eleven years and she is not a lawyer and she is a party to an instrument about a dead woman's name and she has said the price of it out loud in a room ten times and nobody has thanked her once and she did not expect to be thanked once and she would like it written down that she did not, because in about four years somebody is going to read that she said the price ten times and they are going to assume somebody heard it.

Nobody had heard it.

And at about the sixth hour on the fourth day of the last week of the seventh month of the year after, alone, at a counter with a rail on it, in a room with a pane out of the window, she said out loud, to nobody, in the plainest words she has:

"**A person who is not told has not been erased.** A person in a house that is shut has no address.

"Those are two different things and the first one is the harder one, and I have known the second one since the first month and I have never once been able to say it out loud without the first one coming with it, and I am saying both of them now and I am not going to leave one out to make the other one easier.

"This has been about the second one for about a hundred and forty years and it is about the first one since the first month, and a form printed in about nine hundred buildings has been asking the wrong person for about a hundred and forty years, and the only time it got asked at the right person it was in a kitchen with a pencil, and the person who asked it said the price out loud first."

Nobody thanked her. There is no form anywhere that requires it.

**And about nineteen people said no again, and they are still behind a bank, and about four hundred and thirty miles up this river a door is open and nothing has come through it yet.**
