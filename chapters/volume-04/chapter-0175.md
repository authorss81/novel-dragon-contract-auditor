# Chapter 175: Five Lines

What comes out of a house that has been shut is not a secret. It is a procedure, and the procedure is printed, and it is on five lines, and any person can read it and any person can do it.

Marek Kest found that out at about the second hour of the fourth day of the third week of the twelfth month of next year, standing four feet inside a door in a lane about four miles off the old river road, with a tallow candle in a tin and about a hundred and forty sheets of paper on a shelf that he did not count to the bottom.

A month ago, in a room in a city about four hundred and thirty miles up this river, a woman of twenty-four had said to him that the door in this matter is a person. He had written it down. He had not known what it meant until he was standing in a lane with a candle.

---

The top sheet of the stack had a heading over it about a foot long and he could not make the third word go with the first two, in the dark, at four feet, on the second day he had tried.

On the fourth day he had a lamp and the whole of the top sheet and about four hours, and the heading is this:

> *To be done in a room, and nowhere else.*
> *1. A person says a thing.*
> *2. A second person is in the room and is not a party to the thing.*
> *3. The second person writes the thing down, and the day down, in their own hand.*
> *4. The second person does not read it back to the person, and does not show it to them.*
> *5. The second person asks the person, before the thing is said, whether she is a party to it, and the answer is written here.*

There is nothing above it but the office's name, and the office's name is not the name of anybody, and the office's name is the reason this sheet exists and the reason it was printed about a hundred and forty years ago and the reason there is one of them on a shelf in about nine hundred buildings in this empire.

Under the heading, at the foot of the sheet, in a smaller hand than the heading and in a different ink: **the office's name again.**

And that is the whole of the form. Five lines and a heading and a name at the foot of it, and no form anywhere in this empire for it, and no copy of it in any book of forms on any shelf in any gallery, and the only person who has ever read it is standing in a lane with a lamp.

---

He read it four times. The fourth time he read it out loud to an empty room, which is a thing a copyist does and which he is, and which he has not done in about four months because there has been nobody to read it to.

Then he sat down on the bench, which is about the width of two men and has a groove in it about the length of a forearm, and worked out the fifth line for about nine minutes, and the working out was not difficult and it is the easiest thing anybody has worked out in this matter.

To ask the person whether she is a party to the thing, the person asking has to know what she is a party to.

The second person is not a party. **A party is served. A person who is not a party is not served. A person who is not served is not told things. And a person who is not told cannot be asked.**

So the second person cannot ask.

The fifth line is written for the second person. The second person is the only person in the room entitled to ask it. And the second person is the one person in the room who can never be told the answer to it, and therefore the one person who can never ask it, and it has been printed on the top of this stack for about a hundred and forty years and it is not secret and it is not hidden and it is not been cut off or blotted or taken out.

**It has never once been done.**

---

He did not shout and he did not say it to anybody, because there was nobody. He said it out loud twice, both times to the bench, and then he wrote four lines in his own book, in his own hand, with the day on it, and read them back twice, the second reading slower.

He did not write *I have found the mechanism.* He wrote what he had found, in the plainest words he could find, which are not plain, and he wrote the reason there is no eleventh column and no eleventh heading and no form:

*A witness is made by being asked and by not being made a party. A person who is never asked whether they are a party has been made one without anybody doing anything.*

---

The register is on the bench and it is a bound book about the size of a hymn book and it is heavier than a hymn book, which is a thing about bound books in this matter that he has now been able to measure twice in two buildings.

It has five columns.

The first is a name. The second is a day. The third is the second person. The fourth is what was said, and it is the widest of the five, and it is full, and it is full in about nine hands and every hand in it is the hand of a person who was not paid.

The fifth column has a heading over it.

And in every line of the fifth column, from the first page to the last page, the same thing is written, and the same thing is written in the same hand in every line, and it is not an answer to anything.

It is the office's name.

---

**The heading at the foot of the sheet is the office's name, and the fifth line of the sheet sits under that heading, and a line under a heading looks like a name.**

A second person keeping a register from a form does what a second person keeping a register from a form has always done. They copy the sheet. They copy the heading, because a heading is a heading. And they copy the line under the heading, because a line under a heading is part of the heading as far as the eye is concerned and nobody has ever had to think about it at about the ninth hour in a cold room with a lamp.

So the fifth line — which is a question, and which begins *the second person asks the person* — went into a column headed *told*, and it came out as a name, and it came out as the same name in every line, and it has been a name in every line for about a hundred and forty years.

And every register in this empire that is kept from this form says that every witness was told.

**And a register is a copy of a form, and a copy of a thing is a fact about the copying, and the copying is not the thing.**

---

He got the lamp up and put it on the bench and made himself do it properly, because a thing a man does in about four seconds at the fourth hour with a lamp is a thing a man does in a hurry, and this one is the whole of what he came up the lane for.

He went to the first page. The fifth column on the first page has the office's name in it in a hand of about a hundred and forty years old, and the line beside it, in the fourth column, in a hand of about the same age, is about four lines long and it is a thing a person said in a room.

He went to the last page. The fifth column on the last page has the office's name in it, in a hand of about twenty years old, in ball, badly, by a person in a hurry. The line beside it is about two lines long.

**Nobody has compared a register to a form in a hundred and forty years, because comparing is work, and the work is not anybody's job, and a person who has been copying the same thing for a hundred and forty years is the last person in the world to think of doing it differently.**

He wrote that down too. He wrote it in his own hand with the day on it and he wrote the number of pages, because a number of pages is a fact about a book and not a fact about anybody.

---

And then he came to the line.

It is on a page about a third of the way in and it is the only line in the whole of the register that has no day in the second column.

That is not a gap. Every other line in the register has a day, and the days run in order and they are in five or six different hands, and the running of them is the only proof anybody has that the register has been kept by people and not by a machine, because a machine does not miss a day.

A person misses a day.

**There is no date printed here and there is no age printed here and there is no number of years printed here, and the reason is not that the page is shy. The reason is that the page has never had one and does not know one and there is nobody left alive who could put one in.**

The first column has a name in it. Marek Kest read it and read it again and then put the lamp down on the bench about a foot away from the book so that the light would not be on it, which is a thing a person does with a book they are not going to steal from, and he noticed himself doing it.

The name is **Ilyra Kest.**

The third column has a name in it and it is a different hand and it is a woman's hand and it is not the same person.

The fourth column is full and it is about nine lines and it is the only thing on that page that anybody ever wrote on purpose.

---

He read it.

He read it standing up, at about the fifth hour, in a room about four feet deep and nine feet across with a lamp on a bench and a floor of lime two inches deep, and a person outside in a lane about thirty feet away doing a wall and not knowing what was being read and not asking.

He read it once.

And then he did not read it a second time, and the reason is in his own book with the day on it and it is the correct reason and it is the whole of what he has learned in four months:

**A man who reads a document in a room in a house that is nobody's, to himself, with no witness, in a lane four miles off a road, and then says out loud what is in it, has put himself in that room. And a person who has been in a room is a person a book can be true about. And a book can be true about him and about what he said in there and about what he thought, and there is no way in this empire to un-say a thing in a lane, and a copy of a document is a fact about the copying but a person who was in a room is a fact about the person, and the copying is the part that can be checked.**

So he read it once, and he wrote nothing about what was in it, and he stood there for about nine minutes, and then he said one sentence out loud into an empty room, which was not a fact and was not a document and was the only sentence he was going to say in that lane.

He said: "You wrote it down and you were not told what it was for."

---

He did not ask what she was looking at when she died.

The fourth column of that line is about a family and it is not about anything else. It is about a house and a boy and a person who was not asked where the idea came from and did not say. **There is nothing in that register about what she was working on, and there never was, and the only person in this empire who could put it in a sentence is dead, and that is not a mystery and it is not a sealed thing and it is a woman who died in a building in a city about four hundred and thirty miles from here.**

He wanted to ask it so badly that he put his hand flat on the bench twice before he noticed he was doing it.

He wrote four words in his own book, in his own hand, with the day on it, so that a hand could be compared against that hand for the next forty years, and the four words are the only four words in this matter that he wrote because of what he did not do.

*I did not ask.*

---

He did not take the register out of the house and he did not take a leaf out of it and he said out loud why, once, to the lane, because there is nobody else to say it to and a man who works out loud in a lane for about four hours stops noticing.

There is no custody entry for this house and there never will be. **A custody entry wants a person in charge of a thing, and nobody has ever been put in charge of this building, and there is no form that makes a person be in charge of a thing, and the absence of an entry is not a gap. The absence is the record.** He had that in a corridor in the second week of the tenth month of next year, from a man of sixty-one or two who had walked about nine miles to a lock to give away a piece of paper with four lines on it, and it is the reason he is writing four words in his own book and not four lines on somebody else's form.

So he copied nothing out of the book. He copied the five lines of the form onto a sheet of copyist's paper, off the top sheet, with the lamp on it, in his own hand, with the day on it, and signed it, and **a copy of a thing is a fact about the copying, and a copy of a form that is printed in about nine hundred buildings is worth exactly what a form is worth, which is that anybody may have it for nothing, and that is the point of it.**

And the finding he took out of the lane, in his own hand, dated, signed, findable in about four years, is four lines long and it is the whole of what this batch knows:

*A witness is made by being asked and by not being made a party. To ask, the person asking must know what the person in the room is a party to. The person the asking is written for is not a party, and is therefore not served, and is therefore not told. The fifth line cannot be done by the person it is written for. It has been printed for about a hundred and forty years and has not been done once, and every register kept from it says that it was, and the register is a copy.*

He folded the sheet into four and put it in the inside of a coat that already had three things in it, and went out into the lane and shut the door behind him, and did not put the mortar back.
