# Chapter 166: A Document Given at a Lock

The man at the lock was about sixty-one or two and he had walked there.

That is the first thing anybody found out about him, and it was found out because a man of thirty-eight said it before anybody asked, and the man of thirty-eight said it because the lock is about nine miles from anywhere and there is a cart road on the other side of the water and the cart does not come this way.

"You walked."

"I walked. "The man with the burn-scarred hand shifted the parcel under his arm. "**A carrier would have written it down.** And a document that a carrier has carried has an entry against it somewhere, and there is nowhere at a lock to keep an entry, and I have been about sixty years in a trade where the entry is the thing and I am not having one made about this."

He had come on the towpath on the fifth day of the second week of the ninth month of next year, and he had not been asked to come, and nobody in this matter knew he was on the road, and he had not sent word ahead, and the reason he had not sent word ahead is that a message is a thing a person can be shown.

---

He had said his name at a card in a window once, in a city four hundred and thirty miles up this river, on the eighteenth of the third month of last year, because the card says what the room is for and he had read it on his way up.

**Oren Tal.** He is a scale notary. He is not one any more. There is a fire behind the whole of that and it was not his and he was in the room and he got out of it and the hand says so, and he has never once said to anybody what he was doing when he got out of it, and he is not going to now.

He had said one sentence out loud in that room in the last year — that the old office of First Custodian was supposed to be temporary, and that the word is in the operative words, and that every office that has ever widened that office has done it by not putting the word in the sentence.

He had refused a question in a form. He had left a copy of a schedule of the term, made about forty years ago, in a copyist's hand whose name he would not give. He had asked not to be thanked, and said he would leave if anybody thanked him, and a man had not thanked him and had said afterwards that he was not obedient, he was obeying the fact that there was no form for it.

**Nobody asked him a single question about the only thing either of them had come to ask.** A man of thirty-eight and a woman of twenty-four stood at a lock for about four hours and did not ask him, and the not-asking took the whole of the fourth hour and cost one of them something she has never been able to put a price on, and he noticed it before either of them said a word about it.

---

He sat down on the bench outside the hut, which is the only bench, and he put the parcel on his knee and he did not open it for a while.

"I have brought you a form," he said.

"You have brought us a piece of paper."

"**A form.**" He got it out. It was a single sheet, folded twice, and it had been folded twice before that, and the folds had gone soft. It was written on the back of a sheet in a hand of about sixty and it was in ink and not a print. "It is a return of an impression. A scale notary's practice kept one, because a scale instrument is a thing that has to be accounted for and its custody is recorded, and a record of custody is a return and a return has a shape, and the shape has been the same for a hundred and forty years, and I have never seen it printed and I have seen it in about nine offices."

He put it on the bench between them, and did not open it flat, and put his own hand flat on it.

"Read it. Do not take it off the bench."

The man of thirty-eight read it, and then a woman of twenty-four read it over his shoulder, and it said four things and there were ruled lines under each of them, and the day at the top of it was the day the man of sixty-one or two had written it, and not the day of the thing the form was for.

> *Return of an impression, and of the keeping of it.*
>
> *The thing:*
> *The person in charge of it:*
> *The day it was taken:*
> *The signature of the person who took it:*

"That is the whole of it," said Oren Tal. "Four lines and about a hundred and forty years."

"Where is the fifth line," said the woman of twenty-four.

"There is no fifth line. There has never been a fifth line. **And there is no line on that form for a body, and that is not a decision anybody made.**"

---

He said that last part twice, and the second time he said it slower, and it is the only time in four hours that he said anything twice.

"A body is a word with a seal on it," he said. "A body cannot answer for itself. A body cannot be instructed, because nobody has authority over a body's conscience, and a body cannot stop, because a body does not know it is doing anything, and it will go on doing it after every single person in it is dead. **And that is why there is no line on that form for a body, and the reason nobody has ever printed the reason is that everybody who has ever used that form has known it without being told, and a thing everybody knows without being told is a thing nobody thinks about.**"

"Somebody printed a line for a person," said the man of thirty-eight.

"Somebody printed a line for a person and a person can be instructed and a person can stop, and the office in your river district is nine years into finding that out and it has not asked anybody's advice. "He turned the sheet over with two fingers of the hand that still works. "**A recorded custody entry is a thing a person writes down, and not a thing a court grants and not a thing a form produces.** And the whole of what it requires is a person who was in the room."

The man of thirty-eight read the four lines again.

"This is a receipt for a person," he said.

"It is a receipt for a **custody** . "The old man said the word as though it had a door in it. "Four hundred and thirty miles from here there is a press in a wall in a corridor behind a court on a flood terrace, and it has been in that corridor longer than anybody in that building has been alive, and there is a key in a person's hand, and there is a bound schedule in it, and the schedule does not travel and cannot be taken out."

"I know that."

"Then you have worked out the second half of it and I am going to say it anyway because you have not said it and I have about nine minutes. "He put the sheet down flat. "**A person in charge of a bound schedule in a press is a person.** A press is not a person. And a person cannot be appointed to it by anybody who is not standing in the corridor."

"So what happens to a press nobody stands in."

"**For a hundred and forty years, nothing has happened to it.** And if nobody has ever been put in charge of it then there is no entry, and there never has been one, and the absence is not a gap. The absence is the record."

Nobody said anything for a while. A barge went through and the water under the lock made the kind of noise that a thing makes when it is doing the only thing it does.

"That is the whole of what I have got," said Oren Tal, "and it is a form with four lines on it, and I made it out in my own hand off a memory of about sixty years, and it is not evidence of anything and it is not a copy of anything and there is no office in this empire that would accept it, and I have given it to you anyway, and I am not going to tell you what to do with it."

"Then what do you want us to do with it."

"I do not want anything. That is the whole of my method and I have said it in a room in a city before and I am saying it on a bench. **I want you to do exactly what you have been doing, which is to hold it, and to decide for yourself what it is for, and to put it where it can be found.**" He got the parcel off his knee and gave it away. "And I want you to understand that this is the second thing I have given away in about sixty years and the first one is in a file of a man's own in a city four hundred and thirty miles up this river, and it is the only thing he kept, and he wrote the rest of what I said down in a book he keeps at home with the day on it, and I did not ask him to."

---

**And then he refused one word, and he refused it before anybody asked, which none of the three of them expected and which changed the shape of the last half hour.**

"Mr Tal—"

"There is a question coming and I have had it in a form for about a year and I know the shape of it, and I am going to get in front of it. "He put his hands on his knees, both of them, and the left one does not straighten all the way. "**You are going to ask me a first name.** I am not going to say it. And I am going to tell you what I am doing while I do not say it, because the second time I did this I let a room find out on its own what I was refusing and it took four months off both of us."

The woman of twenty-four had not moved. She had a satchel and a book with no office on it in it and the pen out and she had not written anything for about nine minutes.

"A refusal is a document," she said.

"**A refusal is a document, and a document is findable, and there is no way in this empire to un-ask.** So say it back to me in those words, because I have never once in sixty years had anybody say it in those words while it was happening."

"You are refusing to confirm a first name and it is a document and it is findable and there is no way to un-ask."

"That is correct and it took you about four seconds and I am not going to thank you for it. "He turned his head away, about four inches, and then brought it back. "**And I am going to say the other half of it out loud, because you are both going to go away from this bench believing something and I would rather you believed the correct thing.** A refusal is not a denial. It is not a denial and it is not a confirmation. It is a document about a person who would not say a word, and every one of you is entitled to read into it whatever you have already decided to read into it, and that is your business and not mine, and I have watched three people in this matter do exactly that and do it better than I could."

"You are saying she is your mother."

"I am saying **no** , and I said it four years ago in a doorway in a building that smells of fired scale and I have not improved on it and I am not going to today. "He looked at the man of thirty-eight. "And you are going to write that down, because you have been writing everything down since the first week of the fifth month of next year and you are not going to stop now, and so here is what you write: a man of sixty-one or two was asked a first name and refused it, and the refusal is a document, and a document is not a denial, and a document is not a confirmation, and a person who has been refused once knows what a refusal costs, and that is not the same as being loyal, and I do not know the difference either and I have had about sixty years."

The man of thirty-eight wrote it down.

---

Nobody thanked him.

Somebody started to — the man of thirty-eight got about four inches into a sentence — and the old man stood up.

"No."

"It was going to be—"

"**If anybody in this matter thanks me I will leave, and I said that once in a room in a city and I am saying it on a bench, and it is not vanity and it is not the old man in the story.** It is that a thing given for nothing and thanked for is bought on the way out, and I have watched about nine people get bought that way in about sixty years and I am not going to be the tenth. "He picked up the empty parcel and folded it. "I will be walking back. I am slower going back and I am not taking the cart road and I would be obliged if neither of you said anything to anybody at a landing about a man of sixty-one or two having been on a towpath this week."

Then he said one more thing, to the woman of twenty-four, and it is the only thing he said in four hours that was not about the form.

"You did not ask me."

"I did not ask you."

"**You have been not asking me for about nine minutes and it is the most expensive thing anybody has done on this towpath in about nine years, and I am not going to be the one who tells you that you should have asked, and I am not going to be the one who tells you that not asking was loyalty either, because it was not loyalty, it was a refusal with a cost, and a refusal with a cost is a document, and you know what a document is.**"

He went up the towpath. It took him a long time to get to the top of the bank and neither of them followed him and neither of them said anything about that either.

**A form for a recorded custody entry, in a hand of about sixty, on the back of a sheet, with his own day on it and not the day of the thing.** A first name refused, entered, findable, and not a denial. A schedule of the term in a file in a city. And a person in charge of a press, who is a person, and a press which is not, and a corridor that has to have somebody standing in it.

**And about four hundred and thirty miles of road between the bench and the wall, at about six days each way, out of a wage of seventeen shillings and a penny the week, and there is no fund and no form for a fund.**
