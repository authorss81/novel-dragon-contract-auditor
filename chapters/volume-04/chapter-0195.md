# Chapter 195: About A Hundred And Thirty People, And A River Coming

Leda Rell is fifty-one and she is a magistrate of the Mosswake Roll Court and she has been one for nine years and was a clerk of it for fourteen years before that, and this is the first time in nine years she has sat down in a room that is not on a flood terrace, and she had four files with her out of about a hundred and forty, and she said the number out loud in the corridor before she went in, because a number is findable and a person who says a number can be held to it.

She is about four hundred and thirty miles from her own district. The four files are on the table in front of her. The other hundred and thirty-six are in a press in a back room on a flood terrace and she is not going to get them and did not try.

She is not a villain and she has never sent a person to a jail. She has a marble thing about a rule and about keeping people in roofs, and she gave it up on the third day of the fourth week of the fifth month of the year after, in about nine minutes, in front of a room, **and what she gave up was not the rule.**

---

They did not start with her. They started with the four files, and the four files were produced in the ordinary way by a person who is not in this, and then somebody asked her the question that a room asks a person who has brought four files out of a hundred and forty.

"Why four."

"Because I was asked what I could bring and I counted," said Leda Rell. "**There are about a hundred and forty of them and there is my own hand in every one and I have never in nine years taken one of them out of that press for anybody, and I brought four, and the four are about a river.** And I want it entered that I chose the four and I chose them for the same reason I have chosen everything for nineteen years, which is that I know which of them will hurt me and I have brought those."

---

Then they asked her about the river.

It was in the first of two, both in the same flood season, both chosen by hand, both with a day on them, and she can name the year and cannot name the file and said so.

"There were about a hundred and thirty people in front of me who could have been held," she said. "There was a river coming. I held about forty of them."

"Chose forty out of a hundred and thirty."

"I chose which forty."

"And why."

"Because a predictable claim in the right place stops an eviction in the wrong place." She said it without any weight at all, the way a person reads a thing out of a minute they wrote a long time ago. "That is the reasoning and it is in a minute in my own hand in that year and it reads like sense. It reads like the best sentence anybody has ever written about a river. And I have not improved it and I am not going to, and I would like it read out exactly as it is written because in about four years somebody is going to come in here and say a good reason was applied unevenly and I would rather the sentence were in the room than my memory of it."

It was read out exactly as it is written.

"And it is right," said Leda Rell. "**The reasoning is the reasoning every flood authority in every district in this empire uses and has always used. It is not a bad rule. It is a good rule. That is not the thing that is wrong with it.**"

---

Then there was a witness, and the witness was not asked to be one and had not come to be one.

He was about seventy-four. He had come up because a man in a room four hundred and thirty miles from his own district had sent him a paper with a day on it and a carriage paid for, and the carriage is not a wage and the paying of it is not a kindness and both of those facts are on the record.

"My name is Ludd," he said, and then he gave the rest of it, and a clerk wrote it down, and he watched her write it.

"You are one of the ninety," said Leda Rell.

"I am one of the ninety," said Wren Ludd. "I had somewhere to go. That is the whole of the reason and it took her a flood season to work out which ninety and it took me nineteen years to be able to say this in a room."

"Where was it."

"Four miles up a lane, on the low side, and it belonged to a man whose name he had to stop and think about and there was water standing in his ground from the second day of that week and there was nowhere on it for anybody to go. And I was on the high side of the same lane and I had a mother in the house and about nine feet of floor between the front door and the back."

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds, which is what a room does.

"He died in the fourth week," said Wren Ludd. "**I have not said that in a room in nineteen years and I have said it to about four people on that lane and I am saying it here because she is in front of me and I am not going to make her say it first.** I did not know him. He was a man whose ground had water in it. That is the whole of why I am sitting here and I would like the reason written down as the reason I am sitting here and not as a reason about her."

---

Leda Rell had it written down. She asked for it in those words and it went in with the day on it, and the witness was still in the room while it went in.

Then she was asked the second question, which was the one that had been asked of her before, in a room on a flood terrace, and she had answered it then and the answer was a sheet of about a hundred and sixty words and she had read it back once and not twice.

"In nineteen years," she said, "I have enforced continuation clauses against about nine hundred and forty people in this district. Not in one flood. In about a hundred and forty matters, I have taken an instrument, found a person, and held that person to a duty the person had inherited. **And in the same nineteen years I have not enforced them against about four hundred.**"

"Four hundred what."

"People I chose not to." She did not put her hand flat on the table. "The first of them could not be found afterwards, and the service says they were served, and no court in this empire has ever gone back and said a person was not there. The second of them are of a household and the household is not a person, and I entered *household* in the third line of about a hundred and forty files because there was a column for it and nothing else in the line would take."

"And the third."

"The third is the one I have never written down and I am writing it down now and it is the reason I am in this room four hundred and thirty miles from my own district with four files." She said it in the flat way a clerk says a thing and there is nobody in this empire better at it than a magistrate of nine years. "**About two hundred and forty people in front of me in the second of the two floods, and I held about nine of them, and I chose those nine because they were the nine who had water standing in their ground and everybody else had somewhere to go.**

"I chose. Both times, by hand, on a day, in my district, and the choosing is in my own hand, and the reasoning in the first one is the reasoning that every flood authority in every district in this empire uses and has always used, and that is right."

---

Then she said the thing, and it is the thing the whole of the last nine years of her life has been walking towards, and it took her about four sentences and she did not enjoy saying it.

"**A good reason applied unevenly by one person in a district for nineteen years is not a rule and cannot be defended as one.** A rule is a thing that is applied the same way each time. This was not applied the same way each time. I chose, and the reason I chose is good, and the second four hundred are the reason the first nine hundred and forty cannot be defended as a rule. I have known that since the sixth year and I have not said it in a room that was writing it down until this month."

The clerk asked her whether she wished the finding recorded in those words.

"Record it in those words," said Leda Rell. "**And record that I am not naming anybody.**"

"You are not naming anybody now."

"I have not named anybody in nine years and about two hundred and forty people in this district could be described and I have not described them and I am not going to describe them in this room, and I want the reason entered in the first person. The reason is the reason I gave the last time and it is not a new reason and it is not a better one. **A person who names a body in a minute becomes a person whose minute can be asked about, and a minute that can be asked about is a document.** If I put a name to one of them then in about four years a clerk will read a name and then read another, and somebody will ask why those two and not the others, and I will not be here."

---

Nobody asked her the question she was waiting for, which is the one about whether a finding entered against oneself on one's own motion is worth anything, and she volunteered it, and it is the reason she came.

"**A finding entered against a person by that person on that person's own motion names no person, is appealable by nobody, and is not a discovery and is not a seam.**" She said it in the room and then said it again to make sure it went in whole. "It cannot be discovered. It cannot be unseated. It cannot be carried somewhere else and used. It binds me and it helps nobody and it is entered in the record of a court that has never once in nine years been asked to enforce it against me, and I have made it mine so that it is here, and I would like it understood that I have not made anybody else's it."

---

---

Then she was asked what she wanted, out loud, in the room, by a person with a pen, and it is the fourth time in nine years anybody has asked her that and the first time the answer has been written down.

"Read the finding back," said Leda Rell.

They read it back. It is about four hundred words and it took about a minute and a half and it names her and it names her district and it names the two floods and the hundred and thirty and the forty and the nine hundred and forty and the four hundred and it says that she has named no person and why, in the first person, and at the end it says that the finding is not a discovery and is not a seam and is not appealable by anybody, and the words are in her own because she dictated them.

"Wren Ludd."

"I am here."

"**You have been in a room for about an hour and a half and you have said one thing about a man whose ground had water standing in it, and you have given your name into a record, and nobody has offered you anything and nobody is going to. Do you want anything.**"

And he thought about it, and he took long enough that the clerk stopped writing, and what he said was this.

"About four years ago I was owed a wage for a fortnight and a district took it out of a thing with no name on it, and I went to a window and a woman of thirty-eight would not take my name off a board, and she was right and I did not know she was right and I have known since." He put his hands on the back of the chair in front of him, which is not his chair. "I am not owed anything. I have been in this room for about an hour and a half and a magistrate has said out loud in front of witnesses that she chose to let me go, and I have been seventy-four years on this earth and that is the first time anybody has told me why I did not drown, and it cost her nothing to say it and it cost me the carriage and I would not have come without the carriage and I am not going to pretend otherwise."

"Nobody has thanked you," said the person with the pen.

"I did not come to be thanked," said Wren Ludd, "**and a thing that is written down is not a favour and I have found that out today and I have got about four hundred miles of road behind me and I am going home and I am not going to be asked about it again, and if anybody does ask me I will say the same four sentences and they will be the right four sentences and I will not improve on them.**"

He said the four sentences. They were about a lane and a low side and about nine feet of floor and a man whose ground had water standing in it, and nobody wrote them down because nobody asked him to, and he got up and went out.

---

The court recorded it. About nine hundred and forty people made answerable in nineteen years by choice; about four hundred not made answerable, by choice; a river, a hundred and thirty people, forty of them held and ninety not held; a witness in the room who had somewhere to go; a sentence read out in the words it was written in; and a magistrate who has named no person and has said why, in the first person, twice.

**The shift is in the record and it is not absolution and it is not a conversion**, and the difference between those two things and what has happened is that nothing in this room has made anybody safer. A person who has said out loud in a room that about four hundred people in her own district were let go by her own hand, on purpose, with a reason that reads like sense, has not been forgiven by anybody, and has not asked, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that requires it to be entered that she has not been forgiven.

Nobody thanked her. She is not thanked in her own court and has never been and there is no form anywhere that requires a person to be thanked, and she said in advance that nobody was to and nobody was.

And about four hundred and thirty miles down this river there is a foreman of fifty-one who has never once in nine years been asked to read anything by anybody at the district works, and a seat's paper arrived with her name at the top of it in the third week of the fourth month, and she has not opened it, and there is no form anywhere that says she has to.
