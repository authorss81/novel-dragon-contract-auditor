# Chapter 198: The Hearing She Is Owed

The paper came up the road in the third week of the fourth month of the year after and it came with a boy on a cart and not with anybody who knew what was in it, and it had a seat's device cut into the head of it and no office on the wrapper, and it had her name at the top of it in the ordinary way, and it sat in a box on a shelf in the shed at the Slade end for about eleven weeks.

Halla Wray is fifty-one and she is the foreman of the Slade Cut, which is an office with no office in it: it is a thing nine families agreed to about eleven years ago and did not write down and cannot produce, and she holds it the way a person holds a job that nobody is going to ask her about. Her term ran out in the third week of the third month. She has put her own name at the foot of three lists of works and it is the first name at the foot of anything on that cut in sixty years.

**She can read. That is not in dispute and has never been and is in nine years of a shed nobody's business.** It is the other thing about her that is not in dispute: nobody at the district works has ever once in nine years asked her to read anything.

---

The shed is about nine foot by eleven. It has a door that shuts, a bench along the north wall that four men can sit on, and a lamp that is not lit until about the seventh hour because there is not enough daylight in there to work by before then and there has not been for sixty years.

About nine men came into it on the second day of the first week of the seventh month of the year after, and they came because somebody put a word in the yard and not because anybody asked them, and two of them came late on purpose.

She knew why the two of them were late, and she did not ask, and she said so afterwards in the yard to the man who had come down the lane, and not to anybody in the shed.

"They said no in the first season," said Halla Wray. "Both of them, at the gate, in the same words anybody uses, with a reason, and the reason went in the book in the place where the no is, and they have not been asked since and they are not going to be asked. **They came late because they wanted to hear what I said before they decided whether to still mean it.** That is not a thing a person is supposed to be able to do and they have done it and I am not going to make them explain."

"Will they say it again."

"**If they say it again it is because somebody asked them again, and nobody is going to ask them again until the season comes round, and when the season comes round somebody is going to have to.** And that somebody is going to be me, because I hold the list, and I have been putting my own name at the foot of a list of works for nine years, and the day somebody hands me a season to ask about is the day I am asking a person who has already said no once."

"Forty-two years on this bank and nine years holding the only job on it that is written down nowhere," she said. "And there is no form in this empire that makes a person be in charge of a thing, and the nine families agreed it out loud about eleven years ago in a shed, and it cannot be produced, and there is nobody in this district who would know how to write it down. I would like that said."

He had not got a book and had not been asked for one. She said, "Then say it to the man of thirty-eight when you see him, and he will write it down, because that is what he does, and he will do it for nothing, and I am not going to thank him and you are not to either."

"I have not got his name."

"Everybody on this bank has got his name," said Halla Wray. "That is the whole of what a name at the foot of a list is."

---

Nobody sent her. Nobody asked her. There is no form anywhere in this empire for a person who comes on her own account, and she came on her own account, and the whole of what she wanted was to be asked something in a room where she had to stand there while she said it.

"I am going to be asked about the bank," she said, before anybody had said anything at all. "I have been asked about the bank five times this year and I said no five times and every one of the five was right, and I have said in this shed that I would rather be asked than not, and I have been asked twice and said yes twice, and I have refused to be asked a third time without being asked."

Nobody had said anything. One of the men at the back said, "Then ask her."

"I cannot," said the man who had come down the lane, "and I am not going to pretend that I can. **The reason I am in this shed is that a thing happened in a city four hundred and thirty miles up this river and I have read about it, and I cannot get there, and I am not going to send anybody.**"

---

So they asked her. It took about four minutes to arrange and it was arranged by a man who had never arranged anything in his life, and it was arranged in the ordinary way, out loud, in front of about nine men, and that is the whole of it.

**A foreman of fifty-one, in a shed, being asked, out loud, in a room she has to stand in, for the first time in nine years.**

She answered it in about four minutes and she did it as arithmetic, and there was no speech in it and there was nothing in it that anybody could put on a board.

"The bank is not mine. I have said so five times and I am not going to say it a sixth time differently. It is a thing nine families agreed to and it is not written down and it cannot be produced and that is why there is nobody to sue and that is why I can be the whole of it and I am not a person anybody can be angry with.

"The form came up the road with a heading over it and nothing under the heading. The person who filled it in filled in a thing, because the box says the person, institution or thing engaged in the matter, and a person fills a box the way the box is printed, and nobody has ever been asked what the words mean and there is no post in this empire whose job is to stand in front of a heading and ask. **I have been on this bank since I was nine and I have never once in nine years been asked to read a word of anything.** A man in a city has now found out the rate on a wall and I have known the rate on this wall since I was seventeen."

"You could have told him," said a man of about thirty from the second gang.

"I could have and I did not and I am not going to now," said Halla Wray, "because a person who is paid nothing can be asked, and a person who is paid nothing is also the only kind of person in this cut who can be told no by anybody. **If I start telling, then in about four years there is a piece of paper in this shed with a sentence of mine on it and a day on it and a name at the foot of it**, and I have watched a man of thirty-eight do exactly that with his mother's handwriting and I have read what it cost him, and I am not starting at fifty-one with a boy at home."

---

Then she gave the other three things, and she gave them because she had been asked and nobody had ever asked her, and she gave them flatly and in order.

"The second season began in the first month of this year and it is about the length a season is, and about forty-one people are on it, and nobody agreed it and nobody refused it and nobody signed anything, and the wages of all of them are held against a company whose whole money is four shillings a year and all of it is the rent of a roof. **That is the cheapest hold there is and it is not a villain's hold and it is an ordinary hold, and it is cheap because there is nothing in it to break.**"

"There is a factor's letter on a bench in the store up the road," she said. "Four lines. Nineteen days when it went on the bench and about forty now. It wants the name of a person who may be served and that person's consent, and it says that without both it cannot proceed and will not proceed, and the matter will stand against the works. It is not going to be answered. Every day it is not answered is a day nobody has been entered, and I know what a day of that is worth and I am not going to be the one who spends it."

"And about four people in that bay cannot read a paragraph," she said, "and they have never been asked once and were not asked a second time, and there is no column for them and there is not going to be one, and the reason there is not going to be one is that a column for them would be a column somebody would have to fill."

---

Then she said the sentence she has been carrying since the sixth month of last year, and she said it once, and about nine men heard it, and two of them had understood the first half of it before she had finished the first half.

"**A carriage is not a wage.** A thing that pays for a person to come and does not pay them for the day is not an offer, it is a way of counting how many rooms a person is in. I said that in that bay in the sixth month of last year to about thirty people, and it was the truest thing anybody said that month, and it has not been useful to anybody since, because being right about a thing is not the same as a thing being done about."

Somebody said, "You have said that before."

"I have said it once," said Halla Wray, "and I have not said it to this room. **There is a book-keeper on that bank who has wanted the other half of it since the third month and is not going to ask me for it, and the reason is that the day he asks is the day I stop saying the half I have got.**"

"You have never given me the other half either," said the man who had come down the lane.

"No," said Halla Wray, "and I am not going to, and it is not in this shed, and I have had it since the third month and I use it about four times a season, and it does not go into a room that writes it down, and you can put that in whatever you keep and I will not be asked about it again."

---

Nobody thanked her. She is not thanked in her own trade and there is no form anywhere that requires a person to be thanked, and she said in advance that nobody was to and nobody was, and the man who had arranged the asking did not thank her and said afterwards, in the yard, that he had wanted to and had been told not to and had understood why.

Then the shed emptied and she got the box down off the shelf, because it had been sitting on that shelf for about eleven weeks and it was in her way, and there was nothing in the world that had ever told her to open it.

She opened it.

It is one side of a sheet of good paper and it is not printed, and there is a seat's own device cut into the head of it, and it is set out in six numbered lines and a foot, and the foot is a seal, and there is nothing under the seal, and the second line asks that no inference of any kind be drawn from the absence of any name.

**Her name is at the top of it. Not at the foot. At the top**, in the ordinary way, in the hand of whoever sent it, and nobody has ever asked her whether she wanted it there and there is no form anywhere in this empire that requires anybody to ask.

The third line is the third line. She read it, and she read it a second time, and the second reading was slower, and the men who had not gone had come back in and were standing at the door.

> *Where a recorded claim is entered and marked disputed, and the family of the claimant holds no enforceable asset, the person of that family who is of age and resident is the family's representative in this matter, is to be entered as such in the office of the seat, and no further consent of that person is required.*

"That is me," said Halla Wray. "**That is me, and I am not a person of any family and I have never been in a book of anybody's, and that is because the job is not written down.** A company of nine families and forty-one people is a family, and the only one of us with a place of custom or of work in a book is the one they cannot write down either. I have been that for about eleven years without knowing it, and I have been unfindable on purpose, and now I am findable by accident."

"Who put your name on it," said the man of about thirty from the second gang.

"**Nobody put my name on it. Somebody put a class on it and the class has got one member**, and there is a woman in a city four hundred and thirty miles up this river who is a member of the same class, and she found out in the fourth week of the fifth month by a district clerk walking down a lane with a board, and she said no out loud in a room with a day on it, and it did not work."

She put the sheet down on the bench, face up, in the middle of the shed, where anybody who came in could read it.

**Nobody has been asked. There is no form anywhere in this empire for a person to be asked, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for a person to be let out of a thing that does not need them, and the word for what she has just been put into is the same word the mason's four days are under and the same word the wage of a dyer of twenty is under, and the word is *held*, and a person who is held cannot get up and go.**

Halla Wray got up and went, and left the sheet on the bench, and shut the door, and it is a quarter past the seventh hour and the lamp is not lit.
