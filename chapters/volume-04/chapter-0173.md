# Chapter 173: A Grant With a Name In It

A court direction can be made to change. What it cannot be made to change is a copy, and the difference between the two of them is the whole of what four weeks have cost a man of thirty-eight.

Tamsin Rook worked that out at about the second hour of the second day of the first week of the twelfth month of next year, at a clerk's window about four feet off the ground, with a form in her hand that had come back in four days from a court about a hundred yards and a bit further up the same terrace, and she had read it twice and the second reading slower, and she had not said anything about it for about nine minutes.

The form was a refusal. It was a refusal because it was right, and being right is what makes a document, and a document is findable, and there is no way in this empire to un-ask.

> *Refused. The thing asked for is bound, and the whole is held to be the whole, and a direction is not made for a part of a thing that is not severable. The applicant may apply again with the whole.*

There is no box on that form for a why. The word *bound* is in the middle of the sentence and there are about nine words on either side of it and it does not say what it means, and the clerk of fifty-five who has been at that court nineteen years has never had a form come back with a word in it like that and said so and asked not to be told.

She is twenty-four. She is a junior roll-keeper four bays from her own counter because a paper says so. She is a party to an instrument about a dead woman's name and she was made one in a city four hundred and thirty miles up this river in the last week of the seventh month of next year by filling in the third of three ruled boxes on the back of a blank, and there is no form anywhere for taking a party back out of a box, and she has said the price of that out loud four times and has not softened it once.

Her third line forbids her advice. Her fourth line says that a fact is not advice and she has said the words in rooms for about four months and has never once had them tested until a man of thirty-eight put a sheet in her hand in a corridor in the last week of the tenth month.

---

"You can apply again," she said. "Not that one. A different one, with a different thing in it, and I want to write it before either of you are pleased about it."

"Show us."

She had the sheet on the sill. It was her own paper and it was a copy of the first application and it had the first two of the three ruled boxes on it, filled, in a hand that sloped about nine degrees to the right, and the third box was filled in her own hand as well, and nobody had signed either of the first two boxes and that is not a thing anybody is going to be able to fix now.

"The first one asked for a leaf," she said. "A leaf of a bound schedule. And the court said a direction is not made for a part of a thing that is not severable, and it was right, and it told us the way round: **apply again with the whole.**"

"And the whole is in a cart going about nine days from here with a district at the foot of a list of works and four men on it who are working."

"And a person who is working is not a person in charge of a thing, and therefore the whole has nobody in charge of it, and a whole that travels in a cart for nine days is a whole that can be read on a road by anybody who stops it in a lane." She put the sheet down. "**The court is not refusing the man. The court is refusing the cart, and the court does not know there is a cart, and I am not going to tell it there is a cart, because the court is not the office that put the cart there.**"

---

The clerk of fifty-five read the second application twice and asked one question, which was whether the applicant was asking for the thing to be produced in the district or out of it, and was told that it was being asked for in the district, in the room where it is, in the presence of the person in charge of it, and that the thing was not to be removed.

He read it a third time.

"That is not a direction to produce a thing," he said. "That is a direction about a room."

"It is a direction about a room, and the room is in your district, and you have a court room on the ground floor of a low building on this terrace and a clerk's window at the back of it, and a court can direct that a thing in a repository in its own district be produced to a person in a room in its own district, and I have looked for the wording and it exists and it has a name."

"It has a name?"

"It has a name and nobody at that table has ever used it and I have been in this building eleven years and I have never seen it used once, and I know it exists because I went and found it in a book of forms on a shelf in the third gallery that is not a gallery any more, and it took me about nine minutes and I am telling you the size of the looking."

The clerk of fifty-five wrote it out himself, in his own hand, on the back of a form, and read it back, and Tamsin Rook made him read it back a second time and the second reading was slower.

> *Granted. The bound schedule, one volume, held in the fourth press in the embankment corridor behind this court, is to be produced to the applicant in the room in which it is held, in the presence of the person in charge of it, on the second day of the twelfth month of next year, at the second hour. The thing is not to be removed from the room or from the district. The person in charge of it is named below and is to be present.*

And under that, in a hand of about fifty-five:

> *I. Sarn, clerk, this court, by a key on a string and not by any appointment, nine years.*

---

It came back in four days.

Ivet Sarn was at the window when Tamsin Rook got there and she read it once, at the sill, standing, and she did not sit down and she did not go inside, and she read it a second time slower, and then she said the price out loud before either of the two of them could, in the flat way she says everything.

"**I have been served.** A person named in a grant is served. That is what a grant is: it is a thing that names a person and can be shown to her and can be shown about her to anybody else who has it. And served is party. And a party is a person a book can be true about, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for taking a party back out of a box, and I have known that for about four months because a woman of twenty-four has said it to me four times and I did not understand one word of it until about nine seconds ago."

Marek Kest said, "Ivet—"

"I am going to finish, and then you are going to be able to say whatever it is you are going to say, and it is going to be the wrong thing, and I would rather it came out after." She put the grant on the sill. "**I have been the only person in this matter who was not a party. That was not a privilege. That was the only thing I had, and I have had nine years of it, and it went this afternoon because a man of thirty-eight asked a court for a direction and the court did what courts do, which is to name a person.**"

---

"A direction that names no person cannot say to whom a thing is produced," said Tamsin Rook. "That is not a defect in the form. That is what a direction is. And you cannot have one of them and not have the other, and the one that costs is the one that names."

"I know what it costs her," said Marek Kest.

"You know what it costs a clerk," said Tamsin Rook, "and you have been in a room with a woman of twenty-four since the last week of the seventh month of next year and you are about to do the thing that everybody does, which is to start paying for somebody else's sentence with your own."

"Stop."

"**I am not saying it is not yours. I am saying the sentence is about her and I have said the price of a party out loud four times and I have just said it for a person who is not me, and the sentence works in both directions and I am asking for that to be noticed, and I am not forgiving anybody and nothing has been done that could be forgiven.**"

She put the grant back on the sill and squared it.

"I am going to say one more fact and then I am going to stop, and it is the fourth one and it is a fact and not advice." She did not look at either of them. "**A grant is findable. And there is no way in this empire to un-ask. And in about four years anybody with fourpence can read that a man of thirty-eight asked this court for a direction twice and was told no once and yes once, and that the yes has a woman of thirty-eight in it, and she is not the reason it was refused and she is the whole of what it cost.**"

Nobody said anything for a while and a barge went under the terrace.

---

The witness was the difficulty and it took about nine minutes and it took the same nine minutes it took in the first week of the tenth month of next year, because the arithmetic of it has not changed and nothing anybody has done since has changed it.

A party is served. A witness is asked. **A person cannot be both in the same room about the same thing, and that is not a rule anybody made, it is what the two words mean.**

"I am a party," said Marek Kest.

"You are a party, and you have been since a day went at the top of a form in the second week of the fifth month of next year, and I am a party for the same instrument, and neither of us can be in that room as a witness." Ivet Sarn wiped a part of the board above her own window that had nothing on it. "So the witness has to be somebody who has come about something else. There is nobody in this building who has not come about something else. That is not a difficulty. That is what the building is."

Then the man with the trowel and the barrow was in it.

He had been patching the terrace wall about thirty feet from her window for most of a week and he had been in the corridor every one of them, because a terrace wall in a wet season is a thing that wants doing every day, and there is no form in this empire that makes a person be in charge of a wall, and the way he came by it is that he is the only one left holding the trowel.

He was fifty. He had a boy of nine at home who was not in the room and was not named. He had come down the corridor with a barrow of mortar in it.

"I have a barrow," he said, before anybody had said anything to him. "I am not going to put it down outside and I am not going to be long."

"You are going to stand in a room for about twenty minutes," said Ivet Sarn, "and you are going to be told once, out loud, that being a witness does not mean you are to blame for anything, and that the whole of it is that in about four years there is one person who can say you were standing in a room."

"All right."

"That is the whole of what I am allowed to say to you and I have said it and I am not going to say it again."

"All right," said the man with the trowel, a second time.

His name is Nold Prawle and he is a mason and he did not give it until about the eighth hour, and he gave it then because a clerk asked him for it in the ordinary way, and it went into a book with no office on it with the day on it, and **he is owed nothing and he was not thanked and he said in advance that he did not expect it and he was not thanked harder than anybody else in the building.**

---

He is the first person anybody has asked to do a thing in this matter and said yes to in five words, and that is not a triumph and nobody made anything of it and the only reason it is written down is that in about four years it is the only sentence in the whole of this that will be true in a way that is pleasant.

And he is a person in charge of a wall, and that is why he can stand in that room at all, and it is also why anybody can ask him to do a thing about a wall on a lane four miles off a road, and **those are two different things on two different days about two different walls, and a person cannot be both in the same room about the same thing, and the reason he is useful to this matter is the same reason he is ordinary in it: nobody appointed him and he is the only one holding the trowel.**

---

And the copy.

Ten entries on four sheets of copyist's paper, in his own hand, dated and signed, in the inside of a coat on a flood terrace, and there is a second one of them now, in a clerk's hand, at a counter in a city about four hundred and thirty miles up this river, and a third of them in a bag in a coat, and all three are the same ten entries and all three of them are copies.

"A copy of a thing is a fact about the copying," said Marek Kest, "and the copying is not the thing, and I have had that in a book with no heading since the second month of next year and it is the first thing anybody ever taught me that I have kept." He did not take the four sheets out. "**The direction can be made to change. The copy cannot. And the copy can be read and it cannot be leaned on, and the direction was never the thing I wanted and the copy was never going to be the thing I wanted, and the difference between those two sentences is five journeys and about thirty days out of about forty-four pounds a year and there is no fund and there is no form for a fund.**"

"That is a fact," said Tamsin Rook, "and I am not going to do anything with it."

"I know you are not. That is why I said it out loud."

---

He put a choice in writing in her hand that evening with a day on it, in a room, and it is their rule and it is from the twelfth month of last year, and either of them may put a choice in writing with a day on it and the other must answer in a room, in words, with a date.

> *Two things. One of them is a room and one of them is a person, and I cannot tell you apart, and I am going to find out. — M. Kest, the first week of the twelfth month of next year.*

She read it twice and answered it in a room, in words, with a date.

"The rule does not apply."

"Because it is a person and not a work."

"Because it is a person." She gave the paper back. "**A rule with a day on it makes a dated thing against a person. And what you want me to do is stand in a room on a day. And a person in a room does not go in a book with a day on the end of it, because the day on the end of it is how a person gets turned into a duty, and that is the whole of the thing you have been fighting for since the first week of the fifth month of next year, and you have been fighting it on a bank four hundred and thirty miles down a river and you are about to do it to me.**"

He said, "That is four."

"That is four and it is the fourth anybody has made with it and I have kept the count in my own book and you did not know there was a count until just now." She put the paper in the inside of her satchel and did not take it out again. "You have never once put a person to me. It is the only thing about you that has been consistent for about four months and I have not said so and I am not going to."

"You have just said so."

"I have said it out loud once, in a room, with a date on it," said Tamsin Rook, "and that is not advice and it is not a thank you and I would like it entered in whichever book you are keeping that in."

---

She had a drawer in a room four hundred and thirty miles up this river with four envelopes in it, and one of them had been there since the first month of the year before last, and she did not think about it on a flood terrace in the first week of the twelfth month of next year.

That is the fifth time in two years she has not thought about it, and she is not going to start now, and it is not going to be a decision, and nobody in this batch of her life has ever been going to open it and nobody is going to open it now.

---

And a clerk of thirty-eight stood at a window about four feet off the ground with a grant in her hand that has her name in it and a second day of the twelfth month of next year on it, and there is a man of fifty with a trowel who will be in the room, and a man of thirty-eight who is a party, and a woman of twenty-four who is a party and who has said the price of a party out loud four times and once of them was about somebody else.

A grant with a name in it is a thing that is findable and a thing that is served and a thing that cannot be un-asked, and the whole of what it cost was not a man of thirty-eight's thirty days and not a clerk's key on a string and not a direction that came back with a word in it in the middle of a sentence.

The whole of what it cost was that a person who was not a party became one, on a form, in a court, about a room, and she was not asked, and she was the only one in the building who could be asked in the ordinary way, and nobody did, because **a person who is going to be asked to stand in a room ought to be told how long they have, and she asked, and she was told twenty minutes, and twenty minutes is a length, and a length is the first thing anybody has been able to give her.**
