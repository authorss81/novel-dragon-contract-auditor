# Chapter 152: What a Table May Hold

The second thing Marn Ottery had for a person who came up to the fourth counter of the Notaries' Table in Auremar was not on the board, and she had been keeping it for eleven years, and nobody had ever asked her for it.

"I have already said the first thing," she said, on the second morning of the third week of the fifth month of next year. "I said it on the back of a blank in the sixth month of last year and I gave it to a carrier at fourpence and I did not put it in a file, and it went up a towpath in a hand and not in an envelope, and you have it, because a copyist of twenty-four who is not a lawyer has read it out to me twice in this room. **What a table may certify, and what a table may not.** I am not saying it again. I have said it about four times in eleven years and twice of those were to myself and one of them was to a courier who had brought me something for nothing."

"Then what is the second thing."

"**What a table may hold, and for how long, and who is allowed to take a thing out of it.** Three questions. Two of them are about money and one of them is not about money at all, and the one that is not about money is the one nobody has ever asked me and I have been waiting eleven years for somebody to ask me and I am going to be honest with you that I would rather they had not, because I know the answer and the answer is bad."

She put her forearms on the oak and did not sit down.

"A hundred and forty years. That is what a table may hold. A thing brought to this counter in any year of the last hundred and forty is a thing that is still here, and that is not a rule anybody made, it is what a room with a press in it does. **Fourpence to take a thing out of it. In person. At this counter, or at whichever counter of the eleven the thing belongs to, and there is a form of words about which, and I will read you the form of words and it is nine words and none of them is a name.**"

"And who may take it."

"**A person. Only a person.** Either the person the thing is about, or a person holding a written authority from the person the thing is about, and there is a form for the written authority and it is the same fourpence and it has to be got first." She stopped for about two seconds. "**And there is no provision for anybody else. There has never been one. I have looked. I looked for one in my second year, on my own time, and I did not find it and I did not tell anybody I had looked, and I have never told anybody I had looked.**"

"Why not."

"Because a person who says out loud that they have looked for a provision that does not exist has told a room that the provision is wanted."

Marek Kest said, "By anybody."

"By anybody who has a reason to want it. That is the whole of the difficulty and it is not a difficulty anybody in this building has ever solved, and it is worse than it sounds, because the person who most wants a provision that does not exist is the person who is right about something and cannot get at it, and there is nothing in this room that can be amended because of that, and I am not going to pretend otherwise, and it is not going to be amended in my time."

---

The fourth line of the form at the foot of a scale instrument asks in what way the instrument touches you. It asks about one person. It has always asked about one person.

**It is not a form about a body and it is not a form about a household and it was never intended to be either, and in the hundred and forty years of this table no body has ever been able to put a person in front of it.**

"You are going to want to know what happens when somebody tries," said Marn Ottery. "So I am going to tell you, and then you are going to buy the wrong question off me on purpose, and I am going to let you, and I am going to tell you at the end that I have let you, because that is the fourth time in a month that I have done something at this counter that I have not entered in the day-book and there is a limit to how much of that there is going to be."

"The answer is given to the person who asked."

"**The answer is given to the person who asked, and the person who asked is a party from the day the day goes at the top of the form.** A party can be served. Everything from that morning forward, anything at all about that instrument, can come to a person at that address. And nobody tells you that when you buy a question, because nobody in this building is paid to tell you what you will be afterwards, and the price on the wall is a shilling and the thing you become is not on the wall."

"You said the day at the top may be before the instrument takes effect."

"About four times a year in this empire, and about nine of them in eleven years in my own hand, and it is used by people who have found out the price of waiting." She almost smiled and did not. "**The free thing on that form is the fourth line, and the free thing on that form is the part that changes what a person is, and it is free because nobody has ever been in a position to be charged for it.** And a person comes up this counter and pays a shilling for a piece of paper and fills in three lines about themselves and a fact about a document, and then gets to the line that matters and it is free, and so nobody in a hundred and forty years has ever had to think about what it costs, and neither have I, until a woman of sixty-four who cannot read a paragraph said a word about a word in a bay in the fourth month of last year, and I have not got it out from under me since."

"You do not like it either."

"I do not like either of the two times I have heard that sentence said, and they were said about two entirely different objects in about four weeks, and I would like somebody to find out what the two of them have got in common, and I have looked for about four days, and I am a clerk of a notary's table and not a person anybody is going to ask."

---

He bought the question on the second afternoon and it was the question she had told him he could afford.

It was about eleven weeks. It cost a shilling. She wrote the day at the top of a form in her own hand and turned it round and pushed it across the slab, and the rest of the form was blank, and the blankness was the point of the whole instrument.

"The fourth line is yours and not mine," she said. "I have written three lines and a day and I have not written the fourth, and if I wrote the fourth I would be a party to the question, and I have been a clerk at this counter for eleven years and I am not going to be a person an instrument touches in a room where I am the only door."

"That is not in the rules."

"**No. It is not in the rules and there are no rules and there is a fee board with four things on it and none of them is time and none of them is a person.** It is in me and I have been keeping it in me for eleven years and I would like you to notice that it is the first thing I have said to you that I cannot put in a form, and that it is also the reason you are now a person who asked, and that those two things came out of the same mouth in the same afternoon."

He read the form back to her. She made him read it twice, the second reading slower, and he did not know why and did not ask, and she did not tell him, because a rule she has not explained to a person is not a rule and it is a habit, and she has one habit and she is not giving it away in a public room for nothing.

"It is a question about the run," she said. "**What is the run, and what is in the row, and what does the row do.** That is what you have bought, and it is the right question for this counter, and it is not the question you came for, and I have let you buy it, and here is why."

"Tell me."

"Because a table may refuse a question and be entirely right. That is on the back of a blank in your book and I wrote it and I did not put it in a file. And a party may put the same question differently and be served inside four days, and the second version gets an answer, and the only defence anybody in this empire has against that is **a person who is in the room when the second version is written.**" She put her hand flat on the slab. "And there is no form for that. There is a person for that. And I have been that person for about four days without knowing it, because you stood four feet behind her in this room on the first morning and you were not going to say the thing that puts her in it, and I could see that from where I was standing, and nobody has ever been able to see that from where I was standing."

Marek Kest did not look round. Tamsin Rook did not move.

"Eleven weeks," said Marn Ottery. "First week of the eighth month of next year, and I will write the day on this counter when I know it, and you will come up and I will read it out, and if you are not here I will put it in the press and you can have it for fourpence whenever you like, and that is the whole of the arrangement and it is the only arrangement this room has ever made with anybody."

---

And on the second morning of that week he bought a blank as well, at a shilling, and did not fill it in, and she did not ask him why, and put it in the box under the counter with the other about four hundred of them.

**A form bought and not filled in is four pence of proof that the question was asked.** It is not a record, it is not a step, it will not be in anybody's file, and about four hundred of that building's small commerce is in that box and about two hundred and sixty-one of the fourth lines in it are empty. And none of the four hundred is a record of anything either, and I have not been able to work out how to say why, and I have had eleven years.

She put the shilling in the drawer and wrote the day against it in her own column on the back of the counter, which is the only honest place for a shilling she took for something that is not on the board.

Then she said it to the empty counter, because the counter is where she says things, and it is about the fourth hour and the room had gone:

"**A thing nobody is charged for is a thing nobody thinks about.** That is the second time. The first was in the fourth month of last year, to about thirty people in a bay, about a duty. The second was in the sixth month of last year, to an empty counter, about a form. And I did not know they were the same fact until about four days ago and I have not been able to put them next to each other since, and I do not like it, and I would like somebody to find out."
