# Chapter 130: A List of Works

The south end of the hired grain store at the Slade end is about eleven feet by nineteen and it has a floor a foot off the ground and a stove in it and a bench along one wall and a door that shuts. The book is on the bench along the wall, in a chest that came with the building, and the chest has a lid and the lid has a hasp and the hasp is not locked and has not been locked in six years.

**Rennick Adley is thirty-eight and has kept the book for six years and his aunt kept it for nineteen before him, and before her a man who could not write whose wife wrote the page.** The book has about nine hundred and forty names in it and no heading at the top of it and, until the sixth day of the third month, no name at the foot of anything.

The first leaf says what the book is and what it is not, with the day on it, and a line is ruled under the six years one man has kept it and there is no name under that line on purpose, because a book that says *nobody has taken this on yet* is a different sentence from *nobody will ever*.

The bay at the other end of the same building has a bench in the middle of it and a door that does not shut and about thirty people in it, some of whom have come for the fourth time in a month.

The second week of the fifth month is cold in a way the first month was not.

---

He had nine things to go in the book and he did them in the order he had learned in nine years, which is: **write down what you are reading, before you copy anything, and say who is not in the room.**

Then he did them.

The first was the rule of the meeting, entered a second time, with the day on it, in his own hand, above the entry of the twenty-eighth day of the third month, because a rule a body writes for itself with a date on it is the only paper this body has ever had and it should be in the book and not only on a bench.

He read it out in the bay that evening, all nine lines, and he read it out a second time, and about nine people in that room followed the second reading by watching the sheet go up and down, which is a thing people do. It is issued by nobody, binds nobody, and any person in the room may stand up and say they never agreed to it and be believed, and the denying is in the fifth line on purpose.

"I wrote it," he said, "and I did not agree to it, and I have never agreed to it, and if anybody in this room wants to stand up and say they never agreed to it they may do so now and I will write their name in the book as a person who has denied it, with the day, and that is the one column I have ever wanted to start.

"**And I am not going to start it, and I am not going to start a column called *why not* either, and I have been asked about both of those by about four people in six weeks and the answer is the same and it is that a name in a margin is not a debt and not a claim and not a vote and not a party, and that the honest thing to do about a body that cannot be compelled is to be easy to compel yourself.**"

The second was the water: in on the nineteenth day of the fourth month at about the second hour of the morning, down over the twentieth and part of the twenty-first, about eleven inches in the middle of the bay at the fourth hour, nobody drowned, the bank at the third-of-the-way mark eleven feet high throughout, the ford full.

The third was the lapsed list. He wrote out the whole of it, in his own hand, with the day on it, and the two figures, and the word **lapsed**, and the reason, and the foreman's name at the foot of it and the date, and he read all of it back to about nine people in the bay that evening and made a second reading of it for about nine people who could not read a word of it, and the second reading is the one he did not skip and the one he would have skipped when he was thirty-one.

The fourth was a line about the ford, the stones and the two posts, and about a clerk of the Inheritance Desk in a city four hundred and thirty miles from that bank, whose name is at the foot of it with a date of the first week of this month, and whose term is a year and whose review is in the first month of next year.

**He entered it in the Company's book.**

Then he sat on the bench for a while with the pen out, and the clerk of the district works' office is not the only person in this matter who has found that out — a man of thirty-eight, keeping a book, about four hundred and forty foot of bank, understood the whole of what he had just done in about eleven seconds and got up and put the pen down and wrote a sentence instead.

> *The fourth of these entries is not of this Company. It is put in this book because a list of works is the only paper on this cut that anybody can point at, and because a thing that is kept off the book is a thing that is findable in one place only, and I would rather it were findable in five. He was not asked and cannot be asked. There is a warrant on him from a court that is not this court and the office that watches him is not named and cannot be named, and I have been told neither of those things by him and I have not asked.*

**And a name in a margin is findable, and that is the only thing anybody has ever wanted out of a book, and a name against an entry is a thing somebody can be asked about the entry, and there is no way in this empire to give a name back.**

---

The fifth thing was the figure. It is the only figure anybody on that cut could say with confidence, and he wrote it at the foot of the new list in his own hand, which is the hand of thirty-eight and not the hand of sixty-four.

> *About one hundred and forty foot of ditch on the south side. The scour at the third-of-the-way mark. The ford, the stones and the two posts. To be read as a work and not as a place where a cart went in.*
>
> *H. Wray, foreman, Slade. The second week of the fifth month, and not one day past it.*
>
> *There is no figure on this list for what it costs and there is no figure on it for who pays it. The only figure in ninety-one households that anybody can say is a day rate of forty-five pence on five men and a boy, which is two days at seven shillings and sixpence, and the Company has four shillings a year, of which the whole is the rent of the south end of this store at a shilling a quarter, taken out of this book four times a year. **Nobody has offered to pay this and nobody is going to be asked, and this list is going to sit on that bench with nothing on it but a name and a day until somebody finds a figure, and finding one is not a thing this body can do.***

Then he read that out twice as well, and about nine people in that bay could not read it and were spoken to by somebody sitting next to them without being asked whether they wanted it, which is the fourth of a rule written on the twenty-eighth day of the third month and entered in this book a second time this week.

---

The sixth, seventh and eighth things were the four records, and he wrote them on one page and did not line them up.

> *The return book of the Reed Cut district works, four miles down the road, in a foreman's own hand, in a column with no heading over it, about four hundred names in three years, not one of them answerable.*
>
> *This book. About nine hundred and forty names in about sixty years, no heading, no list of persons, one name against an entry since the sixth day of the third month and one more since the first week of this month.*
>
> *A slate in a wooden frame on a nail in the open at the end post of a landing a hundred and forty yards below the ford, in a man's own hand, with his age on it, kept there about nine years because a customer wants to know whether his stone has gone. It is a record and not a register and it is not evidence of anything against anybody.*
>
> *A minute in a court on a flood terrace four miles down the road, in a clerk's hand, with a figure on the face of a form that is the clerk's own and is out of her own nine years.*
>
> **These are four records. They are not copies of one another and they do not confirm one another. Any two of them can be compared and a comparison will be wrong, because they were made for four different purposes by four different people for four different reasons, and one of them has a column with no heading and one of them has no heading at all and one of them has a clerk's figure on it and one of them has a man's age on it.**

He read that out twice and then he said the rest of it out loud, in the bay, standing at the west end, in about nine sentences, and about four people heard all of it.

"**A line between two things is a chain and a chain is an invention.** That is not mine. A magistrate of that court entered it in the ninth of the second month in a minute, on her own motion, about a file of about nineteen years back, and she wrote that a clerk who thinks somebody owes something is the beginning of everything that has gone wrong in that district since the sixth year.

"And I am not going to line these four up. **I have wanted to since the fourteenth of this month and I have not, and the reason I have not is that the day somebody lines them up, all four of them become evidence of one thing instead of four, and about four hundred and forty foot of bank will then be a matter with a record behind it, and we have not got one and we are not going to get one by joining four things that were never joined.**"

And then he said the other thing, which is the thing he has been not saying since the second of the third month, and he said it in the bay and not in a room, and about nine people heard it and about four of them understood it.

"There is a name in this book on a page from ninety-one years back. **Tarn Skell, of the Slade.** The eleventh of eleven names on that page, and a map of nineteen years back has him in it as the head of a household of nine at the north of the ford, and there are four houses of Skells at the north of the ford now and one of them belongs to a woman of about seventy-one who cannot read a paragraph and has a leg.

"And I found that name on the second of the third month and I did not go, and I have not gone since, and I have asked at all four doors and **nobody at any of them could tell me whose grandfather Tarn Skell was.** One said she had been given the name in a kitchen and had never asked. One said there had always been Skells. One said the name was not on any of the stones in that lane and that a man who wanted a family should try a churchyard instead of a lane.

"**A name is findable and a family is not.** *Skell* is a list of four doors and *Tarn Skell's son* is not a thing anybody can look up, and the oldest person on that bank was not born ninety-one years ago, and I have had about nine weeks to do something about it and I have done nothing, on purpose, and I am not going to do anything in the second week of this month either.

"Because going would make a person a party. And in about four years the paper would be the only thing anybody could act on and it would act on them, and they would be four doors in a lane and none of them would have asked for any of it."

---

The ninth thing was a copy, and it went up the towpath on the eighth of the fifth month in a hand and not in an envelope, because a copy that goes in an envelope to a body is a copy about the body and a copy that goes in a hand to a person is a copy about a person and that is the whole of the difference and it cost four pence and no carriage was paid because the person carrying it walked.

It went up to a clerk's window on a flood terrace with a board above the sill, and the clerk read it and entered that it had come, and asked a question about it, and the person carrying it answered in about nine words and did not know the answer to the rest of it and said so.

The clerk entered that too.

**A copy in the hands of one man is a copy about one man, and a decision with nobody's name at the foot of it is worth about a fifth of one with a name, and the figure is a clerk's own and out of her own nine years and it is the only figure on the form.**

---

A letter came up the road on the eighth of the fifth month and it has been in the mill yard ever since, which is about two days, and it is still in the mill yard.

It came in a cart with some seed and it is addressed to **the Slade Cut Company, Slade End** in a hand nobody on that bank recognises, and it is a letter and it is about a matter and it wants somebody's name and a person's answer, and there is a line on the outside of it in the same hand that says a thing is to be entered against a person and not against a body.

**A body cannot be served. A body cannot refuse. A body cannot open its own post, and there is no form in this empire in which a body may say *I have no person*, and there is no head of the Slade Cut Company and there has never been one and a magistrate has entered that one cannot be had.**

So the letter is standing against the wall of a mill yard in a tarpaulin, and about nine people have looked at it and read the outside of it and about four of those can read a paragraph, and nobody has opened it, and nobody in ninety-one households can be shown why not.

And Rennick Adley, who has walked four miles up a towpath in the dark and four miles back to find out whose grandfather Tarn Skell was, and who asked at four doors at the north of the ford and got nothing at any of them, and who found a name on a page from ninety-one years back and did not go, and who has still not gone, and who has said out loud why and has not changed his mind —

Rennick Adley looked at the letter for about a minute and a half and then he went and got the book out of the chest and wrote a line in it with the day on it, and the line says:

> *A paper has come to this cut addressed to a body. It wants a person's name and a person's answer and it is the ninth such thing anybody in this cut has ever had and it is the first one that has come about anything we know of. It is not opened. It is not going to be opened by me, and there is nobody in ninety-one households it can be opened by, and that is the answer and it is the whole of the answer and it took me a minute and a half.*

**Nothing is agreed. Nobody is chosen. The Company is not a guarantor, not a party and not answerable, except that it is, and cannot say so, and has been told by a person in a room of about thirty people that a body that is given a duty and cannot refuse has accepted it.**

There is a bench in the middle of a hired bay with a list of works on it and a foreman's name at the foot of it and a date, and there is a book in a store with a new hand in it, and about nine people in that bay heard a book-keeper read a page out twice and about nine of them could not read a word of it and were spoken to by somebody sitting next to them.

And a woman of about thirty-one with two children came down a towpath on the twenty-third of the last month to be told that there was nothing, and is coming back, and a foreman of fifty-one is going to be asked a fifth time in a year and has not said what she will do about it, and a man of about fifty is going to be asked in about a year whether he will stand a ford for a second term, and a man of thirty-eight is four hundred and thirty miles away with a wage of seventeen shillings and a penny the week and a line on his wrist about two inches long.

**A burden moved onto ninety-one people is not shared. It is distributed. And distribution is what makes it permanent.**

And in a mill yard, against a wall, under a tarpaulin, a letter stands unopened with a body's name on it, because a body has no person to sign for it.
