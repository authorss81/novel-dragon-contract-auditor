# Chapter 125: The Four Lines, Sent

The Received Room has a stove in it, a bar on the inside of the frame, a window onto a wall, and eleven feet of shelf, and it is where things go when nobody wants to be the one who has them.

Marek Kest is thirty-eight and has been a clerk of it for two years and is under a formal watch entered in one sentence on the nineteenth of the ninth month of last year, together with an eligibility, deliberately, because a court that puts them in two sentences may be asked to move one of them and that court has been asked twice in fourteen months and has moved both.

**The watch is the price and not a qualification.** A person from an office, in this matter unmentioned, may read anything he writes. He may not be in a room without a person in it who is not from that office, and the office is not named and cannot be named, because a person who signs a column headed *by whose authority* is discharging an office, and an office cannot be examined, and an office that is named and cannot be examined cannot be instructed.

There is one person in this matter who is not from that office. She is in the room because of the sentence, and he is in the room because she is, and both of them know that, and neither of them has said it, and it has been four months.

---

He had the shape in his own book since the sixteenth of the second month, in a weigh-house, in four lines at a bench, after a woman with about nine hundred loads in her ledger had spent four minutes arguing with four other people about one word on a line.

He had stated its price in three rooms since then and had never once said what it was for, and on the twenty-first day of the third month a man of sixty had asked him in the ordinary way, in a room with a copyist in it, and he had answered a different question correctly, and had written in his own book that he would rather be asked a second time than answer it wrong in a room with a copyist in it.

Nobody had asked him anything since.

**The page came back on the fourth of the fourth month**, and it says what it said the last time: the question was examined and not answered, no person was named, no document was produced, and the day and a half of the afternoon of a man of about sixty is in his own day's book with no fund against it.

**There are about four hundred names on it and his is one of them.** Not people who were asked — people who were *in the matter*, put down by a registrar because a register of a proceeding records what a proceeding was about.

**That is the trade.** In about four years somebody will come up that page wanting a bank and not a man, and they will find four hundred names, and the man who asked for the question to be entered in a book with a name in it will be on it too, and it is not made by anybody and it is not stopped by anybody, and he has been in it three weeks and has done one thing with it, which was to put it down on a bench and stand next to it.

---

He opened his own book on the eleventh of the fourth month at about the ninth hour, at the page written in a weigh-house in the second month, and the four lines are these.

> *One. It moves one specified consequence off two named persons onto ninety-one named persons in rotation.*
> *Two. It gives the rotation a term and a date and a right of refusal, and it says the refusal costs nothing, and the refusal is the whole of the instrument and everything else in it is bookkeeping.*
> *Three. The whole of its cost is named labour and named liability. Not a fund. Not a levy. Not a mark. Labour and liability, and both of them have a name and a face on them.*
> *Four. It is not a gift and it is not a fund and I am not paid.*

He read them twice, and then he read the sentence underneath them, which he had written on the second of the third month, and it says: *What I have not been able to say in a room and could not answer when a man of sixty asked me in the ordinary way: what the four lines are for.*

Then he looked at the wall out of the window, which is about nine feet away and has nothing on it, which is a thing he has done for four months.

**What the four lines have to run on.** That was the whole of the difficulty, and it took him about four minutes to arrive at it, and he had not arrived at it before, and when he had he sat on the edge of the table with his hands hanging and was in that room about nine minutes without writing anything.

A rotation of ninety-one persons needs ninety-one persons.

The Slade Cut Company is a body of about ninety-one households and is not a person and cannot be made one, and a magistrate has entered that, and there is no form in the district or the empire that does it.

There is no list of the ninety-one. There has never been one. The count of about ninety-one households and about sixty children under sixteen is a count off the Company's own book, **added on the back of a hand by a woman who cannot read a paragraph**, and it is a figure and not a roll, and a body has no roll because a body has no clerk and no door and no head.

What exists is a book. A book in a hired stone store at a shilling a quarter, with **nine hands in it over about sixty years and about nine hundred and forty findable names in it and no heading at the top of it and, until the sixth day of the third month, no name at the foot of anything.**

**That is the only list there is.** So that is what the rotation runs on, in the order the book happens to be in, and the book has never once been chosen from, because there has never been anything to choose.

He could have asked a book-keeper of thirty-eight four hundred and thirty miles down that river what the Company's book was, and the answer was probably nine seconds long. **He did not ask. The reason is that he was asked on the twenty-first of the third month what the four lines are for and he answered a different question correctly, and a man who has answered a question he was not asked does not go and ask a second one for about four months.**

---

He wrote it in the afternoon of the eleventh and it took him until about the ninth hour of the night, and it is four lines long and then nine pages, and here is the whole of the operative part of it.

> *An exception, in the sense that one specified consequence is moved, and by nothing else.*
>
> *The consequence moved: the works at the outer face of about four hundred and forty foot at the third-of-the-way mark on the Slade Cut, and the seventh work of eleven on the page from ninety-one years back, being the ford, the stones and the two posts. No other work and no other bank.*
>
> *From: the two persons named in the flood sheet of the ninth of the first month, which is void on the refusal of one of them, entered by the keeper of the district water office with the day; and from the district of Reed Cut, which is undispensed in respect of those works, entered with a date.*
>
> *To: the Slade Cut Company, of about ninety-one households, of which about sixty are children under sixteen, which is a body and is not a person and cannot be a party and cannot refuse; and the persons named in the schedule, in rotation, each for the term stated against that person's name, no term longer than one year.*
>
> *It is recorded as accepted. **No person in the said Company was asked. The Company is a body and is not a person and cannot be asked, and nothing in this instrument requires it to be.** A body that is given a duty and cannot refuse has accepted it, and this instrument records the acceptance and does not evidence a consent.*
>
> *The schedule is the book of the Company kept at the Slade end. A person is the person whose name stands in the book and not the household. **A child is not a party, is not named and is not in the rotation, and no child born into a household after this is acted on is in the rotation at any time.***
>
> *Right of refusal. Any person named may refuse the term falling to them, in writing or by word to a person present, and may refuse during it, and the refusal costs the person refusing nothing, and no person, office, court or body may proceed against a person who has refused.*
>
> *Conditions. One: the schedule is produced and read out, where the persons in it can be found, twice, once for those who can read it and once for those who cannot. Two: the day, the term and the review date are read out with it. Three: a refusal is not a vote and is not a matter for a meeting. Four: **this exception binds nobody who has not been heard about it, and no person is in the schedule who has not been heard, because every person in the schedule is named individually and individually may be spoken to.**
>
> *Limits. It moves that consequence and no other. It creates no office and gives no power to any person over any other. **It does not make the Company a party, a creditor or a debtor, and it does not make the Company a guarantor of any future flood on that cut, and nothing in it survives the last term of the last name in the schedule.** It is not a fund and holds no money.*
>
> *Cost. The labour is named: five men at eight pence a day and a boy at five, being forty-five pence a day on the five and one, paid out of the hand of the person whose term it is. **No figure is put on what a term costs that person to stand, and I cannot put one, because I do not know it and I have asked nobody.** The liability is named: a person in the schedule is answerable, alone, for the works named against that person's term, and no household and no child is answerable, and the person who is answerable can be found, served, put in a room, and can say no.*
>
> *M. Kest, clerk, Received Room, the eleventh day of the fourth month. **I have read no instrument and I have taken no reading. There is no mark on me and there is no certification and there is no office behind this paper and nobody has paid me to write it.***

---

He read the whole of it out loud in the room at about the ninth hour of the eleventh to a woman of twenty-four who had been sitting at the other end of the table for two hours without saying anything, which is a thing she does and which he has never got used to.

"That is the correct instrument," said Tamsin Rook. "**And you have not asked what it is for, and I am not going to ask you, because a person who answers a question he was not asked twice in a month ends up answering a question nobody has.** I have four things to say. Three of them are the price and one is not."

---

The first thing was the schedule.

"**The rotation runs on a book with no heading at the top of it and about nine hundred and forty names in it in about sixty years, and it runs on it in the order the book is in, and the book has never been chosen from.** That is the sentence that will do the work in about nine days and you have written it on the ninth page like a clerk writing a date.

"Ninety-one households, and a book with nine hundred and forty names in it, and about four hundred of those people are dead. **You are going to hand a boy of six the third term of a rotation because his mother's name is in a book because his grandfather could not write and his aunt wrote the page.** And you are going to call that a distribution of a public duty, and you are going to be right, and there is not one word in the nine pages that stops it, because the ninth page is where the schedule comes from and you did not know what the schedule was."

"Then what should it run on."

"Nothing that exists. **That is the answer, and the reason you have not had it is that the man who wrote the name in the book did it on purpose to be findable, and you have not asked him what he did it for.**"

The second thing was the Company.

"**The instrument gives a duty to a body.** It says so in the first recital and it does not pretend, and that is honest, and it is the worst thing in the document, and I am going to say the worst thing in the document out loud in a room with two people in it, because nobody is going to read it.

"A body cannot refuse. **A body that is given a duty and cannot refuse has accepted it, and the acceptance is on the face of your paper in words nobody ever said, and you have made the Slade Cut Company the guarantor of every flood on that cut for as long as the book has names in it, and the whole of how you did it is that you never asked anybody twice.**"

"I have given it a right of refusal."

"You have given ninety-one people a right of refusal. **You have not given a Company one, because there is nobody to refuse for it, and you put that in the first recital in a sentence of your own drafting, and a sentence of your own drafting in the first recital of an instrument is the only part of it that cannot be argued with.**"

The third thing was the cost, and it is the thing she could not get round.

"**The cost to a person on that rotation is a wage. And you have not put a figure on it, and I cannot work one out, and neither can you, and neither can ninety-one households. Your ninth page has a named figure for the work — forty-five pence a day on five men and a boy — and not one figure for the man who is to stand the bank for a term of a year.**

"A duty with a figure for the work and no figure for the person is not a duty anybody can refuse, **because they cannot know what they are refusing.** You have given ninety-one people a right to say no and not given one of them the price of saying yes, and that is the whole of the moral defect in nine pages."

"And the cost to me is a wage of seventeen shillings and a penny the week and an afternoon nobody pays me for. The cost to a man of sixty in a city is a day and a half of his own afternoon with no fund. The cost to a foreman in a shed four miles away will be a ford that does not get done. **None of those three is the price of your paper, and the price of your paper is that about sixty children are inside it and nobody has been asked.**"

"And the part you have got right, said out loud in a room, because it is the only reason I am still sitting here: **it does not create a perpetual office, it does not carry into the ninth year, and it says on the seventh page that nothing in it survives the last term of the last name in the schedule.** Nobody in this matter has ever written that sentence down. It is on your page and it will be the thing that is read in about four years when somebody decides you were a villain."

The fourth thing was not the price.

"The undertaking of the fourth of the first month says I take every sheet that goes out of this matter to a solicitor, to a household, or to an office outside the Roll Hall, and read it in front of you before it goes, and give you any reason against one in a room and out loud and not on a page, and ask you for it before you act if you ever decide I am wrong again. **It goes to an office outside the Roll Hall. It is the undertaking. It has applied to this since the fourth of the first month, and I have been in this room for the whole of it, and you have not once put it in front of me.**"

---

He did not answer that for about eleven seconds, which is about how long Oswin Marrow waited for him in a different room in the third month, and neither of them mentioned that.

"You are right," he said. "It has been in my coat since the ninth hour on the eleventh and you have been in this room for two hours."

"Eleven days. I have not asked and I am not going to, because you would have brought it to me in about nine seconds and you have not, and **the reason you have not is that you knew what I would say, and that is the same thing that happened with a sheet in your coat in the second month, and I have written that down before and I am writing it down again, and the second time it is worse, because the first time you had never been watched.**"

"I have been watched four months."

"You have been watched four months and nobody has ever read anything you wrote until about an hour ago, and I want you to understand the exact shape of that, because it is the shape of the instrument and you cannot see it because you are holding it. **A person from an office may read anything you write. And I am in this room because a court said so. And the reason the instrument is technically good is that there is a person in the room to catch it, and the reason it is going to be wrong in shape is that the person in the room was not asked whether the room was the right one.**"

Then she used the rule, which is that either of them may put a choice in writing with a date on it and the other must answer in a room, in words, with a date.

> *T. Rook, the eleventh of the fourth month. The choice is one of two. One: he does not send it. Two: he sends it. Answer in a room, in words, with a date.*

"I am going to say the answer now, and it is two, and the date is the twelfth, and I am not going to soften it and you are not going to thank me for not softening it, because I did it for a book, and there is no book, and the column in it is the only one there is."

"Say it."

"**Two. He sends it.** And the reason is not that the paper is right, because I have told you three things that are wrong with it and I have not stopped, and the reason is that the water is in the third week of this month and about nine acres and thirty-one households are behind four hundred and forty foot of bank with **no name on them anywhere at all**, and every day this sits in your coat is a day that nothing is answered, and there is no other instrument in this empire that would answer it, and I do not get to be the reason a bank is under water in the wrong week because I have a column in a book with my hand against every line of it."

Marek Kest said: "That is not a legal reason."

"No. It is not. **It is a reason, and it is the worst of the ones available, and I have given it to you in a room and out loud and not on a page, and I am a person who keeps copies, and I have never in nine years had to be the reason anything went wrong, and I would like it written down that I said I would rather it went to a court that binds nobody who has not been heard, and that I said that before I said the rest.**"

"Write that down."

"**No.** If it is in a book then in about four years it is a sentence in a file about a person's state of mind on the eleventh of the fourth month, and you have a watch on you, and a state of mind is the one thing in this matter a copy can take away from you and nothing else can. **If I say it in a room and you hear it, then it is a thing two people said in a room, and a room is the only place in this empire where a thing can be said and not kept.**"

---

He did not give it to her. He said so out loud, which was the second half of the undertaking, and he honoured it.

"**I am not giving you the sheet, and I am not acting on it tonight, and I will not give it to you again, and the reason is not that you are wrong.**"

"That is permitted, and I want it said that it is permitted, and I have thought about it for about nine minutes and I am not going to make it a condition. **The undertaking asks you for it. It does not make it a rule, and I would have to be an office to make it a rule, and I am not an office, and the whole of what I have is that anybody can ask me to stop and I have never once been able to say no to one of them, and that is a person and not an instrument, and it is the only difference between this room and a court.**"

---

He sent it on the twelfth of the fourth month at about the fourth hour of the afternoon, to the foreman of the Reed Cut district works, four miles from a bank, by the ordinary post of a district that goes up a road once a day.

The reason he sent it there and not to the water office is the best thing he did in nine months, and he did it in about four seconds and told nobody why. The water office had given its own authority away in writing and could not take it back and had no form for getting it back, and a keeper who had put two people in a box on purpose would have had to be handed a document by a man in a good coat and write something down about it. The district works could not do the work either, had never been able to, and had been saying so out loud in its own return book for nine years with its own name at the foot.

**A district works is a body and cannot refuse, and a person at a gate who has never refused anything cannot begin on the twelfth of the fourth month either — and a paper with a person's name at the top of it is the only thing in this empire that can be put through a gate.**

He wrote nothing about that in his own book. He wrote four lines: that the page came back and it is worth nine days and a page, and that about four hundred names are on it, and that he has done one thing with it, and that the thing he is doing is on the other side of this.

Then he said the date to the woman who had put a choice to him, and she read it back and crossed nothing out, and then she took out her own book with no office on it and looked at the column with her hand against every line in it, which is the only heading in that book and the whole of the design, and she did not write under it, and said why.

"**A person may ask me to stop, with a reason, written under that column with the day, and on that day the copies and the column go to four holders in four hands, and there is no office to instruct anybody to take them and there is no successor.** I am not going to write under it this month. You have not asked me to stop, and what I have to say about you does not stop me and does not stop the copies, and I have said it in a room, and a room is the only place a thing does not sit in a file." "

Then she took out a drawer, and out of it came an envelope addressed to a standing assessor of a furnace town, written in the fourth week of the first month of last year, and she looked at it for about two seconds and put it back and shut the drawer.

"It is still there," she said, "and it has not been sent and it has not been opened, and I am telling you it is there because you have not once asked me whether I have anything else, and I have, and I am not going to give it to you."

"**The whole of the arrangement is that I am in this room, and the reason I am in this room is a sentence, and the sentence is the reason your paper is any good, and I have watched you spend four months not noticing that, and I am not going to say it out loud, and I have just said it out loud.**"

---

A form bought and not filled in lies in a book four hundred and thirty miles from the desk he is paid for — a third of a sheet, four lines, the fourth of them blank, bought for a shilling out of a copyist's own hand and never entered. A page upstairs has his name on it and about four hundred others and says the question was examined and not answered.

And nine pages went four miles down a road to a man at a gate, with the writer's own name at the top of the first and a foreman's name at the foot of the last, and there is a mark on the inside of nothing at all yet, and he has read no instrument and taken no reading, and he does not know what the thing is for and has known for seven weeks and has not looked.
