# Chapter 128: A Body Cannot Answer

The rule of the meeting of the Slade Cut Company was written on the twenty-eighth day of the third month by a woman of sixty-four who cannot read a paragraph, and asked for by nobody, and agreed to by nobody. It is nine lines on one side of a sheet. It is not an instrument, not a resolution, not a vote, not a demand, not an office and not a Roll Assembly.

The second line is that the person who called the meeting counts the people in the room out loud at the beginning, and counts the people who are not in the room out loud at the end, and the two figures are written down together and not apart.

The fifth line is that any person in the room may stand up and say they never agreed to it and be believed.

It was called for the fourth week of the fourth month and it was the fourth meeting in that room and the first one anybody had called for a subject.

---

**Tamsin Rook is twenty-four and she was in the bay because a court order requires it.**

She had come up the river in the last six days, on a towpath and in a cart and on a bench, with a man who is not from the office that watches him, and neither of them had said why, and it had been four months and it was four months on the twentieth of the fourth month. He had come up the river himself on about the nineteenth, in the water, six days on a towpath and in a cart and on a bench, and had walked into a flood season four hundred and thirty miles from a desk he is paid for, and had slept two nights in the south end of a store with a stove in it and had not complained about it and had not been thanked for it either.

She had come because of the sentence. He had come because of the sentence. The whole of the room knew there was a sentence and none of the thirty people in it knew what it was about, and the two of them had not said so, and the arrangement is the arrangement and it is not a romance and it is not going to be one this year.

There were about thirty people in the bay, in about a foot and a half of mud that had dried into a crust, on a floor a foot off the ground, at about the seventh hour on a cold night with the door that does not shut open behind them.

The nine pages were read out first, because a thing on a bench is findable and a thing that is read out is heard, and about a third of the people in that bay cannot read a paragraph and had not been asked in six weeks whether they wanted anything read to them.

The reader was a girl of seventeen who is not paid and cannot be, and who read it out once at the ordinary speed and then again slower, and nine people in the room were spoken to by a person sitting next to them without being asked whether they wanted it, and the number of people who asked her afterwards what it meant was four.

She did not tell them what it meant. She said that when somebody asked her, in the ordinary way, in about four words, and then she read the fourth page a third time to about three people who wanted that page and not the other eight.

Doreth Ferrow counted the room at the beginning, out loud, standing at the west end.

"**Thirty.**"

And then she counted the other one, at the end, and wrote the two figures on the back of her own hand together and not apart, which she has done twice now and which is the only method in this matter that has worked twice.

"**And about sixty of about ninety-one households are not in this room.** That is the same two figures as on the twenty-eighth of the third month and I am not going to pretend I have discovered them, and I am not going to pretend they are a rule or an instance of anything, because a chair of nineteen years who starts joining figures together to make a shape out of them ends up with a shape and no people in it."

---

She put the question at about the seventh hour and she had written it on the back of a miller's paper and she could not read what she had written and Rennick Adley had to read it out for her, and about nine people in the room could not read it either, and nine of them were spoken to by a person sitting next to them without being asked whether they wanted it.

"**One question. There is a paper on that bench with nine pages on it, and it came down four miles from a man in a city four hundred and thirty miles from here, and the seventh of those pages says, and I have had that page read to me twice, that it does not make the Slade Cut Company a guarantor of any future flood on that cut.**

"**I am asking the Company whether it accepts the duty. That is the question. I have put it in the ordinary way and I would like an answer before the ninth hour.**"

Nine people said something. A man at the back said the Company had not asked for it. A woman near the door said it was not theirs. A man who has been coming for nineteen years said the Company was not a person and could not be asked, and that was in the room and it was right, and Doreth Ferrow let it stand for about four seconds and then she answered it.

"No," she said. "**It cannot be asked. I want that said out loud in this room and I want it said by me, because I am the chair of it and I have been the chair of it for nineteen years and I have never once been able to say this, and it took me a week to find the honest way to say it and I have been carrying it since I found it.

"A body cannot refuse.

"That is the whole of it and it is not complicated and it has taken me nineteen years. A man can be asked and he can say no. An office can be instructed and it can be told to act and it can be told not to. **And a company — a cooperative, a parish, a district, a household — is a body, and a body cannot be asked and cannot refuse and cannot be instructed and cannot be summoned and has no clerk and no door and no head and no list.**

"So when you ask this body whether it accepts a duty, and it does not answer, **the answer is yes. A body that is given a duty and cannot refuse has accepted it, whether it was in the room or not, and the paper on that bench has about nine words on its first page recording that it was accepted and saying in the same nine words that the acceptance does not evidence a consent, and about nine words is a very cheap way of not asking anybody.**

"And it says on its seventh page that it does not make the Company a guarantor of any future flood on that cut. **It does. That is the only new thing I have found out in four days. A paper that says it does not do a thing, that cannot be asked whether it did it, and that is the only paper in this district that says anything at all about any of it.** Nobody in ninety-one households said those nine words and I am one of them and I did not."

"And I have sat in this chair for nineteen years and every word that has ever been agreed to in a meeting in this cut has only ever bound the people who were in it, and about thirty of us are in it, and about sixty are not, **and the ones who are not in the room are the ones the thing binds.**"

She stopped there, and then she said the other thing, and she said it to the room and not to the paper.

"Nineteen years ago the first thing I ever did in this chair was stop about forty people from doing something in a mill yard, and I was right, and nobody ever told me I was right until a man I have never met put it in a book four miles away with a figure on it and his own name at the foot of it. Nineteen years and a stranger.

"And I have been sitting here all evening braced for the right thing to turn out to be the wrong thing. **I want it noticed that the braced woman is a woman of sixty-four in a cold bay and not a magistrate. And I am not braced. The right thing is the wrong thing. That is all. It has not come as a blow, it has come as a sum, and I have done the sum four times and it comes out the same four times.**"

Then she sat down on the bench in the middle, which she has never done in a meeting before, because the bench in the middle is for things, and she has said so.

---

Then Tamsin Rook said the thing about the bill, and she said it standing, at the west end, with her back to the door that does not shut, and it took about four minutes, and nobody interrupted her.

"There is a sheet of foolscap in a book with no office on it in a city four hundred and thirty miles from this room and it is a third of a sheet and it has four lines on it and the fourth line is blank, and it cost a shilling out of a copyist's own hand on the nineteenth of the last month, and I have never filled it in and I am not going to.

"**And here is what the whole of this costs, and I am going to say it in one go and I am not going to soften it, because I said in a public room in the last month of last year that I would not, and because I have found out since that a cost nobody can be shown the bill for is not a smaller cost.**

"A wage of seventeen shillings and a penny the week. Three pounds eleven and threepence of it into a bank on that cut over nineteen days, being three weeks of it, and all nineteen days paid and not one of them unpaid. Nobody paid his carriage either and there is no fund in this matter for his carriage and there never has been. **A man of sixty in a building in that city lost a day and a half of his own afternoon to a sentence that put a watch on a clerk, and there is no fund in this office or any other for that either.**

"About four afternoons and three weeks of one man's wage in a fortnight, and the four afternoons are not even about the bank. And then nine pages, and no fund behind those either, and a miller with nine tons of stone in a yard and a foreman with a book that has an empty head since the third week of the twelfth month, and about nine people from this bay have been standing in that yard since the seventh hour on the morning of the twentieth looking at a household that was not hurt, and they are not paid and they were never asked.

"**That is the bill. There is no figure at the bottom of it that anybody can pay, and I have looked, and I want it said in a room that I looked.**"

Doreth Ferrow said: "Say the last of it again."

Tamsin Rook said: "I have looked, and there is no figure at the bottom of it that anybody can pay."

And then she said the thing that made it a professional act and not an apology, and she said it before anybody could say it was kind.

"**I am not advising anybody. I hold an office that does not exist and the whole of what it says is that I keep the copies and I advise nobody and I ask nothing, and advice by proximity is the ordinary way advice happens and it is why I have not said one word to one person in this room. Everybody in here who has heard me tonight has heard a price and not an opinion.**"

"If anybody thinks that is not true," she said, "they should say so in this room while I am standing in it, and there is nobody here who has said so, and I have not asked whether anybody thinks so, because asking is advising, and I am not going to."

Rennick Adley read the four minutes back to the woman who had asked for it to be said again, in a cold bay, in about eleven hundred words, to a woman of sixty-four who cannot read a paragraph, and did not skip a word of it, and nobody in the room asked him to.

---

The copy was the last thing, and it was nine minutes, and it is the only part of that night that Doreth Ferrow wrote down afterwards.

"A copy in the hands of one man is a copy about one man," said Tamsin Rook. "**A copy sent to a body is a copy about the body. And the difference between them is about four hundred and thirty miles of carriage and a clerk's decision, and I have made that decision four times in nine years and I have got it wrong three of them and there is no form in this empire in which a clerk is asked whether she has.**

"The copy of the sitting of the fifth of the fourth month, at a court on a flood terrace four miles down this road, at which four persons at the north of the ford were heard and were told they are parties — that copy was bought at a shilling by the man who filed the application, and he took it himself, and he did not send it to the Company.

"And the judge said out loud that she had never in nine years seen anybody do that, and she was right, and he is the only person in ninety-one households who can be asked about that piece of paper, and **that is not an accident and it is not a favour. It is the only way any of this is survivable, and it was decided by a man with a shilling in his own hand and nobody asked him and I have thought about that for two days and I have decided that I am not going to find a fault in it.**"

Then she said the last of it, to nobody, in about nine seconds, and Doreth Ferrow wrote it on the back of her hand under the two figures.

"**A rule that only binds the people in the room binds the people in the room.** That has been said out loud in this cut three times now — in this room on the twenty-eighth of the third month, in a shed on the thirtieth of the last month, and in a chamber on a flood terrace on the ninth of the second month by a magistrate who entered in a minute, on her own motion, that a body of persons is not a person and cannot be made one by her court or any other, and nobody has ever joined those three together and neither shall I.

"And a decision with nobody's name at the foot of it is worth about a fifth of one with a name, and that figure is a clerk's own, out of her own nine years, and it is the only figure on any form in this matter, and **it is the only number I have ever been able to find in this whole matter that anybody can be shown the bill for.**"

---

She was on the far side of the room from him for the whole of it, from the seventh hour to the ninth, and she had not looked at him once in a way that anybody in about thirty people could have put a name to, and he had not looked at her once either, and about four people in that bay noticed the fact and told about nine other people about it over the following week.

And at about the ninth hour, when the meeting had stopped and there was nothing agreed to and nothing resolved, Wendt Sayer came in from the cold with a road on him and asked the woman of fifty-one, in front of about thirty people, whether the Slade Cut Company accepted the duty at the bank and the ford.

And about thirty people looked at Doreth Ferrow.

And Doreth Ferrow said: "**I have asked it twice this evening and about nine people have answered it in about nine different ways and not one of the answers was from the Company, and I am going to say the answer a third time and it is the same one, and then I am going to go home.**

"It cannot refuse. So it has accepted it."
