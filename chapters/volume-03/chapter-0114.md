# Chapter 114: In What Way the Instrument Touches You

The Notaries' Table in Auremar is not a court and does not look like one. It is a long room on the ground floor of a building with a plate-glass front, and it has eleven counters in it, and at the back of each counter there is a rack of blanks in a pigeonhole, and above each counter there is a board with the fee of the four things that office will do, and the fee of nothing else, because there are only four things.

**It does not answer a question.** That is the first thing and it is on no board in the room. It *takes* one — a question, from a person, in a form, on payment — and it answers it in about eleven weeks, in a room upstairs, by a person nobody at the counter can name in advance.

The fourth counter is the one for scale instruments, and it has been the one for scale instruments for a hundred and forty years, and the reason it is the fourth and not the first is that the three before it are for estates, for marriages, and for a thing nobody at this table will explain to a stranger.

---

Tamsin Rook had been in the room about four minutes before she asked her first question, and she had spent them looking at the boards.

She was in the room because a sentence said she had to be. She was not going to say that out loud at a counter, and Marek Kest was not going to say it either, and neither of them had said it in a corridor four miles from a bank in the first week of the month and neither of them was going to say it here, and that was the arrangement and it had been the arrangement for four months.

Marek Kest was four feet behind her, which is the distance a person stands when they are not a party to a thing and want to be able to hear all of it.

"Do I need to be a person for this," she said, "or a solicitor, or a witness, or something worse than both."

"You need to be a person," said the clerk, "and if you are not one I cannot take the form from you and there is nobody at this table who can."

The clerk at the fourth counter is **Marn Ottery**. She is thirty-four and she has been at the fourth counter for eleven years and she came to it from the second counter, where estates are, and she has never once been a lawyer and has never wanted to be one and knows a good deal more about scale instruments than anybody who has ever been a lawyer at this table.

"Somebody at some point will tell you a clerk is the wrong person to want," she said. "I am going to say it now, before you want me, and then you cannot say I sprang it.

"**A foreman can be sent for. You can be sent for a foreman and he will come and it will cost the office a day. A clerk cannot be sent for. A clerk is a person, and you may ask a clerk things, and there is no form anywhere in this building that says a clerk may be asked.** About nine people a year find that out. It is the worst fact about this room and it is the reason this room has not been able to do anything wrong in eleven years that anybody could put in a minute."

She put a blank on the counter. It is about a third of a sheet, good paper, and it has four lines and a space at the top for a day, and it is the ordinary form of the Table, and it has been the ordinary form of the Table for about ninety years.

"This is a shilling. It is a shilling whether you fill it in or not, and I have watched about nine hundred people find that out in about four seconds each and it is the only thing about this form that nobody objects to."

She turned it round, and put her finger on the first line, and went down them.

"One. Your name.

"Two. Where you may be found.

"Three. The instrument — its day, its year, and what it is called.

"**Four. In what way the instrument touches you.**"

She took her hand off the paper.

"There. That is the form. Everything up to four is a fact about you and about a piece of paper, and I do not care what the answers are, and I have never known a clerk to refuse a form for the first three lines in eleven years.

"**The fourth line is the whole of the door.** And I will tell you what I have watched happen at that line, because you are standing at the counter of a building where a hundred and forty years of instruments have gone through it, and nobody has ever been told this before, and I have said it out loud about four times in eleven years and both times to myself.

"About forty a year write it badly. About four a year write it well. And about a hundred and forty a year do not get to it, and the ones who do not get to it are mostly people who have been told what the instrument is for and have gone to find a lawyer, and there are about nine hundred blanks in that rack behind me and I could tell you what the fourth line is for the price of a good dinner."

---

"And before you go any further with it," she said, "there is a fifth thing on that form and it is not a line, and it is the only thing about this Table that I am allowed to say out loud.

"**The day at the top may be a day before the instrument takes effect upon you.** Not after. Before. It may be put in on the fifteenth of a month and name the instrument of the twentieth, and this form is used that way about four times a year in this empire and I have filled in about nine of them in eleven years.

"You will want to know why it is used four times a year and not four hundred, and there is a man four feet behind you who has come four hundred and thirty miles to tell you, and I am going to stand back from the counter and let him, and I want it understood that I am not adopting anything he says."

Marek Kest came up to the counter.

"It is used about four times a year because of a finding of the district court of Auremar's lower district, of the ninth of the eighth month of last year, which is a court of the same rank as every court in this city and is not this building and is not in this building and has never sent anything to this Table in ninety years. The finding is that a person affected by an instrument or a form of words in general use may be heard about it in a room, in a place that recognises the room, **before it binds anybody and not only afterwards.** It binds nobody who has not been heard, it lifts nothing, and the price said out loud in the room when it was made is that every precedent in the empire is now open to challenge, including the ones that are right, and that there is no way to charge that to anybody.

"There are two halves to that sentence. **The one that says a person may be heard is nine days older than the one that says when, and neither court has read the other, and neither of them takes a recognition from the other, and the court that made the first half does not know that the second half exists.** I have read both. I have never been in a room with both. I have tried four times in nine years to write to the other court and I have never sent it, and the reason I have not sent it is that I would have had to say which of the two halves I thought was right, and I have not got an answer to that and it is not the kind of question you answer on a form."

"Now," said Marn Ottery, "I am going to say three things and none of them is mine, and then I am going to go back to the fee board.

"**One. That is a finding of a district court four hundred and thirty miles from this counter, and it is not a rule of this Table and it never will be, and there is nothing in this room that can be amended because of it.** Two. Nobody at this table has ever entered what he has just said in a day-book, because nobody said it in a form, and I am not going to, and if you want it you will have to write it down yourself and it will be your paper and not this office's. Three. And this is the one I actually need said: **if that finding is the whole of why the fourth line is sometimes filled in before the instrument bites, then the fourth line is being filled in early on the strength of a sentence that has not been read by the court that made the other half, by anybody, anywhere.** And every one of the about four times a year is a person gambling that. And I do not say that to them. I have never said that to them and I am not going to start."

"Then what do you say to them," said Tamsin Rook.

"I say *the fee is a shilling and the answer is about eleven weeks*," said Marn Ottery.

---

The fourth line took eleven minutes, and the eleven minutes were not a scene. It was a woman at a counter saying the thing she had been carrying for eleven years in about eleven sentences, and about four people in the room heard all of it, and the room carried on.

"Here is what the form wants, and the form is right to want it.

"**A person.** A person with a name and somewhere to be found and a statement in their own mouth of how a piece of paper has touched them. That is the correct design and I would fight anybody who tried to take it out of this form, including the four hundred people in this building who have told their clients it is a formality.

"And here is the trouble, and it is a real one, and I have never solved it and I have solved nothing at all in eleven years.

"**What an instrument does, it does to a lot of people at once, and this form gives me room for one of them.** So a person who has been touched along with eighty other households — ninety, in the case I am thinking of, and it is a real case and I am not going to invent it — cannot say so on this form, because the honest sentence is a sentence about ninety households, and a sentence about ninety households with one person's name at the top of it **is a paper about a body with a person written on it**, and in about four years somebody is going to use that paper about that person. About that person, and about the bank, and about the nine dates, and about the other eighty-nine.

"**And the reason they cannot say it is not that the form is too short. The reason they cannot say it is that the day they say it, they are a party.** That is all it is. It is the shortest thing I have ever said at this counter and it took me eleven years.

"Everything up to the fourth line you can write down without becoming anybody anything. **The fourth line is the line you are a party on, and there is no way to say a thing about a body without becoming a person, and that is not a fault in the form, it is the form being honest, and I have watched about four hundred people decide in about nine seconds that it was a fault in the form.**"

She squared the blank on the counter.

"So. Have you got the instrument?"

"No," said Tamsin Rook.

"Then you have not got line three either, and I want you to understand that that is not a thing I say to be unkind. Line three is a thing you can buy off anybody who has a copy, and a copy of a copy proves that a clerk of a district copied something, and there are about four hundred and sixty households in a district four hundred and thirty miles from this counter and I have never seen one of them come up here with a line three and a line four that went together."

---

Marek Kest did the sum out loud, standing at the counter, and it took him about nine seconds and he did it twice.

"**The water is in the third week of the fourth month. The form takes about eleven weeks. It is the nineteenth of the third month.** A question put in this week is answered in the first week of the sixth. That is not a question about a bank. That is a question about a bank that has already had the water, and I am not going to say what the answer would be, because I do not know, and there are two of us in this room who do not know and one of us is the only person in the empire who has been paid to find out and that payment was a wage of seventeen shillings and a penny a week and a bank on a river."

Marn Ottery wrote nothing down and said: "**That is the price and I have never seen anybody write it down as a price.** A shilling is a shilling and everybody objects to a shilling. The eleven weeks is the price, and the eleven weeks is not a thing this Table does to anybody. The eleven weeks is the length of the road between a question and the person it is about, and there is no Table in this empire that can shorten it, and it is not a fee and it is not charged and I cannot put it on the board, because the board has four things on it and none of them is time."

Then she said: "I am going to say one more thing and then I am going to let you buy the paper or go away, and I would like you to buy the paper, and not for my sake."

"Go on."

"**You are not going to fill it in and you are going to buy it anyway, and I want you to know that I have watched people do that and it is the only sensible thing anybody has ever done at this counter.** A form bought and not filled in is four pence of proof that the question was asked. That is all. It is not a record, it is not a step, it will not be in anybody's file, and if you bring it back in four years with the fourth line written on it I will take it and I will not have any feeling about it at all."

---

Tamsin Rook said her four minutes then, and she said them standing at the counter with the shilling already on it, and Marek Kest did not say anything and did not move and there is no form anywhere in this empire that says a person may be heard being right about herself in a public room.

"You are going to want to know why I am not putting my name to it, and I would rather tell you than let you work it out.

"**No instrument in this empire has ever touched me.** Not once, in twenty-four years, and I have been fourteen months making myself findable on purpose — in a book with no office on it, at a counter, in a column with my hand against every line of it, and I am findable to anybody who comes and asks, and I have said out loud that the keeper is findable on purpose, because a column kept by somebody nobody can find is a column that can be denied.

"**And I have never once been a person anything happened to.** Not a party. Not a person an instrument touched. Not a person a thing was done to. I have been a person who read other people's paper out, and a person who kept other people's copies, and a person who put other people's names in boxes, and every one of those is a thing you do *to* somebody and I have never once had it done to me.

"So I am standing outside all four lines. And the fourth line is the only one of the four I could not write if I had wanted to, and I have thought about that on a river for four hundred and thirty miles, and I have not been able to make it be about me, and I have decided that is a fact and not a failure, and I would like it written in whatever book you keep that a person of twenty-four in Auremar can say that sentence out loud and be believed by a clerk."

"I have no book," said Marn Ottery. "I have a rack. The rack is behind me and you can see all of it and none of it has ever wanted that sentence in it."

"Then I will say it to the person who keeps your blanks," said Tamsin Rook, "and if there is no such person, then it was said out loud in a room and that is all it is owed."

"And the cost of it," she said, "which I am going to say because he is standing four feet behind me and has paid three pounds eleven and threepence out of his own wage in this matter and worked the sum out in a weigh-house in front of a woman he had met twice, and because I am not going to be the person who makes that sound like a story.

"**A wage of seventeen shillings and a penny a week. Three pounds is three weeks of it. He has been four hundred and thirty miles from his desk for a fortnight and nobody pays his carriage either, and there is no fund in this matter for his carriage and there never has been, and a man of sixty in a building in this city lost a day and a half of his own afternoon in the second month of this year to a sentence a court put into a watch.**

"That is what it costs. Not a dragon, not a mark, not a reading, not a bar. **About four afternoons and three weeks of one man's wage in a fortnight, and the four afternoons are not even about the bank.** And I am not going to soften it, and I have not said that sentence in a room before, and I am saying it because a person who turns up is never paid and the whole of what she has instead is that she is the only one who can be asked."

Nobody at the counter said anything for about a minute and a half, and then Marek Kest said, "It is not a soft thing to say and I would not have said it either, and I am glad you said it, and I am not going to thank you for it, because that would make it a thing you did for me."

"I said it for the book," said Tamsin Rook.

"There is no book."

"There is a column in a book with my hand against every line of it and there is a foreman on a bank who can read a paragraph and a girl of seventeen who cannot be paid," said Tamsin Rook, "and that is the book, and you are in it, and that is the whole of the arrangement and I have not read it back to you because you are not going to like it."

---

The last thing was four words long and she asked it of the clerk and not of the man, which was the correct thing to do and which Marn Ottery has said since was the only time in eleven years anybody at that counter has asked her a question about herself.

"Who was the last one you had?"

Marn Ottery looked at the rack behind her for a moment and then at the counter.

"A woman of sixty-one. Lower district, and I will not say which, because she did not tell me and I did not ask and that was the correct decision and I made it in about two seconds. Fifth month of last year. She came up with a stamped paper she could not read — the stamp, the wax, none of it; she said so herself and she did not apologise for it, which I have thought about since.

"**She did not fill it in either.** She stood at this counter for about eleven minutes and asked me four questions and did not put a single word on the paper, and one of the four was how much a copy is, and it is four pence, and she bought one.

"And she has not been back, and nobody has come for her either, and I have kept hers, because I have kept all about four hundred of them in a box under the counter, and I keep them because a Table is the only place in this empire where a person can come up and ask a question and walk away with a piece of paper that says they asked it, **and in about four years somebody is going to come up here and want to know whether a particular person asked, and I am going to be able to say yes with a date on it, and that is the only power anybody at this counter has.**

"That is the last one I had. And if you come back in the second week of the fourth month with that form filled in at the fourth line, I will take it, and I will not have said any of this to you, and you will not have heard any of it from me, because none of it was said in a form."

---

There is a shilling on the counter, a third of a sheet of good paper with four lines on it and a space at the top for a day, and the space at the top is blank, and the four lines are blank, and the paper is in a copyist's hand in a book with no office on it, and nobody's name is on it anywhere.

And in a locked drawer four hundred yards from that counter there is a fourth envelope addressed to a standing assessor of a furnace town, and it was written in the fourth week of the first month of last year, and it has not been sent, and it has not been opened.
