# Chapter 140: A Name at the Foot of It

The north bay of a miller's grain store off the Slade is thirty-one foot by nineteen with the floor a foot off the ground. It has a bench against the north wall that came with the building and a bench in the middle of it and a door in the west end whose lintel has dropped about half an inch in about forty years and which has never been packed and does not shut.

**Doreth Ferrow is sixty-four and has kept this meeting for nineteen years.** She cannot read a paragraph and has not pretended to for fifty years. She can add, she can count a room, and she has about sixty children under sixteen in that cut and has met the two youngest of them.

The last week of the sixth month is cold in a way the fifth month was not and the bay is full at about the seventh hour because the weather has turned and there is nowhere in ninety-one households with a fire in it that is not somebody's kitchen.

There is a sheet on the bench in the middle of the room with a seal on it and about nine words at the foot of it, and it has been read out twice already and is going to be read out twice again.

---

The first reading was by a book-keeper of thirty-eight and took about eleven minutes. The second was by a girl of seventeen who is not paid and will not say what it means, and it took about nine minutes, and the second one was the slower one.

What it says, in the whole of it, is this.

> *In a matter touching a water guarantee sealed under the fire-and-water device and offered to an unincorporated company working a public cut, a notary's table of a House was asked to be satisfied of two things.*
>
> *It was satisfied of one. It certified that the Company is an unincorporated body upon a register, that it has no head, that no court may make it a party, and that a body which has been given a duty and cannot refuse it has accepted it. **That certificate is true and it is worth nothing.**
>
> *It refused the other, in these words, which are copied from the original: **a book with names in it is not a list of persons.** The book has about nine hundred and forty names in it over about sixty years, and no heading at the top of it, and about four hundred of them are of persons who are dead, and the number of households is not in the book and is a count written on the back of a person's hand.
>
> *A seal is a promise that the paper and the ground are the same thing. The notary refused to put one on a statement that is true of a book and false of a cut.*
>
> *The concession with which that House certifies on this reach is entered as lapsed. **The name of the person who gave it up is at the foot of the entry and it is not the name of a House.**
>
> *Nothing in this instrument affects any person in ninety-one households. No person has been ordered to do anything. No household owes any sum. No fund has been found. The guarantee stands offered and unanswered and may be taken up at any time afterwards by a person answering in its terms.*

And at the foot of the sheet, in a hand about five hundred years old, is a dragon's name.

---

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

Then Doreth Ferrow said, "**Read the last line again.**"

The book-keeper read it again. She made him read it a third time, which nobody asked him to and which took him about eleven seconds longer.

"You have all read that and not one of you knows what it is," she said. "So we are going to do it the way we do it. Rennick. **What is a concession.**"

He said: "It is a licence. It lets that practice put a seal on a water paper on this reach, and without it they cannot, and nothing else."

"And what is a seal."

"It is a promise that the paper and the ground are the same thing. **It is not a decoration and it is not a signature, and a seal on a thing that is not true is not a mistake, it is a lie with a House's name on it.**"

"And what has just happened."

"A House has lost the right to say that kind of thing about water on this reach, and a man has written his own name at the foot of the page that says so, and about four hundred miles away in a book about nine hundred years old there is a line with his name on it, and in about four years anybody can pay four pence and read it."

Doreth Ferrow stood at the west end of her own bay with her hands behind her back, which is where she puts them.

"**Then say the next bit, and say it in the plainest words anybody in this room can stand.**"

---

Here is what she said, and it took about four minutes, and nine people heard all of it and about four of the nine understood it, and the woman of sixty-four had worked it out in a cold corridor four miles away about a fortnight earlier and had been carrying it around in her own head ever since.

"**A person who is not a person in the world can be made to pay for a thing by having his name written down.**

"That is the whole of it. He is a dragon. Nobody in this bay has ever been asked to think of a dragon as a person who pays anything, and a dragon cannot be sued, cannot be fined, cannot be sent to a district house, cannot be made to carry a stone up a bank, and cannot be asked.

"**And a name is a road. That is the only thing a name is. It is not a punishment and it is not a debt and it is not a party and it is not a vote. It is a road, and about four hundred yards down that road there is a house with four pence in a fund and about a hundred and forty years in it, and a person can walk up the road and pay the four pence and stand in the room and read the name and say, *that is the man who lost it, that is the man who did it on purpose, and now I know whose it was.***

"And an institution cannot do that. **An institution cannot be sued and a person can be. That is the whole of the difference and it is not a fair one and I am not going to stand in this bay and pretend it is.**

"So. A dragon can pay. **A dragon can pay by having his name written down, and the price of the payment is that he is findable for ever, and that is the only way anybody in the world has ever been able to make one of them pay for anything and it is going to cost a person about four hundred miles of his life and he has to agree to it before it happens.**"

---

Then she said the other thing, and it is why this chapter is not only about a dragon.

"**And I am going to say the second half of it and nobody is going to like it, including me, and I have been a chair for nineteen years and I have never had to say a thing like it.**

"That is a name at the foot of a page. I have spent a month in this room telling about thirty people that a name in a book is a chain round about sixty necks. I said it in the mud in a mill yard in the fourth month and I have said it every time anybody has come to that bay since, and I meant it, and I was right.

"And on the sixth day of the third month a book-keeper of thirty-eight wrote the miller's name against an entry in that book on purpose, so that the Company would be easy to compel itself, and I have watched what that did to a man of about fifty for eleven weeks, and on the eleventh day of the fifth month a man of about fifty opened a letter that had a body's name on it, in this room, in front of all of you, and he told us that he was the door to it and had been since the ninth.

"So the same thing has happened twice in about four months, about four hundred yards apart. **Once to a man in a cooperative, who chose it, and once to a man in a House, who had it chosen for him, and the difference between those two is about a hundred and forty years of law and about four hundred and thirty miles of road, and the result on this bank is identical: there is a name in a book and a person who can be found.**"

"**I am not saying the two are the same. I am saying a name is a road and that I have spent a month teaching this room that and I have just been shown what a road is for, and it is for the person who walks up it, and about four years from now somebody is going to walk up one about a man of fifty who opened a letter, and he will not have a dragon behind him, and he will not have a hundred and forty years, and he will have a mill.**"

---

Then she asked the question she has never asked in nineteen years, and the bay found out it had been sitting in one for about two months without knowing.

"**Who came to this bay in the fourth month.**"

That is the nine people who stood in the mill yard at about the seventh hour on the morning after the water, in nine different places in a hundred and forty yards of beaten earth, facing the mill, not carrying anything and not asking for anything, the way you stand at a gate.

"**And why.**"

It took about half an hour and it was not a comfortable half an hour and about nine people had come for the fourth time in about two months and one of them had come for the fifth.

Four of them could not say. Two said *to see*. One said *to help*, and nobody in the room believed him and he knew it and stayed in the room.

And a man of about thirty said, in about nine words, and it was the fifth answer and it was the fourth week of the sixth month and he said it to the middle of the floor:

"**I wanted to see if it worked.**"

---

Nobody said anything.

Then a woman of about forty-four at the third house said, "That is not a bad thing to want."

"It is the most frightening sentence anybody has said in this bay," said Doreth Ferrow, "**and I have had a dragon lose a hundred and forty years in this room tonight and that did not frighten me as much as that did.**"

"Why not."

"Because a dragon losing a licence is a thing that happened in another building and it cost a person who could see it happening. **And a man standing in a yard watching a household to see whether being helped works is a thing that happens in about four years, in about nine yards, on this cut, and nobody will have been asked, and I do not know what I am supposed to do about it and I am sixty-four and I have one question left in me and I have just spent it.**"

"Are you going to ask him why."

"**No. He told me why. I asked and he told me and that is what asking is. What I am not going to do is stand in nine yards over the next four years making sure that is what a person came for.**" She turned round to the book-keeper. "Write that down and put the day on it and put it in the book and not on the back of my hand, and I want it in your hand and not mine."

He wrote it and read it back and she made him read the last of it back twice.

> *The twenty-ninth day of the sixth month. A person came to this bay in the fourth month to watch a household that had been helped and was asked, in a room of about thirty, why, and said: to see if it worked. I record it because I asked and because nobody had asked in nineteen years, and because a chair is not a person who is exempt. **And I record that I am not going to do anything about it, because the whole of what a chair can do is ask, and I have asked, and a person answered, and that is the end of what a chair is.**

---

The book has four things in it in a month with a name at the foot of them and a book with no heading at the top of it and about nine hundred and forty names in it over about sixty years.

The last of the four is a sentence about a dragon's name on a page, and it is the first thing anybody in ninety-one households has ever entered in that book about anything that is not work.

---

A copy of the sheet went up a towpath on the thirtieth of the sixth month in a hand and not in an envelope, at four pence, and the person who carried it is not paid and was not asked to and is going anyway, and the whole of the cost of that is that he is not paid and cannot be, and it is not going to be paid, and that is the arrangement.

It is going north. Not to a court, because there is no court here that can do anything about it, and not to a table, because the table has already said what it can and cannot certify and has put it in a clerk's own hand with a day on it. **It is going to a waystation on the northern marches, about four hundred and thirty miles the other way, where House Sorn's border law requires that a thing offered to a border household at no charge be answered, and where the Sorn border households answered the same form in the second month of this year and refused it and the refusal is entered.**

Nobody on the Slade Cut has seen that entry. A factor of forty-six has seen the title of it at a trade counter in a city about nine hundred miles up the road and has not read it.

**It is the only document anybody has mentioned in four months in which a House was made to put something in a book about itself and could not leave it out, and it is the only one anybody on that bank can point at, and it is four hundred and thirty miles away, and there is a man walking.**

And the things that are live on the last day of the sixth month, and there are five of them and nobody has done anything about any of them, are these.

**A clerk of a court four hundred and thirty miles from that bank holds a ford for a year and the review of his term is in the first week of the fifth month of next year, and nobody has promised to come to it, and the paper says that if nobody comes then it stops on the day and there is no successor and nothing carries.**

**A seat's notice has been declined by a foreman of fifty-one in nine words and a district court has been asked, in writing, for the first time in nineteen years, and has answered with five names, and the seat's carriage of anybody should be heard is live and inoperable and will stay inoperable, because it pays carriage and not attendance.**

**A list of persons is on a bench in a hired bay with a line at the foot of it and no figure against that line, and it is blank, and a factor of forty-six has said out loud in that bay that he is not able to refuse it.**

**A water guarantee under the fire-and-water device stands offered and unanswered on a bank about four hundred and forty foot long with about sixty children under sixteen inside it, and it costs nothing, and it can be taken up at any time afterwards by a person answering in its terms, and there is no such person and there is not going to be one.**

**And a book in a stone store has a rule in it written on the twenty-eighth day of the third month, entered a second time in the second week of the fifth month, that binds about thirty people and says nothing whatever about the other about sixty, and both figures are the same figures as they are on the back of a woman's hand in two other places, and nobody has joined them and nobody is going to.**

A day has been fixed for the third week of the seventh month, by a chair, in a bay, for the purpose of asking a body whether it will accept a duty that stops.

**A body cannot answer that, and a body cannot refuse it, and a body cannot be made to stop. And the whole of the arrangement that would work is a person, and there are about sixty of them needed, and there is no list.**

---

The woman of about thirty-one came back up that towpath on the twenty-third of the fourth month and did not come again, and nobody sent for her, and that is the fourth thing in this matter that has been left alone on purpose and it is the only one of the four that nobody has written down anywhere.

Rennick Adley wrote it down anyway, on the thirtieth, in his own hand, in a book with no heading, at the bottom of a page, and he read it back to about nine people in a bay that evening and made the second reading for the people who could not read it.

> *The first house at the north of the ford came down on the twentieth day of the fourth month and was told there was nothing. She was not there on the twenty-third and nobody has sent for her and nobody is going to. She is a person in a form by description and she has been asked one question about her own name and refused it. **I am not going to go up that lane a second time on my own account and I have written that down so that in about four years there is one sentence in a book that says a person was not asked twice, in a thing where everybody else in this matter has been asked two or three times and did not know it.***

And the woman of about seventy-one, who cannot read a paragraph, and who is the fourth house and not the first, and who has a leg and who walked four miles of towpath in about two hours each way on the advice of nobody, sent a message down the lane in the third week of the sixth month, and it was nine words and a person carried it and the person was not paid.

> *Tell them I have read it twice and I am one of the five. Tell them I would like the asking again.*

**A person of seventy-one who cannot read a paragraph asked, in writing, to be asked again, and nobody on the Slade Cut has been able to work out, in about nine days, whether that is a request or a warning.** It is the fifth thing that is live and it is the smallest one and it is the only one anybody has asked for.

The person who carried the message down the lane had a copy of a form with a dragon's name at the foot of it in his coat, and he was going four hundred and thirty miles north with it, and he asked the man of seventy-one, at the door, in four words, whether he would want to come, and the man said he would not and gave a reason, and the reason was good, and the carrier wrote it down and read it back and it went into the book the same night with the day on it.

The reason was: **I cannot stand in a room with about thirty people in it for nine minutes, and I would rather be asked at my own door four times a year than be in that bay once, and both of those are my choice and I have been able to make one of them and not the other and I do not know which one that is.**
