# Chapter 148: The Choice About Forty-Nine

The room in the Roll Hall corridor has a stove in it and a bar on the inside of the frame and a window onto a wall about nine feet off, and a bundle of proofs on the desk that is not about this.

**Marek Kest is thirty-eight. He holds no certification, is entered in no register of persons who read, has no office and no fund, and is under a formal watch entered in one sentence on the nineteenth of the ninth month of last year, together with an eligibility, deliberately, because a court that puts them in two sentences may be asked to move one of them.**

The watch is the price and it is two sentences and he has them by heart and neither of them has been amended since the nineteenth of the ninth month of last year. The first one lets somebody from an office read anything he writes, and the office is not named and cannot be named. The second one will not let him sit in a room with nobody in it who does not come from that office, and the only person in this matter who does not come from it is in the corridor outside the door with a satchel, having come down the same road in the same cart, because a court order requires it.

Neither of them has said that. It has been four months.

The fourth week of the ninth is the fourth time this year he has been four hundred and thirty miles from that desk, and the whole of what it costs is out of seventeen shillings and a penny the week, and nobody pays his carriage, and the woman in the corridor has said that out loud in three rooms in five months.

---

The thing that came up the road before he did was a form with two questions on the back of it, carried four hundred and thirty miles in a hand and not in an envelope, at four pence, by a woman of about twenty who walked six hours and would not take anything.

The first question was the one a notary's table asks every person it reaches, and it had been refused in the fourth week of the sixth by a man in a room with a stove in it, and the refusal is in two hands in a book with no office on it four hundred and thirty miles up the river.

The second question was this:

> *And what is the paper for.*

Four words, a question mark, in the same hand, on the other side of the same sheet, in the same ink, in the ninth month of this year, by a book-keeper of thirty-eight in a bay a hundred and forty yards below a ford.

Nobody on that bank had thought to ask it. A factor had asked a woman of sixty-four what a person would have to be able to do to refuse a guild, and a carter had asked a foreman what he would have asked the next person, and a district court had written a reason into a box that was two lines deep. And a book-keeper had written four words on the back of a form and had them carried four hundred and thirty miles by a person who is not paid, at four pence, and had not told anybody he was sending it.

---

He got it right the first time in a bay, in about nine minutes, standing, to about thirty people, and he is going to say it here exactly as he said it there, because she wrote it down and it is in her hand and she has read it back once.

"**It is for a person to be found by it. And nothing else.**

"**That is all a paper does. It does not do the work, it does not stop the water, it does not make anybody safer, it does not make a child not a debtor. It makes a person findable.** A clerk at a window in Auremar can find you because there is a copy of the Venn certification on a wall in your district. A factor can find the man who was on a bank on a day because there is a list of persons in a strongbox nine hundred miles up a road. A magistrate can find a man because a foreman wrote his name at the foot of a return. **Every single thing anybody in this matter has found in fourteen months has been a person being found by a piece of paper, and not one of them has been a fact about the water.**"

Somebody said, "Then why write any of it."

"**Because the alternative is a man with a tray and a good coat, and he has never once been worse than the paper.**" He did not stop. "**That is the honest answer and it took me a fortnight on a road to get to and I have got it in nine words and I would like somebody to write them down, because they are the whole of what four months has been and they are not a comfort.**"

---

Then he said the second thing and it is the sentence this volume is built on, and it took him about four minutes, and about nine people in that bay could not follow it and one of them repeated it to the person next to her and that is the fourth of a rule written on the twenty-eighth of the third month.

"**An exception is a piece of paper.**

"It has four lines and a day on it. That is all. And the whole of the difference between an exception and a speech is this.

"**A speech can be made about people who are not in the room.** I have made one. I made one in the fourth week of the sixth month about about ninety-one households and I stood in a room with a stove in it in a city and I said *a person who speaks for ninety-one other people is not hearing them, he is speaking for them*, and that was true, and I was a party to the whole of it, and I could have made that speech with an empty room and the sentence would have been identical.

"**A paper cannot.** A paper with a name at the foot of it requires that somebody has been asked. Not that somebody has been spoken to. Not that somebody has been written down. **Asked.** And if the person was not asked then the paper is a speech that somebody has written down and got notarised, and the notarising is the only difference, and in this matter that is the whole of what a notary's table is: a machine for putting a seal on a speech and calling it a document.

"And that is the only law in this matter that has never once been broken. **Not one of them, in fourteen months, in four hundred and thirty miles, in about nine hundred rooms.** Not by a House, not by a guild, not by a court, not by a dragon and not by a foreman. Every form anybody in this empire has put in front of a person in the last fourteen months has been drafted in the direction of making it possible to say yes without asking, and not one of them has succeeded, and I do not believe that is anybody's doing. I think it is just what happens."

---

Then Rennick Adley read the count out, in a bay, and it took about four minutes, and the second reading was slower.

> *The twenty-fourth of the ninth. Asked: about twenty-eight of the sixty wanted. Yes: seventeen. No: twelve, with reasons, entered, not argued with and not asked a second time. **Of the seventeen, one is the foreman of the Slade Cut and one is a person entered as a description and not by name, at her own insistence, twice.** Not asked: the balance, and the foreman has refused to have that sum written down in a book and has given her reason and the reason is that a moving number in a book stops being a record and becomes an opinion.*

"And the reader," said Rennick Adley.

"And the reader is in the rotation, at forty-five pence a day, like everybody else, and she is not engaged yet and nobody has asked her, and I am going to say now, in front of about thirty people, **that a person who reads and is not in the rotation is a person who cannot be told to stop**, and that a foreman of fifty-one worked that out on the seventeenth of this month and a carter of thirty-one put the question that led to it, and neither of them is a lawyer and neither of them has ever been in a room with a table in it."

---

Then he put the choice in writing, on the back of a proof sheet, which is what it is always written on.

This is the sixth time. He has used it five times in fourteen months and each time it caught something: a word, a fact, an absence, an answer, and once a thing he did not know he was going to be asked.

> *M. Kest. The twenty-fourth day of the ninth. The choice is one of two.*
>
> *One: I write the instrument on the strength of seventeen answers out of about sixty. It is a valid instrument. It will hold. The consequence moves to seventeen people and then to about forty-one, and there are about sixty children under sixteen behind a bank on this cut who are in it, and none of them will be asked, because the children of a person who has said yes are in it whether they are asked or not. **The instrument is one sheet of paper and it takes about eleven minutes and there is no legal reason on this earth why it cannot be written on forty-one people who have not been asked, and about twenty-eight of the sixty have not been.**
>
> *Two: I write nothing until every person the consequence can land on has been asked, and the bank is held by me and by nobody else until then, out of a wage of seventeen shillings and a penny the week, for about eleven more weeks, and the first exception stays in force that whole time, and it guarantees every future flood on that cut, and about sixty children are in that as well.*
>
> *Answer in a room, in words, with a date.*

He read it out and gave the sheet to her and did not hold it.

---

She read it twice, which is the rule, and then she took about four minutes, and then she answered, and it is not what he expected and it is not a legal answer, and she said so twice.

"One. **You are wrong and I am going to tell you which part.**"

"Which part."

"**The first part. Both of the halves are wrong and the first half is wrong in a way that has taken me fourteen months to see and that you have just said out loud in this room without noticing that you had said it.**" She turned the sheet round. "**You have written *none of them will be asked, because the children of a person who has said yes are in it whether they are asked or not.* That is a true sentence and it is the finding of this volume and it is the reason a body in a box is worse than the substituted wording and it is the reason a cooperative is a chain with a heading.**

"And you have written it in the middle of a form that asks you to choose between writing a paper about forty-one unheard people and not writing one. As though those were the two ends of it. They are not. They are two ends of a question you have not asked.

"**The real question is: what happens to a child.** And nobody in this matter has ever put that on a piece of paper and offered it to anybody, and you have just put it on a piece of paper and used it as scenery."

"Then tell me the question."

"**I am forbidden to advise. My third line forbids it and advice by proximity is the ordinary way advice happens and I have said that sentence to you in four rooms and I am not going to unsay it.**"

"Then what is this."

"**This is a fact.** A fact is not advice. A fact is a thing that is true whether you act on it or not, and I have a drawer with four things in it and one of them has not gone out since the first month of last year, and if I could give advice I would not, because advice is a thing a person hands you at no cost and it costs about four hundred and thirty miles to be useful."

---

Then she answered the choice, in words, in the bay, with a date, in front of about thirty people, because the rule says a room and there were thirty of them and it is the sixth time the rule has been used and the first time it has been answered in a bay four hundred and thirty miles from a clerk's desk.

"**One is not available to you and I am not saying that because I have standing.**

"**You cannot write it on forty-one people who have not been asked, because the instrument says in its own second line that no person is to be entered on the schedule who has not been asked in that person's own doorway, and you wrote that line yourself in the second week of the seventh month and you wrote it because Rennick Adley walked up a lane on the seventh of the seventh and came back with three answers and two noes, and it is the best sentence anybody has written in this matter and it is four months old.**

"So the instrument forbids you. Not me. **The instrument forbids you, and you are the person who wrote it, and that is the first time in fourteen months that anything anybody in this matter has written has stopped me doing anything, and I want you to notice what it was: it was not a court and it was not a House and it was not me. It was a line you put in yourself in a room with a stove in it in the second week of the seventh month because a man asked a woman a question in a doorway.**"

"Then the choice is two," he said.

"**The choice is two. And the date is the second of the fifth month of next year, and the reason is that it is the day your own term is reviewed and if the instrument is not written by then the bank is held by you alone and by nobody else, and the second exception stops on that day because that paper says it stops on that day, and there is no successor and nothing carries.**"

"So the answer to the choice is the review."

"**The answer to the choice is the review, and you knew that in the second week of the seventh month, and you put the choice in writing anyway, and I want that entered: the choice was not necessary and you made it because you have not slept properly since the fourth of the fourth month.**"

---

Then he did the thing, and it is the only thing in this chapter that anybody came for.

They made him do it in the bay because a bay is where a thing gets said. He read nine sentences and then he gave them to the girl of seventeen, who is not paid, and who read them out twice, the second reading slower, and about nine people in that room could not read a word and were spoken to by a person sitting next to them.

> *This is the withdrawal of an instrument. The instrument is dated the twelfth of the fourth month, is nine pages long, has my name at the top of the first page, and its effect was that the flood authority of the district in respect of the spring works at the third-of-the-way mark should be discharged by the Company working the Slade Cut. It is in force. **A Company cannot refuse it and it guarantees every future flood on that cut by never asking anybody twice, and about sixty children under sixteen are inside it, and the clause that takes the children in is on the third page and the clause that says no child may be sued is on the fourth, and both are honest, and the second one does not stop the first one and does not need to.***
>
> *I withdraw it. From the date at the foot of this page, the spring works at the third-of-the-way mark are held by nobody under that instrument.*
>
> *And I record the whole of the consequence of withdrawing it, in my own hand, because a withdrawal that does not say what it costs is a speech somebody has written down.*
>
> ***About nine acres and about thirty-one households are behind that bank. The district's flood authority in respect of those works is undispensed and has been since the first week of the first month and there is no office in this district with power to give it to anybody, because the keeper gave that authority away in writing in the first month to two people without asking either, and I was one of them, and she was right to, and it cannot be got back.***
>
> *So from this morning the bank at the third-of-the-way mark is held by me alone, at seventeen shillings and a penny the week, out of my own wage, until a person is named on a list of works and takes it for a season.*
>
> ***And this is the sentence I have to say and I am going to say it in front of about thirty people and not in a room with a stove in it: the fourth month of next year is when it rains on this cut and there are about thirty-one households behind that bank and about sixty of them are under sixteen, and if I have not got somebody on a list of works by then, then I have put about sixty children on a bank on my own, by myself, in a city, and there is no form in this empire for asking them whether they would rather it was me.***
>
> *This withdrawal takes effect on the second of the fifth month of next year, and not before, and on that day the first season of a list of works begins, and the two are one document and not two, and there is no morning in between, and that is the only reason it is not the worst thing I have ever done.*

---

Then Tamsin Rook said the price of it out loud in that bay, in about four minutes, to about thirty people, and she said it before he could and she said she was going to say it and he was not going to stop her.

"**That is nine weeks of a man's wage and it is not the price.**

"**The price is this: for nine weeks there is exactly one name against that bank and it is his, and if the bank goes in the fourth month of next year it goes onto one household, in a city, about four hundred and thirty miles away, and nobody in ninety-one households will be able to find him and he will be able to find all of them. That is the arrangement he has been building since the fourth month of the fourth and this is the first time he has said it out loud in a room where about thirty people can hear it and he said it himself and I did not have to make him.**

"And I am going to say the second half of it and he is not going to like it and I am not going to be tender.

"**You are going to do this alone and you are going to tell yourself it is humility, and it is not. It is that you have not found out whether anybody else can, and in about nine weeks you are going to find out, because about forty-one people are going to be asked by somebody who is not you, at about nine doorways a week, by a woman who cannot read a paragraph and a foreman who has never volunteered for anything and a carter who is on the list last because he asked to be.**

"And when that happens you are going to be a man with nothing on his wrist and a term and no bank, and it is going to be the first time in fourteen months that you have handed something to somebody, and I do not know what you are going to do with your hands."

---

He did not thank her. He said, "**I am not thanking you for that and I am not going to and you told me in the fourth week of the sixth month that if I did you would write it down.**"

"I have written it down," said Tamsin Rook. "That is not a reward, that is a record, and I want it understood in this room that the two of us have not had a moment in four months and there is not going to be one, and that I am here because a paper says so, and that the paper is not why."

And a book-keeper of thirty-eight in a bay in the seventh month of this year said, "**I will enter that in a book with no heading if the two of you want it in one.**"

"**No,**" said Tamsin Rook.

"**Then I will enter my own half of it,**" said Rennick Adley, "**and hers is hers.**"

---

There is a line about two inches long on the inside of Marek Kest's left wrist and it answered nothing that week and nobody looked at it except him, twice, and it is a symptom of a legal burden and not a separate power, and it reads nothing, decides nothing, imposes no geographic tether and no obligation that is not written on a paper in front of about thirty people, and **it does not know where the ford is.**

**The mark is not the price. The wage is the price.**

**No reading of any instrument was performed in the four hundred and thirty miles in either direction, and there is no certification in this matter and no register and no office and no fund, and the only two readings in fourteen months remain the second chapter of this story and the fortieth, and neither of them decided anything, and one of them was four years ago and cost a girl of fifteen her afternoon.**

The undertaking of the fourth of the first month is live and was not exercised in this week. It has caught a word three times, a fact once, an absence twice and an answer once, and it stands, and the rule of the twelfth month of last year was used for the sixth time and was answered in a room in words with a date, and neither of those two things is an exception and neither of them is a precedent, and the Venn certification has five operative terms and is not said in this chapter to have four.

And on the second of the fifth month of next year there is a date at the foot of a withdrawal and a date at the foot of a season, and they are the same day.
