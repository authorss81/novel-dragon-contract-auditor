# Chapter 105: A File Is Made of Other People

The Docket of Powers is three rooms off the corridor behind the third gallery, with a card in a window and no sign on the door, and the third of the three rooms is the one with the long table in it, and the long table has been in that room for about sixty years and it came with the room.

Marek Kest had never been in the third room. He had been in the first one twice, on the eleventh of the first month of last year, when he was examined in it for about forty minutes, and on the twelfth of the ninth of last year, when he wrote one line in a book on it and discharged a bar with the line.

The day was fixed on the twentieth of the ninth of last year in the master's own day's book and it was the twenty-second of the first month, at about the second hour of the afternoon, and it is the first time the training has been held and there is no mechanism anywhere in this matter for holding it a second time.

He came down the corridor at about a quarter before with a sheet of foolscap in his coat that he had ruled himself at his own table the night before, and Tamsin Rook came down the corridor behind him at about the same time and a minute later, and neither of them said anything about the minute, and the minute is a fact about the arrangement of two people who have been walking corridors together for fourteen months and who have never once discussed where they go.

Oswin Marrow had the file on the table when they came in. It is about an inch and a half thick and it is the Docket's own file of the two entries in the register of persons who read, and the examination, and it has been on a shelf in the first room since the eleventh of the first month of last year and it has not been opened since the day the register entry was ruled through.

"I am going to say the three powers," he said, "and then I am going to do the thing you have come about, and I say the three first so that you can see whether the thing you have come about is one of them, and it is not, and it has not been one of the three in the whole sixty years of this book and I want that visible before I open the file.

"One. I can enter a finding in a public register. Two. I can bar a person from any audit in any jurisdiction until examined. Three. I can refer one question to the court sitting in room four. I have used all three on you and I have never once pretended that a fourth existed, and there is no fourth, and I have thought about it since the nineteenth of the ninth and I have not found one."

He put his hand flat on the file.

"Now the cost, and I am saying it before I start rather than after, because a man who has had an afternoon taken off him should be told at the beginning.

"**A training is a reading of a file. That is the whole of it. It is not a certification, and no certification exists, and none is asked for, and none has been granted by this office in nine years and none is likely to be.** You are going to read this file out loud, in this room, to me, and the copyist is going to read it too, because a training is a reading of a file in a room with a person in it who is not the person being trained, and that person is not me and is not to be me, and I said that on the eleventh of the first month of last year and I have not moved.

"It is a quarter past the second hour. It will take about nine minutes to read and about an hour to do what comes after it, and that hour is an afternoon of mine, and **there is no fund for it in this office or in any other, and the person who gives the afternoon is losing an afternoon, and the court that ordered it entered that it knows who that person is in nine cases out of ten and does not know in the tenth, and this is the tenth and I have told it and nobody entered it.**"

---

He read it out. He read it in the order of the file, which is the order it was made in, and he read it in a plain voice, and he did not perform it, and Tamsin Rook read it with him about a line behind, which is a thing copyists do.

The file opens with the register's own page: the two entries, the twelfth of the ninth of the year before last and the sixth of the fourth of last year, four lines each, name, date, instrument, authority. The authority in the first entry is the grant of the limited audit of the eleventh of the third. The authority in the second is the same grant. Then a card, four lines, entered in the fifth month at a counter in four minutes, reading *independent copy exists, not produced, no power*, in the hand of a copyist of the third gallery. Then the bar, in the master's own hand, and the day of the examination. Then the examination itself.

The examination is nine pages and about two thousand words and it is the only document in the file that anybody in it said anything in.

It has the master at the top of it and a clerk of the Chancery intake desk on the left and a copyist of the third gallery on the right, and it records the questions and the answers in about the order they were asked, and it does not record who asked what, and it records a fee of four pence and a halfpence of carriage.

Marek Kest read about a third of it and then stopped and said:

"Go back nine lines."

Marrow went back nine lines.

"I did not say that," said Marek Kest.

"No," said Oswin Marrow. "It is not in there. It is in a day-book in the first room in a hand of about fifty and it says *the twelfth of the ninth, fourth hour, the bar is off, I did not want to be in a room*, and that line is the reason the bar is off, and that line is in a different book, and **that is the first of about nine things I am going to show you and it is the smallest.**

"Go on."

---

He read the rest of it and it took about nine minutes and it is the whole of what there is.

The finding of the Cinder Court of the eighth of the third is entered in it as a copy, and the master's referral of the fifteenth of the ninth is entered in it, and the answer of the nineteenth of the ninth is entered in it as a sheet with a judge's name at the foot of it. Then the entry of the twelfth of the ninth: the one-line application, the authority that has ended, the disposition struck with a single stroke, and the fourth line of the entry reading that the bar was discharged by the applicant's own line and by nothing else that has occurred in this matter.

Then there is a sheet of about a quarter of a page at the back which is headed, in the master's hand, *the file on the person*, and it is a list of nine items with the date against each, and it is a list of what other people did.

> *One. The twelfth of the ninth of the year before last. Two entries in a public register, made by this office, on a finding of two unauthorised scale readings, in the words of the office.*
> *Two. The fourth of the second. A bar entered, in the words of this office, under the second of the three powers.*
> *Three. The eleventh of the first. An examination held in this office, at the fee of this office, before two persons chosen by this office and named in the file.*
> *Four. The eleventh of the first. The finding of the examination, in the words of this office: that the bar is not discharged by the examination.*
> *Five. The third of the third. The answer of a court, in the words of that court, that a reading so performed is of no legal effect whatever, and that the record in which it was entered stands.*
> *Six. The eleventh of the third. A limited audit granted, in the words of a court, in a form that may not be carried out in any district.*
> *Seven. The fifteenth of the ninth. A question referred, in the words of this office, under the third of the three powers.*
> *Eight. The nineteenth of the ninth. An eligibility and a watch, in the words of a court, in one sentence.*
> *Nine. The twenty-second of the first. This.*

He read that out, too, and then he put the file down and did not pick it up again for about a minute and a half.

"That is the whole of the file," he said. "And I have been in nine rooms in this matter in fourteen months and I have not once been in a room with the whole of it in it, and I want to say the thing about it out loud because I think it is the finding and I think it is the only thing this afternoon is for.

"**There is not one line in that file that I wrote.** Nine items and every one of them is something that somebody else did to me, or decided about me, or charged me for, or chose to be in the room while it happened. My name is on it. My name is on about eleven lines of it, in four hands and in my own, and there is not one line in it in my hand that says anything anybody could call a decision of mine, except the one line in a different book in a different room, which is *I did not want to be in a room*, and that line is not in this file because that line is mine and this file is not.

"So this file is a document about nine other people. It is a very good file. It is the best file in this matter, I have no complaint about one word of it, and the master of it is a man who did not have to do any of the nine and did four of them and entered the fifth as his own initiative and asked for the day and a half. And the finding is not that the office is unfair. The finding is that **a file about a person is made of other people's acts, and a man who has been in fourteen months of files has not once been in one, and he has thought about what the words in files mean for about a year, and he has not noticed that he has never looked at one.**

"I have spent fourteen months teaching anybody who would listen that a name at the top of a document finds a person. That is right and it is the whole instrument of this case. And it is also, it turns out, a sentence about a *file*, and a file is not a document with a person's name at the top of it. A file is a box of what people did. And putting a name on the box does not make the box about the name. It makes the name findable."

---

Oswin Marrow said the thing that he says four times a year and never to a person, and he said it to a man this time, and it is on the page because he said it out loud with two other people in the room.

"No. **You have it half right and I am going to give you the other half and you will not enjoy it.**

"A name on the box makes the box findable *about* the name, and that is not a smaller thing, and you have spent a year making people findable on purpose and you are not wrong about why. The half you are wrong about is the other half: a file is the only thing in this empire that can put a person in a room who did not want to be in it, and a register cannot, and a file can, and **a file does it with other people's hands and never with its own, and the reason a file is stronger than a name is that a file does not have to be believed. It only has to be produced.**"

He put two fingers on it.

"Item three. An examination. Held in this office, at the fee of this office, before two persons chosen by this office. **You were in a room and it was not because anybody asked you to be in it, and there is no form in this empire for asking a person whether they want to be examined, and you have written that down yourself, and you have written it down from the other side of it, and you are the only person in this matter who has ever been on both sides of that sentence.**

"And item nine is this, and it is on this list because I put it on this list, and I put it on this list so that the file would say the same thing about this afternoon that it says about the other eight, and that is what I have done and it is the only piece of authorship I have put into a file in twenty-two years and I have just told you about it out loud."

Then he said the part of the afternoon that was the afternoon.

"You are eligible. You have been eligible since the nineteenth of the ninth and nobody has asked you whether you want to be, because there is no form, and I have looked for one twice. **A training is not a certification. I am not certifying you and this office cannot and there is nobody in this empire I know of who can.** What this office has done is put a man in a room with a file and a copyist and an afternoon, and what that is worth is exactly what a file is worth, which is that in four years it can be produced against him in front of anybody.

"So I am going to ask you the question I have never asked, and it is not about the file. **What do you want to be able to do, and what are you willing to have produced about you, and I am asking them in that order because the second one is the one nobody in this matter has ever asked anybody.**"

Marek Kest said: "I want to be able to read a file and not be able to argue with it. And I am willing to have this one produced, and I would like it produced with a line in it saying that the training cost the master of the office an afternoon and that there is no fund, because in four years somebody is going to read a register and see a name and be satisfied, and nobody is ever going to be satisfied by a name, and a name is the cheapest thing anybody in this empire produces."

Oswin Marrow wrote it down. He wrote about nine lines, and he read them back, and made Marek Kest read them back, which he does with everybody, and then he added one line of his own and read that back too.

> *Held the twenty-second of the first month at the second hour in the third room of this office. The applicant read a file out loud in this room in nine minutes. This is entered as a training and not as a certification, and no certification is granted and none is asked for. The cost of the afternoon is the master's and there is no fund for it in this office or in any other, and the court that ordered the training entered that it knows who gives the afternoon in nine cases out of ten and does not know in the tenth, and this was the tenth, and nobody entered it and I have entered it. The applicant asked that the cost be on the record and it is on the record.*
> *— O. Marrow, master, Docket of Powers.*

---

He walked to the door with them and stopped, and said the last of it, and it is the fourth thing he says to a person on the way out of a room and it is about a card in a window and it took four minutes.

"I have had a card in that window for twenty-two years and I have had four hundred and ten people in this room in thirty years and I have never in twenty-two years had a person come in and ask me what a name in this book is *for* before they wrote one. You asked me on the twelfth of the ninth, on the stair, in four words, and I have thought about the four words for four months and I have not stopped.

"And I will say the thing about the copyist, because she is standing here and she is the tenth and I am not going to do it behind her back.

"**A room a watched person is in has to have somebody in it who is not from the office that watches him, and there is one such person in this matter and she is not from it and she cannot be instructed and she is here this afternoon because a court said so, and neither of them has said so out loud in a room to me, and I have known for four months and I have said nothing, and I am saying it now because I am sixty and I have been the master of a register of persons for twenty-two years and this is the first afternoon in my life in which the instrument and the protection are the same person, and I would like somebody to write that down who is not me.**"

Tamsin Rook said, "You have written it down."

"I have written it down badly and with my name at the bottom of it," said Oswin Marrow. "That is the best that this office can do and it is worse than a page in a copyist's book and both of you know that and neither of you has said it and I have given you about an hour."

---

In the Received Room that night, with the stove in and the bar on the inside of the frame and eleven feet of shelf and a bundle on the second shelf he has not opened, Marek Kest wrote out the whole of the afternoon in his own book, and read it back, and crossed nothing out, and it is the last of it.

> *The twenty-second of the first month, at about the ninth hour. I have been trained. I have written that word down and I have read it back and it is still the right word and I am not going to improve on it by calling it something else.*
>
> *What the training is. A man read a file out loud in a room with two people in it and the file was about him and had nothing in it that he wrote. Eleven lines with his name on them and not one decision. A file is a box of what other people did and putting a name on the box does not make the box about the name. It makes the name findable, and findable is not the same as real, and I have spent a year telling anybody who would listen that a name finds a person, and it does, and I had never once looked inside one.*
>
> *What I have taken out of it, and it is one thing and it is not a power and nobody is going to be impressed by it. **Write down what you are reading before you copy it, and say who is not in the room.** That is the whole of the method. A woman at a clerk's window four hundred and thirty miles from this desk has been doing it for nine years in a book with no office on it, in the first person, before she copies anything, and I have known about that wall for fourteen months and I have never once asked her how she does it, and I am going to ask her, in a room, on a day, and I am going to write the day down when I have.*
>
> *What I have not done. I have read no instrument tonight and I have read nothing tonight at all except nine pages about other people, and there is no mark on me, and there is no reading in this matter that is of any legal effect, and I want that written down in my own hand in a book that nobody can reach, because in four years somebody is going to be told that a Continuity Auditor read something on the twenty-second of the first month and there is no such thing in this world yet and there may not be for another four years.*
>
> *What it cost. An afternoon of a man of about sixty with a card in a window and no fund, entered in his own book with his own name at the foot of it, and about nine minutes of a file that I will not be able to unhear for about a month, and the whole of what is in it is other people, and I have not yet worked out whether that is a comfort or an indictment and I have about a month and a river to find out in.*
>
> *And the fourth envelope is in the drawer with the lock on it and it is addressed to a standing assessor of a furnace town four hundred and forty miles from here and it was written in the fourth week of the first month of last year and it has not been sent for a year and it is not sent tonight and I did not open it, and the reason I did not open it is that the reason I wrote it is a reason I had at the time and the reason I have not sent it is a different one, and the second reason is that the third time somebody in this matter has asked me a question in a room I have been the person who could answer it and I have answered it and I have been wrong about a fact once and caught nothing else, and I am not going to be a man who goes to a drawer at about the ninth hour and starts something.*
>
> *I am going four hundred and thirty miles in the first week of the second month. A question came up a road to me in the third week of the first month and I have had four days to decide what to do with it, and what I have decided is to go and stand in the room where the question is and let somebody else put their hand on the paper, and the reason is the sentence at the top of this page, and I would not have been able to say the reason four days ago.*
>
> *M. Kest, clerk, Received Room, the twenty-second of the first month, at about the ninth hour.*
