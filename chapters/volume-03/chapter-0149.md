# Chapter 149: The Engagement, and the Answer With No Why

The north bay of a miller's grain store off the Slade is thirty-one foot by nineteen and the floor is a foot off the ground and the door in the west end has a lintel that has dropped about half an inch in about forty years, and in the second week of the eleventh the bay has a stove lit in the south end of the same building and about nine people sitting on the benches with their coats still on because it is warmer standing up.

**Doreth Ferrow is sixty-four and has kept this meeting for nineteen years and cannot read a paragraph.** She can add, she can count a room, and she has about sixty children under sixteen in that cut and has met the two youngest of them, and there is a chest in the south end of that store whose hasp has not been locked in six years and the engagement that was settled in this room is on the bench in the middle of it with a keeper's signature at the foot of it.

Nobody in that room has read it. She cannot. About nine of the thirty in the room cannot read a word of anything and are spoken to by a person sitting next to them, which is the rule of that bay, and which is going to be the last thing this volume has to say about who is standing in a room.

---

She started the way she has started about four hundred times, which is by putting the thing on the bench and then telling the room what it is not.

"There is a sheet of paper on that bench and it is a wage and I cannot read a wage and I have not pretended to for fifty years, and I am going to say four things about the cost of it before anybody asks me a question, and then the girl is going to read it out twice and the second one is slower, and that is the whole of the morning.

"**One. A reader's day is between four pence and a shilling.** Four pence is what a copy of a thing costs. A shilling is a question at a notary's table and about four days of a bay of thirty people's breakfast between them. Those are the two numbers anybody in this room has got for the price of a person sitting in a room and reading.

"**Two. This bay has four shillings a year and that is the rent of a stone store and it is taken out of the book four times a year and it is the whole of the Company's money and it has been for nineteen years.**

"So there is no money. There has never been any money. Ninety-one households have never had four pence in a pot to give a person for turning up, and I have been a chair for nineteen years and it turns out that the reason nobody has ever paid a reader is not that the reader is not worth paying. **It is that there is no such thing as four pence.**"

---

"Then somebody put a price on it," said a man of about thirty-eight at the far end. "And I have watched a man of about fifty price nine tons of his own kerb at seven shillings and sixpence in the fourth month and refuse to give them, on purpose, because a gift is money with no figure on it and is the worst kind. So say the figure, and then say why the figure is forty-five pence and not four pence."

"Because **a person who turns up is never paid and cannot be, and I have known that since the fourth of the fifth month of last year when a girl of seventeen said it in this room and I did not understand a word of it and I have had about eighteen months to understand a sentence.**

"And the reason a person who turns up cannot be paid is not the money. **It is that a person who is not paid is doing you a favour, and a favour has no end and no terms and no notice, and you cannot tell a favour to stop, and a duty you cannot stop is the whole of what this volume has been about since the ninth of the second month when a magistrate entered that a body may not be made a party.**

"So it is not four pence and it is not a shilling. **It is forty-five pence, because that is the figure on the lapsed list about a hundred yards up the road, and a reader goes in the rotation with everybody else, for a season, with a day at the foot of her name, at the same rate as the men.**"

---

Then she said the part that had to be said and that nobody had asked, and it took about four minutes, and a girl of seventeen stood up in the middle of it.

"And I am going to tell the room why that girl is standing up.

"In the third week of the ninth, in this bay, about nine people thanked her. Four or five of them said thank you and one of them said it twice and it was the first time in four months that anybody had said it to her in a room and she has been read to about nine hundred people since the fifth month and nobody had ever said it.

"And she said no, and she asked for something, and I want it in the book and I want it read out.

"**She said: enter that I have been thanked, and enter that I have asked for the thanks to be withdrawn, and enter why. And Rennick wrote it down with the day on it and read it back to her twice and the second reading was slower, and it is in that book, and I would like about nine people in this room to hear it read out because about nine of you were in the room on the twenty-first of the ninth and you have not once said anything about it since and I think some of you noticed and thought it was modesty.**"

She looked round the room.

"**It was not modesty. It was a girl of seventeen doing arithmetic. And the arithmetic is this: a person who is thanked is a person who reads for thanks, and in about four years somebody is going to come to that bay and ask her to do it for nothing, and say it is her mother's river, and she will have to say no to somebody who has already said thank you to her, and that is a harder no than this one.**

"So. **The thanks is withdrawn in the book. And I am not going to thank her this morning and I have asked nine people in this room not to thank her, and one of you is going to want to and I am telling you now so that it is a thing you are not doing rather than a thing you are managing.**"

---

The girl of seventeen said the four words, standing, without being asked, which is what she has said four times in this bay since the fifth month.

"**I read it, I do not say.**"

"**You are going to say it this morning,**" said Doreth Ferrow, "because you are going to be paid for it from the first of the fourth month of next year, and the second reading is the one you are paid for, and the first one is the one you have been doing for nothing since the twentieth of the twelfth month of last year, and I am not going to have that put in an engagement in that order.

"**You are going to be paid for both.**"

---

The keeper came four miles on the second of the eleventh and was in the yard before the bay was full.

**Sivra Oris is the keeper of the Reed Cut district water office.** In the first month of this year she put two human names into a box headed *who is to answer* on a district flood sheet, without asking either of them, wrote on the face of her own sheet that she had asked nobody, left a boy of sixteen out of the box on purpose and entered the leaving-out as the only protection the office was able to give anybody, **and disclaimed the office's own flood authority in respect of the Slade spring works in writing, to do it, and it cannot be got back, and there is no form in this empire for getting it back.**

She is a keeper and not a lawyer and she has not asked anybody for anything in four months and about four people in that district have noticed that and about nine have not.

She put a single sheet on the bench with a date on it and her own signature at the foot of it and nothing else on the table, and then she stood at the west end and let the room do it.

---

The girl of seventeen read it out.

> *Engagement, in writing, of a person to read to a list of works and to any person in any house behind the bank, being about nine persons who cannot read a paragraph, and to do the second reading and not only the first.*
>
> *Four days a week. Forty-five pence a day, the same rate as every other person on the list.*
>
> *To begin on the first day of the fourth month of next year.*
>
> *For a term, to be stated at the foot of the list of works, and the reader may give it up on one day's notice in her own hand, and the notice is the same notice every other person on that list may give.*
>
> ***This is a written engagement and not a verbal one, and the person engaged may have a copy of it, and there is a copy.***

She read it out twice and the second reading was slower, and about nine people in that room could not read a word of it and were spoken to by a person sitting next to them.

And then she said, out loud, in that bay, before anybody had asked her anything:

"That is the first time in five months that a sheet has been read out in this room with my name at the top of it. And I asked for the written one. I asked twice. I asked a keeper of a water office to put it in writing instead of saying it at a gate, and she said no the first time.

"**And I am not thanking anybody, and I have asked about nine people not to, and I want the asking entered in the book with the day on it, and I want one more thing entered and I want it in my own hand.**"

"Say it."

"**I want it entered that I am the reader, and that the reason I asked for it in writing is that a verbal engagement is a thing a master can end at a gate in about nine seconds, and a written one has a day on it and a copy, and I have watched what a day and a copy are worth in this matter because a man of fifty-one in a shed in the sixth month said that a name at the foot of a page is a road, and I would like a road.**"

---

Then the chair asked the keeper the one question she had left, and she asked it in the ordinary way, standing at the west end with her hands behind her back.

"Sivra. **Is this because it is right, or because you cannot stop.**"

And the keeper said:

"**That is the fourth question on a board in a lane four hundred and thirty miles down this river, and you know it is, and I have been told about it by a girl of seventeen who read it out in that lane and did not say what it meant.**

"And I am not going to answer it in this bay, and I will tell you why, and it is not modesty. **Because I gave away my office's flood authority in the first month of this year on purpose, to two people, without asking either, and I have been right and everybody says so, and I have not had to do a thing since the first week of the fourth.**

"And I can still sign a wage. **That is the whole of what is left of nine years. I can put a name on a piece of paper and a day and a term and a notice and a copy, and the office can do that, and the office can do nothing else at all.**"

She looked at the sheet on the bench.

"**And the size of what I can still do is one girl's reading, four days a week, from the first of the fourth month of next year. That is it. That is the whole of me as an instrument of anything.**

"And I am going to tell you what I have found out about that and then I am not going to say anything else about it and nobody is going to ask me. **A thing that can be refused is worth more than a thing that cannot. I have had four months to work that out after the first week of the first month and the working out took me a keeper's authority with a seal on it to get. I would have given that back at the start of the second month if somebody had asked me to.**"

---

**Nobody asked her.** And the girl of seventeen wrote that down, and read it back twice.

Then the second half of the week came, and it came in a form, and it was not a surprise and it was the answer to the one thing about the first of the fourth month of next year that nobody in that bay could fix.

The district court of Mosswake had been asked, in writing, on the ninth of the eleventh, whether it would treat the second of the fifth month of next year as a date of its own, so that a foreman could act on it in a room and put a name at the foot of a thing on that day.

It is a court on a flood terrace four miles down the road. It is not the court that sat in room four in Auremar in the seventh month of last year. It is nine days younger than the other half of the winning sentence and it has never read it.

The answer came back on the sixteenth, in a hand and not in an envelope, and it is one of the ordinary forms of that court and it has a box for a reason and no box of any kind for a why.

> *Answered: no.*
>
> *Refused. **This court cannot hold a term in another person's paper.**
>
> *The order of the court sitting in room four of the eighteenth day of the seventh month last year gives a hearing and does not say when. The finding of this court of the ninth day of the eighth month last year says when. **The two are nine days out of order with each other. This court has not read the first and that court has not read this, and there is no form in this empire for one court to ask another court what it has found, and this court has tried to write one four times in nine years and has not sent it and has written why not against it three times.***
>
> *And the reason is entered with the day on it, and the box is two lines deep and this is longer and the clerk has been told it is all right.*

---

The girl of about thirty-four who wrote the form read it out twice in that bay, the second time slower, and about nine people who could not read a word were spoken to by a person sitting next to them, and she said at the end:

"And I am going to read you the last line of the letter, which is not part of the form, and which is in the magistrate's own hand, and which I have read four times and could not make sense of until about a week ago and have now made sense of and I am not sure I am right.

"**It says: I have been asked a second time in nine years and the answer is the same and I am going to keep giving it, and if the two halves of that sentence are ever put on the same table in a room with a witness in it then this court will read both and this court will not say which of them is wrong, because this court does not know, and a court that does not know should say so in a form with a day on it, and that is what I have done.**"

That sat in the room a while, and the bench at the back went quiet first.

Then Doreth Ferrow said, "**Then nobody in this bay is going to get a court to fix that day.**"

"No," said Rennick Adley. "What they are going to get is a person to turn up on it. And there is no form in this empire for compelling a person to be brought to a room, and a seat has had a standing offer open for about sixteen months to pay the carriage of anybody this court thinks should be heard, and it has never worked once, because it pays carriage and it does not pay attendance, and a woman of sixty-four worked that out on the twenty-ninth of the sixth month of last year in a room with about thirty people in it.

"**So the date is going to be kept by somebody walking. And it has to be a person who is not paid, or it is a favour, and a favour has no notice.**"

"**I am on it,**" said Tavi Venn.

"**You are on it and you are in the rotation and you have a term and a notice and a copy, and that is the first time in five months that anybody in this matter has said that sentence about a person who turns up.**"

---

The count was done at the end, in chalk on the back of a hand, and read out, and wiped off, and put in a book, and it is the last entry in that book for a long while.

> *The eighteenth day of the eleventh. Asked: about forty-one of the sixty wanted. Yes: about forty-one, with days against them. **No: about nineteen, with reasons, and no reason is the same as any other reason and about nine of the nineteen cannot read a paragraph and about four of those nine have not been asked again and are not going to be.** The reader is engaged in writing, from the first of the fourth month of next year, on a written engagement, with a copy, at forty-five pence a day, for a term, on the same notice as everybody else.*
>
> *A court will not fix the day. A seat will not pay the attendance. **The day is going to be kept by somebody walking, and there are about forty-one of them, and the first of them has asked to be last, and a chair of sixty-four has entered that untidied.***

A single sheet of paper is on the bench in the middle of a hired grain store with a keeper's signature at the foot of it and a date on it of the first of the fourth month of next year, and a copy of it is in a satchel, and about nine people in that room cannot read either of them.
