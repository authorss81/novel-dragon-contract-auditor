# Chapter 113: Not One Day Past It

The Reed Cut district water office stands two hundred yards above the gate so that a man can come up out of the water and see the office and the water at the same time, and it is a stone building with a counter, a stove in a back room, and about nine hundred forms in a shelf, and the forms are the office's whole learning. It issues flood memoranda, gate schedules and inspection dates for about four hundred and sixty households' worth of channels, and about nine or ten of the memoranda in a year are flood works, and the only one anybody ever feels is a stop.

Piotr Wray came up the two hundred yards at about the seventh hour of the tenth day of the third month with nothing in his hands, which took him four minutes to decide to do and about half an hour to walk, and the office was shut behind the counter, and the clerk let him in because he is the second name in a box on a sheet that is nailed nowhere and folded in a drawer in that building.

He is twenty-nine. He works the far end of the Slade. He is the only person on that bank who has ever been in a position to say no to anybody about anything, and on the seventh day of the first month he asked his sister whether she was going to put the Company's name on the spring list and was told that was not a question he had standing to ask her, and on the twentieth of the first month he stood in a mill yard in front of about forty people and said, in nine words, *Nobody asked me, so I am not going to sign* — and added that the box was the right box and the name was the wrong one and he was not going to be the one who is asked twice.

He said the nine words again in a kitchen on the last day of the first month, to about eleven people, when a girl of seventeen asked him whether he was going to sign the sheet, and the clerk of that court wrote what he said down and read it back to him.

The sheet itself is the ninth of the first month. It carries the office's device, the water, the works, a list of works stapled to it, and a box headed *who is to answer* with two names in it and no body anywhere on it, and four lines on its face, and the keeper's own name at the foot. The four lines say that neither person was asked and neither has been told the sheet exists, and that the Company is not entered and is not answerable, and that the district's flood authority in respect of those works is given to the two persons named and to no other person and to no body, and that **this office cannot compel the works to be done, does not claim to, and cannot be compelled to do them itself.**

And the last line of the four, in the keeper's own hand, is the line that has made the sheet work: **if either of them says no this sheet is void and the keeper will enter the void and the day.**

---

Sivra Oris had the sheet out of the drawer before he had finished saying what he had come to say, which she has done twice in nine years and both times it was to somebody who had come to be told no.

"Sit down. No — stand, if you would rather, and I will stand, because I am not going to sit down and be handed a thing I have been carrying seven weeks."

"I want to be taken out of the box."

"Yes," she said. "I know. Sit or stand."

He stood.

"Then here it is in four words, and it is the correct procedure, and I am not going to improve on it, because I wrote it and I have had seven weeks to think of something better and there is not one. **You say no. I enter void. I enter the day.** That is the whole of what you can have and it is the only thing in this matter that anybody has made that works, and I would like you to hear me say that before I tell you what it is worth, because you have come in here to be let out of a box and I am going to be the person who tells you that being let out of the box is the end of the good news."

She put the sheet flat on the counter, which is the way she does it, with the writing turned for the person standing on the other side, and it is a thing she learned from a girl of seventeen who read it out in a kitchen and had turned it round for eleven people.

"Now the price, and I am going to say it as a person and not as an office, and then you can decide which of the two of us is doing this.

"**That sheet is the only instrument in this district that gave a flood authority to a person instead of a body.** That is all it is. It is one piece of paper of the ninth of the first month in a drawer in a building two hundred yards from a gate, and there is nothing behind it — no fund, no crew, and no other piece of paper in this district that says the same thing. In about eleven months the district office will have printed a form that does the same thing and put a body in the box again, because bodies are convenient, and this sheet will go in a drawer and stay there.

"And the moment you say no, it is void, and then there is no name on that bank anywhere at all. Not mine and not yours and not anybody's. There is a box with a body in it at the office upstairs, and there is a company in a store that is not a person, and there is a foreman whose term ran out in the third week of this month and has not been renewed, and **behind the bank at the third-of-the-way place there are nine acres and thirty-one households, and I have got nothing to send any of them with.**"

"That is your side of it," he said. "What is mine."

"Your side of it is that you were entered in a box you did not ask to be in and you have had to live in it for seven weeks, and every day of those seven weeks somebody has had to know where you are and what you are doing, and your sister put your name in a list of works and you could not get the list back off her. That is real and I am not going to tell you it is small."

He put his hand on the counter, about where the box is, and did not touch it.

"Ask me the third thing," he said.

"There is no third thing."

"You have not heard it."

"I have heard it," said the keeper. "**A person does not want to be asked twice.** You said that in a mill yard in front of forty people and you said it in a kitchen in front of eleven, and a clerk of a district court wrote it down and read it back to you, and it is the best sentence anybody has said about this sheet, and it is also the one sentence in this matter that I have no way of writing down anywhere."

She said the next part standing very still, which is a thing she does.

"**Every form in this empire is a form of one of two sentences. A person accepts, or a person refuses.** I have about nine hundred of them in that room and that is what all nine hundred are for, and the flood form is one of the two, and your box is the *yes* box, and a person who is in a *yes* box and is not going to be in a *yes* box is the *no* box, and there is no third shape in any office in this district or in the district above it or anywhere I have been.

"And there is no form for me to say *give it back*. I want that on the record in this room, and it is on the record in this room, and the record is that I said it, and not one person can use it. **And there is no form anywhere for a person to say *I am here and I do not want to be asked again*, and that is the same absence twice, and it is the only authority this office has ever given away and there is no form for getting it back, and I have looked.**

"You could have it another way. I could put in the day's book, in my own hand, in the ordinary book I keep, *the person named second on the sheet of the ninth of the first month was asked on the tenth of the third month and said he was not going to be asked twice.* That is thirty-three words and it costs me about a minute and it is done in ninety seconds. **And it will not be available to anybody. Not to you, not to her, not to a court, not to a clerk. That book is mine and it is not a record and there is no office behind it. That is the whole of what this office has ever been able to give anybody in nine years, and I am telling you the price of it before I do it, because I would rather you heard the price and refused it than have it and find out in four years.**"

"Do it," said Piotr Wray.

She did it. It took her about a minute. And then she put the pen down and said, "There. And you cannot have it, and I would have written a different thing if you had asked me for the different thing, and I want you to notice that you did not ask."

---

"What if she says no as well."

The keeper put both hands flat on the counter, which she had not done before.

"Then it is void on two," she said, "and it is void on two in four words and a half, and it is **the best sentence in this district**, and I have wanted her to say it for seven weeks and I have not asked her and I am not going to and I want you to hear the reason for that as well.

"**Because a person who says no twice and a person who says no once are not the same instrument, and one of them is a refusal and one of them is a decision, and a decision made by two people who were both entered without being asked is the only thing in this matter that a magistrate four miles from here could put in a minute without lying about it.** And I get one of the two. I cannot get both by asking. I have got to be given it.

"And that is the whole of what an office can do about a person's want, and I have had nine years to get better at it and I have not got better at it, and I have got a form with a *yes* in it and a drawer."

He nodded at the sheet.

"Then say it again to me," he said. "What you said about her term. She is the one at the foot of the list."

"Her term ran out in the third week of this month and nobody has asked her to hold it again. She has said out loud in four rooms that she would rather be asked than not, and she has not been asked, and she does not know that she cannot say yes twice." The keeper turned the sheet round and squared it. "**That is between her and whoever is going to ask her, and it is not between her and this office, and if you go up that bank and tell her what is in this drawer you will have taken a thing out of a drawer that I put there, and I did not put it there for the district.**"

"You put it there for a man who had not been asked."

"I put it there so that a man who had not been asked would have something to say no to," said Sivra Oris. "**That is not the same as putting it there for him, and the difference is the whole of this room, and if I cannot hold the difference in my own head then I have no business being the keeper of anything.**"

"You are not going to say it now," he said.
"No."

"You are not going to say it this week either."

"No," said the keeper. "**And I am going to give you a date, and I am going to give it to you without being asked, and you are going to hate it, and I am going to tell you the reason before I say the day.**

"The flood season's memoranda begin in the fourth week. That is the first week any of this district writes anything about the water, and the standing of that sheet is settled by them and settled upstairs and not by you and not by me, **and I am not going up there in the fourth week to find out which way it has gone without me. The last day of the third month. Not one day past it.** Because on the last day of this month I go upstairs with it, and I am going to take the sheet with the two names in it, and the four lines on it, and the fact that you were entered and refused, and I am going to put all of that in front of a man who has to decide whether a district flood authority is given to persons or to bodies in this district in the next nine months, and I am not going to do that with a live sheet and a live refusal in it that has no date on it. The refusal is worth more with a day on it. It is worth exactly nothing without one.

"And after the last day of this month the sheet is either void or it is not, and if it is not void then a woman of fifty-one and a man of twenty-nine are the district's flood authority for four hundred and forty feet of bank, and neither of you has been asked and one of you has said he will not be asked twice, and **I will have handed that to a magistrate in a building four miles from here with my own name at the foot of it, and I am not going to pretend to you that I know what I am doing.**

"Three people know that date. You, and me, and a man in a city four hundred and thirty miles from this counter who does not know it yet."

He said, on the way to the door, in the ordinary way, the thing he had been carrying since the first month: "Are you the First Seat's, or the First Seat's agent."

"No," said the keeper. "And I said both of those in a shed on the seventh day of the first month, in the first minute of a room, before anybody asked me, and I have said them since in a yard and in a shed and every time before anybody asked, and I am not going to say them again in my own building.

"A person who has heard a thing once does not need to hear it twice. That is not a rule and it is not a courtesy, it is the only thing there is about a person being told a thing they cannot use, and the moment a keeper starts repeating herself in her own office she has begun to need somebody to believe her, and I have not got anybody here and I am not going to go and get anybody."

"You are the keeper of the office."

"I am a woman in a room with a stove in it who has just written thirty-three words in a day-book that nobody can use. And if you go up that bank and say *she told me she is not the First Seat* you will have spent the only thing this office had left, on a thing nine houses do not need."

The clerk of the office, who is about twenty-three, put the day's book down without looking at the page, and has said since that she could not read the thirty-three words upside down and that was a lie, and that she read them upside down, and that she has read them nine times since, and that this is the first thing in two years of that job that has made her want to come in on a day she is not paid for.

---

He walked the two hundred yards back down at about the fourth hour of the afternoon with nothing in his hands, the same as he came with.

He had been in the box for seven weeks and he had not once, in those seven weeks, thought about what the box was for.

He had thought about who had put him in it and whether he could get out of it. He had not once thought about the bank, and the reason he had not is that a man of twenty-nine is a person and the box is about the person, and the box had a name in it and the name was his, and a name in a box is a thing you can be asked about yourself.

**The box was never for him. The box was for the bank.** His sister had said the volume's whole question in a shed in the first month without knowing she had said it — *the district's flood authority is given to the two persons named and to no body* — and he had been in the box ever since reading it as a punishment, and it is not a punishment, **it is the only thing anybody has done on that cut in ninety-one years that made a person answer for a bank instead of making every child in ninety-one households answer for it**, and he had been standing in front of it for seven weeks being the wrong thing about it.

And the price of it is seven weeks of being findable, and it is not his price, because the bank is not his.

---

The sheet is not altered. It carries the device, the water, the works, the list of works stapled to it, two names in the box and no body, four lines on its face, and the keeper's name at the foot, and the line that says if either of the two says no the sheet is void and the keeper will enter the void and the day, and the keeper is going to hold it open until the last day of the third month and not one day past it.

Nobody in the matter knows that date except three people, and two of them were in the room, and the third is four hundred and thirty miles away at a counter in a city and does not know yet.

And about nine minutes after he had gone, the keeper opened a book on the front of her own desk that is not a form and has no office on it, and read a line in it, and it was the line she has read on the first of every month for nine years, and she read it today the same as always, and there is a person in the next room who is about eleven years old and works the channel and is called Nery and is in no record whatever.

She has never been asked about her and she is not going to be asked about her by this office, and the reason is on the face of the sheet of the ninth of the first month in four lines, and the reason is that a keeper who enters a person is the only authority this office has left.
