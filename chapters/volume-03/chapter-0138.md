# Chapter 138: The Fourth Line, and Who Can Fill It In

The Received Room has a stove in it, a bar on the inside of the frame, a window onto a wall, and eleven feet of shelf, and it is where things go when nobody wants to be the one who has them.

Marek Kest is thirty-eight and has been a clerk of it for two years and is under a formal watch entered in one sentence on the nineteenth of the ninth month of last year, together with an eligibility, deliberately, because a court that puts them in two sentences may be asked to move one of them, and that court has been asked twice in fourteen months and has moved both.

**The watch is the price and not a qualification.** A person from an office, in this matter unmentioned, may read anything he writes. He may not be in a room without a person in it who is not from that office, and the office is not named and cannot be named, because a person who signs a column headed *by whose authority* is discharging an office, and an office cannot be examined, and an office that is named and cannot be examined cannot be instructed.

There is one person in this matter who is not from that office. She is in the room because of the sentence, and he is in the room because she is, and both of them know that, and neither of them has said it, and it has been four months.

He came back down the road on the nineteenth of the sixth month, having been eleven days in a bay four hundred and thirty miles from this desk, and the room was as he had left it, and there was a letter on the shelf that was not a letter anybody had sent him.

It came to the desk of the Inheritance Desk in a bundle with three other things and the runner had put it in the wrong place and found it and left it, and it is a single side of good paper and it is not printed.

> *To the clerk of the Inheritance Desk of this city, being a person who has been named in a document touching a water guarantee sealed under the fire-and-water device.*
>
> *The instrument touches you. It touches you as follows, and the table asks the same question of every person it reaches, and it asks it in the fourth line of a form, and the fourth line is not long.*
>
> ***In what way this instrument touches you.***

And under that, in a clerk's hand at the foot, which he read and which he read again:

> *The answer is free and the answer is not required for the instrument to be true, and a person may answer or not answer and nothing follows for them either way. **A person should not answer without knowing what they are agreeing to, and there is no person in this matter who has been told what they are agreeing to.** No person has been named in the instrument. It has been offered to a body.*

He put it down on the table and then picked it up again, because he had been carrying it for about eleven minutes by then and he wanted to hold it.

**It was addressed to him by name.**

---

Here is what he worked out, and it took him about four minutes at about the ninth hour of the nineteenth of the sixth month, standing up, with the thing in his hand, before anybody came into the room.

**In the fourth month of this year nobody on the Slade could have answered that.**

He has that in his own book from the fourth counter, in a clerk's own note in a hand of thirty-four, and it says: *a party has to be a person, and for eleven weeks there was nobody on that cut who was a person an instrument could touch, and the Company is a body and cannot be made a party and has no head and there is no form for a head.*

And that was true on the eleventh of the fourth month and it was true on the thirtieth of the last month.

And it is not true now.

**Four households at the north of the ford are parties from the filing of a form on the last day of the third month and cannot be un-named, and two of them are described and a described person is a person.**

**A foreman of fifty-one has her own name at the foot of a list of works in a hired bay, and a clerk of a court four hundred and thirty miles from that bay is in the same store in a book with no heading, with a date against his name.**

**And a man of about fifty opened a letter addressed to a body in a bay in front of about thirty people and became the person it was about, on purpose, having worked out in six years that he should not.**

So the fourth line can be filled in. Somebody on that cut could put a name to it. About five of them could, this morning, in about nine seconds each, and two of the five cannot read a paragraph and one of the five is a woman of about thirty-one who has refused a name once already and would have to be asked a second time.

**And in four months this matter has done nothing whatever else except make those five.**

He sat down on the edge of the table with the letter in his hand and he was in that room about nine minutes without writing anything, which is the second time in a year he has done that, and the first time was on the eleventh of the fourth month with nine pages in a weigh-house four hundred and thirty miles from this desk.

---

She came in at about the half hour with a bundle of proofs and put it down and said, "You have been holding that for eleven minutes."

"I have been standing up for nine."

"Then sit down, and tell me what it is, and then tell me what you have worked out, in that order, because you have got a face on you that I have seen once this year and I did not like it then either."

He told her. It took about eleven minutes and he did not do it well and she let him get all the way to the end of the working-out before she stopped him, and then she said one thing.

"**Say the last of it again. The part about the five.**"

"There are about five people on that cut who could put a name to that fourth line this morning. Four months ago there was nobody. **The reason there is somebody now is this case.**"

"**Say the other half.**"

"**The other half is that it is the only thing this case has ever done that has worked.**"

"Yes," said Tamsin Rook. "And now say the thing that is underneath it, and you will not like it, and I am not going to help you get there."

---

He got there in about nine seconds.

"A person who speaks for ninety-one other people is not hearing them. **He is speaking for them.** And hearing is one person at a time, and the form has one line, and the line is for one person, and that is not a defect in the form. **That is the whole of what a form is.** It is a line with one name's worth of room on it and it was built that way by about a hundred and forty years of people who had watched what happens when you put a body in one.

"And the honest answer to that fourth line, for anybody on that bank, is a sentence about about ninety-one households. And the day anybody writes it, that person is a party. And the reason nobody could write it in the fourth month is that there was nobody to be a party. **And now there is, and that is the only reason the door is open, and the door being open is not a thing to be pleased about. It is the same finding as everything else in this matter wearing a different hat.**"

"Then what is the answer."

"**No. And I am not going to write it, and I could not write it if I tried, and here is the reason and it is the reason the whole of this matter has run on and I have not said it out loud in a room in fourteen months.**

"**If one person is to fill in that line, then the person who fills it in is the answer for the Company. And a person who answers for a body is its head. And there is no head of that Company, and a magistrate has entered that one cannot be had, and I would rather say that in a room than have a person appointed to it in a city by a person who cannot be named.**

"So the answer to *in what way does this instrument touch you* is: there is nobody to put in that box. Not because nobody will. **Because anybody who fills it in is doing the thing the instrument wants done, and doing it in the box, in the box, where it is a line with a name on it.**"

---

She wrote it down. She does that, and she has never once pretended she was doing anything else, and it is a bundle of proofs she was not working on and she put on the desk.

"Now the rule," she said.

He put it in writing on the back of a proof sheet, which is what it is always written on, and it took him about four minutes, and this is what it said.

> *M. Kest, the nineteenth day of the sixth month. The choice is one of two. One: he answers the fourth line, in whole or in part, in any form, to any table, on any matter, touching this instrument. Two: he answers nobody and nothing, and a copy of the fact that he has refused goes to the two people on that bank who can read it.*
>
> *Answer in a room, in words, with a date.*

She read it twice and then she stood up and came round the table and sat down again and did not take nine minutes about it, which she has told him since was the first time in four months that she had not needed nine minutes.

"**Two. And the date is the twentieth, and I am going to say it in about four sentences and then I am not going to say anything else about it for four days.**

"One. I agree with you and you are right and I want it entered that we agree.

"Two. The reason is not a legal one and it is the same reason as in the fourth month and it is the only reason I have got, and it is that **the moment a paper is in a bundle with a name at the top of it, somebody is going to come and find you, and you cannot be found and serve a person and also be the reason ninety-one people are inside something.** That is not a principle. That is what it has taken a man in a city four hundred and thirty miles from here over about fourteen months, and a bar discharged on a page of his own, and a line on a wrist, and I would rather you did not find that out at a cost.

"Three. **The copy goes to two people and to no body.** A copy sent to a body is a copy about ninety-one households and the difference between that and a copy sent to two people is about four hundred and thirty miles of carriage and a clerk's decision, and I made that decision in a bay in the fourth week of the fourth month and I have not changed it and I am not going to argue it again with you.

"Four. **I am not going to be thanked for it and if you thank me I will write it down.**"

He did not thank her. He said, "I am not giving you the sheet, and I am not acting on it tonight, and I will not give it to you again, and the reason is not that you are wrong."

"That is permitted," she said, "and I have thought about it for about nine minutes and I am not going to make it a condition, and I have never once made it a condition and I am not going to start."

---

Then she said, in the ordinary way, at about the second hour, and it took her four minutes, and he did not have a note for it afterwards because he did not write it down and has never wished that he had.

"Two things and they are about you and not about the paper.

"One. I asked you in a bay in the first week of this month, in front of about thirty people, four things about a line on your wrist, and you have not once mentioned it since, and it has been six weeks, and the review is in the first week of the fifth month of next year and nobody has promised to come to it. **A mark is a symptom. It decides nothing. It reads nothing. It does not know where the ford is. And you have stopped mentioning it, and I want to be plain about why that is not the same as it having gone quiet, because a thing that has gone quiet is a thing that will come back louder.**

"Two. **You are treating your body as a place to store public cases.**"

He did not say anything for a moment, and she did not help him.

"That is what it is. It is not brave and it is not admirable and it is not a thing I am going to be tender about, because the moment anybody in this matter is tender about it, it becomes a virtue, and it is not a virtue, it is a **system of storage.** You have carried a fund, a rail, a bank, a child of sixteen and about sixty children you have never met, and you are the shelving in it. And the reason you do it is that it is faster than asking, and because a person who asks can be refused and a person who carries cannot.

"**Stop. Not for me. I have no standing and I have said so in this room before and I will say it again. For the eleven people on a towpath who are going to be asked to carry something in the seventh month, because there is not going to be anybody left who can.**"

"I do not know how to stop."

"No," said Tamsin Rook. "**Neither do I, and I have been doing it for fourteen months and I have got a drawer with four things in it and one of them has been there since the first month of last year and it is not going out.** So we are two people who cannot stop. That is not a reason to carry more."

---

A copy of his own refusal went four hundred and thirty miles down a river in a hand and not in an envelope, on the twentieth of the sixth month, at four pence, to a foreman of fifty-one at the Slade end and a book-keeper of thirty-eight in a hired store, and to nobody else.

A form bought and not filled in lies in a book on that desk — a third of a sheet, four lines, the fourth of them blank, bought for a shilling out of a copyist's own hand and never entered — and a clerk's own note about the fourth line is in a hand of thirty-four in a book of no office in a building two hundred miles from here, and neither of those two pieces of paper has a person's name at the top of it and that is the reason they were not put in a bundle with one.

**There is a line on the inside of his left wrist about two inches long and it answers nothing and it has been asked about twice in a day by two people who were not talking about it.**

And a House, four hundred and thirty miles up the same river, went on waiting for a word it had not been sent, and a seat's form went back to a practice with a reason on it and no why, and a court on a flood terrace had answered a question nobody had asked it in nineteen years, and a factor of forty-six had put a blank list on a bench and had not filled it in, and the water was down and the bank was eleven feet high and about sixty people under sixteen were in a guarantee, a rotation, and a guild's blank list, and had been told about none of it.

---

He did not stop there, and the reason is that the thing on the shelf had one more line in it that he had read four times and had not gone near.

> *A person should not answer without knowing what they are agreeing to, and there is no person in this matter who has been told what they are agreeing to.*

He sat down again.

"That is the sixth door," he said. "**And it is the first one in fourteen months that has told the truth about itself on its face, and I want somebody to understand what that does to a man who has spent fourteen months being careful.**

"Every other form in this matter has lied in the direction of being easy to fill in. A box that wants a person and will take a body. A line at the top for a head that no cooperative has. A fourth line that is a question about a person and is printed underneath a letter about a company. **Every one of them has been drafted to make it possible for somebody to say yes.**"

"And that one is drafted to make it possible for somebody to say no."

"That one is drafted **honestly and at length and by the other side**, and it costs them nothing to do it, because the sentence protects them and not us: *a person should not answer without knowing what they are agreeing to.* That is a warning, and a warning is free, and a free warning is the most expensive object in this entire matter and it has been sitting on my shelf since the nineteenth."

"Then why is it a trap."

"Because **a warning that is free is a warning that has been read by nobody.** Four hundred and thirty miles away a House has written the most careful sentence anybody in this case has produced in four months, on a form that costs a shilling, and nobody on that cut has read it, and the miller's yard has a copy of the guarantee and nine people in a bay have heard the fourth line read out twice and not one of them knows it is a warning, because a girl of seventeen read it out and did not say what it meant. **The most honest sentence in the matter reached ninety-one households through a person who is not paid and is not allowed to explain it, and that is exactly how it was designed to arrive, and I do not think anybody designed it. I think that is just what happens.**"

He turned the sheet over and looked at the back of it, which was blank, which he had already known.

"Write it down somewhere," he said. "**Not in a file. Somewhere it will be read out twice.** Write that the guarantee is honest, that its warning is honest, that its warning reached nobody, and that the reason it reached nobody is that the answer to it is a person and the whole of this matter has spent four months arranging for there to be one."

---

Then, because he could not leave it alone and because he is that sort of clerk, he worked out the five.

It took him about nine minutes and he wrote them on the back of a second proof sheet and did not show them to anybody for a day and a half, and then he showed them to her, and she read the list and then read the bottom of it and then put it face down on the desk.

> *The five who could put a name to that line, this morning.*
>
> *One: a foreman of fifty-one, whose name is at the foot of a list of works in a hired bay, and who has a term, and who was put in a box in the first month of this year and taken out of it without being asked, and who is the only person on that cut who is a person an instrument touches on purpose.*
>
> *Two: a book-keeper of thirty-eight, whose name is in the same book with a date against it, and who is the reason anybody can be found on that cut at all, and who has told about nine people that he would rather a thing were findable in five places than one.*
>
> *Three: a miller of about fifty, who opened the letter on the eleventh day of the fifth month in front of about thirty people and became the person it was about, and who has said out loud in a bay that he was the door to it and has not slept properly since the sixth of the third month, and whose house was dry on the nineteenth of the fourth month because of a bank he stood on all night with a lantern.*
>
> *Four: a woman of about seventy-one at the north of the ford, who cannot read a paragraph, and who is a person in a form, and who has said in a room that she is not going to have anything to say and would like the asking.*
>
> *Five: a woman of about thirty-one at the first house at the same end of the lane, who has two children, and who was asked her name once in a doorway and refused it and let herself be written down as a description of a person instead, and who came two hours down a towpath in the mud on the twentieth of the fourth month to be told that the water had not come into her house, and who is coming back on the twenty-third and did not, and who has not been to that bay once.*

He had written under the list, and this is the sentence he showed her:

> *If any of these five fills in that line, the guarantee is answered and the answer is against the person who wrote it, and each of the five is answering it alone and about ninety-one people, and none of the five has been asked by anybody whether they understand that those are the same act, and two of them cannot read what is being answered, and one of them has already been asked once in a doorway and said no to a different question about the same paper.*
>
> ***I am not going to send this list. I am going to keep it in my own book, which has no office on it, because that is where I keep the things I am not able to do anything with. And in about four years somebody is going to find it, because that is what happens, and they are going to find five names and no answers, and I would rather they found that than found nothing at all.***
