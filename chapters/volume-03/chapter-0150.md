# Chapter 150: A Name at the Foot of the Fourth List

The first week of the fifth month of next year is cold on that bank at about the seventh hour and about four hundred and forty foot of outer face stands eleven feet above nine acres of ground that about thirty-one households have been living on the low side of since before the flood of the fourth month of last year.

**Marek Kest is thirty-eight and he got up there on the first, having come down the road in a cart with a woman of twenty-four, and the carriage of both of them came out of a wage of seventeen shillings and a penny the week and nobody paid it and the office that requires her to be in the room cannot be named and has not been named and is not going to be.**

The north bay of a miller's grain store off the Slade holds about thirty. There were about thirty in it. About nine of them cannot read a word of anything and were spoken to by a person sitting next to them, which is the rule of that bay and has been since the twenty-eighth of the third month, and the second reading is the one that is not skipped.

---

Here is what happened at about the seventh hour of the second day of the fifth month of next year, and it is in a book with no heading at the top of it in a clerk's hand, and the whole of it is about nine minutes long, and after it there is a page of a different thing entirely.

**A clerk of a court four hundred and thirty miles up this river held the seventh work of eleven on a ninety-one-year page for one year, at forty-five pence a day on five men and a boy, out of his own wage, and the paper said that the review of the term was in the first week of the fifth month of next year, and that if nobody came to the review then the term stopped on the day and there was no successor and nothing carried.**

Nobody came.

**Nobody was ever going to.** The seventh work of eleven is a ford, and a ford with two depth posts and nine stones is a thing a bay can read about and cannot act on, and the ford work had lapsed in the last day of the second week of the fourth month of last year with seven shillings and sixpence on a lapsed list, which is two days of a crew, and Hob Bray still has not put nine stones back in the throat of it and nobody has asked him to and there is no list that says he must.

The term stopped on the day. That is what the paper says it does and it did it.

And a foreman of fifty-one read it out in a bay, in about nine sentences, and then she said the part that matters and she said it in the ordinary way to about thirty people.

"**A clerk of a court four hundred and thirty miles away has given a year of his own wage to a ford on a list of works, and the review of it was this morning, and nobody came, and the ford is now on nobody's list again, and I am not going to stand in this bay and tell you that was a waste of it.**

"**It was the arrangement. It was in the paper. It said if nobody comes it stops on the day, and it stopped on the day, and there is no successor and nothing carried, and about nine of you in this room worked out four months ago that that is the only way a duty can be made to stop.**

"And I want it said that nobody in this bay was under any obligation to get on a cart for four hundred and thirty miles to keep it alive, and that the person it was written for did not expect anybody to, and that he came anyway, and he is standing at the west end of my bay at about the seventh hour and I would like somebody to say something to him that is not about paper.**"

---

What happened next took about eleven minutes and it is on the back of a sheet and it is a human clerk's writing and it is a record and it is findable, and in about four years anybody who wants to know whether a clerk of the Inheritance Desk ever moved a consequence onto a cooperative will be able to pay four pence and read it.

Here is the whole of it, in four lines, and it is the third instrument of this kind in fourteen months and the first one that is worth anything, and it is read out twice in that bay and the second reading is slower.

> *The consequence specified is the flood duty of the bank at the third-of-the-way mark, and of about one hundred and forty feet of ditch on the south side, and of the scour at that place, which are on nobody's list and are on this one from today.*
>
> *It is moved from me, who am a named party, being the person who has held it, to the persons named on the schedule in the order set out, in rotation, for a term of one season each, at forty-five pence a day, the same rate as every other person on the schedule, with a day stated against every name, and with a right in each of them to give it up on one day's notice given in his own hand into a book that has no heading, and with a review at the end of each term on a date stated against that name.*
>
> ***No person is entered on the schedule who has not been asked in that person's own doorway, and has not answered, and the answer and the day are entered. No person is entered who cannot say no. And the schedule is to be read to any person on it, and to any person in any house behind the bank, by a person entered on the schedule for that purpose and at the same rate as every other person on it, being about nine persons who cannot read a paragraph.***
>
> *This takes effect today. My own name is first on the withdrawal of the instrument it is moved from, dated the twenty-fourth day of the ninth month last year and effective today, and the two are one document and there is no morning in between. **The cost named is my own labour for the length of the term and my own liability for it, and there is no other cost, and nothing in this instrument is a debt of any household, and no child is named in it or is party to it.** A copy of this is in a book with no heading, and a copy is on the bench in this room, and I am not sealing it, because I have no seal and there is no notary behind it on this reach and a man of fifty-one or two put his name at the foot of a page in the sixth month of last year and lost a hundred and forty years of his House's work about it.*

There is no seal. It is four lines on the back of a sheet in a clerk's hand with a date on it, and it is a record and not a magic, and it decides nothing on its own, and it will be produced in about four years by anybody who wants it, and there is no way in this empire to give a name back.

**It is the third instrument of this kind he has written in fourteen months and the other two are both on a page in the same book with his name on them and one of them was withdrawn this morning.** The word for a thing like this is *exception* and he has used it twice in fourteen months and used it both times about a river, and about nine hundred rooms have heard the word from two different courts and have not understood it, and it is not the word of a House and it is not the word of a guild and it is not the word on the rail at a gate.

---

Then the chair of sixty-four read the count out, in about four minutes, and the second reading was slower.

> *Asked: about forty-one of the sixty wanted. Yes: about forty-one, with days against them and a day against the day they were asked, and one of them is entered as a description and not by name, at her own insistence, twice, and a described person is a person and is harder to serve and easier to lose and the court entered that about fourteen months ago.*
>
> *No: about nineteen, with reasons, not argued with and not asked a second time.*
>
> *Not asked, and never going to be asked: about four people who cannot read a paragraph, and a woman of about seventy-one at the fourth house at the north of the ford who walked four miles of towpath in about two hours each way in the fourth month of last year on the advice of nobody, and who cannot stand in a room with about thirty people in it for nine minutes, and who has said the same reason twice and has said she is not going to say it a third time, and who has asked in writing to be asked at her own door four times a year, and who is being.*

Then a book-keeper of thirty-eight read the whole of the count back twice, and the second reading was slower, and the chair made him read the nineteen noes back a third time because about nine of them are reasons and she wanted the room to have them.

---

**And then the first name at the foot of the season was written, and it was written by the chair, in her own hand, in a bay, in front of about thirty people, and it was a carter of thirty-one called Kem Ostry, and he had asked on the fifteenth of the seventh to be asked last.**

He came to the front of the bay and would not go to the front of the bay.

"**Read it where I am,**" he said. "**I am not going to stand where a person reads a name. I have been in this bay four times since the fourth month and I have not said one word in any of them until the sixth month and I am not standing at the front of a room in the fifth month of next year.**

"And I said in the ninth month of last year that I would rather be the man and the boy in a rate with no column in it," and I meant it, and I have had five months of meaning it, and I am not going to pretend to you that I have changed my mind.

"**I have changed my mind about being watched. That is a different thing and I would like a person in this room to write down that it is a different thing and not the same thing.**"

He did not explain it and nobody asked him to, and the book-keeper of thirty-eight wrote it down, and the chair read it back twice, and the second reading was slower, and he was entered first.

"**The reason I gave in the ninth month of last year was that a name at the foot of a list means somebody comes and finds out whether it is working every fortnight for a season, and that I had been the one watching since the fourth month of last year and I would like it stopped.**

"**It has not stopped. It has got worse. I am still the one watching. There is a list now and I am at the top of it and the first fortnight I am somebody's business.**

"So I am taking it, and I am first and not last, and I am not asking to be last again, and I have taken a fortnight to work out how to say that and I have said it badly and it is in the book.**"

Somebody started to say something about thank you and about nine people in that bay looked at Doreth Ferrow, and she put her hand up, and it stopped, and nothing about it is on the page.

---

The second name at the foot of the season is a foreman of fifty-one and it has a day against it and the day is not stated by her.

The reader is on the same list at the same rate as every other person on it, and there is a name on the schedule and there is a copy of an engagement in a satchel with a date on it of the first of the fourth month of next year, and she is three weeks into it.

**A line about two inches long is on the inside of Marek Kest's left wrist and at about the fourth hour of that afternoon it did something for about nine seconds, and the room saw it and about four people said so afterwards and the two people who were in the matter said nothing.**

It is a symptom. It reads nothing, it decides nothing, it imposes no geographic tether and no obligation that is not written on a piece of paper in front of about thirty people, and **it does not know where the ford is, and it did not tell anybody anything when it did it, and it will be recorded here that it did not tell anybody anything.**

**The mark is not the price. The wage is the price.** A wage of seventeen shillings and a penny the week, three pounds eleven and threepence of it for nineteen days on a bank in the second month of last year, entered in a weigh-house ledger under the word *held* because a clerk of fifty-one found it in a box with *given* and *paid* and *advanced* in it and chose it.

---

The book in the stone store got a new hand in it in the second week of the fifth month of next year, and it is a woman's hand, and it is the fifth hand in about sixty years, and the page it is on has no heading at the top of it on purpose.

The old page from ninety-one years back carries eleven works and eleven names and says the terms are in the instrument and not in the book. **The instrument it referred to is the one that was withdrawn on the second of the fifth month of next year.** The book is now a record of a duty nobody holds, and the ninth hundred and fortieth name in it is of a person who has been dead for about twenty-two years, and the new hand is on a different page, and the new page is a list of works with a name at the foot of it and a day against that name.

**It is the fourth list of works on that cut in ninety-one years.** The first three were a page from ninety-one years back, a spring work held by a clerk in a city with a wage, and a rotation of about thirty people entered on the twenty-eighth day of the third month of last year by a man of fifty-one who put it in a chapter of a court sitting in the wrong form under a written undertaking, and it bound about thirty people and said nothing whatever about the other about sixty, and it is still in that book and neither figure has ever been joined to the other by anybody.

**And it is the first one anybody on that bank will be able to point at.**

---

The last thing that happened that day happened at a gate about four miles down the water, at about the seventh hour, and it is the last page of this.

There is a rail at the Reed Cut gate and it is a piece of iron with a curve in it that somebody's grandfather set into the platform so that a boat has something to hold against while it is being worked, and there is a schedule nailed to it. It is on good paper, and it is about eleven inches by nine, and it has been folded once across the middle, and it is held on by four nails and the holes of an earlier set, and somebody tied it on with a length of oiled cord on the evening it went up and the cord is long gone and the four nails are still in the rail and they will be in the rail after the rail. **It carries the water office's device, which is not a name, and a memorandum number, which is a number of a book, and a date, and a foreman's name at the foot of it, which is the only person's name on the whole sheet.**

Nobody came to read it, for longer than anybody at that gate is alive to remember.

**A second sheet went up beside it on the second of the fifth month of next year, on a length of oiled cord tied on the evening it went up, on four new nails, and the four old nails were left in the rail, and nobody pulled anything down.**

It is a list of works, and it has a name at the foot of it and a day against that name, and it is the fourth such sheet on that gate and the first one anybody can point at.

A girl of seventeen stood on that platform at about the seventh hour and read it out, in plain words, twice, and the second reading was slower.

**And there was a girl of eleven on that platform with a pail, because there is a pail on that platform every evening of the fourth and fifth months and there has been for years, and she cannot read a paragraph, and nobody has ever given her a reason for it, and she is not a device and not a symbol and is owed nothing at all, and the only thing that happened to her on the second of the fifth month of next year is that somebody stood there and read it to her.**

The girl of eleven asked four questions. Three of them were about the work and were answered, and the fourth was asked in about nine words and it was the same question, near enough, that a carter of thirty-one had asked a foreman of fifty-one at a gate in the seventh month of last year, and it is this:

*And who is it that reads it to the ones who cannot read it.*

And the answer was, in about nine words, and it was given by the person standing there, and it is the only answer anybody has ever given to it:

"**Me. And I am on it, and I am at the same rate as everybody else, and I have a term, and I can be told to stop, and I have never been able to be told to stop in five months of turning up.**"

---

Then she said the other four things, standing on that bank in the cold with a pail and a rail and about four new nails, and nobody asked her to and she has not thanked anybody and was not thanked, and about nine people at a gate and about four in a bay and about thirty at a landing have all found out about it afterwards from somebody who was not paid to tell them.

"**One. I have read that list and every other paper in this matter out loud about nine hundred times since the fifth month of last year and I have not been paid for one hour of it, and nobody paid my carriage, and I came because there was nobody else.**

"**Two. I have been paid since the first of the fourth month of this year, and I am on that list at forty-five pence a day like everybody else, and the reason I asked for a written engagement and asked twice is that a favour has no notice and a duty has a notice, and about four years ago a girl of eleven stood on this platform and said that to me and I have been carrying it since.**

"**Three. I have not been thanked and I am not going to start, and a book-keeper wrote down in the ninth month of last year that I asked in writing for the thanks to be withdrawn and why, and the reason is that a person who is thanked is a person who reads for thanks, and in about four years somebody is going to come to this gate and ask me to do it for nothing and say it is my mother's river, and I will have to say no to somebody who has already said thank you to me.**

"**Four. A reader who is paid can be told to stop. That is the whole of what this rotation bought and it is not a wall and it is not justice and it is a list with a name at the foot of it and a day against the name. About nineteen people behind that bank said no and are behind it now, and one of them is a woman of about seventy-one who cannot stand in a room of about thirty people for nine minutes, and I am not going to stand on this bank and pretend the list is the river.**"

---

And here is what the volume leaves standing on the first week of the fifth month of next year, in the plainest words there are, and none of it is a question and none of it is answered by anybody in this book yet.

A house about two hundred miles the other way has lost about a hundred and forty years of fire-and-water licence on one reach of one river, and there is a name at the foot of the entry in a book with a crack in the spine, and in about four years anybody with four pence can read it, and a copy of it is enrolled in a second book about four hundred and thirty miles up a towpath, with a number against it and no name at the foot, and the man whose name is in the first book has not been told about the second one and is not going to be.

A water guarantee under the fire-and-water device stands offered and unanswered on a bank about four hundred and forty foot long, at no charge, answerable at any time afterwards by a person answering in its terms, and there is no such person and there is not going to be one, and **about sixty people under sixteen are inside it, and inside a guild's blank list, and inside a rotation they have not been asked about, and have been told about none of it.**

A district court nine days out of order with a high court is still not joined, and the door to Auremar is still a door, and there is no form in this empire for one court to ask another what it has found.

**The thirty-one of Mosswake are still thirty-one.** Twenty-two seen and nine told, eleven with a word and no name, twelve back from the post with a stamp, six copies never made, eighteen returns that did not come back. The fifth heading of the register of pending duties is *Copy sold*. The fourth column says *next review: none named* thirty-one times. There is a sixth space at the bottom of every page of that register with one line in it and no heading over it, and it is worth nothing at law.

**The Lowcross bill is nineteen pounds three and fourpence and it is unpaid and nobody is liable and there is no line for a bridge in that fund and there has not been one in nineteen years.** Four hundred and eleven people live on the far side. The district's return for four weeks says three hundred and eighty-eight crossings. Those are two figures and they are never the same, and a penny a day is the real cost, and about fifty-seven pounds of a tally has gone in over nineteen years and never been claimed.

A factor of forty-six has a blank list in a wagon and has asked in writing for the name of the man who instructs him, and has not been answered, and has said in a bay that asking may have cost him the contract.

A magistrate of fifty-one has entered a finding of fact about her own practice in nineteen years against herself, and named no person, and has refused to name about four hundred people she chose not to hold, and has said nothing new about whether she was right.

**A run number in a notary's table in a city about four hundred and thirty miles up this river, and a category of person who may be made a party, and a clerk of thirty-four's own note in her own hand about what a table may certify and may not.** That is the road to a document in an archive that names a blank heir, and it is a road and not an answer, and nobody has walked to the end of it and the volume did not.

**And in a drawer in a city there is an envelope that has been there since the first month of the year before last, and it has not been sent and it has not been opened, and there are three others in that drawer, and the woman who keeps them holds an office that does not exist with a limit that does, and she does not advise, and advice by proximity is the ordinary way advice happens, and she has been in four bays this year because a paper said so, and the paper is not the reason and both of them know it and neither of them has said it and it has been sixteen months.**

---

And a woman of sixty-four who cannot read a paragraph put the list in a book on the second of the fifth month of next year, and read the count back twice, the second reading slower, for the people who could not read it.

The first name at the foot of it is a carter of thirty-one who has said out loud that he is still the one watching and does not intend to stop.

**A list of works on a bank, with one person's name at the foot of it and a date against that name, and a book in a stone store with a new hand in it, and a girl of eleven who cannot read the list being read to by somebody who stood there.**
