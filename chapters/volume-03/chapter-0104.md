# Chapter 104: No Resolution

The mill yard off the Slade is about forty feet by sixty and it is cobbled in the middle and mud at the edges, and there is a wall along one side of it that a wagon can back against, and the miller's own door is in that wall with a light over it.

The miller is called Aven Tace and he is about fifty and he does not use this mill for four months in the year and rents the rest of it, and on the twentieth of the first month at about the seventh hour he stood in his own door with his arms folded for the whole of about an hour and did not say one word, and afterwards he told a man in a lane that he had not said one word because there is no form for a miller to say *you may not stand in my yard*, and because forty of his customers were standing in it and a miller who uses his yard to stop people is a miller who uses it twice.

About forty people came. That is not all of the ninety-one households and it was never going to be, and the reason it is not is worth nine seconds of a man's attention: a cooperative is not a person and cannot be instructed and cannot be compelled and cannot be summoned, and a body has no clerk and no door and no list, and the only list a body has is a book in a hired grain store that is kept by a man of thirty-eight whose mother has the key, and a man in a lane four hundred and thirty miles away wrote to that man in the third week of this month and asked him for a list of the households, and Adley wrote back that there was not one, and that if there was one it would not be the kind he would want written down.

---

What Doreth Ferrow had to do at seven o'clock on the twentieth of the first month was stop ninety-one households from signing something inside the following week.

There was nothing to sign. That was the difficulty and she had known it for six days and she had called the meeting anyway, and eleven people had told her so, including her own nephew.

"There is nothing to sign," she said. "That is correct. There is nothing to sign, and that is why we are here, and if any of you have come to sign something you can go home and I will not think less of you, and four of you have come to sign something, so you may as well stay and find out what is going to happen instead."

Nobody went home, which she had not expected, and which she has since said was the only reason the meeting worked.

The fourth thing that had happened in the preceding thirteen days was the keeper of the Reed Cut district water office coming up the river on the thirteenth and standing in a shed at the Slade end for twenty minutes and telling forty people, in plain words, that she had put two people's names in a box on a form without asking either of them.

Her name is Sivra Oris and she is not the First Seat and is not that seat's agent, and she said both of those things herself, in the shed, unasked, in the first minute, and nobody in the shed knew what either of them meant and about two of them understood.

What she said, and the twenty minutes were mostly her saying it, was this: that the district's flood authority for the spring bank at the Slade had been given to two persons and to no other person and to no body, in writing, on the ninth; that the keeper had entered the second of the two persons without asking him, on purpose, and had written on the face of the sheet that she had done it without asking; that the boy on the list of works, whose name is Raff Ordway and who is sixteen, was **not** in the box, and that the reason he was not in the box was that a keeper is supposed to be able to say out loud what she has chosen not to do, and that was the whole of his protection, and it was the whole of the protection anybody in that shed had ever had; and that the office could no longer compel the work and had said so on the face of its own paper; and that if the bank went in the fourth month then there would be no authority anywhere on that bank that could order anybody to do anything about it.

She also said, because nine people had asked her and she had been asked the same question four times in nine years and had never once been asked it honestly: that her sworn statement of the third of the fourth month could not be used in this, and could not be given to anybody in this yard, and would not be, and that anybody who wanted to use it would have to go and ask the office that took it and would be told the same thing, and that she had not offered it and would not.

---

Piotr Wray had asked to speak and had said he would say it whether or not she let him, and she let him, and he spoke for four minutes, standing on the cobbles with his back to the wagon wall.

He said three things.

The first was that the bank work had been done on that cut every spring for sixty years by men who were not paid for all of it and had never once been asked to do it, and that the district's own book stopped carrying the Slade at all nine years ago, and that this was not anybody's fault and that he had stopped arguing about it in his own head about four years ago.

The second was that a foreman is a person and can be asked questions and a company is not and cannot, and that his sister had worked that out in a shed on the seventh and had said it out loud, and that he did not agree with the whole of it and agreed with all of the part that mattered.

The third was this, and he said it in nine words, and it took him four seconds, and nine people in this matter have said something in that length of time and it is in the same class as the rest of them.

**"Nobody asked me, so I am not going to sign."**

And then he said, in about nine more words, which were not part of the nine and which Doreth Ferrow wrote down afterwards because she wanted them: "You put my name in a box and I am telling her it was the right box and the wrong name and I am not going to be the one who is asked twice."

---

Doreth Ferrow let that sit for about half a minute, which is a thing a chair of nineteen years knows how to do, and then she stood up on the sacks' edge she had brought out for the purpose, which is a thing she has done at nineteen years of meetings and has never once been asked to explain, and she said the following, and it took her about eleven minutes, and twenty of the forty people in that yard could repeat it afterwards and nine of them could repeat the whole of it.

"Two things we will do and two things we will not, and then we will go home, and then there will be no resolution, and I am going to say that out loud now so that nobody stands about hoping for one.

"**We will keep the book.** Adley keeps it, in the store, in his mother's building, at a shilling a quarter, and it has nine hands in it and about nine hundred and forty names in it, and it is the only paper on this cut that says who did what, and we are going to keep keeping it and we are going to tell people that we keep it.

"**We will put a person's name at the foot of a list of works.** That is now what the shed list is, and that is what the weigh-house line says, and I have read both of them and I am not going to have anybody take a name off one of them without saying so in front of the other one.

"We will not sign anything as the Company. There is no instrument, there is no form, there is nobody who could enforce one, and I have been keeping this meeting in a kitchen for nineteen years and I have never once in nineteen years been asked for a resolution because I did not know what one was worth, and I know now, and it is worth nothing at all, and a resolution that nobody can enforce is not a resolution, it is a promise, and a promise with a date on it is a record, and a record is the only thing this Company has ever had.

"And we will not put a household in a box. Any of you. And I want to say why in the yard and not in a kitchen, because a kitchen is where I say things that are not quite true.

"A house is bad because a house ends. It ends when the last one of you is dead and then there is nobody holding anything and the work stops, and that is a terrible thing and it is a small thing and I can live inside it.

"A company is worse, and I want the company part said out loud, because everyone in this yard has been thinking about their own house and about the Company, and it is the Company that is the danger. **A company cannot be asked and a company cannot say no and a company does not know it is doing anything, and it goes on after every one of us, and the only people left in it are the children.**"

Somebody at the back said something about the boy, about Raff, and Doreth Ferrow said, "Raff is not in the box, and that is the only good news in this yard tonight, and it is good news because a keeper did not do something, and that is the whole of the protection this business has ever been able to give anybody and it took twenty minutes in a shed to get it and nobody had to pay anything for it."

Then she stopped, and looked at the yard, and did the thing she does, which is to count, which she has done at the beginning of every meeting for nineteen years and which nobody in that yard has ever thought was a strange thing for a woman of sixty-four to do.

"There are about forty of you," she said. "And there are ninety-one houses, and about sixty children under sixteen, and we have never once had all of them in a room and we are not going to have them in a room this month either, and I have known that for nineteen years and I have never said it out loud, and I am saying it now because I have just said *every child in it* and I meant it and there are about sixty of them and I have met the two youngest.

"So when I say that a body cannot say no, I am not talking about a word. I am talking about the fact that **there are about sixty people in this cut who are under sixteen and who are not in this yard, and who cannot be in this yard, and whose parents are in this yard, and if anybody in this yard agrees to a thing tonight then those sixty people have been added to it without being in the room, and that is the whole of the case that has been running for fourteen months in a city four hundred and thirty miles away, and it is running tonight in a mill yard, and it is running with a body instead of a family and it is exactly the same.**"

Somebody said, "Then what do we do."

"Nothing," said Doreth Ferrow. "We do the two things and we do not do the other two, and I have said the two and the other two, and that is the whole of what I have. **And I am going to say the last of it and then I am going to stop, and I would like it understood that I am stopping because I have nothing else, and not because I think it is enough.**"

Then she said the last of it, and this is the sentence the volume is about, and she said it to forty people in a mill yard in the cold in the first month of the year, and she has said it since to about nine hundred people in nine or ten rooms and it does not get easier and it does not get shorter.

"So nobody is going to be told what to do about this. **The water office has asked the district's question and the district's answer is that a body cannot hold a duty without putting every child in it behind the duty, and that is the answer, and I am not going to improve on it tonight because there is nobody in this yard who can.**

"And the only thing I will say about the fortnight of work that is not going to happen, and it is not going to happen because there is no money in the works fund and no person to hold it and the fund is empty, is this: **the bank at the third-of-the-way place is four hundred and forty feet long and it will go in the third week of the fourth month, and behind it there are nine acres and thirty-one households, and the miller standing in that door is one of the thirty-one.**"

Aven Tace unfolded his arms.

"There is a bank at the third-of-the-way place," he said, "and I have been telling the district since the sixth year that it goes, and I have a piece of paper about it that nobody has ever asked me for, and I would rather somebody asked me for it, and nobody has, and that is the whole of what I have to say about it and I am going to go inside."

And he went inside, and shut the door, and four people in that yard laughed, and it was not a laugh about him.

---

They stood about in the cold for about eleven minutes afterwards, which is what forty people do at the end of something, and then they went home, and the bank is four weeks off.

And Doreth Ferrow went home too, and it is a two-roomed house above a shop nine hundred yards from the mill yard, and she went in and put the stick in the corner and stood in her own kitchen for about a quarter of an hour without taking her coat off, and her daughter-in-law came in and asked her whether she wanted the fire lit and she said no, and then said yes, and then said that she had been keeping a meeting for nineteen years and had never once in nineteen years come home from one and not known what to do with her hands, and that tonight she did not know, and that this was the first time.

"Nineteen years," she said, "and the way to win a thing in a kitchen is to let the loudest person have it. That is the whole of what I know about it and it has been useful to me about four hundred times. And tonight the loudest person was right, and I said no to him in front of forty people in the cold, and **the no was mine and the rightness was his and I have got to hold both of those and I am sixty-four and I have about as many of these as I have had.**"

The book-keeper entered it that night, in the book, in the form the book uses, with the day on it, and the entry has no name against it either, because a person who enters a thing they were not asked to enter is in the same position as a person who keeps a thing they were not asked to keep, and Rennick Adley wrote about three lines and then stopped and then wrote one more.

> *The twentieth of the first month, at about the eighth hour, the mill yard. About forty. No resolution and none to be expected, and the reason is written here so that nobody stands about hoping for one later: a resolution that nobody can enforce is a promise.*
>
> *The Company will keep the book and will put a name at the foot of a list of works. The Company will not sign as a Company and will not put a household in a box. The water office has put two names in a box without asking and one of the two men present refused, in front of about forty people, in nine words, and was right.*
>
> *The spring bank is four weeks off and the works fund head has been empty since the third week of the twelfth month and there is no money in it and the two names on the district's sheet are two people and not a payroll. The bank at the third-of-the-way place is four hundred and forty feet and it goes in the third week of the fourth month and behind it are nine acres and thirty-one households, and there is nothing in that yard tonight that will stop it and nobody in it pretended otherwise.*
>
> *— entered by R. Adley, book-keeper, no resolution.*

There is one more figure in that entry and it is the only one in this chapter that anybody can check, and it is not about law.

The store is hired at a shilling a quarter and the quarter's shilling comes out of the book four times a year, and it has come out of the book since before Rennick Adley's aunt kept it, and it has been entered on a page as a cost of keeping a book, and the page has no heading and the entry has no name against it, and it is the only money the Slade Cut Company has ever spent on anything in sixty years.

It is a shilling a quarter and it is four shillings a year. It is less than the bank will cost when it goes, and it is the entire annual budget of a cooperative of about ninety-one households that has been running a public work for sixty years, and when Adley wrote *the Company will keep the book* in a book in a store on the night of the twentieth of the first month, he was committing about ninety-one households to four shillings a year, and nine of them were standing in the yard when she said it, and none of them said anything about the shilling, and he noticed, and has written in the margin of the first leaf, in pencil, that he noticed.

In Auremar, on the same day, at about the second hour of the afternoon, a man read a file out loud in a room with a stove in it, and it took him about nine minutes, and two people listened, and one of them was there because a court order said so and neither of them said why.
