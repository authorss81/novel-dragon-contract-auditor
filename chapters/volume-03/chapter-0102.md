# Chapter 102: The Box at the Water Office

The Reed Cut district water office is a low building with a stove in it and a counter with a slot in it, and it stands about two hundred yards above the gate so that a man can see the water and the office at the same time, which is the only thing the building is for.

It issues flood memoranda, gate schedules and inspection dates for about four hundred and sixty households' worth of channels, and it has done so for one hundred and forty years under four names, and the four names are on the door in the form the district uses for names of buildings and not in the form of houses. There are four rooms: the counter, the back room with the stove, a room with the day's sheets in it, and a room nobody goes into except in the fourth month.

The person who keeps it is **Sivra Oris**, and she has kept it for nine years, and she is not the First Seat and is not that seat's agent and has never been, and nothing in the building is her House's, and the district's flood authority is hers to give and hers to withhold and there is no form in this empire for a district office to say which it has done.

On the seventh day of the first month a sheet came up the river with a clerk's list of works stapled to it, and the sheet had a box on it.

---

The form is a district flood form and it is about the age of the district. It has a head with the district in it and the year, and it has a line for the water, and it has a line for the works, and then it has a box.

The box is headed **who is to answer**.

That is all it says. Four words and a rule under them and about four inches of white space, and it wants a thing in it, and the four inches have been filled in nine or ten times a year for a very long time, and the ordinary way of filling them in is the way anybody fills in a box that wants an answer to a thing and finds that a person is inconvenient: you put in whatever is nearest to hand that is the shape the box is.

The shape the box is, in this district, on a bank work, is a body. The Reed Cut district water office's ordinary practice for nine years is to enter the body that does the work, and a body is easy, because a body is a fixed string of letters that comes in a book and does not have to be found, and a body can be spelt once and then copied nine or ten times a year for forty years, and nobody ever has to stand in a room and ask anybody anything.

She has done it twice herself and she can name both, and the second one was four years ago and was a different shape of work, and she has never written down either of them in this building and is not going to now.

The first was a body of about sixty households on the upper Cut that took the works for a season and did them badly, and the entry went in as *the upper Cut households* because the form wanted a thing, and when the bank slipped in the fourth month of that year there was no person to write to about it, and the district wrote to the households, and one of the households wrote back and said the work had been done by a body and not by them, and that was true, and it was the only answer anybody could have given, and it took a clerk four days to establish which forty of the sixty had actually turned out.

The second was three years ago and it was a landing, and the entry went in as the landing's name, and the district's own clerk at that landing was a person who could have been entered in the box and was not, and the reason she was not is that nobody had gone and asked her, and eleven months later a rising came and a question came with it and the district did not have one name to ask.

**"A body in a box does not cost anybody anything at the time. That is the whole of the reason it is there. It costs later, to somebody, and the somebody is never the person who wrote the body's name in the box, and it is nearly always a person who cannot be told what happened because the thing that happened happened to a body.**"

Sivra Oris read the sheet on the seventh at about the fourth hour and put it down, and then she picked it up again, and read it a second time, and the reason she read it a second time is that she wanted to be in a room when she did what she was about to do.

The clerk's list of works was stapled to it. The list was the one that has been on the nail in the shed at the Slade end since before anybody now living. It had the crew's names on it and a day against each name and a man's name at the top of it in a hand about forty years old.

The list had **a foreman's name at the foot of it, and the foreman's name was new**, and the date against it was the seventh of the first month, and the foreman was Halla Wray of the Slade, and there was nothing on the list about the Slade Cut Company except that the clerk who had copied the list off an older one had written *Slade. Spring bank.* in the corner in his own hand about nine years ago and had never been asked by anybody what that meant.

---

What the authority actually is, and it is worth about four minutes of that morning, because a person in a yard four miles up-channel has no more idea of it than a person in a lane in a city four hundred and thirty miles away, and about nine people in that yard will be told something wrong about it in the next eleven days.

A district flood authority is nine or ten things a year. It is a memorandum saying *this bank, this week, this many men*. It is a gate schedule saying *this gate is worked on these days and set to a maker's gauge and not to the hand*. It is an inspection date, which is a day on which somebody from this building stands on a bank and looks at it and writes down what it is like. And it is a stop.

The stop is the only one anybody in that yard will ever feel. Twice in nine years the keeper of this office has stopped a channel work, and both times it was in the week before a rise, and both times the crew went home and came back after the water went down and did the whole thing four days late. **There is no penalty in this district for being stopped by this office and no reward for not being stopped, and about nine crews have said privately that they would rather be stopped late than started early, and the reason is that a rise in the third week takes out a season and a rise in the first week takes out a fortnight.**

That is the whole of it. About nine things a year, one of which can stop a crew, none of which can make anybody do anything, and a stove, and a counter with a slot in it, and a building that stands two hundred yards above a gate so that a man can see the water and the office at the same time.

"**That is what I am, and I have been it for nine years, and about nine people in this district think I order the water here, and I do not order the water, I cannot order the water, and the only part of this office that anybody ever feels is the stopping, and the stopping is the smallest part and it is the only one that is a power.**"

---

There were four people in the building on the seventh and on the eighth and on the ninth, and Sivra Oris said the thing out loud to all four of them on the ninth, at about the second hour, standing, and the reason she said it out loud was not politeness.

**A person can stop.**

She said it like that, three words, and then she went round the three other things, and she took nine minutes about it, and the four of them heard all nine.

"An office can be instructed. That is the whole of what an office is for and it is why this building has a stove in it. Anybody with authority over this district can come in here and say *do this* and I have to do it or answer for not doing it, and about twice a year somebody does.

"A person can stop. That is the other thing. A person can be asked a question and can say no, and can be told they are not to and can go home, and can die, and in all four of those cases the work stops, and the work stopping is a thing a person did.

"A body cannot do either of those. A body cannot be instructed, because nobody has authority over a body's conscience, and a body cannot stop, because a body does not know it is doing anything, and it will go on doing it after every single person in it is dead. **And that is the whole of what a body is good for and the whole of what it is bad for, and it is why a company is the right place to keep a book and the wrong place in the world to keep a duty.**"

Then she said the fourth thing, and it is the thing the chapter is about, and she said it in the ordinary voice.

"**This form wants a person. And a person has to be found.** And a body is only ever in a box because finding a person was inconvenient on the day somebody filled it in. I have watched that happen for nine years and I have done it myself, twice, and I am not going to do it again, and I am going to be exact about why, because I am not going to do it on principle and there is nothing in this building that can be run on principle.

"I cannot compel the work. I have never been able to compel the work. I can issue a memorandum and a schedule and a date, and I can inspect, and I can shut a gate, and if the bank goes in the fourth month the seed goes with it and there is nobody in this building who can be sent for, and the district will ask me what happened and I will have to say *the bank went*, and they will ask who was holding it, and the answer has to be a person, because if the answer is a body then the answer is nobody and *nobody* is a thing this district can be told to go and look for, and this district has tried to go and look for *nobody* before and it cost it a year."

The clerk of the counter, who is about twenty-three and has been in it two years, said the only thing anybody said in that nine minutes, and she said it to the sheet and not to her.

"Then the box is empty, miss."

"No," said Sivra Oris. "The box is not empty. **The box is the whole of the work and I am going to put two people's names in it without asking either of them, and I am going to write on the face of the sheet that I did not ask, and I would like one of you to fetch me the ink.**

---

She fetched the ink.

The sheet went up the river on the tenth and it is the only piece of paper in this matter that anybody can point at, and here it is in full, and the four lines on the face are the four lines and the rest of it is a form of about a hundred and forty years old.

> *Reed Cut district water office. Flood works, spring. The ninth day of the first month.*
>
> *Water, the Slade Cut, outer face. Works, the spring bank, second week to the third week. On the list of works stapled to this sheet, five men and a boy.*
>
> *Who is to answer, for the works above:*
> ***H. Wray, foreman, Slade.***
> ***P. Wray, of the far end of the Slade.***
>
> *These two names are entered by the keeper of this office. **Neither person was asked.** Neither person has been told that this sheet exists. The first of them put a name at the foot of the list of works on the seventh of this month in her own hand, which is the only reason her name is here. The second has not put a name at the foot of anything. The keeper has entered him because the works cannot be run with one name in the box and because he is the only other person on that bank who can read a list of works aloud to a person who cannot read a paragraph, and for no other reason, and if either of them says no this sheet is void and the keeper will enter the void and the day.*
>
> *The Slade Cut Company is not entered above and is not answerable above. It is a body. A body cannot be instructed and cannot stop and the keeper will not enter one in a box.*
>
> *The district's flood authority in respect of the works above is given to the two persons named and to no other person, and to no body, and the keeper gives it with this sheet and does not ask anybody's leave.*
>
> *And it is entered, on the face of this sheet, that **this office cannot compel the works to be done, does not claim to, and cannot be compelled to do them itself.** The bank will be done by the persons named or it will not be done. If it is not done, this office has no power to make anybody do it and has given away the only power it had, on the ninth of the first month, in writing, on purpose.*

That is the whole of the instrument and it is four lines and a form of a hundred and forty years old and it is the second document in this matter that somebody could lose.

---

Two things happened in that room afterwards and one of them is in the file and one of them is not.

The first is in the file. The clerk of the counter copied the sheet in plain words in a hand of about twenty-three, because a keeper's clerk copies, and the copy went into the day's book under a heading that says *issued under the flood authority of this office*, and it went up the river on the tenth with the works clerk's list, and about nine days later it came back with a line on it in another hand, and the line is in Chapter 106.

The second is not in the file, and Sivra Oris said it out loud to four people in a room with a stove in it on the ninth of the first month, and it took her about four minutes, and this is what she said and there is no form for it and there never has been.

"Two of you will go home tonight and one of you is going to have a sheet in your hand, and the boy is going to be told that there is a name in a box at the water office, and about nine days after that he is going to ask why his name is not in it.

"He is sixteen and he is on the list of works at eight pence a day and five for the boy, and I did not put him in the box, and I want that written down somewhere, and it is going in the day's book under my own hand, because **the one thing I have done in nine years that I am not ashamed of is leaving a sixteen-year-old out of a box.** He will be on the list. He will be paid out of the list. He will be worked by a foreman who is a person and can be asked questions, and that is the whole of the protection there is and I have given it to him by not doing something, which is the only kind of protection this office has ever been able to give anybody.

"And I will say the last of it, because I am a keeper and a keeper is supposed to say the last of it and then go and do the work.

"**I am a dragon and I have just given away my own office's authority in writing to two human persons, and there is no form for getting it back.** There is a form for asking me to do a thing. There is a form for a district that says *this keeper has not exercised this authority properly*. There is not a form anywhere in this empire for saying to a keeper, *give it back*, because giving it back would mean putting a body in a box and I have just written four lines saying I will not do that. **I have made myself the only person in this district who is able to do this work and the only person who is not able to order it, and if the bank goes in the fourth month then this building has got a stove in it and a form with an empty box in it and nothing else at all, and about a hundred and forty acres of channel-side ground goes under the wrong water in the wrong week, and there is no dragon in this office to blame because I have put my own name to that as well.**"

Nobody said anything for about a minute and a half, and then the clerk of the counter said, "Then somebody had better tell them tonight."

And Sivra Oris said, "No."

---

She said it about four days early, and the reason she said it four days early is that she has learned in nine years that a thing left in a drawer in a building where nobody can be instructed is a thing that gets found in about four years by somebody who is looking for a person to blame, and she would rather the two names she entered without asking were told about by her, in person, at the Slade end, on the thirteenth, by a person who is not a clerk and does not know what a memorandum is.

That is the sheet, and that is the office, and that is the keeper.

A box four inches deep and four words over it, and a hundred and forty years of a district's practice behind it, and on the ninth of the first month a keeper filled it with two people and left it empty of a company, and wrote down on the face of the sheet that she had asked nobody, and gave away the only thing the building had that anybody could be compelled to use.

The sheet went up the river on the tenth in a bundle with about nine other things and it was read out twice in a kitchen on the last day of that month, and on the twenty-second of the same month, four hundred and thirty miles away in a city, a man read a file out loud in a room with a stove in it, and neither of them knew the other had happened.
