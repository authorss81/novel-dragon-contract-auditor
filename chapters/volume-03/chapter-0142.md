# Chapter 142: Four Lines and Two Ends

The Received Room has a stove in it, a bar on the inside of the frame, a window onto a wall, and eleven feet of shelf, and it is where things go when nobody wants to be the one who has them.

**Marek Kest is thirty-eight and has been a clerk of it for two years and is under a formal watch entered in one sentence on the nineteenth of the ninth month of last year, together with an eligibility, deliberately, because a court that puts them in two sentences may be asked to move one of them, and that court has been asked twice in fourteen months and has moved both.**

The watch is the price and not a qualification. A person from an office, in this matter unmentioned, may read anything he writes. He may not be in a room without a person in it who is not from that office, and the office is not named and cannot be named, because a person who signs a column headed *by whose authority* is discharging an office, and an office cannot be examined, and an office that is named and cannot be examined cannot be instructed.

There is one person in this matter who is not from that office. She is in the room because of the sentence, and he is in the room because she is, and both of them know that, and neither of them has said it, and it has been four months.

The second week of the seventh month is hot in a city with a river in it and the window looks at a wall about nine feet away and the wall is the same colour it was in the second week of the fourth.

He has been down the road in the second week of the fourth, and in the last week of the fourth, and in the fourth week of the sixth, and in the third week of the sixth, and he keeps a list of the dates in the back of his own book because a man who cannot account for his own travel is a man who cannot account for a day rate.

---

He has been carrying one thing since the second week of the fourth month and he has written it in his own book four times and sent it three times and had it refused twice, and it is in a book on this desk with no office on it, and here is the whole of the fourth version, which is the one he has been carrying for eleven weeks.

> *The district works shall stand the flood authority in respect of the spring works at the third-of-the-way mark upon the Company. The Company is an unincorporated body. It is not a person. It cannot refuse, cannot be summoned, cannot be heard and cannot answer. **It is not proposed that the Company be made a party and there is no form by which it could be.** It is therefore proposed that the duty be discharged in rotation by persons named on the schedule, in the order set out, at forty-five pence a day, and that no person be named on the schedule who has not been asked and has not answered.*

That is four lines. He has got the first three right for four months and it took a woman who is not a lawyer to find the error in the first line in the first week of the third month, and the error was that it wanted a party.

The fourth line is the one he has been getting wrong for fourteen months and it is not a legal error and it never was.

He wrote a fourth version in the second week of the fourth month with a line at the top for the name of the person who holds, and struck it out himself, in his own hand, four seconds after writing it, and did not explain it, and nobody asked him about it because there was nobody in the room.

He has written two versions this week. She was in the room for both of them.

---

Here is the first of them, and it is what he had, and it is four lines again:

> *The duty shall be discharged in rotation by persons named on the schedule in the order set out, at forty-five pence a day, **for a term of one season ending on a day stated against the first name**, and the duty shall cease on that day unless a person named on the schedule is then found to hold it, and **if no person is then found the duty ceases and nothing carries it forward.***
>
> *The person who holds it may give it up at any time on one day's notice, given in his own hand into a book that has no heading.*
>
> *A person may be entered on the schedule who is not of the Company, and the reading of the schedule to any person is to be done by a person also entered on it.*
>
> *The person writing this is the person it is moved from and is liable for it until the first name is on the schedule.*

He read that out in about four minutes. She wrote it down, which she does, and she has never once pretended she was doing anything else.

Then she said, "Read the second line of the second paragraph again."

> *The person who holds it may give it up at any time on one day's notice, given in his own hand into a book that has no heading.*

"That is the only line in it that is worth anything," she said, "and it is there because you put it there, and I want you to notice what you have done, because you have not noticed it and I have watched you not notice it for about eleven minutes."

"I have noticed it."

"You have written a duty that a person can stop, and you have not written anywhere what stops it, and I want you to say out loud in this room what happens on the day it stops."

He said it.

"**The water does not stop.**"

"**Yes. And that is a duty that ends and not a duty that survives stopping, and you have built the first one for four months and called it the second one, and everybody on that bank has been careful with you about it for four months because they did not want to be the one who said it.**"

---

He got up and put the door open about nine inches, which he does not do, and stood at it, and then came back and sat down.

"There are two ends to it," he said. "**And I have been building the wrong one since the second week of the fourth month, and I want to write both of them down and then I am going to strike one of them out, and I am not going to explain which and you are not going to ask.**"

"Go on."

"**End one. Somebody comes on the day.** A date, and a person at the date, and the person is findable because their name is on a list, and on the day the person is either there or is not, and if they are not, the next name goes up. Sixty names, a season, a day at the end of each of them, and a door at the end of every one of them with a person standing at it. **That is a duty that stops and there is something else standing behind it when it does.**

"**End two. Nobody has to come.** A date, and if nobody is at the date then the duty ceases and nothing carries it forward, and that is on the face of the paper in nine words, and the paper is honest about it, and I have had that paper in a book on this desk since the first week of the fifth month and I did not know until this morning that I had built the wrong end of it."

"And which one is the paper on this desk."

"End two. And I built it in about eleven minutes, in the second week of the fourth month, and I have carried it for eleven weeks, and the reason is that I could not stand on a towpath for eleven weeks. **The end that works is the end with about sixty people in it, and the reason I built the other one is that it is the only end I could carry myself, and I have been carrying it for eleven weeks and about four hundred and thirty miles of it is out of seventeen shillings and a penny the week and nobody pays my carriage.**"

---

She wrote that down as well. It took her about four minutes and she read it back and he made her change one word in it, which was *carried* to *carried by me*, and she let him.

"**And that is the first time in this matter that you have said a thing to me that you did not write on the back of a proof sheet first,**" she said. "And I am not going to make anything of it, and I am also not going to let it stand in a room without the other half of it, which is that you have just described a man building a paper around the size of himself, and you did it in eleven minutes, and you were twenty-nine weeks into a case at the time and nobody had told you to."

"Nobody has told me to now either."

"**I have just told you to. It is not advice, because I have no standing and my third line forbids it, and I told you in the fourth week of the fourth month and I have said it in three other rooms since.** I am telling you the fact that you have done it. Facts are not advice. I have got a drawer with four things in it and one of them has been there since the first month of last year and it is not going out, and if I could advise I would not, because advice is a thing a person hands you at no cost and it costs about four hundred and thirty miles to be useful."

---

Then he priced it, and he priced it out loud, and she wrote the figures down, and it is the part of this chapter that anybody in ninety-one households is entitled to.

"**Forty-five pence a day. Five men at eight pence and a boy at five, and that is the Company's own rate and it is written on a lapsed list about a hundred yards up the road with a foreman's name at the foot of it.**

"**A season is about ninety days.** Ninety days at forty-five pence is three pounds fifteen and threepence. The spring bank took nineteen days and cost three pounds eleven and threepence out of my own wage at seventeen shillings and a penny the week, being three weeks of it, and it is entered in a weigh-house ledger under the word *held* and the word is the best one anybody in this matter has found.

"And here is the whole of the arithmetic of four months. **The Company has four shillings a year. That is the rent of a hired stone store at a shilling a quarter, taken out of the book four times a year, and the whole of the Company's money for nineteen years is the roof.**

"So. A season on that bank, one person at a time, at forty-five pence a day, **costs the Company no money at all.** It costs nothing because a company cannot pay anybody and does not know what a wage is.

"**And the whole of the cost is the wages. And a wage has to come out of a person, and there are about sixty of those on that cut, and about forty-one households behind that bank have nobody who can be asked to stop.**

"So the price of this paper is not a figure and the price of this paper is that about sixty people are going to be findable. **A person who can be found is a person who can be asked about the bank, and there is no version of this arrangement in which they can be found and the bank cannot.**"

---

Then he wrote the second version, and it took about eleven minutes, and this is the whole of it, and it is the shape of the thing that eventually gets written in the first week of the fifth month of next year:

> *The duty shall be discharged in rotation by persons named on the schedule in the order set out, at forty-five pence a day, for a term of one season, and the term of each person shall end on a day stated against that person's name.*
>
> *No person shall be entered on the schedule who has not been asked, in that person's own doorway, and has not answered, and the answer shall be entered with the day on it.*
>
> *No person shall be entered on the schedule who cannot say no.*
>
> *The person who holds it may give it up at any time on one day's notice, given in his own hand into a book that has no heading, and **the schedule shall be read to any person on it, and to any person in any house behind the bank, by a person entered on the schedule for that purpose and at the same rate as every other person on it.***
>
> *The person writing this is the person it is moved from, and is liable for it until the first name is on the schedule, and the term does not begin until then.*

He put it in his own book with the day on it and did not send it.

---

"Why not."

"Because of the third line," he said. "**No person shall be entered on the schedule who has not been asked, in that person's own doorway.** And the person at the foot of a rotation is whoever is next, and I do not know who that is, and I cannot find out, and I am not going to write a piece of paper with a box at the foot of it that I cannot fill with a person."

"**Then write the box with a nothing in it.**"

"**And then the paper is a promise about about ninety-one households in my own hand and I am a party to it, and I have been telling that bay for four months that a person who speaks for ninety-one other people is not hearing them, he is speaking for them, and I would be doing it in a room with a stove in it where nobody is.**"

He put the pen down.

"**Also, if I write it now, I write it on about eleven answers out of about sixty. And the number is eleven. And the thing I have learned in fourteen months — which I learned in about nine seconds in this room in the fourth week of the fourth month from a woman who was not advising me because her third line forbids it — is that you do not get to choose which eleven.**"

---

She put her pen down too, and looked at him for a while, and then she said the thing he had been waiting nine minutes for and had not known he was waiting for.

"**Then put the choice in writing.**"

He looked at her.

"You have a rule." The two of us, in the twelfth month of last year. Either of us may put a choice in writing with a date on it and the other must answer in a room, in words, with a date. You have used it five times. **You have not used it in eleven weeks and I have noticed, and I have not asked, and I am asking now, in the ordinary way, and it is a question and not a condition.**"

"No."

"**No.**"

"**No. And the reason is that the choice is not mine.** It is whether to write a piece of paper on the strength of eleven answers out of about sixty, and I am not the person who is asked either way. **I am a clerk in a city with a discharged bar and a watch, and the persons to be asked are in ninety-one households, and the asking has to be done in their doorways by somebody they can be asked why.**"

"Then the rule does not apply," she said.

"**The rule does not apply.**"

"**Then that is the first correct use of it anybody has made of that rule in a year,**" said Tamsin Rook, "**and it is not using it, and I am going to write the date on it anyway, because I want a record that you thought about it.**"

She wrote the date at the top of a page in a book with no office on it and nothing else under it, and turned the book round so he could see it, and that was all.

---

At about the sixth hour he said, in the ordinary way, in the way he asks her things and she does not answer, "**What is a rotation for.**"

She looked at him for long enough that he thought she was going to do the thing she has done four times in fourteen months, which is to answer a different question correctly.

"**About eleven people have said yes and about forty-nine have not been asked, and the answer to what it is for is that somebody is going to ask the other forty-nine in about nine weeks and it is not going to be you, and that is the correct arrangement, and I want to say it out loud in a room on purpose because I have watched you for four months build every single thing you have touched around the size of what you can carry, and the next one is going to be built around about sixty people and you are going to find out in about nine days that you are the wrong shape for it.**"

He did not say anything.

"And you asked me what it is for, and that is the fourth time you have asked me a question you have already written the answer to," she said, "and my third line forbids me to advise, and advice by proximity is the ordinary way advice happens, and I have not advised you and I am not going to, and I have just told you a fact about a number and you may do what you like with it."

---

The rule was not used and nothing was sent and no instrument was written and no court was asked and no name went anywhere.

**The undertaking of the fourth of the first month is live and was not exercised in this week.** It has caught a word three times, a fact once, an absence twice and an answer once, and it stands, and she read every one of the four lines of both versions before he wrote either of them, in front of him, out loud, and gave no reason against either, and asked him for nothing.

There is a line on the inside of his left wrist about two inches long and it answered nothing this week and was not looked at by anybody except him, twice.

Four hundred and thirty miles below him a bay has a chair of sixty-four in it who has fixed a day in eight days, and a book-keeper of thirty-eight got back up a lane at the ninth hour with three answers and two noes, and one of the noes came from a woman who told him the finding of the whole volume for nothing, on a doorstep, at the end of the day, and he wrote it down in a book with no heading and read it out twice and could not think what else to do with it.

In a drawer in a room he has not been in since the second week of the fourth month there is an envelope, and it has been there since the first month of last year, and it has not been sent and it has not been opened, and it is four lines long.
