# Chapter 129: What It Is For, the Second Time

The bay has a bench in the middle of it and nine people come to it, and on the first week of the fifth month there were about thirty and about twenty-one of them were standing up because the mud had come back with the rain.

**Marek Kest is thirty-eight and he is four hundred and thirty miles from the desk he is paid for, and he has been on this bank for eleven days.**

He slept two nights in the south end of a store with a stove in it. He has not asked for a wage to be advanced and there is no fund in this matter and there has never been one, and he has not put his name at the top of any of the ninety-one households' anything, and a woman of about fifty-one read his nine pages out loud in a bay to about thirty people on the fourth week of the fourth month and did not say one word about who wrote them.

There is a matter on the bench. It is the fourth work that has been in this room and the first one anybody has brought here that anybody in ninety-one households cannot undo.

---

He had been in the room about nine minutes when the foreman of fifty-one said it, and she said it in the ordinary way, at the west end, with about thirty people in it, which is a thing he has learned in this matter is the only way a question gets asked properly.

"You have been in this cut for eleven days," said Halla Wray. "**What is it for.**"

He had eleven seconds. He had had seven weeks to have eleven seconds and had not used one of them, and the reason he had not used one of them is that on the twenty-first day of the third month a man of sixty had asked him the same question in a room with a copyist in it and he had answered a different one correctly and had written in his own book that he would rather be asked a second time than answer it wrong in a room with a copyist in it.

This was not that room. There was no copyist. There were about thirty people, a third of whom could not read a paragraph, and a woman of sixty-four who had just told a room that the right thing is the wrong thing.

"It is for a water," he said.

Nobody said anything.

"**It is for a bank that is eleven feet high and nine acres and thirty-one households behind it, and it is for a gate a person can shut.**

"That is the whole of it and I want to say the second half of it properly because a man of sixty asked me for it in the third month and I gave him a different answer and it was correct and it was not the answer.

"A public duty with no name on it gets carried by whoever is standing nearest. **The nearest is a household. And a household that holds a duty produces a child that holds the duty.** That is what the four lines are for and what they have always been for, and I did not know it on the eleventh of this month and I knew it on the fourteenth of the third month and I did not look, because looking at it was in the only direction I have been forbidden to go for nine months, and I would rather have been asked a second time than be wrong in a room with a copyist in it.

"And I have now been asked a second time, in a room with no copyist in it and nine people in it who cannot read a paragraph, **and I am answering it to those nine, and not to the man of sixty, and he is not in this room and I have not written to him.**"

It took about nine seconds and it was the right one and nobody in the room could tell.

Then a woman of sixty-four said, "Say the last of it again, and say it slower, and I want the foreman to say it after you so that I know a woman said it."

He said it again. Halla Wray said it after him, in about the same words, badly, and it took her nine seconds as well.

---

He wrote it on the bench at about the third hour of the afternoon, on the back of the lapsed list, because there was no other paper anybody would lend him and because the lapsed list was the only paper in that room with a name and a date and two figures on it and it was the right size.

It took him about twenty minutes and about four people read it over his shoulder while he did it, and about two of those four could read it.

> *A second exception, in the sense that one specified consequence is moved, and by nothing else, and the consequence is different from the first.*
>
> *The consequence moved: **the seventh work of eleven on the page from ninety-one years back, being the ford, the stones and the two posts**, and the duty of standing them and the cost of them, taken off the Slade Cut Company and off the schedule and off the rotation, and put onto one person.*
>
> *That person is M. Kest, of the Received Room, Auremar, four hundred and thirty miles from this bank. **I am not of this Company. I am not of this district. I have no house here and no household here and no child here, and there is no household in ninety-one households into which this can pass.***
>
> *Term: one year from the day this is acted on, and a review on the day, and the review is not a promise to continue. **If nobody comes to the review then this stops on the day, and there is no successor and nothing carries.***
>
> *The labour, named: five men at eight pence a day and a boy at five, being forty-five pence a day on the five and one, and about two days' work, being seven shillings and sixpence, and it is paid out of the wage of seventeen shillings and a penny the week of the person named above, out of his own hand, and not out of the Company and not out of a store's rent and not out of any fund, of which there is none.*
>
> *The liability, named: **I am answerable for it alone. No household, no child, no person in the schedule and no member of the Company is answerable for one penny of it or for one hour of it. I can be found, served and put in a room, and I can say no, and I am not going to be able to say no to a person standing in front of me, and I would rather be able to.**
>
> *Conditions: this is read out in this room twice, once for those who can read it and once for those who cannot. The person whose consent is required is asked in this room and the answer is written down with the day, and so is the other answer if there is one. **A refusal is not a vote and is not a matter for a meeting. If the person refuses, this stops today and the ford waits and that is the correct outcome and I have said so before anybody signs.**
>
> *Limits: it moves that consequence and no other. It is not a fund. It creates no office. It does not touch the first exception, or the Company, or the schedule, or any name in the book, and it does not put me in the book.*
>
> *M. Kest, clerk. **I have read no instrument. I have taken no reading. I have no certification, no office and no register.***
>
> *Consent of the foreman, asked and given in this room: the day — and her answer, and the day.*

Then he ruled the last line and read all of it out, twice, in about eleven minutes, and nine people who could not read a paragraph heard it a second time at the speed it is meant to be heard at.

---

**Halla Wray said no first.**

She said it in about nine sentences and she had it ready before he had the pen down for the last line, and nobody in that room had expected it, and about four people later said that they had.

"I have been asked three times about that ford since the first month of this year and I have said no three times," she said. "**Once in a mill yard in the first month, which was not about the bank. Once by my own book-keeper in a shed on the thirtieth of the last month. And once by a man of the district works in the rain on the fourteenth of this month with nine pages in his hand, and that was the third time and I said no in about eleven seconds.**

"And you are the fourth, and you are the first one who has asked me properly, and I am saying no anyway, and I am going to say the reason so that it is on the record and not on your paper.

"**Nobody asked me in the first month of this year whether I would hold the district's flood authority either.** I was put in a box by a keeper who did it on purpose and who has said since that she did not ask anybody, and I stood in that box for eleven weeks and nobody asked me a single question, and then my brother said one word in a water office four miles up that road and the box was opened from the outside without anybody asking me whether I wanted it open.

"So no. **I am not going to be the instrument of a man's confession, and a man in a city four hundred and thirty miles away who has spent four months not knowing what his own paper was for is not going to fix it by asking me to hold a work that nobody in ninety-one households has money for.**"

About thirty people in that bay were extremely quiet, and a woman near the door said, "Well. That's the ford, then."

And then Halla Wray said: "**But I am not finished, and I want it noticed that I said no before I said yes, and I have been a foreman on this cut for nine years and I have never once been asked for anything, and I know what a condition is, and I am going to set one and it is not about the work.**

"It is this. **Write down that I was asked, and write down that I said no, and put the day against both of them, and put both days on the list of works and not only the second one.**

"And the reason is not a clever reason. The reason is that in about four years somebody is going to come up that paper in a room four hundred and thirty miles from here and read the word *consented* on it, and there is going to be a day against that word, and if there is not another day on the same page with *refused* on it then in about four years the whole of this matter is going to look like a thing that was agreed, **and it was not agreed, it was asked twice and answered twice, and I was the one who answered it, and I want the no in the same hand as the yes.**"

Marek Kest wrote it down, and read it back, and made her read it back, and it is in the same hand as the yes.

"Yes," she said, and then, before anybody had said anything: "**And I want it read out that I said no first, in this room, and that nobody was asked afterwards to change my mind, and that the man who wrote it did not ask me twice because he did not need to.**"

---

The mark came about nine minutes after that and nobody in the bay understood it.

He was holding the paper down on the bench with the heel of his left hand and there is a line on the inside of his wrist, about two inches, and it was not there at the seventh hour.

**A counter-seal, the record of it, is the exception itself with his name at the top of it and a witness in the room, and it is a record and it is findable, and it is a human clerk's instrument and it does not cross a species and it will be produced in about four years by anybody who wants it.**

The mark is not the price.

That is the thing he had wrong in a book he has been reading for fourteen months and that a woman of twenty-four told him to look at in an office in the second month of this year, and it was she who said it in this room, in about nine minutes, to about thirty people, and she refused to soften it.

"The cost is a wage of seventeen shillings and a penny the week," said Tamsin Rook, "**and about seven shillings and sixpence a year for the term, out of your own hand, in a district four hundred and thirty miles from your desk, paid to five men and a boy in a currency you have never handled, and a review in the first week of the fifth month of next year that nobody has promised to come to and that you have promised to stop on.**

"That is the whole of it and it is a bill and it can be read out in a room to about thirty people.

"And there is a line on your wrist about two inches long, and I want to say four things about it and I am not going to be tender about any of them, **because the moment anybody in this matter is tender about a mark, the mark becomes the reason, and it is not the reason.**

"One. **The mark is a symptom and not a power and it decides nothing.** It has not read a document and it will not and it cannot. It is a line and it is a record that a burden has fallen on a person and it is findable, and findable is the thing we have spent two months being frightened of on other people's behalf.

"Two. **It is not a tether and it does not know where the ford is.** It is a line on a wrist in a city. If you go back to a desk in Auremar in a week it will still be there and the ford will still be full, and neither of those is caused by the other, and I want that said in a room because a man who has read about this in a book for fourteen months has been trained to expect the opposite and I have watched him expect it and I have not corrected him until now.

"Three. **You have bought a gate.** A gate a person can shut is the only thing worth having on this cut and there are about sixty of them shut on purpose at the foot of that bank at this moment, and the price of shutting one is that the person who shut it can be found, and that is the arrangement and it is the only one on offer and you have now paid for one of them out of your own wage and nobody else's.

"Four — and this is the one I would like written down and I am not going to write it down myself. **In about four years this is going to be the only gate on that bank with anybody findable behind it, and the reason will not be courage.** It will be that the other sixty are a rotation in a book with no heading, and this one is a man in a city with a wage, and a man with a wage can be written about. **You have not made that ford safer by being brave in a bay. You have made it findable, and the finding is the whole of what happens next, and I am saying it to you in a room with about thirty people in it about four days before it happens rather than about four years after.**"

"Then what is the mark."

"It is a line," said Tamsin Rook, "**and a wage is a figure, and you can read a figure out to thirty people and you cannot read a line out to anybody, and if you go home in a year with that still on you and no ford behind it then the line will be the only thing you have, and everybody in that room will be sorry for you, and being sorry for you is the worst thing that can happen to a thing that is holding a bank.**"

---

Doreth Ferrow asked him one question, at about the ninth hour, and she asked it in the ordinary way and it was the only thing she said in nine minutes.

"Son. **Your term is a year. What happens to the ford on the day the year is up and you are not in it.**"

"Somebody else stands it."

"Somebody else stands it and nobody has asked them and they do not know about it and there is no paper about it," said Doreth Ferrow. "**And you have written the day of the review on a piece of paper in a bay and you have written on it that if nobody comes then it stops, and that is the first time in nineteen years that I have seen anybody put the day on a thing.**

"And I am not going to argue with you about the wrist, because I cannot read a paragraph and I have not the least idea what a wrist is for, and I have been told by a young woman that it decides nothing and I have believed her because she said it in a straight voice and has not been paid to say anything.

"**But I am going to say the thing that is in the room, and it is this, and then I am going to bed.**

"A gate a person can shut is the only thing worth having, and about sixty of them are shut on purpose at the foot of that bank, and every one of them is shut by somebody somebody can find, and the price of shutting one is that you can be found.

"And a man from a city has now been found four hundred and thirty miles away and he has shut one of the sixty from about four hundred and thirty miles away, and nobody in this room is going to be able to hand it on, and I have been the chair of this meeting for nineteen years and I have never in my life had to hand anything on and I do not want to learn at sixty-four.

"So somebody had better tell me what the ford is going to be in the second week of the sixth month, **and there is not one person in ninety-one households who is going to be told about it in a room, and I have been counting rooms for nineteen years and I know exactly how many of them there are.**"

---

The paper went on the bench in the middle of the bay with two names at the foot of it, and two days, and about nine figures in the middle of it.

A counter-seal is a record and it is findable, and it will be produced in about four years by anybody who wants it, and it is a human clerk's instrument and it does not cross a species, and the person it is about is a person and can be found and served and put in a room and can say no, and **the counter-mark is what a person has instead of a promise that somebody else will hold it.**

The counter-mark is a symptom of the legal burden and not a separate power. It does not read anything, it decides nothing, and it imposes no geographic tether and no obligation that is not written on the paper in front of about thirty people.

And a foreman of fifty-one said no, in about nine sentences, in a cold bay, to about thirty people, and then said yes on a condition about her own record, and the condition is in the same hand as the consent and the day of the refusal is on the same page as the day of the yes.

He had read no instrument and taken no reading in this room, and nobody in ninety-one households had read one either, and the only two readings in the whole of this matter happened in a different city fourteen months ago in an audit of a file about a field.
