# Chapter 112: A Column With No Heading

The Reed Cut district works is a yard, a slipway, a wet rope and a book, and it is four miles below the Slade end on the same side of the channel, and it is a stone building with a door that is painted the district's colour and shuts, and the door is the only door in the district of its kind that shuts.

Wendt Sayer is a foreman. He has nine men, and a boy, and the wet rope, and he has had the nine men for between four and nine years depending on which of them you ask, and he has been in this office eleven years and in the district thirty-one. He keeps the district's return book, which is a book of what the district has been asked to do and what it did, and he is paid out of a works fund under a head that says bank works, and that head has been empty since the third week of the twelfth month, and across a good quarter it has held as much as three pounds and it has never held more than a fortnight of a crew at one time.

He is the man who paid two shillings and sixpence out of his own fund, out of the head *labour, examinations, engaged*, on the sixth of the fourth of last year, for a girl of seventeen's examination in canal work, and entered it as a recruitment cost and not as a kindness, seven weeks before the examination, and has not been thanked and has not asked.

He holds one of the four pages of the copy list in his works book, which is an office that does not exist, and anybody may have a copy at four pence, and he gave his page away in the fourth month of last year without asking her a single question about it.

---

The book is about two inches thick and it is the district's own and it has been kept in this yard for thirty-one years and it has four columns.

> *One. The day.*
> *Two. What was asked.*
> *Three. What was sent.*
> *Four.*

The fourth column has nothing over it. It has never had anything over it. There is a ruled line and under the ruled line, nine years of entries, and in the space at the top of the column where a heading goes there is about an inch of bare paper, and the inch has been bare for the whole thirty-one years, and it is bare in a different way from the rest of the book because the rest of the book was ruled by a man who was told what to write and the fourth column was ruled by the same man and he was not told.

The fourth column holds the name of whoever reported the work.

That is all it does. It does not hold the name of the person who did it, and it does not hold the name of the person who is answerable for it, and it has never held either, and **it is a column of *who spoke* and not a column of *who answers*, and the difference between those two things is the whole of why nobody from the Slade is in a box in this district and has been for nine years.**

Wendt Sayer has known that for nine years and has never said it out loud to anybody, and he has never been asked, and there is not anybody in this district who is going to ask him.

---

The letter came up the road on the seventh day of the third month, at about the seventh hour, with a man who was going to the gate anyway for his own reasons, and Wendt Sayer read it standing at the bench with the gate open behind him and the book shut.

Four lines. Two of them about the court and not about this office. One of them asking a question. One of them saying there was no money in it.

*Can the district works put a person's name against the Slade? Not the Company's. A person's. And if they can, what is done to that person afterwards, and who asks about it, and how often.*

He read it four times and put it in his coat, and he did not answer it that day, and the reason he did not answer it that day was that he could not answer it at all, and a man who cannot answer a question in a room should not answer it in writing, and he has watched two people in his life answer a thing in writing that they could not answer in a room and he has never forgotten either of them.

---

The next morning he did the thing he does before he does anything else, which is to go down the yard with the book on the arm and read the last year of it, because nine years is not a long time in a book that has been kept for thirty-one and he is a man who reads his own book and not the other way round.

The Slade is in it nine years out of nine.

It is in it the way it is in everything this district has ever written about it, which is as a place a crew goes. In the second column: *works requested, bank, outer face, Slade.* In the third: *crew of six, three days, sent.* In the fourth: nothing at all for the first three years, because the first three years the yard clerk was a man who did not put anything in the fourth column at all.

In the fourth year the fourth column begins, and there are four entries in it for the whole district in that year, and one of the four is the Slade, and the name in it is the name of the man who walked up to the yard gate and said the bank wanted doing. He was the man about the works gate. He was not a foreman and he was not a holder and he was not answerable for anything and he was not paid by this office and he did not want to be paid by this office. **He was the only person in the district's own book for that bank between the second year and the present, and he was there because he came.**

The fifth year has two entries for the Slade and the fifth year has eleven in the district. The sixth year has three. And then it stops, and the reason it stops is not a decision anybody made; the reason it stops is that the man who wrote in the fourth column went to another district in the sixth year and there was nobody after him, because there has been no fourth clerk in this office since the sixth year and the works' own return goes up to the district office on the first of the month in a bundle with the returns of two other yards.

Wendt Sayer has been writing the fourth column himself for three years, and he has written about four hundred names into it, and every one of the four hundred names is a person who reported something.

The district has been mapped twice since he was born, and in thirty-eight years the district's own book has not had a person against that bank since it was last mapped, and the last time it had one it was in a map, in a column, in a hand nobody in this yard has ever met.

---

He got the pen out at about the sixth hour of the eighth day of the third month and he did not do it in four minutes and he has not told anybody since how long it took him, and it was most of the morning.

What he wrote is in his own return book, at the back, on a leaf he has ruled himself, and it is the only thing in that book in three years that is not a return.

> *Entered the eighth day of the third month, on the foreman's own initiative, and asked for by nobody, and not a finding, and not a claim, and not a return, and sent to nobody.*
>
> *The spring bank at the Slade. Outer face from the third-of-the-way mark to the eleven feet, the north ditch, the kerb, the coping. Begun the seventh of the first month and done the twenty-first of the second month, nineteen days, five men and a boy. It was done by a body of households from this cut and by nobody else. The five men and the boy were paid by a clerk of a city four hundred and thirty miles from this yard, out of his own wage, and not out of any fund of this district or of this office, and the works fund's head was empty and had been since the third week of the twelfth month, and no sum of it went to that bank.*
>
> *This office sent no man to that bank in the first month or the second month or at any time in the spring. This office was not asked. This office was not asked because a company is not a person and cannot ask an office for anything, and this office could not have been asked by anybody else, and this office could not have gone and done it, because a district works cannot compel a work and cannot be compelled to do one, and the office that holds the flood authority for that cut is not this office and gave it away in writing on the ninth of the first month to two persons and to no body, which this office has read and has entered here, and is not answered and does not approve.*
>
> *The district of this office has a flood authority in no matter in nine years, in this book or in any other book, and has had none since it was last mapped.*

And then, at the foot, in his own hand, with the day against it:

> *W. Sayer, foreman, Reed Cut district works. I have put my name under this because nobody asked me to and because I have been the keeper of the fourth column for three years and have never once put my name at the foot of anything in it, and there is no reason on this earth why a column of who spoke should want the name of the man who spoke it, and I am not going to say in this yard that the reason is that a name under an entry is a name a man with a tray can ask about, and I am writing it here because it is true and because I am the wrong side of sixty and I would like one true thing in this book.*

Then he read it back, and he did not cross anything out, and he did not put a heading over the fourth column.

The cost of it is that the works' return goes up to the district office on the first of the month in a bundle with the returns of two other yards, and that Wendt Sayer has signed that bundle on the first of the month for eleven years, and that a leaf at the back of his own book is now on its way upstairs once a month to a man he has met four times, and that no yard in this district has ever returned *nothing done, not asked, could not be asked* in eleven years of returns.

"You have written down that this office did nothing," he said to the book, "and it is true, and there is a man above me who reads this book on the first of the month and has four times asked me whether my figures were complete. And on the first of next month he is going to get a leaf with a sentence on it that is not a return, and he is going to ask me what it is, and there is no form in this office for a foreman to say *it is not a return and I wrote it anyway*."

---

He said the rest of it out loud in the yard at about the seventh hour, standing at the bench with four men and the boy in it, and he says the last part of it every month and it has not got shorter.

"**You are all going to think I have gone clever.**

"I have not gone clever. I have done a thing I have wanted to do for nine years and I have not done because nobody has ever asked me to, and that is the whole of my career in one sentence and I would like somebody to write it down.

"Here is the finding and it is nine years old and it is mine and I am giving it away for nothing like everything else in this district.

"**A column with no heading over it is findable without being answerable.** That is the whole of it. A man comes to this yard and asks me what the district did at the Slade in the fifth year and I can tell him, and I can tell him the name of the man who told me, and the name of the man who told me is the name of a man who came up a road one day and said the bank wanted doing. That is what is in the fourth column. It is not a list of anybody's servants. **It is a list of about four hundred people over three years who walked into a yard and said a thing, and not one of them is answerable for it, and not one of them has ever been asked to be, and that is because the man who ruled this book was not told to write a heading and he did not write one.**

"And I have thought about writing a heading over that column. I have thought about it in the first month of every year for nine years. I have got as far as a piece of paper with four words on it in my own hand more than once, and I have thrown it away twice.

"**Because the day there is a heading over that column, every one of those about four hundred people in three years becomes a person who answers for something.** And I could name them. I have got the names. That is what the column is for. And in nine years I have watched what happens to a name in a column in this district and I am not going to do it to a man who came up a road once to say a bank wanted doing, because he did not know and I did not tell him and nobody asked me to tell him.

"So the column has no heading. And I am not going to give it one. And I am going to say the whole of it out loud in this yard now, once, because **I have just put my own name at the foot of an entry in this book for the first time in nine years and if I do not say why I did it, then a man with a tray in four years is going to read my name and think I put it there because I wanted the work.**

"I did not want the work. There was no work. **I put my name there because the district did nothing and somebody ought to have to write that down, and about nine years is how long it has taken me to work out that a thing nobody writes down is a thing nobody did.**"

Nobody in the yard said anything for a while and then the boy said, "Does that mean the fourth column is yours now."

"It has never been mine," said Wendt Sayer. "It has been everybody's who ever walked in that gate. And **it has got no heading over it, and it is going to keep having no heading over it for as long as I keep this book, and if you want to change that then it is a thing you do over my body and not a thing you do in a meeting.**"

---

He did not answer the letter that day either, and he answered it on the ninth, in writing, in four lines, and sent it up the road with the man going down for his own reasons.

> *From the foreman of the Reed Cut district works to the keeper of the book of the Slade Cut Company, the ninth day of the third month.*
>
> *The district works cannot put a person's name against the Slade. I have looked for a way it can and there is not one, and the reason there is not one is that a district works may send men and pay a crew and keep a book, and has no form in which it makes anybody answer for anything, and the only way a person comes into a district book is by walking in and saying a thing, and that is not the same and I am not going to pretend it is.*
>
> *What is done to a person afterwards is nothing, because nothing has ever been done to any of them, and I have now written down in my own book with the day on it that the district did not do the spring bank and was not asked and could not have been asked, and my name is at the foot of it, and I would like you to know that and to tell the Company, and I do not know why I want you to tell the Company and I have not had time to find out.*
>
> *I am not asking you for anything and there is no money in this. W. Sayer.*

And the two letters went up and down a four-mile road inside three days, and neither of them contained a single word that could be acted on by anybody, and both of them had a person's name at the foot of them, and the man who received the second one read it in a store and put it in the Company's book with the day on it against the entry, **and read it out loud to about thirty people in a room that was not a kitchen six days later, and it was the first thing anybody had said in nineteen years to tell a woman of sixty-four that her no had been the right one.**

---

And on the tenth day of the third month, a man of twenty-nine walked up the two hundred yards from the gate to a counter in a building with a stove in the back room and said he wanted to be taken out of a box.

He had been in the box for seven weeks. He had not asked. He had said no in a mill yard in front of about forty people and he was right, and he had said out loud that he was not going to be asked twice, and he had said that sentence to a girl of seventeen in a kitchen, and a clerk of a district water office had written it down and read it back.

He was going to find out what it cost.
