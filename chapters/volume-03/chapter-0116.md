# Chapter 116: A Rule That Certifies Itself

The north bay of the store off the Slade is thirty-one foot by nineteen with the floor a foot off the ground, and it has a bench against the north wall that came with the building, and a door in the west end whose lintel has dropped about half an inch in about forty years and which has never been packed and does not shut. It holds about thirty and the miller has counted it twice with a crowd in it. It is cold at the seventh hour in the last week of the third month and it is warmer than nine kitchens, because nine kitchens have eleven people in them and this has thirty.

Nobody rented it. It was given on the sixth day of the third month, for a year, in a doorway, in about four minutes, and it costs nothing, and the whole of the Slade Cut Company's money is four shillings a year and it is the rent of the other end of the same building.

Doreth Ferrow is sixty-four. She has kept this meeting for nineteen years. It has been in four kitchens and once in a mill yard, and on the twentieth of the first month it was in a mill yard with about forty people in it and nine who had not been told, and it ended with no resolution, and the entry in the Company's book says so with the day on it. She cannot read a paragraph and has not pretended to for fifty years. She can add, she can count a room, and she has about sixty children under sixteen in that cut and has met the two youngest of them.

She called it for the seventh hour on the twenty-eighth day of the third month and she put it up in a way she has never put anything up, which is that she told four people on the road and three of them told somebody, and the fourth one told nobody, and she wrote the day and the hour on the back of Tace's paper and left it in the south end of the store where the book is.

---

There were about thirty of them. She counted the room at the start, out loud, standing at the west end by the door that does not shut, the way she has counted forty meetings, and she wrote the figure on the back of her hand and nobody argued with it because nobody ever has.

"Right," she said. "**Thirty.** And I am going to do the other half of it now, because I have never once done the other half and I have been ashamed of it for nineteen years and I have got about an hour.

"**There are about sixty people in ninety-one households in this cut who are not in this room.** Not tonight. Not in this bay. And I am not going to ask anybody to guess which ones and I am not going to read the Company's book out loud tonight.

"And I want to say why, and it is the reason I called this in here instead of in a kitchen, and I have been thinking about it since the sixth of the third month and I have got it now.

"**A rule that only binds the people in the room binds the people in the room.** That is the whole of it. I have chaired nineteen years of meetings and every word that has ever been agreed in one has only ever bound the people who were in it, and the rest of the cut have gone along with it for sixty years because nobody has ever written it down that they did.

"So I am going to read you a rule I have written for this bay, and it is mine, and I have written it in my own hand, and I cannot read it, and Rennick Adley is going to read it out, and **I am going to make him read it out twice, on purpose, because the first time is for the people who can read and the second time is for the people who cannot, and nineteen years of meetings in kitchens have been one time only and the one time has been for the people who can read.**"

---

He read it out. It is on one side of a sheet and it is in a hand of sixty-four and it is nine lines.

> *The rule of the meeting of the Slade Cut Company. Written the twenty-eighth of the third month by D. Ferrow, who has kept this meeting nineteen years, and asked for by nobody, and agreed to by nobody.*
>
> *One. There is a meeting when a person says there is going to be one. There is a place. The place and the day are written down, and the writing is put where the book is.*
>
> *Two. The person who called the meeting counts the people in the room out loud at the beginning, and counts the people who are not in the room out loud at the end, and the two figures are written down together and not apart.*
>
> *Three. Nothing is agreed to. Anything a person wants agreed to is written down and read out, and the person who called the meeting says what it would cost, and then it stops.*
>
> *Four. Any person may come. A person who cannot read a paragraph may come, and anybody in the room may read anything to anybody in the room.*
>
> *Five. **This is a rule and not a resolution and not an instrument and not a vote and not a demand. It is issued by nobody. It binds nobody. Any person in this room may stand up and say they never agreed to it and be believed.***

He read it out again, slower, and about nine people followed the second reading by watching the sheet go up and down, which is a thing people do.

Then Doreth Ferrow took it off him.

"Now I am going to tell you what that is, because Rennick has read it and you have all heard it and nobody in this room knows what to do with a piece of paper, including me.

"**It is a certificate of a kind that certifies itself.** Issued by nobody. Binding nobody. Deniable by anybody, and the denying is in it, in the fifth line, on purpose.

"And that is the first paper this body has ever had. Nineteen years, forty meetings, four kitchens, a mill yard, nine hundred and forty names in a book with no heading at the top of it, four shillings a year for a room to keep it dry — and this is the first thing any of it has ever had that is a *paper about the Company*, and it certifies the Company, and there is nobody in this empire who issued it, and every one of you may stand up and say it is not ours and be believed.

"And I have been a chair for nineteen years and I have never once had that. **Everything I have ever done in a meeting has been something people could have stopped, and I have never once had a thing that they can go back on in the morning.**

"Which brings me to the free room."

---

"I want to say the second thing and then I am going to sit down, and the second thing is about the bay.

"**This bay costs nothing.** Not a farthing, not a shilling a quarter, not anything. Aven Tace gave it on the sixth in about four minutes and there is no paper of it anywhere except a line in our own book, and I put that line there and I made sure there was no paper.

"And it took me nineteen years to work out what that means and it took me nine days after the sixth of the third month, and it is this.

"**A body that cannot be paid for cannot be compelled. A room that is given to a body is a room nobody can be asked to open.** In nineteen years of chairing I have never once been able to say *I am going to do this here whether you like it or not*, and I have never wanted to say it, and I want to say it about as much as I want my hand.

"So I am going to say the thing about free, and I have not said it to anybody and I would like it said in a room.

"**A thing which is free is a thing nobody can refuse.** And that goes the other way too, and I have thought about it in four directions for nine days and this is where it lands: a thing which is free is a thing nobody can *take* off you either. Tace cannot take that bay back at the end of the year and go on about his business and never mention it, because there is no price in it and there is nothing to be quarreled with, and if he is a decent man about it then I will be told, and if he is not then I will find out in the ninth month, which is too late, and I have got a year.

"That is the first thing I have noticed in nineteen years that I did not already know, and I have noticed it because somebody gave us a room for nothing, and I would like the man who gave it to us to be in this room tonight and he is not, because he stood in his own door for an hour in the twentieth of the first month and said nothing, and I have not told him any of this, and I am not going to, and if anybody in this room goes and tells him I will hear about it."

---

Then Rennick Adley read the letter out.

It is four lines and it is in a hand of about sixty on the return book of the Reed Cut district works, and it came up the road on the seventh of the third month and was answered on the ninth, and the answer is the second half of the thing that is in the letter, and he read both, and about four people in that bay had not heard the first one.

> *From the foreman of the Reed Cut district works to the keeper of the book of the Slade Cut Company, the ninth day of the third month.*
>
> *The district works cannot put a person's name against the Slade. I have looked for a way it can and there is not one, and the reason there is not one is that a district works may send men and pay a crew and keep a book, and has no form in which it makes anybody answer for anything, and the only way a person comes into a district book is by walking in and saying a thing, and that is not the same and I am not going to pretend it is.*
>
> *What is done to a person afterwards is nothing, because nothing has ever been done to any of them, and I have now written down in my own book with the day on it that the district did not do the spring bank and was not asked and could not have been asked, and my name is at the foot of it, and I would like you to know that and to tell the Company, and I do not know why I want you to tell the Company and I have not had time to find out.*
>
> *I am not asking you for anything and there is no money in this. W. Sayer.*

He read it twice, and about nine people followed the second reading, and then the bay was quiet for a bit.

Then Doreth Ferrow said the thing she had come down to say, and it is the fourth thing she has said in this volume and it is the one she is going to remember.

"**That is the first time in nineteen years that anybody has stood up in a room and told me I was right.** And it is a man I have never met, about a different building, about a thing that has nothing to do with a mill yard.

"And he did not say *you were right, Doreth.* He said *the district could not have been asked, and here is why, and here is the figure on it.* **He put a number on the reason.** Four hundred people in three years walked into a yard and said a thing, and not one of them is answerable for it, because a man ruled a column in thirty-one years ago and nobody told him to write a heading over it.

"And I want everybody in this bay to hear the shape of it, because it is the shape of the twentieth of the first month.

"**I said no in a mill yard to a body and a household and a box, and I was right, and a man with a book four miles away has spent nine years doing the same thing by accident without ever deciding to.** And I want to say to him, and I am not going to write to him, and I am saying it in a cold room with a door that will not shut, and it is enough: *you did it by accident and it worked, and I did it on purpose, and neither of us will ever be asked to do it again, and about ninety of us were in that room and about forty were not, and the forty were not in it for the same reason the sixty are not in this bay tonight.*"

Somebody said, from about the middle, "Whose name is on the foreman's letter."

"Nobody's name is on the foreman's letter except the foreman's," said Rennick Adley, "and his name is at the foot of it, in his own hand, for the first time in nine years, and he says in the third line that he does not know why he wanted me to tell you. **He is a stranger and he has put a name under a thing in a district book on his own initiative and nobody asked him, and that is the first thing a stranger has done for this cut in sixty years, and I am not going to say a word about it tonight, and I am going to read it out again in about four minutes for anybody who was not here the first time.**"

---

She did the last of it at about the eighth hour, and it took about nine minutes, and it is the reason this bay is different from every other room in ninety-one households.

"Last thing, and then I am going to sit down and I am not going to be asked anything tonight.

"There is a paper in this room that is not mine and not the Company's. It is a miller's paper about four hundred and forty foot of the outer face at the third-of-the-way place, and it has got a man's name at the top of it and nine dates on it, and it was written in the sixth year and sent four times to the district works and came back four times, and nobody has ever gone to the mill and asked for it.

"It has been in that envelope in a kitchen for six years and nobody has ever asked him for it. And that is the same as a thing in a box in a water office, and the only difference between them is that a person in a box has been *put* in it and nobody has been put in that envelope. **He put himself in it, in his own hand, and then he could not get himself out, and that is a failure and it is six years long and it has cost that man nothing and it has cost this cut the whole of the evidence.**

"And I am not going to have it in an envelope any more. I have been a chair for nineteen years and I have never once had a bench."

They moved the bench. It is a long bench, about nine foot, and it came with the building, and thirty people got a hand under it and carried it out of the wall and put it down the middle of the bay, and the floor is a foot off the ground and it is stone and the bench is heavy, and it took four people and about four minutes.

Then Doreth Ferrow put the miller's paper on it, flat, in the middle, and stood back about two paces.

"**A thing on a bench is findable.** That is what a bench is. It is not a table and it is not a shelf and it is not an envelope. **A bench in the middle of a room is a thing anybody can walk up to and look at, and in nineteen years of chairing I have not had one of those either.**

"From tonight there is a bench in the middle of this bay and things get put on it. The rule gets put on it. The paper gets put on it. The letter gets put on it. **Anything anybody in ninety-one households has ever wanted to be heard about gets put on that bench at a meeting, and gets read out, and nobody has to own it, and it is not in a book in a store four miles from anybody's house.**

"And it certifies nothing. That is what I want. **I have been afraid for nineteen years of putting anything on a list and here is a list of things and I have made it so that nobody is answerable for it and anybody may deny it in the morning, and that is the only way I can think of to have a paper and not have a debt.**"

She sat down, which she does at the end, and the meeting went on for another hour and about nine people said things and nothing was agreed to and nothing was resolved, and about a third of the people in that room could not read a paragraph and about nine of them were spoken to by somebody sitting next to them without being asked to say whether they wanted it.

---

And that is the whole of the twenty-eighth day of the third month, and it is the largest room the Slade Cut Company has ever had, and it is not large enough.

A woman of sixty-four has a rule with the day on it that is issued by nobody and binds nobody and can be denied by any person in the room. A bench is in the middle of a hired bay with a door that does not shut. About thirty persons of about ninety-one households were in it and about sixty were not, and those two figures are written on the back of a hand of sixty-four in a figure of thirty and a figure of about sixty, together and not apart.

A miller's paper with a miller's name at the top of it is on a bench, in a room, in front of thirty people, for the first time in six years.

And a foreman of fifty-one was in that bay on the twenty-eighth of the third month and her term had run out in the third week of the month, and she had said out loud in four rooms that she would rather be asked to hold it again than not, and nobody asked her, and Doreth Ferrow noticed and did not ask, and has not told anybody she noticed.
