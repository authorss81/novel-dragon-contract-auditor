# Chapter 123: Four Persons, and Sixty Who Are Not

The Mosswake Roll Court is a building on a flood terrace with a court room on the ground floor, a clerk's window at the back of it, a board above the sill, and an embankment corridor behind that with four presses in it.

The clerk's window is the same window a man of about fifty came up the road to in the fourth week of the first month of last year with a shilling in his hand, and the same window a woman of twenty-four put a shilling on before the hour was over, because a person who has paid can answer without owing anybody.

Ivet Sarn is thirty-eight and has been a clerk of this court for nine years and has a book with no office on it in the second drawer, and everything she reads she writes down in her own hand first, in the first person, before she copies anything, and says who is not in the room.

On the fifth day of the fourth month she had four persons on a board in a room four miles up a towpath from a grain store, and two of them were names and two of them were descriptions, and one of the descriptions was of a woman who had refused a name in her own doorway on the last day of the third month.

---

The morning of the fifth was the ordinary morning. The board above the sill had a hearing slate on it with about four entries on it and a register of pending duties in a book behind the window, and the flue in the room behind the window had been cut on the fourteenth of the second month and the room behind the embankment corridor was cold in a way that four presses and about a hundred and forty files do not fix.

At about the ninth hour she wrote in the book with no office on it, on a page of about two hundred lines in her own hand in the first person, the thing she writes at the top of every sitting, which is what she is reading and who is not in the room.

> *Fifth of the fourth month. The application of the thirty-first of the third, in the wrong form, admitted under the undertaking of the ninth of the second, the magistrate not in the building. I am going to read it again before anybody speaks and I am going to say who is not in the room. Not in the room: the Slade Cut Company, which is not a person and cannot be one; the keeper of the Reed Cut district water office, who has written to nobody; the district works, which cannot be asked; the man who took nine stones out of a ford; the boy of fifteen at the north of the ford, who is not on the form. And not in the room and not anywhere: about sixty people in about ninety-one households who have not been asked, who cannot be compelled, and who are not anybody's debtors.*

She read the whole of the application out to herself, in about nine minutes, standing, in a room with a clerk's window and a board.

Then she went and stood at the window and looked up the road for about two minutes, and there were four people on it, and about nine minutes' walk behind them there were no more.

---

The magistrate came in at about the tenth hour and sat without being asked and without anybody having told her anybody was here.

Leda Rell is fifty-one and nine years a magistrate of this court and fourteen years a clerk of it before that, and she has about a hundred and forty files in a press in the back room with her own hand in every one of them, and she has said out loud in nine years of this chair that she does not know whether she was right and that not knowing has not stopped her.

She said, before anybody had spoken: "**I am here on my own motion and I have not been asked and I was not told this was on, and I am going to say why I came and then I am going to sit down and say nothing else for about two hours.**

"I came because a form went in on the last day of the third month that is the wrong form, and it was admitted by a clerk under a written undertaking of mine of the ninth of the second, and I was not in the building and was not asked and do not have to be. **That undertaking has now been used twice in a matter I have never heard of and I have been told about the second use and I have not been asked to agree to it, and I am not going to refuse it, and I am going to enter that I was not asked.**

"And I am not going to say anything about whether I was right. I said in the second month of this year that I do not know, and it is the eighth of the second month that I said it, and it is the fifth of the fourth month now, and nothing has happened in between that has told me anything, and I am not going to manufacture a feeling about it in front of four people from a lane."

---

There were four persons, and they came up the road at different times and for different reasons, and this is what each of them did.

The person at the second house is about fifty-eight and is called Roan Skell and he can read a paragraph, and he is the only one of four houses who can, and nobody has asked him to read anything in sixty years. He was asked to read the application out loud in the room, at the magistrate's own request and not the applicant's, and he read about a third of it and then stopped and said that he could not read the rest of it in a room with a court in it.

"That is a thing you may say," said Leda Rell, "and it is entered, and it is the first time in sixty years that anybody in that lane has asked you to read anything and I would like it noticed that it was asked and not that you could do it."

The person at the third house is about forty-four and gave a name in about eleven minutes on the last day of the third month after asking four times what the paper was for, and she read all of it, out loud, twice, because Rennick Adley asked her to, and she asked again what it was for in the middle of the second reading and was answered.

The person at the fourth house is about seventy-one and is called Bel Skell and cannot read a paragraph and has not pretended to for fifty-five years. She was told, out loud, in her own door, on the last day of the third month, that she was on it, and she did not ask to see it and did not refuse it, and she came up four miles of towpath in about two hours, which is two hours there and two hours back, at seventy-one, with a bad leg, on the advice of nobody.

She said one thing in the room and it took her about eleven seconds.

"I want it read to me twice and I want the second reading to be slower than the first one, and I want to be asked afterwards whether I have anything to say, and I am not going to have anything to say, and I would like the asking."

The fourth person is a woman of about thirty-one with two children, who gave no name and is described in the form as *the woman at the first house who was in her own door on the last day of the third month.* She came up with a boy on her hip and she did not sit down, and she asked, in the fourth minute, whether anybody could tell her what the paper said about her.

"It says what you did in your own door," said Ivet Sarn, from the front of the bench, "and it says that you are a party to this application from the day it was filed and cannot be un-named, and it says that a described person is a person and is harder to serve and easier to lose than a named one, and that you are entered because the alternative is that you are not here at all."

"Then I am here," said the woman, "and I would like somebody to say what a party is, because nobody has said it to me, and I have had three days to work out what it is and I have not got it, and I have a boy on my hip and I am not going to work it out alone in a lane with a leg in it."

---

Nobody said it, and the reason nobody said it is that the only person in that room who could have said it properly was four hundred and thirty miles away on a towpath, and there was nobody else there who knew.

So the magistrate said it, and she said it in about four minutes, standing, and she said that she was not going to pretend she was the right person for it.

"**A party is a person with a door.** A person who can be found. Who can be served. Who can be put in a room. **And who can say no.** And the last of those is the only one of the four that is worth anything, and it is the one nobody in this room has been talking about, and I have been a magistrate for nine years and I have never said that sentence out loud in this room and I have said it four times in sheds and yards to about four hundred people in the last two months, and one of the four was a foreman who got it from a man of twenty-nine in a mill yard in the first month and neither of them knows I have it.

"So. **What I am hearing you about is a bank of about four hundred and forty foot of outer face, and a ford, and two posts and nine stones, and a water expected in the third week of this month.** I am hearing four persons about it. **I am not hearing ninety-one households about it and I cannot, and a hearing that brings four persons out of about ninety-one households binds nobody who was not in the room, and that is not a flaw in this sitting, it is the shape of the only door there is.**"

---

Then the turn, and it came from the bench and not from the clerk, and it was four lines and it was made on the magistrate's own motion and asked for by nobody.

"**I am going to direct that these four persons be given notice.** Not of this hearing — they are in it. Notice that they are parties to this application, and what the application is about, and what was said in this room this morning about them. **Because a person who has been made a party by a form is entitled to know that they are one before a water and not only after it, and there is a district court four hundred and thirty miles from this building that found on the ninth of the eighth month of last year that a person affected by a form of words in general use may be heard about it before it takes effect upon them and not only afterwards, and I have that transcript in a file in my back room and I have read it out of that file once in nine years and I have not read it out of it since, and reading it out would oblige me to say what I think about it and I am not going to.**

"So: notice. To four persons. Two named, two described, **and one of the two described cannot read a paragraph and one of the two described has no name at all, and this court is going to have to serve notice on a person who refused to give a name, and I am going to find out this morning whether there is a form for that in this building.**"

---

There is a form for notifying a body. There is no form for notifying a person who has refused to give a name, and there is no form for notifying a person who cannot read a paragraph, and Ivet Sarn went through the rack at the back of the window in front of nine people and found the first of them in about two minutes and the second of them in about nine, and the second of them is not a separate form — it is a space in the ordinary form with nothing in it, and the ordinary form says *the person notified can read*, in a place where nobody has ever put a question about that word.

"Right," said the magistrate. "**Then this court will write it out by hand, in a clerk's hand, with the day on it, and read into the record that there is no form for a person who cannot read a paragraph and no form for a person who has no name, and both of those are facts about this building and not about the people in the lane.** And clerk, you will enter the word *described* opposite the fourth person, in your own hand, in the minute, and not in a column, because a column is a list of persons who answer for something and I am not making a list of persons who answer for something."

---

And then Sarn admitted it, which is the third time, and she said so out loud before she did it.

"I am going to use the undertaking again and I am going to say that this is the third time, and I am going to say it in a room, and I am not going to say it in the corridor.

"**The form for this direction is the form for notifying a body of persons. It is the wrong form. I cannot admit a direction on my own authority and the magistrate is on the bench and she has made it on her own motion, and this is the third time in this matter that a written undertaking of the ninth of the second month has been used by me to let something through the wrong form with the person who wrote it not in the building.**

"I have noticed what that is. I noticed it four days ago, in the middle of an afternoon, doing nothing else, and I have been carrying it since, and I am going to write it down tonight and not tonight only.

"**It is a sentence written by a person in nine years, in nine rooms, and used by anybody with a pen, about a matter the person who wrote it is not in and has not heard of and does not have to be. The reason it works is that it is written down. The reason that is dangerous is the same thing, and I have been a clerk at a window for nine years and I have been the person who does not ask, and I have got a book in a drawer with about two hundred lines in it in my own hand in the first person, and this is the first sentence I have ever written down in that book that is about me.**"

She wrote the direction out in her own hand, and read it back, and made the magistrate read it back, and the four persons heard it read.

And then, because the direction could not be served by anybody, the court did the only thing it had, which is that it entered the four descriptions in the minute as four descriptions, with a day, and a note that each of the four persons would be spoken to by a person going to the lane, and that **the person going to the lane is not an officer of this court and is not paid and has not been asked to be either, and the court cannot pay her, because she is not doing it as a servant of this court and there is no fund, and a person who turns up is never paid and cannot be.**

---

The sitting was two hours and a quarter and it rose at about the quarter past twelve.

At about the quarter to one, when the room was empty, Ivet Sarn stood at the window with the minute in her hand and read the two figures out loud to the empty room, because she counts rooms and she had counted this one.

"There were four of the four in the room," she said, "and nine of the rest of them were outside it, in the lane and on the road, and about sixty of the about ninety-one households are not anywhere, and have not been asked, and cannot be compelled, and are not parties to anything, and are not anybody's debtors, and there is no form in this empire for compelling a person to be brought to a room.

"**And that is the second time in a month that those two figures have been written on the back of a person's hand together and not apart, and the other one was a woman of sixty-four doing it in a mill yard four miles up the road, and neither of us knew about the other, and neither of us is a magistrate, and I want that written down as the finding of a clerk who is counting rooms and is not a court.**"

Then she shut the minute and went and stood in the embankment corridor for about a minute and a half, in the cold, among about a hundred and forty files in four presses, and she put her hand on the spine of one of them — a file of the sixth year, at about knee height, in the fourth press — and she did not open it and she did not pull it out.

Nobody had asked her to.

She had asked herself, on the fifth of the fourth month, in a corridor, which is a thing she has never done in nine years, and she did not write it down, and she has said since that the reason she did not write it down is that a person who does not ask has not been given a duty and she is not going to hand herself one at a quarter to one in the afternoon on a day when four people have been in her room.
