# Chapter 131: A Body Cannot Open Its Own Post

The letter came up the road on the eighth day of the fifth month in a cart with some seed, and it has been in that yard ever since, under a tarpaulin against the south wall, and it is five days old and it has not been opened.

Aven Tace is about fifty. He has run this mill since he was thirty-one and he can read, and he has read the outside of it four times, standing up, in the rain, without going inside for it.

It is addressed to **the Slade Cut Company, Slade End.** Not to a person. Not to a house. Not to a foreman. The name of a body, in a hand nobody on that bank has ever seen, and the address is a place where a body keeps its book.

There is a line on the outside of it under the address, in the same hand, and it says: *A thing is to be entered against a person and not against a body.*

He has been looking at that line since the eighth and it is the reason the paper is still standing in his yard and he wants that on the page before anything else is said about it, because it is the only line on it that he understood on the first reading and it took him until the ninth to work out why it was there.

A thing is to be entered against a person. That is an instruction from whoever wrote it. And they wrote it on the outside of a letter to a body.

**Whoever wrote that has already hit the same wall we hit, four hundred yards down the road, and has put a note about it on the outside of his own envelope instead of hitting it again.** And then he sent it to us anyway.

---

He worked it out in his own kitchen in about six years and nobody ever asked him and he has never told anybody except a bay of about thirty people in the fourth month of this year.

The paper with his name at the top of it. Four hundred and forty foot of outer face and nine dates on it, written in the sixth year, sent four times to the district works and returned four times. **A name at the top of a paper about a bank is a name anybody can ask about the bank.** The moment the two are on the same piece of paper they cannot be taken apart, and once a man in a good coat has got a name off a piece of paper about a bank, then the bank is a thing that can be asked about the name.

He has never once put a name at the top of a piece of paper about that bank, and Rennick Adley wrote his name against an entry in a book in a store on the sixth of the third month **on purpose**, and the name is now the first name anybody is going to come to, and Aven Tace has thought about that every day for about ten weeks and has not done anything about it.

So he knows exactly what is on the outside of this letter. He has known since the ninth.

**Somebody is going to have to put a name at the top of it and there is nobody on this cut who can do that except him, because he is the one whose hand has been on the outside of it five days and everybody in ninety-one households knows it.**

---

He did not decide to open it. He decided, on the morning of the eleventh, at about the seventh hour, that he was going to stop deciding and go and say so out loud in the bay, and then it got decided for him, because Doreth Ferrow was in the bay at the seventh hour the way she is at the seventh hour, and she had been in there four mornings running, and on the fifth she said it.

"Aven." She was standing at the west end with her hands behind her. "**I have been a chair for nineteen years and I have never in my life had to say to a person *open your post*, and I am saying it, and I want it noticed that I am saying it and not asking.**"

"You have not asked me a question."

"You are right and I did not ask you one, and I am aware that the whole of what I have said for nineteen years has been in the shape of a question, so I have had a morning's practice at it and I have not managed it." She did not sit down. "**It has been standing against your wall five days and about nine people have looked at the outside of it and four of those can read a paragraph and not one of the four has told me what it says, and I have been the chair of this meeting for nineteen years and nobody has ever handed me a thing unread and expected me to hold it.**"

"Nobody handed you anything."

"No. Nobody has. And I want that written down in the book with the day on it, by Rennick, in his hand, because in about four years somebody is going to say the chair of that bay was never told, and the answer is that nobody has ever asked the chair of that bay for anything, and the reason is that a woman of sixty-four who cannot read a paragraph is very useful to everybody and is expensive to consult."

Somebody laughed. She let it go past.

---

So he opened it in the bay at about the ninth hour of the eleventh day of the fifth month, with about thirty people in the room, and the door was doing what it does, and it was cold.

He cut the string with a mill knife and he turned the sheet round and put it flat on the bench in the middle of the room with the writing facing the far side, which is a thing he has watched a girl of seventeen do about nine times in this bay and has never once asked her why she does it, and he now knows why, and it took him until this morning.

**A sheet lying the wrong way round is a sheet nine people cannot read.**

He read the outside of it out loud first, all of it, including the address and the line under the address, and then he read the inside.

It is four pages. It is beautifully made. That is the first thing he said out loud about it and it cost him something to say it in front of about thirty people who cannot read a paragraph.

"**It is a good document.** That is what I am putting first. I have been in this trade thirty-one years and I have had a hand on about four hundred papers and this is the best-made one I have ever had, and I am not going to stand in a bay and tell you it is a bad document because I cannot make it say what I want it to say."

Then he read it.

---

> *Water guarantee, given under the fire-and-water device, to the Slade Cut and to the Company working it, for so long as the cut is worked.*
>
> *The holder of the device will hold the water of the Slade Cut against flood for the length of the works. The holder will stand the cost of the bank, the ditch, the scour and the works at the ford, without charge, and will not ask any household for any sum, and will pay no levy and take no toll of the water of that cut.*
>
> *The labour of those works is furnished by the Company and by such persons as are of the Company at the time the work is done. A person who is of the Company at one time and not at another has furnished the labour of the works done while he was of it. The Company is not discharged by the death of a member and is not discharged by the coming of age of a member, and a member who is not of the Company for a day has not thereby been freed of the works done in a day when he was of it.*
>
> *Nothing in this instrument is a debt of any household. Nothing in it may be sued for by any household or against any household, and no child, widow or person not of the Company is named in it or is party to it or may be sued upon it.*
>
> *Answer, in the fourth line of the form at the foot, in what way this instrument touches you. The answer is to be given within nine days of the day on which this instrument is opened. If no answer is given within that time this instrument stands offered and unanswered, and it may be taken up at any time afterwards by a person answering in those terms.*
>
> *Given under the fire-and-water device, and sealed, and witnessed. — a notary's table of a House, and the House is named at the foot.*

That was the whole of the operative part and he read all of it, and then he handed it to Tavi Venn, who read all of it again, out loud, twice, once at the speed you would read a price list and once slower.

She is seventeen. Nobody is paying her and she has said in this bay in the fourth month that she is the person who comes and that the moment she is thanked in a room she is a person who reads for thanks, and that in about four years somebody will ask her to do it for nothing because she has been thanked.

Nobody thanked her.

Four people asked her what it meant. She gave all four of them the same four words, which were: **I read it, I do not say.**

---

And then the miller asked her the question, because he wanted to know and because he is the kind of man who asks, and he asked it in the ordinary way at the west end with about thirty people in the room.

"Tavi. The nine days. What is the nine days for?"

"I read it, I do not say."

"That is not—"

"I read it, I do not say."

"Right." He nodded. "That is fair and I asked for it."

Then Rennick Adley, who had been standing at the far end with his arms folded for about forty minutes, said the thing that four people had been thinking about and nobody had wanted to be first at.

"**The nine days is not for answering. It is for saying no.**"

The room did not go quiet. It got worse than quiet, which is a thing a bay of thirty people does about nine times in eleven years.

"Read the second half of that line again," Adley said. "Read it out, from where you are."

She read it out. *If no answer is given within that time this instrument stands offered and unanswered, and it may be taken up at any time afterwards by a person answering in those terms.*

"That is not a deadline," said Adley. "**A deadline is a thing that stops something. That is a window on a thing that does not stop.** It stands offered unanswered. **It is true now.** It has been true since the eighth and it became true of this Company the moment a cart with some seed came up this road, and nobody in this room had to do anything at all, and that is what the letter is."

"Then what is the nine days for."

"It is for you to have said no in. It is the only part of that paper anybody was ever going to be asked about, and it is nine days long, and **it is a question put to a body, and a body cannot say no, and the offer does not need an answer to be true.**"

---

Nobody said anything for a while, and the door did what it does.

And then Doreth Ferrow asked for the third page to be read out again, only the third, and Tavi Venn read it out, and the chair counted.

She does it with her hands and she does not pretend she cannot. Nineteen years of this meeting.

"**Such persons as are of the Company at the time the work is done.**" She had the back of her own hand. "**A person is of the Company if the person is in a house of the ninety-one. That is what being of the Company means and there is no other meaning of it in this room or in that paper.**

"So. **A child in a house at the north of the ford is of the Company. A child in this mill yard is of the Company. A child born in the eighth month of next year is of the Company, and a child born in the fourth month of the year after that is of the Company, and there is no age in it.**"

"Nineteen years," said Adley.

"Nineteen years and about sixty of them under sixteen **this month**, and a girl of seventeen who read it out twice, and two men standing in this room who were born on this cut, and about forty of us over thirty-one." She put her hand down. "And I have got the count I have carried for nineteen years and it is not wrong and it is not enough, because it stops at sixteen and that paper does not."

She had one more thing and she said it in a lower voice, and about nine people in that bay did not hear it and one of the nine leaned over and heard it and repeated it, which is the fourth of a rule written on the twenty-eighth of the third month.

"**And the sentence that says no child is party to it is the fourth one down. It comes after.** It is a good sentence. It is honestly drafted and it is true about suing and it is not true about being in it. **Whoever wrote that paper put the one that takes the children in first and the one that says nobody may be sued afterwards, and he did it in that order, and I do not believe he did it on purpose, and it would make no difference if he had.**"

Nowhere in that bay was there anybody who had read a clause in the fourth month and put a line under it in his own words. That line was on the back of a lapsed list about a hundred yards up the road, and it said a child is not a party and is not named and is not in the rotation, and it is in a clerk's own hand and it is the only sentence anybody has written in four months that a four-hundred-year-old House has not already written better for itself.

The miller stood at the west end with his hand flat on the bench and looked at the fourth page for a long time, and what he said next is the thing that this whole business turned on and he said it in nine words and then sat down.

"**I gave you a room on the sixth of the third month and it costs nothing.**"

"Yes," said Doreth Ferrow.

"And I have said, out loud in that room, that a thing which is free is a thing nobody can refuse. I said it in the fourth month and nobody argued with me and I have been pleased with myself about it for about six weeks." He turned round. "**It is not a room. It is a letter with a body's name on it and I have just opened it, and the paper was under my own tarpaulin, and I am the only person on this cut anybody can put a name on, and so I have been the door to this thing for five days and I did not know I was standing in one.**"

"You knew on the ninth," said Adley.

"I knew on the ninth. I have been standing in it since the ninth and I have been thinking about a name at the top of a paper, and I worked that out in about six years in my own kitchen, and it turns out the same thing arrives from four hundred miles away with a better seal on it than I have ever put on anything in my life." He sat down. "**Nine tons of my own kerb and coping are in that yard under a tarpaulin as well, and I priced them at seven shillings and sixpence in the fourth month and refused to give them, on purpose, because a gift is money with no figure on it and is the worst kind, and I told about thirty people in this room that, and I was right and I am still right.**"

"You are right," said Adley.

"Then say the other half of it."

"**A House has answered me with a figure of nought, and nought is a figure, and it is worse.**"

---

Nobody answered the letter. Nobody filled in the fourth line. Nobody gave anybody's name, and the offer stood unanswered, and the nine days ran.

Rennick Adley wrote it in the book that evening with the day on it, in his own hand, and read it back to the bay twice, once for the people who could see the words and once for the people who could not, and the second reading is the one he did not skip.

> *The eleventh day of the fifth month. A paper came to this cut on the eighth, addressed to the Slade Cut Company, and a person opened it in the north bay at about the ninth hour with about thirty in the room, and the fourth line was not filled in and no name was given and nobody's name was taken. The paper is a water guarantee under the fire-and-water device and it is offered without charge. **It stands offered and unanswered and it does not need an answer to be true.** A person has opened a paper addressed to a body and has not answered it, and I record that a person did that, and I am not recording that a person was compelled to, because nobody has found out whether he was and he has not said.*
>
> *Two houses at the north of the ford are already parties to a thing filed in a court four miles down the road and cannot be un-named. This is the second paper. **I am not going to line the two up.** A line between two things is a chain and a chain is an invention, and I said that in this bay in the second week of this month and I have not changed my mind in nine days and I am not going to change it in a year.*

Then on the afternoon of the twentieth of the fifth month, four days after the window had shut, a courier came up the road with a second sheet in the same hand.

It is addressed to the notary's table at whose counter a person in this Empire may ask a question about an instrument, and it asks that counter whether it will certify the guarantee, and it attaches the guarantee, and it asks first to be satisfied of one fact, and the fact is the fourth line again in different words.

**In what way does this instrument touch the Company.** Which is a question about a body, and a body is not a person.

It has a line at the foot of it in bigger letters, and the courier read that one out loud in the yard because he was told to read out the lines in bigger letters: *The practice has also written to the keeper of the district water office in that district, and that answer is required before this table is answered.*

There is no head of the Slade Cut Company. A magistrate entered, on her own motion, in the ninth of the second month, that a body may not be made a party by her court or any other.

The courier stood in the mill yard with the paper in his hand and nobody could sign for it, and the miller did not offer to, and the courier looked at him for about two seconds longer than a courier has time for, and then the courier read the address off the top of it again and said, to nobody in particular, that it was not going back down the road unsigned, and he was right, and it was not going back down the road that day.

It went to a counter four hundred and thirty miles away in a bag with three other things, and on the way the courier worked out that there was a person named on it, and not the Company, and that he had been carrying a person's name up a towpath for nine days and had not been able to say so to anybody, and he said it out loud in the yard to about nine people who were standing in the cold because it was the seventh hour and they had heard all of it.

"**There is a person named on this one. Not the Company. A person, at a counter, four hundred and thirty miles up this river, and it is not you and it is not anybody in this yard, and I am to hand it to a person.**"

And a clerk at that counter, thirty-four, eleven years at the fourth one, read it standing up and said: "This is not a question. This is a refusal with a question on the back of it."
