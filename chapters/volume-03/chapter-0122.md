# Chapter 122: Nine Acres and a Dead Sheet

The district works at the Reed Cut is four miles from the water office and four miles from the north bay of a miller's grain store, and a person can walk either in an hour and a bit, and the walk is flat and has a ditch on the left of it the whole way for about two miles and then stops.

Sivra Oris went up it on the second of the fourth month at about the eighth hour with a sheet of paper folded in four in the inside of her coat, and she went alone, and the reason she went alone is that there is nobody in that building to send.

The sheet is the flood sheet of the ninth of the first month. It carries the office's device, the water, the works, and a list of works stapled to it, and a box headed *who is to answer* with two names in it and no body anywhere on it, and four lines on its face, and her own name at the foot.

The last of the four lines says that if either of them says no the sheet is void and the keeper will enter the void and the day.

The keeper entered the void and the day on the last day of the third month at about the fourth hour, and it is a line in a front desk-book, and the sheet has been in a drawer since the second of the fourth month at about the eighth hour.

---

The foreman of the district works is the wrong side of sixty and has kept a return book for three years and has nine men and a fund with an empty head.

He read the four lines, and then he read the nine words in her hand on the line above them, and then he read the nine words again, and he did all three in about eleven seconds and did not sit down.

"Sit down," said Sivra Oris. "I have come four miles and I am not a dragon in the sense that people mean when they say that, and I would rather not do this standing."

He sat down. He put the sheet on the bench in front of him with the writing the other way up, which she noticed and did not comment on, because a man who has been in a district works for thirty-one years turns a paper round for the same reason she does.

"**I am not going to argue with the four words,**" he said. "They are correct. I want to say that first and I want to say it to the man who wrote them and he is not here and he is not going to be told, which I think is the arrangement."

"Who wrote them."

"He did, about four days ago, and he did it because you did not ask him, and I have read them, and I am going to put my own name at the foot of what I do about them, which I have not done in nine years, and I would like you to understand that I am not doing it for you."

"I did not come for that."

"Then I do not know what you came for and I am going to ask you the only question I have got, and it is the one I have to answer upstairs on the fourth, and I have not got an answer to it and I have had since about the seventh hour."

"Say it."

"Is there any person in that district who will take the flood authority."

---

She stood in a yard in the wet and gave him the answer, and it took her about four minutes, and she gave it as a person and not as an office, and she had given it before in a shed in the seventh of the first month and in a yard in the third month and she was not going to soften it because she was saying it to a foreman instead of to forty people.

"No."

"You have not looked."

"I have looked for nine years and I did not have to look very far, because the sheet of the ninth of the first month named two of them and one of them said no, and the other one is a woman of fifty-one whose term ran out in the third week of last month and who was never asked for it again, and there is a brother of hers who was relieved of a decision he never had by the four words you have just read, and **I want that noticed, because in about nine years somebody is going to come to me and say the sheet was cruel, and the cruel thing in that sheet is not the void, it is that eleven weeks ago two people were put in a box and neither of them was asked and one of them has been carrying it and one of them has been carrying his brother instead of his own duty.**"

"And after the fourth."

"After the fourth there is nobody. **There is no form in this empire in which I can say *there is nobody*, and there is no form in which a district can be told to go and look for nobody.** I have said that sentence in a shed in the seventh of the first month and I have not improved on it since. This district has tried to go and look for nobody before and it cost it a year, and the year is in a book in a building in Auremar and I have never seen it and I have been told about it four times by people who were not there."

He put his hand flat on the sheet.

"That is the fourth time this month somebody has handed me a piece of paper with somebody's name at the top of it," he said, "and every one of them has been handed to me at a gate, and the first time I was told I had not been sent it, and I have not been sent this one either, and I am not going to pretend to you that I know who is putting papers on my gate."

"You know who put three of them on your gate."

"**I know my own book-keeper put one of them on my gate and I know a foreman of a cooperative put two, and I do not know about the fourth and I have not asked, and I am the wrong side of sixty and I have a fund with an empty head and I have nine men and a book with a column over it that has no heading, and if I start asking who is putting papers on my gate then that column gets a heading and about four hundred people in three years are answerable for something they said in a yard.**"

---

The memorandum came back into the conversation about halfway through, and it did it because the foreman had it in his coat, because he had been up all night with it.

He put it on the bench and did not open it.

"Now. **This came to my gate yesterday afternoon and it is the first paper of this season for that cut and it has got a body in it.** I want to say the thing about it and then I want to ask you a question and the question is not fair.

"Four minutes. A clerk of this district was given a box and was given no word of what to put in it, and the box had to have something in it because a form that is not filled in does not go out. And the only thing in that district that could go in a box is a company. And nobody in that office has ever met the company and there is no way they could meet it, because a company has no door and no clerk and no head and a body's name is not a thing you can post a letter to.

"So the box is correct. The box is the only correct thing in it. And the paper is dated the first of the fourth month, which is the day after the void, and that is a coincidence, and I have had a day to think about it and I have got to the bottom of it and I want you to hear the bottom of it, because it is worse than a coincidence and it is better.

"**The box would have had a body in it on the second of the fourth month if your two people had both said yes.** In a hundred and forty years this box has never once been empty on the first memoranda of a season and it is not going to start. The four words did not put a body in that box. **The four words took a person out of it, and a box with a person taken out of it is a box with nothing in it, and a box with nothing in it is a form that does not go out, and so the district sent it back to be filled in, and the thing that filled it in was the only thing there was.**"

Sivra Oris said nothing for a moment.

"That is the best thing anybody has said to me in nine years and I am not going to tell you that either, because you did not say it for me and I have been on the end of that twice."

"Who said it for?"

"My own day-book, about nine days ago, in a room, and the man who was in the room wrote it down and gave it to me on the back of a form, and he is four hundred and thirty miles from here, and I have not sent him an answer, and I am not going to be thanked for a sentence somebody else said first."

---

She picked the memorandum up off the bench, read the head of it again, and then read the line at the foot of it, which is a foreman's name in a foreman's hand, and then she put it down again and turned it so it faced the man.

"I want to do one thing and I want to tell you that I am doing it and why, and then I am going to leave."

"There is nothing to put on that paper."

"**There is my name.** There is a keeper's name and there is a memorandum number and there is a box, and the box is wrong and I cannot fix the box, and a memorandum with a device at the top of it and a date and no person anywhere in it is a paper that can be held by anybody for ever and asked nothing. **Mine is a keeper's name on a paper about this office and about a season's work in a district, and it makes that paper a thing this office can be asked about instead of a thing I can be asked about, and I am the office.**"

"You could refuse."

"**I could refuse, and about four years ago a woman of about sixty-one stood at a counter in Auremar and was asked to fill in a stamped paper and did not, and has not been back, and they kept hers.** And I am not comparing myself to a woman of sixty-one in a city four hundred and thirty miles from here, because that is the sort of thing a keeper says to make herself feel better and I have nine years of having felt better and about four hundred and forty feet of bank that does not care.

"I am giving my name because it is on a paper about an office and I am an office, and I have been trying to tell you since the seventh of the first month that I am not the First Seat and not that seat's agent, and I am not going to say it again, and I am not going to say it here, and you did not ask me, and I noticed that you did not ask me and I am grateful in a way I am not going to put a sentence on."

He wrote it. It took him about four seconds and then he read it back, which he does with everybody, and then he handed her the pen to initial the counterfoil, which she did.

---

At the gate on the way down she stopped, and the four miles had done what four miles does.

"Now the part I came for, and it is four sentences and I have not said any of it in a room.

"You have just been handed a thing with a body in it, and I have been the keeper of a sheet with two people in it, and we are both of us holding a paper in a season, and there is not one person in either box and both boxes are correct and both of them are wrong in the same way, and the way is that **a box has to have something in it.** A hundred and forty years of form design and nothing whatever to do with anybody's hand.

"So that is what it costs. It is not nine acres going under in the wrong week — that is what happens to the acres. **What it costs is that a person who says no correctly, in the right words, on a day somebody gave him for saying it, finds out that the whole of what his no bought is a box with ninety-one households in it, and there is nobody he can be angry with and nobody he can be angry at, and in about four years there will be a form with a clerk's name at the top of it and his will not be on it, and that will be correct, and it will be the correctest thing in this matter.**"

He said, "I have been up all night with nine pages of his."

"Then I will tell you what to do with them and it is one sentence and I have no authority to give it. **Read them to the woman who cannot read them, twice, and count the people who are not there, and do not tell her what they mean.**"

"That is not authority. That is a method."

"Yes," said Sivra Oris. "**And I have been trying for about nine days to work out where a keeper learns it, and I have not got it, and I have got four hundred and forty feet of bank instead.**"

---

On the fourth of the fourth month she was in two rooms in one day and neither room was this one.

The first was the district's own upper room, where a man read her four lines and her nine words and then asked her, in the ordinary way, whether she was content for the district's flood authority in respect of the Slade Cut to stand undispensed until the season was over.

She said that she was content, and that contentment was not the word, and that what she wanted recorded was **undispensed**, with a date on it, and not *no person available*, which is a sentence about people, and that a sentence about people in a return of this office would be read in nine years as a finding that nobody was willing, and that nobody in that district had been asked.

Then she asked for a second line and got it, and the second line is that **this office cannot compel the works, does not claim to, cannot be compelled to do them itself, and cannot give back what it gave away on the ninth of the first month, and there is no form in this empire for giving it back.** Four lines. She had written them before.

And the second room was the back room of the water office, at about the fifth hour, with a stove in it and about nine hundred forms on a shelf and a clerk of about twenty-three who had not gone home.

"Write it down," said Sivra Oris, "and then read it back to me, and then put it away, and I am telling you to do it so that you know the order of it."

"What am I writing."

"**A keeper of this office went four miles on the second of the fourth month and was asked for a person's name and there was not one, and the district has entered the flood authority for that cut as undispensed with a date on it, and a box on the first paper of the season has a body in it, and the body is a body of about ninety-one households of which about sixty are children under sixteen, and the entry is the correct one, and the person who said no was right, and neither of those is a finding and I am not asking you to make it one.**"

The clerk wrote it. It took her about a minute. She read it back. Sivra Oris made her read it back a second time, which she does with everybody, and then she shut the book and said:

"Now say the price of it out loud and do it in nine words or fewer, because I have to walk four miles again in about nine days and I want to know I have said it."

"It will not be available to anybody."

"**Not to me, not to my sister, not to that man, not to a court, not to a clerk of that court, not to you in about nine years.** That book is mine and it is not a record and there is no office behind it and there is nobody I can hand it to, and that is the whole of what this office has been able to give anybody in nine years, and I have told you the price of it before I did it twice, and I would rather you heard the price and refused it than have it and find out in four years."

"And you would rather not have done it this time either."

"I would rather not have done it the first time, and I have had nine years to get better at this and I have not got better at it, and I have got a form with an empty box in it and a district with a body in the same box on the same river four miles away, and the difference between those two boxes is that I wrote four lines on mine." She put her hand on the stove rail and left it there. "**And I am not going to say the other thing. I said it once in a shed in the first month before anybody asked me and I am not going to say it in a yard in the fourth month, and a person who has heard a thing once does not need it twice, and the moment a keeper begins to repeat herself she has begun to need somebody to believe her, and there is nobody in this building and I am not going out to get anybody.**"

She went down the two hundred yards at about the sixth hour.

Behind four hundred and forty foot of outer face at the third-of-the-way mark there are nine acres and about thirty-one households, and the outer face is eleven feet high where a crew of five men and a boy left it on the twenty-first of the second month, and it was paid for in full out of a clerk's own wage, and it will stand until the water is in the third week of this month, and there is no name on that bank anywhere at all.
