# Chapter 127: A Household That Has Just Been Helped

The north bay of a miller's grain store off the Slade is thirty-one foot by nineteen with the floor a foot off the ground. It has a bench against the north wall that came with the building, a bench in the middle of it that four men and about nine other people carried in about four minutes on the twenty-eighth day of the third month, and a door in the west end whose lintel has dropped about half an inch in about forty years and which has never been packed and does not shut.

It holds about thirty. The miller has counted it twice with a crowd in it.

**Doreth Ferrow is sixty-four and has kept this meeting for nineteen years. She cannot read a paragraph and has not pretended to for fifty years. She can add, she can count a room, and she has about sixty children under sixteen in that cut and has met the two youngest of them.**

The water came in on the nineteenth day of the fourth month at about the second hour of the morning and went down over the twentieth and part of the twenty-first, and nobody on that cut drowned, and the bank at the third-of-the-way mark stood eleven feet high where a crew of five men and a boy had left it on the twenty-first of the second month, and the ford filled, as a ford does, because a ford with no depth posts in it and no hard stones in the throat is a place where the water happens.

She was in the bay at about the seventh hour on the morning of the nineteenth with about nine people in it, because that is what she does, and it is what she has done at the beginning of forty meetings, and nobody had thought it worth calling.

They came in about the third hour before the light came and it did not stop. It came up the lane of channel-side houses at the north of the ford and across nine feet of beaten earth and into the bay, and there were about eleven inches of it in the middle of the floor by the fourth hour and about nine inches of mud over the whole of it by the sixth hour, and about four people went out into it and got the miller's carts up off the flat and the two hundred-weight sacks off the low bench and the door off its hinges, which nobody had asked them to do and which is the sort of thing nine people do.

Somebody said, at about the fifth hour, that the bank was going.

"It is not going," said Doreth Ferrow. "**It is eleven feet high and it was paid for in full and it is being looked at by a man with a lamp and nine men from this bay, and I have watched that face for nine years and I know which nine feet of it go in a bad light and it is not those nine feet.**"

And it did not go. That is the whole of it and it took about nineteen hours.

---

At about the seventh hour on the morning of the twentieth the water had gone down out of the bay and out of the yard and there was a foot of mud in the bay and about a hand's breadth of it in the yard, and the sun came up on the wrong side of the mill and made a thing of the mud that Doreth Ferrow looked at for a while and then stopped looking at.

And she went out into the yard to count the room, the way she has counted forty meetings, and she counted about nine.

**Nine people from the bay, standing in a mill yard at about the seventh hour in the morning, in a yard that is not theirs.**

They were not doing anything. That is the thing she has not been able to put down since. They were not carrying anything and they were not fetching anything and they were not asking for anything. They were standing in about nine different places in a hundred and forty yards of beaten earth, facing the mill, the way you stand at a gate.

The miller was out at about the fourth hour and had been out all night, and he had a lantern and about nine men and he had been on the bank at the third-of-the-way mark from about the second hour of the morning when the water came in, and he had come back down about the sixth hour and he was in his own door with his coat off and he had not slept.

Doreth Ferrow counted about nine people in the yard, and then she went and got Rennick Adley out of the store, which is at the other end of the same building with a door of its own, and she did not ask him anything. She said, "Come out here and stand where I am standing," and he came out and stood where she was standing.

"Count them," she said.

"Nine."

"**And tell me how many come to the bay.**"

"About thirty. It is a cold floor and a door that does not shut and it is the twentieth of the month and there is mud in it."

"So about thirty, and about nine, and about sixty of about ninety-one households are not in that bay and are not in this yard and have not been told anything this morning and would find it out from a lane."

"Yes."

"Right," said Doreth Ferrow. "**Now I am going to ask you four questions and I want short answers and I want them out loud, and if I ask you a fifth you are to say so.**"

---

The first question was what the paper on the bench in the bay was, and Rennick Adley had to answer it twice because she sent him back for it and made him read it, and he read the whole of the ninth page and then the first recital and then the fourth page, and he read it twice, and about eleven people stood in a muddy bay and listened to a man of thirty-eight read nine pages of a thing none of them had written.

The second question was how many names were in the Company's book.

"About nine hundred and forty."

"In how long."

"About sixty years. Nine hands."

"**And is there a list of the ninety-one households.**"

"There is not. There has never been one. The figure of about ninety-one households and about sixty children under sixteen is a count off the book, added on the back of my aunt's hand, and there is no heading at the top of the book and no list of persons in it and it says on the first leaf what the book is and what it is not, with the day on it, and it does not produce anybody."

"Then what is the rotation in."

"The book. The paper says the schedule is the book and the persons named in it are the persons whose names stand in it against a household. **And it says a person is the person whose name stands in the book and not the household, and it says a child is not a party and is not in the rotation, and I have read that clause about nine times since the day before yesterday and I want you to notice that I read it nine times.**"

"And what does that word say, the one you have not got a name for." She pointed at the fourth page without touching it. "Somebody read that out to nine men in here yesterday and three of them asked what it meant, and I have been trying to work it out from the shape of your mouth and I am not going to pretend I have."

"It says the schedule is produced and read out, where the persons in it can be found, twice, once for those who can read it and once for those who cannot. **And it says this exception binds nobody who has not been heard about it, and no person is in the schedule who has not been heard, because every person in the schedule is named individually and individually may be spoken to.**"

"Then that is the worst line on that paper and it is the line I believe," said Doreth Ferrow, "**because about nine words out of ten in this matter have been a thing somebody said to make a person feel better, and that one is the only one I have ever read out that is a promise about the future, and it is on a page in a shed four hundred yards from where I am standing.**"

The third question was the first name in it.

"His," said Rennick Adley, without looking at anything. "Aven Tace, of the mill yard, written against an entry in this book on the sixth day of the third month as a person and not as a crew and not a debtor. It is the first name against an entry in sixty years. **I wrote it on purpose so that the Company would be easy to compel itself, and I put it in a book with no heading so that nobody could tell what it was for.**"

"And the next one."

"I do not know. **Nobody knows. It is whatever is next in a book that has never once been chosen from, and about four hundred of the nine hundred and forty people in it are dead.**"

The fourth question was how many children.

And Doreth Ferrow did that one herself, out loud, in a muddy yard, with her hands, which she can do.

"A house is not a person. A house is a door and a fire and a name on a bit of paper, and a man standing in a house is a person and the house is not. And that is not my line, that is the paper's line, and the paper has it right.

"**And a name in a book against a household is a name of a person, and the person is the man or the woman who answers the door, and the duty belongs to the person, and the person is in the house, and the people in the house are the person's.**

"So. **About sixty children under sixteen, in about ninety-one houses, in a cut of about four hundred and forty foot, are inside a duty they were not born into, because their mother's name is in a book on a bench.** And I have had those children counted in my own hand for nineteen years and I have met the two youngest of them and I did not know until about a minute ago that a name in a book is a chain round about sixty necks, and it is not a chain anybody has laid, it is a chain of about nine hundred and forty names in a book with no heading, and the man who laid it is a man of twenty-nine who said *nobody asked me, so I am not going to sign*, and he was right, and he has been right for eleven weeks, and here it is anyway."

She stopped there for a bit, and then she said the rest of it, and she said it standing in the mud in a mill yard to a book-keeper and to nobody else.

"**And the Company.** The paper says the duty goes to the Company and to ninety-one persons in rotation. The Company is a body. A body cannot refuse. **And a body that is given a duty and cannot refuse has accepted it, and the acceptance is on the face of the paper in about nine words that nobody in ninety-one households ever said.**

"So the Company is the guarantor of every flood that will ever come down that cut, for ever, and it will never be asked, because there is nobody in it to ask, and the reason it will never be asked is that it cannot say no and it does not know it has been given anything.

"And I want the last of it said out loud in a yard because nobody is going to be able to hear it anywhere else and because I am sixty-four and I do not have a great deal of time to be the woman who says it.

"**A burden moved onto ninety-one people is not shared. It is distributed. And distribution is what makes it permanent.**"

---

A woman came up the road from the north of the ford at about the ninth hour with mud to the knee on her and a boy of about four on her hip, and Doreth Ferrow knew her for the woman at the first house, and Doreth Ferrow did not know her name and has never known her name, because the woman refused it in her own doorway on the last day of the third month and let herself be written down as a description of a person instead.

She had come down about two hours' walk with a baby and no boots and she had not come about the ford.

"Have you got somebody to sit with him," said Doreth Ferrow.

"I've got a woman of seventy-one sitting with him and she says she is not going to be asked twice about it," said the woman, "and I have got to know one thing, because I cannot read what I am given and I have had it four days and a person came up the lane with it in his hand and read it to me and would not say what it meant."

"Then tell me what it says and I will tell you what I think of it and you will not like it."

"It says I am a party to something," said the woman, "**and that I cannot be un-named. And it says four houses at the north of the ford, and I am one, and there is one of me that is a woman of about seventy-one who cannot read a paragraph, and one of them is me and I do not have a name on it because I would not give them one, and they have put down *the woman at the first house who was in her own door on the last day of the third month* and that is me, and I have been that for four days and I have not slept.**

"And I want to know one thing and it is the only thing. **Did the water last night come into my house.**"

"No," said Doreth Ferrow, and she said it in a way that made the woman put her hand over her mouth.

"Then I am one of the four that got nothing wet, and the boy of fifteen got nothing wet, and the lane got nothing wet, and there is a bank four hundred and forty foot long with a miller on it all night, and about nine people from the bay." She stopped. "**Then why are the four of us a party.**"

"**Because they wrote your lane down before the water in a building four miles away and you would not give them a name, and that is the whole of it, and it is not a reward and it is not a punishment, and I could not tell you that on the fourteenth because on the fourteenth I did not know either.**

"And I am going to tell you one more thing and then you are going to walk back up that lane, because I am sixty-four and I have a bay to clear.

"**Whatever that paper says about you, it did not save your house. The bank did. And the man on the bank was on it because his name was in a book on purpose, six weeks ago, by a book-keeper who wrote it down so that he would be easy to find.**"

The woman stood in the mud for a moment and then said, "That is the second thing that has been a thing about my name and not about my house in about a year and I am not going to stand in a road and take it in, and I will come back on the twenty-third."

"You come back on the twenty-third and I will not have anything for you, because I have nothing, and that will be an answer."

---

Then she said: "I am not going to tell him."

"Tell him what."

"Any of it. **He gave me a room on the sixth of the third month for a year and it costs nothing and he has never asked me for a thing, and I told the bay on the twenty-eighth that a thing which is free is a thing nobody can refuse, and I have not told him that either, and I am not going to tell him any of it now.**

"And I want you to write one thing down and I want it in the book and not on the back of my hand, and I want it entered in your own hand and not mine, because I cannot read what you write and I have said so in four rooms.

"Write: *Doreth Ferrow, chair, the twentieth day of the fourth month. Nine persons of about thirty who come to this bay were standing in a mill yard at about the seventh hour watching a household that has been helped, and about sixty of about ninety-one households were not anywhere. The two figures are the same two figures as in the bay on the twenty-eighth of the third month and they are not an instance of anything.*"

He wrote it and read it back and she made him read it back twice, and then she said: "**Now go and tell the nine people out there to go home, and do it kindly, and do not tell them any of it, and do not tell them who I was standing next to.**"

---

The mud was out of the bay by about the eighth hour of the twenty-first in about nine hours of work by about eleven people who were not paid for any of it.

On the twenty-second there was a list of works on the bench in the middle of the bay with a foreman's name at the foot of it and a date against that name, and about one hundred and forty feet of ditch on the south side and the scour at the third-of-the-way place and the ford, the stones and the two posts, and nobody on the Slade had any idea yet that about sixty children were in it.
