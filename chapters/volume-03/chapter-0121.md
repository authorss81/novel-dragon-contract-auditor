# Chapter 121: A Letter Nobody Sent Him

The Reed Cut district water office is a stone building two hundred yards above the gate, so that a man can come up out of the water and see the office and the water at the same time. It has a counter, a back room with a stove, a room with the day's sheets in it, and about nine hundred forms on a shelf, and the forms are the office's whole learning.

Piotr Wray came up the two hundred yards at about the seventh hour with nothing in his hands, which took him about four minutes to decide to do and half an hour to walk, and the office was shut behind the counter, and the clerk let him in because he is the second name in a box on a sheet that is folded in a drawer in that building.

He is twenty-nine. He works the far end of the Slade. He has been the person in the box for eleven weeks. On the seventh day of the first month he asked his sister whether she was going to put the Company's name on the spring list and was told that was not a question he had standing to ask her, and on the twentieth of the first month he stood in a mill yard in front of about forty people and said, in nine words, *Nobody asked me, so I am not going to sign*, and has not been asked anything since.

On the tenth day of the third month he had come up the same two hundred yards and had been given four words and a date.

---

Sivra Oris had the sheet out of the drawer before he had finished saying what he had come to say, which she has done twice in nine years and both times it was to somebody who had come to be told no.

"Sit down. No — stand, if you would rather, and I will stand."

He stood.

"**I am not going to ask you about it,**" she said. "I want that in the room before either of us says anything else, and I want it said by me and not asked of you. I gave you a date. I do not get to spend it and I do not get to hint at it. Nobody in this building is going to ask you today, and if anybody does you are to say that a keeper gave you a date and did not give you a morning."

"Then I am going to have to do the rest of it myself," he said.

"Yes. That is arranged and I have not arranged it. I only left it."

---

He had until the day ran out.

That was the whole of it and he had known it for fifty days and had spent them.

He had thought about it in four directions. He had thought about it on the bank at the far end of the cut, where he works, in the water, with a barrow. He had thought about it at his sister's kitchen table with a sheet of paper on it that was not about him. He had thought about it at about the third hour of the morning for about nine nights, and the reason he had not said it in fifty days is not that he was building up to it.

It is that he was the only person in the district who was ever entitled to say it, and he had not been asked.

---

"You have not asked me one thing in fifty days," he said.

"No."

"Everybody in that shed has asked me about four things this month. You have not asked me one."

"I gave you a date and I gave you the four words and I gave you the price of using them, and the price was nine acres and thirty-one households, and the date was the day, and the four words were the thing you would say when you had a day. **That is everything an office can do and I did all of it in about nine minutes on the tenth of the third month and I have had fifty days to think of a fifth thing and there is not one.**"

He put his hand flat on the counter, about where the box is, and did not touch it, which is a thing he has done twice now.

"Ask me what the four words do," he said.

"That is asking you a question."

"Yes."

"**No.** And I will tell you why, and it is the same reason I did not ask you for the fourth line of anything on the tenth of the third month, and it is the only thing about this office that I am proud of. The moment a keeper starts asking a person what a paper will do, the keeper has become a second party to it, and there is already a party to it and it is a woman of fifty-one who did not know she was one. **I will not make a third.**"

---

He was quiet for a while. The clerk of the office, who is about twenty-three, did the thing she does, which is to be in the room without being in it.

Then he asked the question he had come up the road to ask, and he asked it in the ordinary way, the way anybody asks a thing in a room.

"What is the sheet worth to you."

"Four words and a minute," said Sivra Oris. "It is the only instrument in this district that has ever given a flood authority to a person instead of a body, and it is one piece of paper of the ninth of the first month in a drawer in a building two hundred yards from a gate, and there is nothing behind it. No fund, no crew, and no other piece of paper in this district that says the same thing."

"And when I spend it."

"Then the district's flood authority in respect of those works is undispensed. **There is no name on that bank anywhere at all.** Not mine, not yours, not my sister's, and she is on a list of works in a store with a date on it that ran out in the third week of the month. And behind four hundred and forty foot of outer face at the third-of-the-way place there are nine acres and thirty-one households, and I have got nothing to send any of them with, and I have said in four lines on the face of that sheet that this office cannot compel the works, does not claim to, and cannot be compelled to do them itself."

"You have given your own authority away in writing."

"On the ninth of the first month. To two people, without asking either. **And there is no form anywhere in this empire for saying to a keeper, give it back.** I have looked. I looked again in the second month."

---

They went on like that until about the eleventh hour, and none of it was a scene, and most of it was the two of them finding out what they each already knew at slightly different times of the day.

At about the eleventh hour he asked whether anybody in the district would be hurt by it, in the ordinary way, and she told him the whole of it in about four minutes, and he listened, and at the end of it he said the thing he had come to say, and it was nine words long and it was not the four words.

"Nobody asked her either, so it does not count as one of us."

Sivra Oris put both hands flat on the counter, which she had done once before.

"Say that again and I will put it in this office's own book in my own hand in about nine seconds, and it will not be available to you, or to your sister, or to a court, or to a clerk, and you will not be able to find out in about nine years whether I wrote it. **That book is mine and it is not a record and there is no office behind it, and I have offered it to you twice now and you have taken it twice and you want it a third time.**"

"I am not asking you for it."

"You are not. And that is the first difference between you and your sister, and I am not going to say it out loud twice."

---

He went down the two hundred yards at about the second hour of the afternoon and stood in his own yard at the far end of the cut for a while without doing anything, which is not a thing he does, and then he came back up at about the fourth hour because the day was not finished and he wanted the day to be finished in a room and not in his own head.

Sivra Oris had the sheet out and the pen out and the front desk-book open, and she had the writing turned for the person standing on the other side, which is a thing she learned from a girl of seventeen who turned a sheet round in a kitchen for eleven people and has never once been thanked for it.

"Stand there," she said. "Not at the box. At the end of the counter. **I am going to write with your name on the page and I am not going to write with you standing at the box, because a keeper who writes a person into a room with the person at the box is making a scene and a scene is a thing people remember afterwards and this is not a scene.**"

He stood at the end of the counter.

She wrote it. It took her about a minute, and the whole of it is nine words on a line in a front desk-book with a rule above it and a day column and an hour column, and the line is:

> *Void. Said by P. Wray, the person named second, on the last day of the third month, in this office, in front of the keeper. Entered at about the fourth hour.*

And then she put the pen down and turned the book round and made him read it, which she does with everybody, and he read it, and she read it back after him.

"**That is all of it,**" she said. "Four words from you and a minute from me and a line in a book that this office keeps, and it is the only line in nine years in this district that anybody in this matter can go and look at, and I want you to sit with how strange that is, because I have not got time and you have got a flood season."

"I am not going to thank you."

"You are not going to thank me and I am not going to be thanked and I have said so in four rooms. **What I want out loud is the other half. If you had said it in a shed on the thirtieth with nine people in it, I would have had to enter that you were asked in front of witnesses, and a keeper who enters that a person was asked in front of witnesses has turned a refusal into a procedure, and there is no procedure in this empire and I am not going to be the first.**"

She put the sheet in a second drawer, and the second drawer shut, and that was the end of the only instrument in the district that had ever given a flood authority to a person.

---

He slept at the far end of the cut and came back up the two hundred yards at about the sixth hour in the morning, which is four days' habit and a bad knee and nothing else, and the office was open and the counter was up and there was a man standing at the counter with a wet coat on him who was not the clerk.

He was about forty-five and he had come four miles up the road and he was standing with a sheet in his hand held down at his side so that the water was not getting into it, in the way of a man who has carried paper a long way in weather.

"From the works," he said. "Off the foreman. **He did not send me. He gave it to me at the gate and said take it up the hill and I have taken it up the hill.**"

Sivra Oris took it off him and did not open it, and looked at him.

"You have come four miles in that with a paper you were not sent with."

"He did not say I was not sent with it."

"**Nobody in this matter has sent anybody anything since the last day of the third month except a form, and a form is not a paper, and a form is about a body.**" She turned it round and read the head of it. "This is the first paper of this flood season for that cut. It went up the road yesterday afternoon and it came down this morning, and the district is in a hurry about it, and you have brought it up the hill yourself instead of waiting for the cart because the cart does not come up the hill."

The man said, "I did not think about the cart."

"**Nobody ever thinks about the cart.** Go down and have the stove. Not the counter — the stove, in the back room, and take your coat off, and tell the clerk I said so and not you."

He went. She opened the sheet.

It is the season's first memorandum for the Slade Cut. It carries the office's device, the water, the works, and a memorandum number, and a date in the fourth month, and a foreman's name at the foot of it. It has four lines on its face that are the same four lines the office has been putting on its own sheets for a hundred and forty years, and it has a box.

The box is headed **who is to answer.**

The box is not empty.

There is a thing in it in a clerk's hand, and the thing is a body's name, and the body is the Slade Cut Company, of about ninety-one households, of which about sixty children are under sixteen.

Sivra Oris read it twice and then put it flat on the counter with the writing turned for the person standing on the other side, and she did not say anything for about eleven seconds, and the clerk of the office, who is about twenty-three, has said since that those eleven seconds were the longest she has ever stood in that building and that she counted them.

---

Piotr Wray came up the two hundred yards twice in two days and both times he came up with nothing in his hands.

He looked at the box for a while.

"Yesterday afternoon," he said.

"Yesterday afternoon."

"So they made it yesterday afternoon."

"About four minutes, from what I am told. **A clerk was given a box and was given no word of what to put in it, and the box has to have something in it, because a box with nothing in it is a form that has not been filled in, and a form that has not been filled in does not go out.** So the clerk put in the only thing anybody in that district could have put in, and the only thing anybody in that district could have put in is a body, and there is exactly one body on that river that works a cut."

He did not say anything for a moment.

"Was it my fault," he said.

"**No.** And I am going to say the rest of it and I am going to say it well, because you have come up this hill twice in two days and you are owed a straight answer and not a kind one.

"It was not your fault and it was not anybody's fault and there is nobody in this building to blame and there is nobody in that one either. **It would have happened inside a month.** A form wants a person and the district had no person and it does not have a form for nobody, and the fourth week of the season is when the first memoranda go, and the first memoranda have always had something in that box.

"And the whole of what your four words did was move it forward by about a day and a half. That is the price. I have been in this office nine years and I have never seen the price of anything come in on foot before, and I want you to understand that what is on this counter is not a punishment and it is not a warning and it is not even a consequence. **It is a form and four minutes and a clerk who was told nothing.**"

He said: "And it has my sister's house in it."

"It has about ninety-one households in it. Yours is one of them and mine is not one of them, and I have a form on that sheet that says this office cannot compel the works, does not claim to, and cannot be compelled to do them itself, and the district has read those four lines and has put a body in the box on the next page, and that is the whole of the conversation between two offices that has ever happened on that river."

He read the box again.

"Then what do I do now."

"Nothing," said Sivra Oris. "**There is nothing to do and I am the person in this district who has looked, and I want that said out loud in a room, because in about nine days there will be a thing on a bank about the fact that somebody in this matter did nothing.**

"On the second of this month I go four miles upstairs with a dead sheet in my hand and I put it in front of a man who has to decide whether a district flood authority is given to persons or to bodies in this district in the next nine months, and I am going to tell him the truth, which is that there is no person. **And I have to do it in one day, and I have to be in two rooms on the same day, and there is nobody in this office I can send, because the person who could read me a form is about twenty-three and the person who could carry the sheet is standing in my back room with his coat off.**"

She turned the memorandum round one more time and squared it against the counter.

"Go down the hill," she said. "**And say nothing to your sister until I have been upstairs.** Not because I am asking you. Because she is a person on a list of works in a store with a date on it that ran out in the third week of last month, and if you tell her this before I have been upstairs then she will be a person who knows she is in a box, and a person who knows she is in a box is a person who can refuse being in it, and **the district has nobody left to put in that box but a body, and you and I have just spent a night and a morning finding out what happens to a body.**"

He went down the two hundred yards. The rain had stopped. Four miles up the road, at a gate, a man who was the wrong side of sixty was standing in a yard with a coat open and nine pages in his hand, and he had not gone to bed, and he was going to have to go and read them to a woman of sixty-four on the morrow because she could not read them herself and there was nobody else.
