# Chapter 0416: A Lock At One End And A Rail At The Other That Is Not Hers, And A Person Standing Where The Decision Would Fall

The lock is at this end of that stone. At the other end of the same four hundred yards there is a room with a rail standing about four feet off the floor, and the rail is not on the work of that passage and never has been, and it has had nothing on it since before she came, and that room is not hers and she has never once been into it.

**She is twenty-six and she has been at this end for four of those years, and she has never been one of the three clerks of the building she works in, and in all that time nobody in it has put a question to her or sent for her or said thank you to her, and not one sheet has ever been put into her hands for her to take up the stone with.**

There is a figure she has carried for four years. It is on no paper of any kind and it is not going on one and she is not going to say it out loud in this passage or out of it, and the reason she is not going to is not a rule anybody made her keep.

It is the second day of the second week of the sixth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fourth hour, and there has been nothing come down that stone all week that a person could have been shown what it was for.

---

He came down that stone at the fourth hour with the rack on his hip, and there was a woman at the lock, and he went on by her without slowing, because in four years not one thing has come up here that a person at this end would have had any reason to stop him for.

The lock is a lock and not a door, and it is a thing a person turns and not a thing a person opens, and a person can count to six in the time it takes, and after that there is nothing in front of her at all. She has stood at it for four years and nothing has ever come down to her she had to decide about, and she has worked out over four years that a quiet passage and an empty passage are two different arrangements of the same length of it, and she can tell them apart from where she stands by the sound the rack makes.

He is in a felt apron and he has the whole length of that passage, and he goes up it four times and comes down it four times, and on each of those eight walks the rack carries what it carries, and it has carried the same number for longer than either of them has been standing at this end.

She has never once written the number on the rack down and never once said it out loud, and it is a number she could say in one breath and has said to nobody, including a man in a felt apron four feet up the stone from her. She knows what it is for and he knows what it is for, and neither of them has ever put it in a sentence, and that is not a rule either of them has been given, and there is no form in this empire which says two people at either end of a passage may not hold a number between them.

"A carrier is a person on a list," he said, going on up.

"That is not what a carrier is."

"That is what a carrier is in this building, and I have been in it forty years, and I am telling you what the word means where you stand, and you have four years of it and I have forty." He shifted the rack off his hip. "**A carrier is a person on a list, and a list is not the same as being asked, and that is the difference that has kept this passage quiet for longer than either of us.**"

---

She had been at the lock since the fourth hour and she stayed at it.

"**The reason nobody has ever asked me what I carry is that being asked is being made a party, and a party is a document, and a document is a thing you can be served with.**" She said it to the stone and not to him, the way a person says a thing into a room that will give it back. "That is not a complaint about this passage. That is the arrangement, and it is a good arrangement, and I would like it noticed that I have never once tried to improve on it."

"You have never once been asked because there has never once been anything here to ask you about."

"There has not, and that is the part I have not said to you before, and I am saying it once." She turned round. "**I am the only person at this end that anybody could turn a thing into something that costs. You have just said the word carrier and you have said it wrong, and you are right about what it is in this building, and neither of those is the half of it.**"

He was about nine feet up the stone by then and did not come down for it, and she said the second half of it at the back of a man going away from her at the ordinary speed he always went away at, and he did not come back down it. What she has just told him is the half nobody on the passage has ever said, and she said it because he said the word first and got it wrong, and the getting of it wrong is the only thing in four years that has ever given her a reason to correct anybody.

---

He went on up the stone without turning round and without stopping, and the rack went up the flags with him on it.

"I am not saying the other half."

"That is the first time in four years you have refused me a sentence and I am not going to ask you for it again." He went on up the stone. "**A sheet came down that passage once in four years. It had a heading printed over a space on it. It went back up with the space clean. And there has been no rule on this passage ever about anything else, and there is not going to be one, and I have not got one written down and I am not going to write one.**"

"There is no rule on this passage about a blank either."

"There is no rule on this passage about a blank because no rule was ever needed, and I have been up and down this stone four times a day for forty years and I have never once had to know what to do with a thing I could not put anywhere." He stopped about nine feet up. "**And if one ever came down here, I would not know what to do with it, and you would not know what to do with it, and neither of us would be able to say so to anybody, and that is the only thing I have got to say to anybody in a year.**"

He said it from about nine feet up and she heard all of it and neither of them moved afterwards. The number on the rack is a number about what goes up, and the figure she has had for four years is a figure about what came down once, and hers is not his, and he does not know hers, and she has never asked for his, and there are about nine feet of the passage between them and about four hundred yards of it lie the other way, and neither of them has ever wanted the other one closer.

---

**She did not ask him where it went back up to.** She had not intended to and she did not now, and the not asking was in the room on both sides of that passage, and it was the loudest thing in it.

Then she stood at the lock for all four of the journeys that day, which she has not done since the spring, and there was nothing on the rack on any of them, and the number on the rack was one going up and one coming down, and the stone at the top end of those flags was as cold at the seventh hour as it had been at the fourth.

A week in which nothing arrives is not a week in which anything has been withheld. Those two are so close together that a person standing at a lock can lose a month in between them, and she lost most of one standing at that end of the stone in the spring and nobody at that end noticed and nobody at the far end of the same four hundred yards would have been allowed to ask.

She had worked it out in the spring and had not said it to anybody, and there is nobody in the building she works in who would have been allowed to hear the difference, and she decided at the time not to put it to anybody, and she has not mentioned it now and is not going to. The standing she gave the lock is the same standing she has given everything else at her end for four years, and the cost of it is that when the wrong thing does come down she will be the only one who knows what it was supposed to be, and there will be nobody to say so to.

---

**She decided at about the fifth hour that she was going to keep the lock.** Not because it was hers and not because anybody had given it to her, and not because there was anything at the other end of that stone worth the standing, and she gave no reason out loud to anybody, because there is no form in that building for a woman at a lock to say what she has decided about her own feet.

That is the whole of what it cost. The only protection either of them has at that end of that passage is that there is nobody there who would have to sign anything, and she has just told a man in a felt apron that she is the person who would have to sign it, and she has not moved, and the rail at the other end had nothing on it, and it is not hers, and nobody has been in that room this week who had any business there.
