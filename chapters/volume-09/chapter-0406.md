# Chapter 0406: Twenty Years At The Bottom Of Those Stairs, A Man At The Door Who Wants A Name, And A Rent Taken Anyway

The ground floor of that house is hers and has been for about twenty years, and there is a door in it, and a table by the door, and a lamp on the table that is lit at the end of the day. The stairs go up out of the room behind her. **She has never once been above the second one.** She takes the rent for the whole of that house on one day in the week, and there are two rooms above the ground floor, and one of them has a man in it who has been there two years.

She has never had his name and she has never given him hers. He writes nothing. She has never asked him one question about where he goes or what he does, and he has never asked her anything at all, and she has said out loud that the not asking is a thing she is doing and not a thing she has been failing to do.

It is the second day of the second week of the third month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fifth hour, and it is the day of the week she takes the rent on.

---

The man at the door was about thirty-one and he had a book under his arm, and the book had a heading over every column in it, and he did not come in.

"Two addresses on this street," he said. "The freeholder wants the second floor let by somebody who can be put in writing to. That is not a threat and it is not an increase. It is a question of whether anybody can serve anybody, and the answer at that address is no, because there is not a person in it."

"There is a person in it."

"There is a person in it and she has taken that rent for about twenty years under nothing, and I have been sent here to write a name at the top of a column, and I have got a man of forty-four upstairs who will not tell me one thing about himself either, and between the two of you that address cannot be served with anything at all." He did not open the book. "**And I want to be exact, because this is the part people get wrong. A person who cannot be served is not a protected person. A person who cannot be served is a person nobody would want to.**"

---

"That is the plainest thing anybody has said at this door in about nine years," she said, "and I have had a man at this door before who wanted the roof."

"I am not here about the roof."

"You are here about my name and I am not going to give it to you, and I am telling you that in the doorway rather than waiting for you to come inside for it, because if I say it inside then you have been in my ground floor and I have had you in here, and I have never had anybody in here who was not a person who had come about the lamp." She put her hand on the door frame and did not open the door wider. "**Twenty years. And I am the only one in that house who knows which side of that thing I am standing on, and I have known since about the third year, and I have not been asked and I have not been told, and I have worked it out on my own and I am not going to hand it to a man with a book in about nine minutes.**"

---

"You think it is a protection."

"I think you have told me it is not a protection and I have believed you, and I am going to go on acting as though it is one, and you are going to go on telling me it is not, and neither of us is going to have a form about it."

"That is not a position. That is two people disagreeing in a doorway on a wet afternoon." He shifted the book. "There is a difference between the two and it is the whole of what you have, and you know which side of it you are on and I do not, and that is the position. **I am going to come back on this day next week and ask you the same thing, and you are going to stand in that doorway again, and we are both going to be right about our own half of it.**"

"You are not going to ask me for the name of the man upstairs."

"I was not going to ask you for the name of the man upstairs."

"You wrote it on the top of the column of the day I saw you writing the last one."

"I wrote what was on the top of that column, which was a description and not a name, and a description can be written over and a name cannot be taken back, and that is why I have put it there." He turned the book round and did not open it. "You have read the top of that column from the other side of that door and I have not asked you how you could see it, and I am not going to now, and I would like it entered that I did not."

---

He went up the lane and the door stayed as it was and the lamp on the table was not lit yet.

**Nobody came down those stairs. The man in the room above had a week in it and had not been in it, and the man on the floor with the book had been four feet from her and had not been able to get one thing out of that house that a person could be written about. There is no form anywhere in this empire in which a landlord asks a woman what her name is and the woman has to give it, because that would be a form, and a form is a machine, and a machine gets asked things all day and does not mind.**

The rent was taken at about the sixth hour in the ordinary way, which is out of a hand and into a hand, and there was nothing written anywhere, and the amount was the amount it is, and the stairs went up out of that room and nobody has come down them in about twenty years and nobody came down them that night.
