# Chapter 0402: Twenty Minutes Before The Fourth Hour On A Floor Nobody Has Needed Yet, And A Woman Who Does Not Know What They Are For

There is nobody in that room for twenty minutes at the start of a working day. The stair has no door at the foot of it, so the twenty minutes is one long grey sound with a person walking up the middle of it, and after that the fourth hour arrives and the floor belongs to the building.

She is twenty-four and her name is Tamsin Rook, and two years is the whole of what she has done at that counter, and she is not a lawyer, and for two years she has been on that floor twenty minutes before the fourth hour every working day. **Nobody has ever put a question to her.** Not one, in two years, from the three people who work on that floor or from anybody who has ever come up those stairs, and she has not been thanked either, and she has never asked anybody anything.

Her undertaking is live. It has never been exercised and it has never been retired and the date of it has not been said out loud by her in two years and is not said out loud anywhere that anybody has to read.

It is the second day of the second week of the second month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is twenty minutes before the fourth hour.

---

She did the same eleven things she does on every one of those twenty minutes and she has done them in the same order for about two years.

The boards down. The twine out from behind the far end, because the twine lives behind the box and has never in eleven years been on any list. The day's ordinary things up from the shelf under the window in the order they go up. The stool out from under the counter. The lamp, which is not lit and does not get lit before the fifth hour and which she takes down and puts back anyway.

Then, at about ten minutes to the hour, the eleven things were done and there was nothing else on that floor to do before the fourth hour.

**She has spent the last ten minutes of the last two years sitting on that stool with her hands in her lap, and she has never once called the eleven things a reason, and nobody has ever called them a reason for her, and the not calling is the whole of the arrangement.**

The eleven things are eleven because she has counted them, and she has counted them by doing them, and no one else has counted them with her. There is no list of them anywhere and no heading over them and no column they are in, and if the whole arrangement of the floor were described to a person who had never stood on it, the description would be eleven ordinary actions done in an ordinary order in about a quarter of an hour by a person of twenty-four who is not going to be asked about any of them, and the description would be wrong, because it would leave out the only part of it that is hers.

She thought about the drawer under the board. It is a foot and a half off the floor and it is shut and it has been shut for eleven years and there are four things in it and two of them she has never opened. In two years she has never once put her hand on that drawer, and this week she put her hand on it at about eight minutes to the hour and took it off again, and that is the whole of what she did with it, and the drawer did not open and she did not rule a line on anything.

In two years not one person has come up those four flights inside the twenty minutes. Not Marn Ottery, who has been on the floor above eleven years and has not come up inside the twenty minutes once in any of them. Not the man from the yard, who has been carrying a satchel up and down for longer than anybody working there has been standing at the counter, and has never once been on the floor at a quarter to the hour. Not the man who comes in on the second of the two days. He is not one of those two and he is not there at all. She knows all of it, and there is no way of knowing whether it is because they would not or because nobody has ever thought of it, and she has gone on being early for two years in a way that has never once been tested.

---

Marn Ottery came up the four flights at the fourth hour and not before it, which is what she does, and she was at the near end of the boards before Tamsin Rook had her coat off.

"Ten minutes," Marn Ottery said.

"I am not saying what I do with the ten minutes."

"You are not going to say it and I am not going to let you, and I am not asking you for it, and I want both halves of that heard at the same time." She put the twine down and did not look at the stool. "**I am going to say one thing out loud and it is not about those twenty minutes, and when I have said it you are going to tell me it is about them anyway, and I am telling you in advance that I will not take it back when you do.**"

"Then I will not bother saying it."

"You will bother saying it. You have said two things to me in a year and one of them was about a book with a fifth column in it and I have thought about the other one every week since." Marn Ottery turned round and put her hands on the boards. "**A person who is in a room before the room is needed is a person who has made herself available in a way that nobody can undo.** Not available to me. Available to whoever comes up those stairs with a piece of paper and finds one person on a floor. That is what the twenty minutes are, whatever you have been calling them, and I have watched you spend them for two years and I have never once said it, and I have not said it because saying it makes it a thing you know about, and I did not want that in this room."

"You have just said it in this room."

"I have just said it in this room and I am not sorry and I am not going to be thanked for it and I will not be thanked for it, so do not start."

---

"And nobody in this building has done one thing about it," Marn Ottery said. "That is the half of it you are going to keep arguing with. Not one person at the top of these four flights has ever come up here early, and not one has ever left early, and not one has ever asked the two of us why the fourth hour has three people on it when it has one. **Nobody doing anything about it is the whole of the arrangement, and the arrangement has not cost anybody anything for two years, and you are the reason it has not, and I am the reason it has not, and neither of us has ever put that in a sentence.**"

"It has cost me two years of my life being the first one here."

"That is the cheapest thing either of us has ever paid and you know it is, and if you say it out loud as a cost I will not be able to argue with the rest of what you said." Marn Ottery did not raise her voice and did not move her hands. "**I am not going to ask you for it, and I have decided that this morning and not this week, and I want that on the floor before you say it and not after. If you put it down here I will leave you alone about it for good and I will never raise it again, and I will not be thanked for raising it now.**"

Every one of the mornings of two years, done alone on an empty floor before anybody in the building had come up, and not one of them has ever turned into anything at all, and the not turning is the whole of what the arrangement has produced, and there is no form anywhere in this empire on which a person can be shown two years of being early and nothing coming of it.

---

Tamsin Rook looked at the drawer for a while, and the drawer did nothing, and the board on the wall behind her carries four items and not one of the four is time, and the third of them is hers and the other two are not.

There are four things in the drawer under the board and two of them have never been opened, and she has known the number of them for two years without ever being told it, in the way a person knows the shape of a room she has stood in every morning since she came. Nobody has ever said which two. She has never asked, and she has not asked Marn Ottery and she has not asked the clerk of about twenty-nine, and the not asking stopped being a courtesy a long time ago, and she has never been able to work out whose it is that the two of them have not opened them.

"I do not know what the twenty minutes are for," she said. "**I have not known for two years and I have gone on doing them and I have never said to anybody that I do not know, and saying it now is the first time I have said it, and I am not going to be thanked for it, and I am not going to retire anything or exercise anything or put it down.**"

"That is not the one underneath."

"That is the one underneath and you know it is." She turned round and faced the boards. "And I will tell you what I have not told you and I am not going to tell you why. For two years I have been able to be the only person on that floor, and in two years nobody has ever used it, and I have spent the whole of that time getting myself into a state that nobody can prove and that I cannot put on anything. **I do not know whether that is a woman being careful or a woman getting ready, and I have not decided, and I am not going to decide it in a room with you in it**, and if you come at that again I will go and stand at the other end of these boards and we will do the morning like that."

---

Marn Ottery went to the other end of those boards and did not say one word about any part of that for the rest of that day.

The three clerks worked the counter from the fourth hour to the sixth. A man came up the stairs at the fifth hour for a folded thing and went down the stairs at the sixth hour with a satchel on his shoulder and asked nobody nothing, as he has done for eleven years. The box under the far end of those boards was where it was, and the drawer under the board was shut, and nothing was ruled on anything.

About a hundred feet of floor had been between the two ends of the boards all morning and neither of the two women had walked any of it, and nobody on the floor said one word about that, and the not saying did not stop at the sixth hour and did not start. The three of them went down at intervals and every one of them heard the other two coming down behind, because the stair carries every sound in it from the top of it to the bottom and there is nothing at the foot to shut it, and none of the three said one word about the hearing of it.

**Tamsin Rook came in twenty minutes early again on the third day of that week, and did the eleven things in the order she does them, and sat down on that stool for the last ten of the twenty minutes with her hands in her lap, and it is not a decision and it is not on anything, and nobody has told her that she is ready, and nobody is going to.**
