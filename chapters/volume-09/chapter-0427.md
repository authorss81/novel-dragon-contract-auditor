# Chapter 0427: Nothing At All Required Of Him For Nineteen Years, A Hand That Went To A Coat And Came Off It, And A Third Answer To A Pot

About nine feet of corridor with a sill at the top of the steps and a pen pot on the sill and a hook in the wall at the far end of it with a basket hanging on the hook. Two chairs on the landing. A bed against the wall at the near end that is made about half the time. Everything in that corridor is the colour of the wall except the pot and the chairs, and there is no room on that floor that any of it is in, and the corridor has never been described to anybody and by nobody.

He is about sixty-one and has been the second hand on a sheet since the year he turned forty-two. He cannot read a paragraph, he has never once pretended to, and the basket goes down at the seventh hour and does not come back up.

It is the second day of the first week of the ninth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fourth hour.

---

He was at the sill with the pot in front of him and his sleeve turned back about the way he has it turned back, and the young man came up with four under his arm and put them on the sill and he signed all four without looking up, and it took the better part of a minute, and the hand did not do anything different on the fourth one than it did on the first, and it never has in nineteen years.

Then he put the pen back in the pot and squared the four with the edge of the sill.

---

"**I am going to tell you what one of those requires of a man at the foot of it, and I am going to say it out loud, and you are not going to thank me and I am not going to be thanked.**" The young man came down the corridor and stood the way he stands. "**It requires nothing. No name. No mark. No reading. No paragraph. No day. And nothing to fill in.**"

The man of about sixty-one did not take his hand off the sill.

"That is the first thing anybody has said out loud to me in nineteen years that is not a question and not a complaint."

---

"**And nothing has ever required nothing of you, not once, in about nineteen years, and that is the price of it and the whole of the price.**" He put his hand on the sill the way a man puts a hand on a thing he is not going to pick up. "**A sheet asks a man to read a heading and then do what the heading says. You have done that about four hundred times a year for nineteen years and you cannot read a paragraph and you have never once pretended to, and it has been the best arrangement either of us has ever been in. And nobody has ever handed you a thing that wanted nothing off you, and if one came up that stair it would not want anything, and you would be the most expensive object in this matter to touch and the cheapest person in this empire to hand a piece of paper to.**"

---

"Nobody has ever handed me anything at that end of this floor and you know it."

"I have never seen one and I have not come up here to bring you one." The young man shifted his weight and stopped, and about halfway through the stopping **his hand went to the front of his coat and stayed there about the length of a breath and came off it again, and the man of about sixty-one saw it happen and he did not look at it and he did not look away from the sheets.**

He cannot read a paragraph and he can see a man put a hand on a coat and take it off again, and that is the whole of what has happened on that floor this month, and he is not going to say a word about it and he is not going to be asked about it.

---

The young man stood on the landing for a while with his hand on the rail.

"**That is not a thing I am going to be told about.**"

"I did not offer it to you."

"You did not and I want it entered that you did not, and I am not going to stand here and work out what it was, and I am not going to bring it up that stair, and there is nobody in this building I would hand it to and I have not got one, and I have not got one because of what I do at that end of this building and not in spite of it."

---

Then the man of about sixty-one laid his palm on the sill and took it off again, and he said one thing, and he said it to the pot rather than to a man, and it is not the sentence he said to that pot four weeks ago and it is not the one he said to it five months that are gone.

"**Nineteen years, and the first thing that would have come up that stair wanting nothing off me did not come.**"

That is the third thing he has ever said to that pot and the other two are his own from closed Chapter 0396, and none of the three was answered by anybody, and he did not want one answered.

---

**Nobody thanked him, and nobody asked him one question about a number, and the four men in a bay on the far side of that town have still not been asked one question about anything at all, and the man of about fifty-five with the chain is not one of those four and never has been.** The figures that man of about sixty-one carries were not printed in that corridor and were not confirmed and were not denied and were not put in a book, and he was not asked about them, and the nine miles is walked zero times.

He went on signing at the rate he signs all of them and he said nothing else for the rest of that day. **A week in which nothing comes down that floor is not a week in which anything has been withheld. That is the part which has cost that man the most in about nineteen years and has never once been said to him: he does not know which of the two his week is, and he has no way of working it out, and the figures he carries are not in a book anywhere and are not in his mouth anywhere and are the only evidence there is.** The four went down the corridor in the young man's own arms because they were not finished.

---

**And the thing that did not come up that stair, and why, and neither of those is going to be known to anybody in that building.** It did not come up that stair because a young man put it inside a coat, and a coat is the cheapest destination there is for a piece of paper that cannot be filed. A man who carries a thing up a stair for money cannot be shown what he is carrying, and that is the whole of what he is worth. He has said it out loud in this corridor twice in about three months and was not thanked either time and is not going to be thanked now. **A thing with no column does not travel to a floor with a man at the foot of about four hundred sheets a year on it. It travels to a coat, and the coat is on a back, and the back is the cheapest party in this matter, and nobody in that building knows it and nobody in that building is going to be told and he is not going to be the one to tell it.**

The basket went down at the seventh hour and **the hook was empty inside a minute, and a thing with no heading printed over it that would have asked him for nothing at all was somewhere else in that town at the seventh hour and not in that corridor. Nobody in that building knows that and nobody in that building is going to be told, and the man who would have signed it at the foot like the other four hundred did not see it and was not going to.**
