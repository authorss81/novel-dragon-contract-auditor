# Chapter 0415: Nine Hundred Sheets And About Nine Of Them Wrong, A Pen Put Back In Its Pot, And A Young Man Paid By The Corner

That corridor is about nine feet long and it has been described to nobody and by nobody, and everything in it is the colour of the wall except the pot on the sill and the two chairs on the landing at the top of the steps. The hook at the end of it has a basket on it. The bed against the far wall has not been made.

The man at the sill is about sixty-one and has been the second hand on a sheet since the year he turned forty-two. He cannot read a paragraph, he has never once pretended to, and about nine hundred of them a year go through his hands, and he signs every single one of them at the same rate and in the same hand whatever is on the sheet, and nobody in that building has stood in that corridor long enough to find out whether he does.

It is the second day of the first week of the sixth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fourth hour.

---

Nobody came up that corridor for the first hour of that day, and when somebody did, it was a man of twenty-six with four under his arm, and he stopped short of the sill instead of putting them on it, because the basket is for the ones that are finished and those four were not.

He is about twenty-six and has been carrying them two years and is paid for a corner and not for a page, **and the corner is the only part of any of it that he is paid for.** The basket goes down at the seventh hour and does not come back up.

He put the four on the sill and took them off the rail one at a time, and the man signed each of them where it lay, and each of the four took about the length of a breath and a half, and the last of them was signed in the same hand as the first one, and there is no way of telling that by standing there.

"You have not asked me what a carrier is," the young man said, from the top of the steps.

---

"You are not going to ask me and I am not going to tell you, and the two of us have known that for two years and I have not come up here this morning to change it."

"I am not going to ask you." He came back down the corridor and put a hand on the sill, the way a man puts a hand on a thing he is not going to pick up. "**I am going to tell you what one is, because you are the only person in this building it applies to and you are the only one in it who could not tell anybody, and that is not a coincidence and it is the whole of the finding.**"

---

"**A carrier is a person who cannot be refused, and that is the whole of it, and everything else anybody has ever said about that word in four hundred years is bookkeeping.**" The young man took his hand off the sill. "**And it is you. It has been you for nineteen years. About nine hundred sheets a year go out of that room and they all go up a stair in the arms of a man about twenty-six and not one of them is signed for by him, and every one of them goes to somebody who cannot be asked what is in it, and you are the bottom of every one of them, and you have never once been told what any of them was.**"

"I have never once been told what any of them was and I have not asked."

"You have not asked, and that is not a virtue, and I am not going to stand in a corridor and call it one." He shifted his weight. "**The only thing you have never done in nineteen years is read a heading. And that is not a gap in you. That is the trade.**"

---

"The reason it is cheap is the reason I am paid for a corner and not for a page," he said. "**An entry goes where there is nowhere to keep an entry. That is the whole of why a man like me is worth what I am worth. If anybody could be shown a list of the things I have carried, I would be an expensive man, and the not being able to be shown it is the only thing I have got.**"

Nobody said anything for a moment, and the corridor did not change, because the only light in it comes down off the landing and the landing is at the top of the steps.

Then the man of about sixty-one laid his palm on the sill and took it off again, and he said one thing, and he said it to the pot rather than to a man, and it is the only sentence he has got for it and it is the one he always says to the pot.

"**A man does not get the pen out twice in a week for a thing he did not do the first time.** That is the reason and it is all of the reason and nobody gave it to me."

"I was not doing that."

"I know what you were doing, and so do I, and neither of us is going to be able to say which of the two it was by the seventh hour." He turned the second sheet over and put it on the top of the first, and put the pen back where it had been standing since the fourth hour of a week that is gone, and squared the four against the edge of the stone with two fingers. "You can put that in with the four and take it down, and there is nothing else for you this week and nothing coming that I know of."

The young man did not go and get it. He stood on the landing for a while with his hand on the rail.

"You told me a thing in this corridor five months that are gone," he said, "and you told me a second one is in you, and I have not asked you for it and I am not going to."

"I know you have not."

"I am not going to say anything else about it. Not this week and not in four weeks. **The only thing I have said out loud about it in a room is that I said four months and I said it as a guess, and I have decided this week that I am not going to say it again until it has either happened or it has not.**" He came down two steps. "That is not me being careful. That is me not wanting to be a second thing on a piece of paper in a room where the first one has got no column."

---

He went on signing at the rate he signs all of them, and he said nothing else for the rest of that day, and the four stayed on the sill until the young man was ready for them.

**Nobody in that building asked him one question about a number, and nobody has, and the four men in a bay on the far side of that town have still not been asked one question about anything at all this week, and the man of about fifty-five with the chain is not one of those four and never has been.** The four went down the corridor in the young man's own arms because they were not finished, and at the seventh hour he took the basket down that corridor and the hook was empty inside a minute.

**The pen went into that pot in the fourth hour and stayed in it.** The second true thing did not come out that week, and it did not come out the week after, and the man who has it did not write it down, and nobody in that building is going to be the person he tells it to, and a pen in a pot is a thing a man has put back in the same place twice and is not going to take out a third time.
