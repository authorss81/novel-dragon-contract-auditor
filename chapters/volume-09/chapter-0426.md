# Chapter 0426: No Rule On That Passage, A Sheet With No Heading Over It, And Whoever Is Standing At This End

The lock is at this end of that stone. **At the other end of the same four hundred yards there is a room with a rail standing about four feet off the floor, and the rail is not on the work of that passage and never has been, and it has had nothing on it since before she came, and that room is not hers and she has never once been into it and is not going to this week.** The flags go up from the lock at an angle that makes a person put a hand on the wall, and the wall is the colour of the flags for about nine foot and then it is not.

She is twenty-six and she has been at this end for four of those years, and she has never been one of the three clerks of the building she works in, and in all that time nobody in it has put a question to her or sent for her or said thank you to her, and not one sheet has ever been put into her hands for her to take up the stone with.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the eighth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fourth hour, and there has been nothing come down that stone all week that a person could have been shown what it was for.

---

He came down the stone at the fourth hour with the rack on his hip and he did not go on by her.

**He has gone up that stone and come down it four times a day for forty years and he has gone by the lock about sixteen hundred times in that time without stopping at it, and this is the first time she has seen him stop at it.**

He set the rack against the wall where it goes and stood on the flags about the length of a sheet off her.

Her hands go to the wall and come off it a good many times in the course of a working day, and that is the work, and a hand goes out for most of what the wall puts in front of somebody going up and not for the rest of it, and she has always been one of the ones who does, and four years of it is a great deal of wall.

---

"**A sheet with no heading printed over it is not a thing either of us has ever had to handle.**"

A rack which goes up and comes down the stone four times a day, just as often coming down as going up, is a thing a man carries and does not push, and a man who has carried one for forty years puts it against a wall without looking at where the wall takes it, and the putting of it down took no longer than it takes to touch a wall and stop touching it.

He said it to the stone and not to her, and then he stayed where he was.

"There is no rule on this passage about one."

"There is no rule on this passage about one because no rule was ever needed, and I have gone up and down this stone four times a day for forty years and I have never once had to know what to do with a thing I could not put anywhere, and that is the whole of what I have got."

---

She did not move off the lock and she did not look at him.

"**And here is the half of it that I have not said at this end before, and I am saying it once and I am saying it standing still, which I have not done at this end in forty years.** Whoever is standing at this end when one comes down is the one who will have to decide what happens to it. **That is the person. And the person is her.**"

The rack was against the wall and the flags were cold and there was nothing on the rack, and neither of them said anything else about it for a while.

---

"You have said that in a place where a thing came down once in four years and went back up with the space clean."

"I have said that and I have said the other half of it, and the other half is that I went up and came down that passage four times a day for four years while it happened and I was carrying the number at that time, and it went back up clean, and I have never known what was in it and I have never once been able to put my hand on it and say so." He took his hand off the rack. "**I am not saying it to you because you did it and because I do not blame you. I am saying it to you because you are standing at the end of the stone and I am forty years old and I have just worked out that if one comes down here it is not a thing that arrives and it is a thing that lands on a person.**"

He came down the stone this morning for the four times a day and for nothing else, and the number is not one anybody will get off him, and there is no asking him for it that is not simply not asking.

---

She worked out the rest of it standing at that lock and she worked it out fast and she did not say one word of it, and she was not going to, and nobody put one thing to her about it.

**A rule would be a form, and a form is a heading over a ruled space, and a heading is a place a thing is kept, and a thing that is kept can be shown. And a passage with no rule about a sheet with no heading on it has been right for forty years because there has been nothing to have a rule about, and the reason there is no rule is not that the rule is forbidden and never has been, it is that nobody has ever needed to print one and nobody ever is going to. And a person standing at this end is not an office. A person standing at this end is a woman of twenty-six. She has never once been one of the three clerks and she is never going to be one. There is no list and there is nothing to be put on, and that has been the arrangement for four years and it has been good and it has cost her nothing she could put a name on, and he has just told her out loud that it is the arrangement and that it is hers, and she has not said a word.**

---

A person going up the stone with a hand out has about nine foot of one thing to reach for and then has to bring the arm back in and carry it, and many of the people who go up never reach for anything at all and get up it exactly the same, and the two kinds of person arrive at the top of it at the same time and neither of them knows the other went up.

She did not thank him and she did not ask him anything and she did not say that she had worked it out, and he did not ask her whether she had.

The rack went up the stone with him on it, and he stopped about nine feet up, the way he stopped in a week that is gone, and this time he did not say anything at all from up there, and she stayed at the lock and watched the flags go cold and then colder.

**A quiet week at that end of the stone is not a week in which somebody sat somewhere and decided to send nothing, and she has known that for four years and has had no way of checking it and has never once been in a position to check it, and that is the whole of what a person standing at a lock can be told about it.** Nothing with a heading printed over it came down that stone this week or the week before, and the rail at the other end had nothing on it, and the four hundred yards was not walked, and the figure she has carried for four years is on no paper of any kind and did not go up and down and does not get a heading.
