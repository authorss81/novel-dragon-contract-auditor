# Chapter 0428: A Rent Out Of A Hand Into A Hand, One True Thing Said Out Loud In A House, And A Tin Behind A Boiler

There is a ground floor with a passage and a stair off it and a door at the top of the stair that she has never been at, and a lamp on a table by the front door that is lit at the sixth hour and has been lit at the sixth hour for about twenty years. The table is the table. The floor is the floor. There is no room on this ground floor and there is no list and there is no form and nothing in twenty years has been written down anywhere in this house.

**She is not the clerk of about fifty-three. She has never been inside that building off the old river road and has not been inside it in her life and does not know what was said in it and would not know it if she were told, and nobody has ever told her, and there is nobody in this city she could ask.**

It is the second day of the second week of the ninth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fifth hour, and nobody in this house has asked her anything today.

---

The rent goes out of a hand into a hand once a week, and it has gone out of a hand into a hand for about twenty years, and nothing is written anywhere and there has never been a piece of paper on that table in her life.

It is a short piece of business and not one hour of it has ever had to be hurried, and it is done in the ordinary voice and without one word of arrangement, and about four feet of far wall has been looked at while it was done and never once by anybody else.

**There is a tin behind the boiler. It has been behind that boiler for about nine years and it is about the size of a loaf and it has a lid on it that does not shut the way it used to, and it has nine years of a man's rent in it in coin, and she has never counted it and there is no reason on earth to count it.**

She puts it back on the same way round every time, and it takes longer than a working lid would, and none of it is a thing anybody can see from the doorway, and nobody in the house has ever gone behind the boiler and she is the only one who has.

---

She was alone and she knew she was alone, and that is the whole of why she did it.

**Two hundred years is not a figure, it is a run of rent days.** There were four men on that stair before him and there are about nine of them after him, and the two before him took their rent in the same room at the same hour and one of them had a dog and one of them had a cough, and none of them ever asked her a single thing, and she never asked them one, and that was not a coldness between her and nine men. **It was the arrangement, and the arrangement was built by the two of them, and neither of them ever said so out loud, and she has never once said so out loud either, and it is the only thing she has had since she was about nineteen.**

There is no form in this empire for a woman at the bottom of a stair to be asked what she carries. There is one for a man who keeps a company books and there is one for a man who sits in a bay and there is one for a woman of fifty-one who is a foreman, and there has never been one for her, and she did not go looking, and she was not asked to go looking, and she would have declined if she had been.

**And the whole of the last twenty of those years is this: she has been the cheapest person in a house that works by being cheap, and she found that out about nine years ago, and she has said nothing to anybody about it since, and she has not been a party, and nobody can be shown anything about her, and that has been the best thing that has ever happened to her and she built it herself out of nothing but not asking.**

She put her two hands flat on the table on either side of the lamp and she looked at about four feet of the far wall, and she said it out loud, once, in the ordinary voice, in an empty room, to nobody.

"**I have known for nine years what he is and I have said nothing to anybody about it for nine years and I chose it, and I chose it nine times a year on a rent day with my own two hands, and nobody in this house can be shown anything because of a tin behind a boiler and I put it there.**"

---

She said it and the room gave it back the way a room does, and she did not say it again, and she did not say a part of it, and she was not asked about it by anybody because there was nobody in the house.

A lamp lit at the sixth hour every day for about twenty years is lit by one person and by nobody else, and she lights it where the table is, and nothing else in the house happens at a fixed hour, and the hour has not moved once in about twenty years, and a fixed hour in a house with nothing written down anywhere in it is the only record the house keeps.

**That is the cost, and it is exact and it is the only thing that happened that evening. A true thing has been said out loud in a house, and a true thing said out loud in a house is a thing the house can be shown. The arrangement that made this the cheapest floor in this city has stopped being an arrangement and has become a thing somebody knows, and there is no form anywhere for taking a said thing back and there is no form anywhere for a woman at the bottom of a stair to be recorded as the one who said it, and there never has been.**

An empty ground floor at the fifth hour is not an unusual thing in the house and it is not a lonely thing either, and the difference between the two words is the whole of about twenty years, and the passage is there and the front door is there and neither of the two has ever been put to her as a question and she has never put one to either of them, and about twenty years is a long time to have gone without either.

She was not thanked. Nobody sent for her. There is nobody to send for anybody in this house and there has not been for twenty years.

---

**And here is the half of it that is hers alone and that nobody in any building in this city is going to hear. A person who has said a true thing in a house cannot be a found person again, and she has wanted to be unfindable for about nine years, and being unfindable was the whole of what she had. She has given it up out loud on a rent day at the fifth hour to an empty room because she has carried it nine years and it was there every day of those nine years and it got no lighter and it was not going to.**

She sat down on the step for a while. She has never once sat on that step with nothing in her hands and she has not done it now either, because there is a week of rent in a tin behind a boiler and that is a job.

---

And the thing she did not do, and the reason, is the whole of the rest of it.

**She did not move the tin.** She got up off that step and went and stood in front of the boiler, and looked at where it is, and did not touch it. The reason is that moving it is a sensible thing to do, and **a sensible thing done on purpose is a thing a person can be shown doing, and everything she has had for twenty years is that nobody in this house can be shown anything, and she is not going to spend it on a boiler.**

She put the lamp where the lamp goes and did not put it out, and the table had nothing on it but the lamp.

---

Nobody in that house said one word to anybody that evening and nothing was written anywhere and no thread in this matter was closed and the four hundred and thirty miles is not walked and the name at the end of that struck line is not in any mouth in this chapter and the hand that struck it is not either. **She did not go up those stairs and she is not going to this week, and the lamp on that table is lit. About nine years of one man's rent is behind a boiler in a house that a person has now said out loud what is in, and the only person who knows it is her. She is not the clerk of about fifty-three and there is no distance between the two of them because there has never been one to state and the two women have never been in one building.**
