# Chapter 0421: A Sentence In Her Head Every Working Day Since, The Form On The Board Again, And A Lamp Not Lit

There is a shed at the end of a lane off the Slade, about nine foot by eleven, and the light in it comes off the lane and off a wall standing about four foot past the open side. The lamp is on its bracket at the high end and it is not lit until about the seventh hour. A man can lie down on the bench in it if he turns sideways. There is a chain across the front of that bench and the chain is on the man at that bench.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the seventh month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fourth hour.

---

**He has said one true thing in his working life and it is about nine weeks old and it has not been spent.** He said it in this shed, in the second week of the fifth month, to a woman standing four steps off him with her hands at her sides, and he said the price of it in the same breath, and she took both, and neither of them has said a word about it since, and there have been about nine weeks of it.

"You have not had anything else," she said.

"No."

"You have had about nine weeks and two ends of this shed." She was not looking at him. "I have had nine weeks with it as well and I have got further with it than you have, and I am going to say what I have got, and I am not asking you for anything, and nobody is going to thank me for saying it."

"All right."

---

"**It has been in my head every working day since and I have not been able to do one single thing with it.**"

She said it flatly, to the bench, and not to him.

She was at the low end of the bench and the high end was his, and in about nine weeks neither of them has said out loud which of the two ends was hers, and neither of them has needed to say it, because the bench is one bench and a person can stand at either end of one bench for nine weeks and not have come to an arrangement about anything.

"Not one. And I have put it to about nine things since and every one of them came out the same, and I am not going to list them, because a list is a thing you can be shown and this is not one."

"You are not showing it to anybody."

"I am not showing it to anybody and that is the half of it I did not know until about four months ago." She turned round. "**A person who is told what a carrier is cannot un-know it. That is all that happened. Nobody did anything to me. Nothing was taken off me and nothing was put on me, and I have a thing in my head now that I did not have in the second week of the fifth month and I am going to have it on the day I die.**"

The chain had slack in it this morning. The slack took up a little every time he turned his head and gave a little back when he stopped, and neither of them counted how often, and a man chained to a bench turns his head a great many times between the fourth hour and the seventh, and the fourth hour and the seventh are a long way apart.

---

He did not move, and the chain did not move, and the light off that wall had not shifted a foot in the whole of it.

"That is not what I said it for."

"I know exactly what you said it for, you told me the price of it in the same breath, and I took it anyway, and I would like it entered that I took it." She put her hand flat on the end of that long bench. "**You do not get to have said it. You said it in a shed to a woman who was the cheapest thing you could find on that morning, and I was the cheapest thing you could find, and it has been in my head every working day since, and that is not a gift and it is not a thing I have come up here to give back.**"

He said nothing for a while, and the two of seventeen at the back of that shed did not move, and nobody said a word to either of them.

She turned her hand over and looked at the palm of it, and the palm was empty, and she put the hand down flat again, and it took no longer than the waiting a man does before he answers, and not one breath longer.

---

"Here is the half of it that is mine," she said, "and I am not being dramatic and I am not asking you for it. **The price for being unfindable is not mine, and the bill is not mine, and you told me that in this shed, and it is the only true thing anybody has ever said to me that I have been able to use, and I have used it exactly once, on myself, and it did not come out right.**"

"You have not told me what you used it on."

"I am not going to." She took her hand off the bench. "There is nobody in this lane to hand that to and you did not ask me for it and I am not going to volunteer it, and that is the arrangement and it is the only thing either of us has got."

---

Then he said his one thing, and he said it slowly, and he said it to her and not to the wall.

Between one person and the next the man on the bench ran his thumb along the front edge of the bench and moved it about two inches and put it back where it was, and he has been doing it since before there was anybody at the low end to do it in front of, and the two inches is not a signal to anybody and has never been one.

"**A carrier's thing goes somewhere.** That is the whole of the trade and I have had it since I was young. It goes to a counter and it goes to a man and it goes into a basket, and about four hundred of them a year go past a man who cannot read a paragraph and not one of them stays with him. It is a thing that goes."

"And mine does not go anywhere."

"**Yours is in a woman of fifty-one at the front of my own shed and it is there for good.**" He turned his head about as far as the chain allows. "It is not in a book and it is not on a form and there is not going to be one, and that is not a fault in either of us, and I am not going to be the one who comes back here and says sorry for it, because sorry is a form and I have not got one."

---

"**You are not going to apologise and I have not come up here to be apologised to.**" She was almost at the door. "I have not thanked you and I am not going to thank you, and I would rather have those two things than a favour, and I want it said in this shed that I would rather have them, because a favour is a debt and I know what a debt is and so do you."

"That is the cheapest arrangement either of us has ever been offered."

"It is the only one on the table and we did not put it there."

The light off the wall comes across the lane and goes over the bench and off it again, and it stops in the same place every morning, and a man on a bench can tell the hour by where it stops without a clock and without moving.

---

The four hundred yards up that lane were not walked that day. They were not walked on her account and they were not walked on his. **The woman who lives at the top of them has wanted the rest of a sentence for about two years and has not asked and is not going to**, and the second half of a sentence she began in a doorway in a week that is gone has still not arrived. She is fifty-one and she has a shed to open in about two hours and no sentence to open it with.

**The form that would take the two at the back of that shed out of the back of it was on the board by the door, and it will be on that board again on the second of the two days next week, and nobody in that lane has standing to ask it to be void and nobody has.** She was not thanked for the four sentences she has said in that room over about a year and nobody asked her for a fifth and nobody is going to.

He did not ask her what she had used it on. The lamp stayed where it was on its bracket. And the only thing that has changed in nine foot by eleven in about nine weeks is that a woman is carrying a sentence she did not have, and the man who put it there has been told exactly what it is costing and cannot do one single thing about it, which is the first time in that lane that anything he has said has come back at him.
