# Chapter 0413: Four Hundred Blanks A Year Out Of A Case, A Courier Nobody Can Put A Name To, And The Price Of Not Naming Him

The counter is at the top of a stair and the stair has no other way up it. There is a board on the wall behind that counter with four items on it and not one of the four is time, and a drawer under that board which is shut and has been shut for eleven years and has four things in it, two of which have never been opened. At the far end of those boards, out of sight of the door, there is a box of about four hundred bought and unfilled blanks. At the near end there is a case the size of a shoe box, and about four hundred blanks a year come out of the case in the hand of somebody.

**She is thirty-four and her name is Marn Ottery and those boards have been hers for eleven years and she is not a lawyer and is not going to be one.** She is the only person on that floor who can say what one of those pieces of paper is for, and she said it out loud once, on the second day of the first week of the second month, and nobody thanked her for it, and she said in terms that she was not going to be thanked then or later.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the fifth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fifth hour, and the counter shuts at the sixth hour.

---

The man from the yard was on the stairs at half past the fifth with a strap across him and the satchel off it and the cold had got into the leather where it sits against a man's hip. He has been going up and down those four flights longer than anybody at that counter has been standing at them.

Everybody who comes up the stair is coming up it for something, and in eleven years at the top of it she has not come up once without one. The boards behind the counter were cut before she came to them and nothing anywhere in the building accounts for who ordered them, and she has looked for a join in them where two lengths meet and has never found one.

He put the satchel on the boards, took a folded thing out of it, set that on the boards as well, and did not take the bag off his shoulder. **Nobody on that floor has ever told him what is in either, and he has never once asked, and there is nowhere on that floor to tell him, and that is not an oversight anybody has been meaning to fix.**

She has watched the stairs from the top of them for eleven years and she has a way of counting she has never told either of the other two, and the way of counting is that she knows the half hour at which he comes up and the half hour at which he goes down, and the difference between the two has come out about the same every day of the eleven years she has been up them. What goes into the satchel and what comes out of it are two different questions and she has never asked the second one, because the first one is not hers and the second one would be.

The man who comes in on the second of the two days was at the head of the tables. He is not a lawyer either and has not been one for about nine years, and he is the third of the three on that floor, and he had been in since the third hour.

"He is a carrier," the man said.

---

"Do not say that word on this floor."

"There is a second half to it and I have had it since a week that is gone." He put his pen down. "**A carrier is a man with a thing in his hand that nobody signed him for and that nobody can send back down the stair. That one came into this building before I came into it and he will still be coming into it after I have gone, and that is nineteen years of a man who has never once been told what he is holding.**"

"You have never asked him either."

"I have never asked him and I have thought about that every working day for about nine years, and I have come to the end of it this week." He did not look at the stair. "**He is the cheapest person who walks into that building. That is all he is. He is cheap because he cannot be asked, and he cannot be asked twice, and he cannot remember, and those three are the same thing and not three things, and if you write that down anywhere it is a piece of paper with a heading over it, so do not.**"

He said all of it to the tables and not to the stair, and there was somebody on the stair while he said it, and he went on with the sentence to the end of it. A long stretch of not asking a man a question gets written on a person, and the marks of it are that he says a thing early and then spends the rest of the sentence making it smaller.

---

"That is the first time anybody has said a true thing about that man on that floor," Marn Ottery said, "and I am not going to have it."

"You are sitting on a case of about four hundred a year that nobody can account for, and a drawer with four things in it that two people in this room know the contents of and the other one does not, and a board with four items on it that the three of us cannot account for, and a man comes up those stairs twice a day carrying things for the whole of this building and goes down them and remembers none of it." He put his hand flat on the table. "**If anybody ever comes up that stair about any part of this, the one person on this floor who could not be shown what any of it was for is him. That is a use of him and I am telling you what it is before anybody uses it.**"

Of the days in a week, the second of the two days falls when the work on the floor is thickest and it is the only one on which the counter is busy from the fourth hour to the sixth, and he has nine years of it in front of him and he has used it once.

"You have been here nine years and you have never once said his name on that stair."

"I have never once said his name and I am not going to start now, and that is the reason I am saying this to you and not to him, and it is the reason I am not going to put his name to it." He took his hand off the table. "**You do not want it said. I can see that you do not want it said and I have not asked you about it. So here it is in a form, and the form is words, and words on this floor have never cost anybody anything because there is nothing to put them on.**"

---

"No," Marn Ottery said. "**I do not want that man to be a thing on this floor. He is a man who comes up a stair and puts a thing down and goes down a stair, and if he is a carrier then he is a carrier, and if he is a carrier then he is a person in this matter, and a person in this matter is a person who can be shown what a thing is going to be used for.**"

"That is the whole of what I came up here to say and you have said it back to me in one sentence and you did not hear yourself do it."

"I heard myself do it."

---

"And the price of it is that a man who is not examinable cannot be examined on your behalf either." The courier came past them with the satchel on his shoulder and nobody looked up and he did not look at either of them. "**You have just arranged for the cheapest person in this building to be the only one in it that nobody can use. That is a good arrangement. It is also a wall on both sides of it, and you are inside it, and there is a man in this room who is going to come at you one day about the far end of those boards and you will not be able to say a single word back that he has not already said for you.**"

"I know the price of it. I have known the price of it since a man came up those four flights on the fourth day of the third week of the third month, and Marek Kest is the name on him, and he did not buy a copy of anything and went back down them, and it is six weeks since and I have had all six of them to work out what he came for." She did not look at the drawer. "I have not worked it out. I am not going to work it out. And I am not going to stand on this floor and be grateful to you for making the one person on it that nobody can use into a person I have to feel safe about."

The working goes on in the ordinary way, at the counter, with her hands, while two other people come up the four flights and go down them. What she has done with the six weeks is go over the case at the near end and count what is in it twice, and the two counts have not differed once in eleven years, and no written thing in the building says where a thing in it is filled, and she has never once asked, because the person who fills it is a person she would have to name.

---

The man at the head of the tables looked at her for a while and then he picked his pen up and went back to the third of the two days.

"That is fair," he said. "I withdraw the shape of it and not the fact of it."

"You have just put the fact of it and the shape of it into the same breath and I have been taking the wrong one of the two for about nine years, and I have worked out which is which inside the last minute, and I am not going to hand you the doing of it."

"The fact of it is that the man from the yard is a carrier and I have said so and it is said. The shape of it is that you have to do something about it, and I withdraw that, and I withdraw it because you have just told me the price out loud in front of a third person and there is no form anywhere for taking that back."

Nobody thanked him.

---

She said the word at the other end of those boards, once, on the way out, to the stair and not to him.

"**He is a carrier and he is the cheapest party in this matter and the reason he is cheap is that nothing about him can be shown to anybody, and I have had the use of that for eleven years and it is not mine to give up, and nobody thanks me for having it.**"

The courier was halfway down the second flight by then, and the cold had got into the leather, and he was carrying a folded thing that nobody on that floor has ever been told about, and he was going to put it down somewhere and go away. He could not have told anybody what he had held that day if a man had stood at the bottom of that stair with a form in his hand, and there is not one form in this empire for a man at the bottom of a stair.

The counter shut at the sixth hour. The drawer was shut, and the four things were in it, and the box was under the far end of those boards where it has been for eleven years, and nothing was ever left in it, and the case at the near end is still not the box and no form anywhere in that building says where the case is filled.
