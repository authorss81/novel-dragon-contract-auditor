# Chapter 0432: The Same Four Flights Going Up, A Piece Of Paper In The Inside Pocket, And A Sill That Did Not Get It

He came up with the four under his arm at about the fourth hour and he had the other thing in the inside pocket of the same coat, and it had been in there about a month, and it had been folded in three every day of that month and unfolded nothing.

Two years on a shipping floor and about four sheets a week up four flights, and the fold of a sheet is the part of it that shows, which is how a man who cannot read ends up with about six of the tops of them in his head. **Not one of the six was looked for and not one of the six has been spoken about in that building, and there is nobody in it who has ever come at him about any of them.**

It is the second day of the second week of the tenth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fourth hour.

---

He had come up that stair a month ago with nothing in his hands, put fourpence on the boards about four flights above, and been handed a piece of paper with nothing printed on it, and filled it in by hand in about a minute and a half.

Nobody asked him what it was for. Nobody thanked him. He said two sentences out loud into a room that had given him nothing to say them to and neither of them was picked up, and he folded the sheet in three and buttoned the coat over it and went down.

**He has not been asked about it since and he has not been thanked for it since and he has not once taken it out.**

---

He got as far as the sill with the four and then he stopped, and he stood there about the length of a sheet being squared, and the man of about sixty-one did not look up from the third one.

He can see a man put a hand on a coat and take it off again. He has no way at all of knowing what is in a pocket and never has had, and about nine hundred of them a year go past that man and there is nobody in this empire he could hand one to, and he has not got one because of what he does at the end of that floor and not in spite of it.

"That is the four," he said.

"Put them down."

---

"I am not going to put anything else on that sill."

The man of about sixty-one signed the third one and the fourth one and the hand did not do anything different on either of them, and then he squared them against the edge with the side of his hand, which he does.

"I heard you and I am not going to ask you what is in your pocket and you knew that when you opened your coat, and I have never asked anybody anything in this corridor in nineteen years and I am not starting this week."

"You have not asked me anything in two years either and I have not been up here in a month."

"You have not been up here in a month because there is nothing on that case to buy."

---

**And the young man said the reason out loud, and it was a price and not a rule and not a refusal, and he said it to the sill and not to the man, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for a person to be entered as the one who said it.**

"**If it goes in that basket it is in a room with you in it. And then it goes down at the seventh hour and it comes back up in the morning in a hand I do not know, or it does not come back up at all, and either of those is a place it has been and there is nothing anywhere that can say who put it there.**"

He stopped, and started again about the length of a breath later.

"**A coat is the cheapest place a piece of paper can be in this empire and I have got one, and you have not got a coat, and that is the whole of the difference between me and you and it took me about a minute and a half to work out.**"

---

The man of about sixty-one put the pen back in the pot and squared the four again, and he did not say anything for about nine seconds, and nobody in that corridor was waiting for him to say anything.

"**You have got it as far as my sill and that is the whole of what I have seen of it, and I am not waiting to hear the rest of it.**"

"I am going to tell you the rest of it and I am telling you because it costs me nothing to say it here and a great deal to carry it another week. I have brought it up as far as your sill and no further. I have not opened my coat and I have not put it on that sill, and I am going to take the four down in about a quarter of an hour and go back to a floor where nobody can be shown anything about me."

"You have not asked me for anything."

"I have not asked you for anything and I am not going to, and I have been carrying a thing in a coat for a month and the price of carrying it is that I cannot put it down anywhere, and I would rather pay that than have you know what is in it, and you cannot read a paragraph and I would rather be safe than sorry."

---

**And he was not examinable and had never been, and the man of about sixty-one could not have examined him if he had wanted to, and this was the first morning on which a thing with no column and a man who cannot read a paragraph were in the same room and neither of them could say one useful word to the other about it.**

Nobody thanked him. Nobody was thanked. Nobody asked him what it was for, and nobody has ever asked him one question about a number, and the four who cannot make sense of a paragraph are not in this building and were not asked anything by anybody this week and will not be.

The four went down the corridor in the young man's own arms because they were not finished, and the piece of paper stayed in the inside pocket of the coat, and the man of about sixty-one did not see him go and was not looking.

**The hook was empty inside a minute after the seventh hour and there was nothing on that sill that had not got a heading printed somewhere over it, and that is a fact about that corridor and not a decision anybody took in it.**
