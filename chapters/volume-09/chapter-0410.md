# Chapter 0410: The Fourth Of Four Items On A Fee Board, A Sentence Shaped Like A Question, And The Counter Shutting At The Sixth Hour

The board on that wall carries four items and not one of the four is time, and the fourth of the four has been on it since the third hour of an ordinary morning and has not been asked about by anybody. **About four hundred of them go out of that counter in the hand of somebody every year, and not one of the three people on that floor has ever been told what a person does with one afterwards, and the woman at that counter is the only one of the three who can say what a piece of paper with nothing printed on it is for, and she has said it once.**

**She is thirty-four, she has the other end of those boards, and the boards have been hers for eleven years.** Her name is Marn Ottery and she is not a lawyer and is never going to be one. The drawer under that board is shut and has been shut for eleven years and there are four things in it and two of them have never been opened. The box under the far end of those boards holds about four hundred bought and unfilled blanks. Nothing has ever been left in it, there is no form anywhere in the four hundred buildings this empire is made of that would be used to account for a box of them, and there has not been one drawn since it went under there.

It is the second day of the second week of the fourth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the third hour, and the counter shuts at the sixth hour.

---

Nobody working there has ever watched the older of the two women open either of the two things in the drawer, and she has not offered and has not been asked, and no form anywhere in this empire will require a person to account for what is in a drawer she did not put there, and she has been the oldest person on the floor for eleven years and has never once been asked to hand anything over to anybody.

A woman of about thirty-eight came in at about half past the third hour and put fourpence on the boards and the clerk of twenty-four gave her a piece of paper with nothing printed on it out of the case at the near end, and the woman folded it in three and put it inside her coat and went down the four flights.

Then a young man of about twenty-one did the same thing, and then a man of about fifty-six, and neither of them was asked what it was for and neither of them said anything at all. Nobody at that counter knows what any of the three of them is going to do with it, and **that is the ordinary morning of that counter and it has been the ordinary morning of that counter for as long as anybody there can remember.**

The woman of about thirty-eight had stood at the near end of the boards and had not said one word. The young man of about twenty-one had done the same thing about a minute later and had gone out faster. The man of about fifty-six had counted the money out of a hand before he put any of it down and had not looked at either of the others, and the clerk of twenty-four had handed all three of them out of the case with one movement of the same arm and had not looked at any of them either.

"You are doing it out of the case."

"I am doing it out of the case."

"There is a box under the far end of those boards with about four hundred in it and nobody can say where that case is filled, and I have been at these boards eleven years and I have never been able to find out, and I have stopped trying, and I want it entered that I stopped on my own account." Marn Ottery did not look at the case. "**The box is not the supply. That is the whole of what I have got and it is not much and it is the only true thing about that end of these boards.**"

Eleven years is a long time for three people to look under a counter at a thing and not once move it. Each of them has put a hand on it at some point in the eleven of them and each of them has done it alone and on a day when the other two were not working, and not one of them has ever put a hand on it in front of either of the others, and there is no rule against that and there never was, and it is the single most consistent thing about the three of them.

---

The clerk of about twenty-nine was at the other end of the two joined tables. She has said a number out loud in that room three times in eleven years and has been thanked for none of them, and she has given up putting her pen away at the end of an afternoon.

Tamsin Rook had been on that floor since twenty minutes before the fourth hour and had done the eleven things in the order she does them and had been sitting on that stool for the last ten minutes of them with her hands in her lap.

"You have been at these boards eleven years," she said.

"I have."

"**And in eleven years you have not once told me what the fourth of those four is for.**"

She said it to the boards and not to the woman, and she said it in the ordinary voice, and it was a sentence and it had no mark at the end of it.

Not one sentence in two years down there had gone into the air carrying a shape like it, and she had built it out of the plainest words she could find and had gone over every one of them before she said it, which is a thing a person does at the end of a fortnight, and she had decided in advance it was not going to carry a mark at the end, and it did not.

Marn Ottery did not turn round.

"I am not going to answer that," she said. "I am not going to answer it because you have not asked me, and I am not going to treat it as a question, and if you had put it to me as one there would have been about four things to do about it this morning and I have not done any of them and I am not going to."

"You have just said the whole of it out loud and called it something else."

"I have just told you what shape that sentence is and I have not touched what is in it." Marn Ottery put her hand flat on the boards. "**And I am going to tell you the thing I have been not telling you since the second week of the second month, and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to say it a second time. A person who is early for a reason is a person who has already been used and has not been told. The not being told is the part that cannot be put back.**"

---

"I have not been used."

"You have been early for two years, and I said out loud in the second week of the second month that nobody in this building had done one thing about it, and I was pleased with the shape of that, and I have been wrong about the shape of it for a fortnight and I have been carrying it." Marn Ottery turned round. "**The not doing anything about it was not a courtesy to you. It was the using. There is no form anywhere in this empire for a thing that has been done to a person and not written down, so the not writing was all there was, and you are the only thing in that room anybody could have said a thing to and every week you came in early was a week that went on being true.**"

Two years of being on the floor twenty minutes before anybody else and two years of nothing at all happening to her, and she had filed the whole of it in her own head as somebody being polite, and it had not been, and the not writing it down anywhere had been all there was for two years, and a thing that has not been written down is a thing that does not exist the next morning, and she is the only person alive who knows about it and she has known since a fortnight ago.

---

"You cannot say that to me now and go back to the other end of those boards."

"I am not going to go back to the other end of those boards. I am going to stand here and let you do whatever you are going to do, and I would like it on the floor that I said it in a room with a third person in it, because that is twice now and the clerk has heard it, and there is no form anywhere for a thing said twice in a room with a witness in it."

The clerk of about twenty-nine did not look up. She gave that promise to him once, on a working afternoon, and she has kept it in every room since, and she kept it in this one, and there is no form in which she could have done otherwise.

"So I am a fact on that board now," Tamsin Rook said.

"You are the fourth item on that board and you have been the fourth item on that board for two years, and the only thing that has changed this morning is that I have said out loud which one you are, and I did not do it for you and I did not do it because you asked me." Marn Ottery took her hand off the boards. "**And I would like it noticed that I have done the one thing in this room in eleven years that I told you on the fourth day of the third week of the third month I was not going to do, and I did it at about the fourth hour on an ordinary morning with nobody to thank me for it, and I am not sorry about that either.**"

---

The counter shut at the sixth hour. Nobody had touched the drawer under the board; the four things were where the four things have been, and half of them have never once been looked at. The box under the far end of those boards was where it was, and the dust on the lid was not disturbed, and nothing has ever been left in it.

The clerk of twenty-four took her coat down off its hook and went out into the stair, four flights of it, one hand on the cold rail and nobody on any of the landings. A man from the yard went up and down those stairs twice between the third hour and the sixth with a satchel on his shoulder and asked nothing and was told nothing and does not know what is at the top of them.

**Nobody was thanked, nobody was forgiven, nobody was sent for, nothing was resolved, and the count of things anybody has asked out loud in this matter is the same as it was on the second day of the first week of the second month.** The four items are on the wall and the fourth of them is the one none of the three of them can account for, and the sentence shaped like a question is in that room, and it has not been answered, and nobody was going to answer it, and that is the arrangement, and it is still standing.
