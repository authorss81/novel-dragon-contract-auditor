# Chapter 0442: One Sheet With The Same Mark On It Twice, A Young Man Who Did Not Finish A Sentence, And The Last Week Of A Year

Between the third hour and the sixth that morning he put his mark at the foot of about two hundred of them, and the pen did what it does, and it has done what it does on every working day of his life since he was forty-two years old.

He cannot read a paragraph. He has never once pretended to, and the people who work that floor are not the sort to have stayed long enough to have found out either way, and nobody has ever made it their business to find out.

**A second mark on a sheet is a thing he cannot see and could not find if he wanted to, and there is nobody in that corridor able to point at it, and there is nobody in that building able to point at it either.**

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the twelfth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fourth hour, and it is the last week of that year.

---

The one that was not like the others was square.

Every bundle that has come up that stair for about two years has been squared by the man who brings it, because he squares what he brings, and this one had been squared as well. That was the whole of what was wrong with it, and it took him about the length of a breath to get at the part that was actually wrong.

The part that was actually wrong is that he knew the sheet.

He had signed it the day before. It was not a guess. It was the same sheet with the same creases in it and the same corner gone soft at the bottom left, which is where the corner of about nine hundred of them a year goes soft.

**Nobody told him and nobody could have told him. A man who is paid for a corner brings a bundle up a stair and a man at the other end signs whatever is in front of him, and there is no form anywhere in this empire in which a person is entered as the one who handed him the same thing twice.**

---

He signed it. The hand took about the length of a breath and a half and then he put the pen back in the pot.

His eyes did not lift off the sill at any point of that, and they have not lifted off a sill on any sheet since he was forty-two. He signed about two hundred and about six that morning instead of about two hundred, and nobody in that corridor has ever counted and nobody is going to start, and the hook was empty inside a minute after the seventh hour.

**And there are about nine sheets a year that are not what the person above him wrote, and he knows which and has known since the spring, and he has never once written one of them down and there is nowhere in this empire to write one down. This is a different thing. It is the first one he has had that is not a number, and it is going to stay in him for the rest of that year, and there is no pot on that sill for it.**

---

Then the young man from the shipping floor came up at the fourth hour with the four under his arm, and the two of them have not said a thing to each other in two years and neither of them is going to start in the last week of a year.

He got as far as the sill and then he stopped, and he looked at the bottom of the bundle for about as long as a sheet takes to be squared, and he can see a mark the way anybody can see a mark, which is the whole of what is wrong with what he has just seen.

"You have not finished."

"I have finished."

"**You have got about a hundred and forty left on that window and there is one at the bottom of that bundle with a mark on it that has already been at the top of it.**"

He said it to the sill and not to a man, and it is the sort of thing he is right about, and being right about it is the whole of what he does.

---

"I am not going to say that to you."

"You are not going to say that to me."

"**I am not going to say it because you cannot do one thing about it, and because the moment a man at that sill is told a thing about a sheet he is a man somebody has said a thing to, and nothing in nineteen years has required a thing of you and I am not going to be the first one.**"

"You have not said a thing to me in two years."

"You have not said a thing to me in two years and I have come up this stair about a hundred times in two years and there has not been one of them in it where either of us said a thing to anybody."

---

He did say one more thing. He got as far as it, and it was four words long and he got through two of them, and then he stopped, and the man of about sixty-one did not look up and did not put his pen down and did not do anything at all, and the two words stayed where they were.

"Put them down."

"You have not finished."

"Put them down."

He put them down.

---

The four went down the corridor in his own arms because they were not finished, and the piece of paper in the inside pocket of his coat stayed in the inside pocket of his coat, and he has not taken it out since the morning he filled it in and he is not going to, and the man of about sixty-one did not see him go and was not looking.

**The second mark is on a sheet about four hundred yards of corridor and nine miles of road from any room in this matter, and it is under a hand that cannot read a paragraph, and it is dated a day before the day it was put there, and nothing anywhere in this empire can lay one of those two things beside the other and say which of them is out of time.**

---

Nobody thanked him. Nobody has been forgiven here either. Nobody has been sent for, nothing has been settled, and the two who are in boxes are in the boxes they were in, and the form that put them there is not being asked to be void by anybody.

The year ran out at the end of that week. Nobody in that corridor said one word about it and there is no form anywhere that a year has to be said anything about when it has gone.
