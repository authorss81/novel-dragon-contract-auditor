# Chapter 0439: A Rent Day With Nothing Written Down, A Tin Behind A Boiler Left Where It Is, And A Woman Who Does Not Go Up That Stair

The same ground floor, the same passage, the same stair off it, and the door at the top of that stair is a door she has never put her hand on in about twenty years. The lamp goes on the table by the front door at the sixth hour and it has gone on at the sixth hour every evening for the whole of that time. Nothing else in that room has ever been moved.

**There is a woman in this city who keeps minutes in a room off the old river road, and this is not her, and the two of them have never been under one roof and there is nobody alive who could put them under one.** What gets said in that room has never come down a street to a rented house and she would not know it if it did.

It is the second day of the first week of the twelfth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fifth hour.

---

**It is about eleven weeks since she said it out loud, and in eleven weeks nothing has happened, and that is not a relief and it is the reason she has not been sleeping.**

She said a true thing to an empty room on a rent day at the fifth hour, in the ordinary voice, to nobody: that she had known for nine years what the man she takes rent from was, and had said nothing to anybody for nine years, and chose it, and chose it nine times a year on a rent day with her own two hands.

A true thing said out loud in a house is a thing the house can be shown. She worked that out about a second after she had finished saying it and she has not been able to un-work it since, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for taking a said thing back.

What she said was about nine years long and it fitted into one sentence, and the sentence carries no name and no date and nothing in it that a person not present could have written down afterwards. The room was empty, and a sentence with nothing in it cannot be entered on a form, and she has not found a way round it since and does not intend to.

---

And the nothing that has happened is this, and it is the whole of the cost.

He came down at the fifth hour on the same day of every week since and put his hand out and she put the rent into it and he went back up the stair, and he has not said one word to her that he did not have to say, and he has not asked her one thing, and he has not been different at her in any way at all, and he is not going to be, and she knows he is not going to be.

Twenty years is how long one day of the week has been a day of the week, and it has never once fallen to either of them to wonder about the other one. A day of the week is the only thing either of them has ever been asked to enter, and it asks nothing of either of them. There was never an arrangement about the door and there is not one now, and the door was never the point of it.

**You are waiting for the other thing. The knock at the front door with a man in it, and somebody from somewhere wanting to know about a woman at the bottom of a stair, and a form with a heading over it. There is not one and there is not going to be, and the reason is not that anybody in that house is kind. The reason is that the arrangement worked for nine years on being unfindable, and a true thing said once in an empty room by a person nobody can be shown anything about is not a thing anybody can be shown either.** That is the part she did not know until eleven weeks ago, and it is the part she has not been sleeping about, and it is the opposite of what she expected and she is not going to say it out loud again to find out whether it is right.

---

**And here is the repair, and she has thought of three of them, and she is not going to do any of them, and the reason is the one that has held for twenty years.**

She could move the tin. That is the first thing and it is the sensible thing and it would take about nine seconds.

She could stop taking the rent and leave the tin where it is and hand the money back, and there is a form for a landlord to be given money back and she has never seen one and would not know where to put it.

She could say it to the next man who comes in after this one, so that whatever she did not tell this one gets told to somebody and the house is not a silence for another twenty years.

"A sensible thing done on purpose is a thing a person can be shown doing, **and everything I have had for twenty years is that nobody in this house can be shown anything, and I said one true thing in a room to nobody and the room heard it and that is the only hole there has ever been in it, and I am not going to make four of them on a boiler.**"

---

She put her hand on the boiler for about the length of a breath without moving anything. The tin is behind it and it is about the size of a loaf and the lid does not shut the way it used to, and there is nine years of a man's rent in that tin in coin and she has never counted it and there is no reason on earth to count it and she is not going to start now.

She did not go round the back of the boiler and nobody went round the back of it this week, and what was behind it stayed behind it and she stayed at the front of it. The lid has never once fitted since it stopped fitting, and nothing has been done about it at all, and a lid which will not go down is not something anybody can be shown to have wanted replaced, and there is nobody she could ask about a lid.

Then she took her hand off, set the lamp back on the table where it lives and left it burning, and there was nothing else on that table and there has not been.

Nobody came in after him on the evening and nobody came in before him on any evening of the week, and nobody has ever used the front door by the table except him. There has never been anything kept about who was let in, and there is nowhere in the house where such a thing could be put, and nobody in it has ever been asked to keep it.

---

The rent went out of a hand into a hand at about the fifth hour and it went in the same way it has gone every week for about twenty years, and nothing was written down anywhere in that house that week or in any week of the twenty years before it, and there is no list and there is no form and there is nobody in that house she could be shown to anybody as.

**She did not go up those stairs. She has never gone up them and she is not going to this week and she is not going to after that, and the door at the top of them is not a thing she has ever been at, and nobody in this city has standing to ask her about it and nobody in this city wants to.**

Nobody thanked her. Nobody sent for her. Nobody forgave her and nothing was resolved, and about four hundred and thirty miles is not walked and a name at the end of a struck line is not in any mouth in this chapter, and the tin is behind that boiler exactly where she has kept it for about nine years and she is not going to move it and she is not going to have anybody thank her for not moving it.
