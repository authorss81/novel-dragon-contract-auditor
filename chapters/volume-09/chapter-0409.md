# Chapter 0409: The Corner Of A Window With No Pane In It, A Woman Who Came Up Those Stairs Once This Week, And A Man Who Was Not Sent Anything

The window in that room is about a foot wide with nothing in the middle of it, and it looks at the back of another house about the same height as itself, and the light that comes through it in the middle of the day is the light off a wall four foot off. **The frame is sound and there is no glass in any of it, and one corner of it is colder than the other three, and it has been colder than the other three for as long as he has had the room.**

About four feet of the wall beside that window has nothing on it, and there is a nail in it about the height of his hand, driven in crooked, and nothing has ever hung on it and nothing is going to this week.

He is thirty-eight, his name is Marek Kest, and there is no office, no money, no roll, no leave and no certification behind him, and none of those five is on its way. **Nothing has been sent to him. Nothing has ever been sent to him, in eight volumes or in the two years he has had that room, and there is nothing on the table in front of him now, and he has not asked anybody for anything, and nobody has asked him anything.**

It is the second day of the first week of the fourth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the seventh hour, and the cold in that room comes off the plaster behind the middle chair rather than off the glass.

---

About two years now there has been a leaf in the inside pocket of the coat he wears, and somebody struck a name out of it in a hand that was not his mother's, and the claim belonging to it went out of the world at the same time and in the same pen. He has wanted it back since he was a boy and has never once had it in a form he could read out loud, and he has not taken the leaf out of the coat in front of anybody since he came into the room. He has had the coat on every day of the two years and nobody has ever asked him what a man keeps in a pocket he does not put his hand into, and the wanting has not got smaller in two years and has not got any larger either.

She came up once that week and she came up with nothing in her hands, which is the first time in two years, and she did not bring the lamp and he had not asked for it.

"I have been at the bottom of those stairs twenty years," she said, "and I have had a man in that room for two of them, and I have never once had anything to carry up to you, and I have spent a fortnight working out why, and I am not going to tell you the working out. I am going to tell you the answer, and then I am going to go back down, and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to come up about it again."

"You have not brought the lamp."

She had spent a fortnight on it at the foot of the stair with nothing to do it in and had come out of the fortnight with an answer and no person to put it to, and the evening she chose to come up on was the one evening when the lamp was not wanted, and she had not arranged either, and a woman who has been at the foot of a stair about twenty years does not stop to arrange anything.

"I have not brought the lamp because you have not wanted it twice this week and I have noticed." She stood in the middle of that floor where the middle chair is not. "**There is no form in this empire for a man to be given a piece of paper.** That is the plainest thing I know and it has taken me a fortnight and it is not about you. It is not that the paper does not exist. It is that being given a thing is being made a party to it, and a party is a person, and a person is the only thing in this business that can be told what a thing is going to be used for."

---

"**Then it is not that they have decided not to send me anything.** He said it in the ordinary voice and he did not raise it, and it is the nearest he has come to putting a thing out loud in two years, and it is not a question and it has no mark on the end of it.

"Because nothing has ever been sent to anybody." She said it flatly and without any weight on it, the way a person says a thing they have measured. "Not to me. Not to anybody in this row, and I have watched this row for twenty years. **A man nobody sends anything to is a man nobody can be shown anything about, and I have known that since about the third year of my standing at that door, and I have never had anybody to say it to, and you are the first.**"

She had watched the row for twenty years from a ground floor with a door in it, and had seen what went in at one end of it and what came out at the other, and had never once seen a piece of paper in this empire address itself to anybody. Not to the shopkeepers and not to the ground floors and not to the rooms above them, and she has not had one either in about twenty years, and she has never once mentioned it to anybody, and she is not going to mention it now.

"You did not know me."

"I have known that about a house for twenty years. I have never known a man." She did not sit down and she did not touch the table. "**And that is the whole of what you are, and you knew it in about four minutes in a bay in a year that is gone, and I have said it out loud in a room tonight, and you cannot get it back.** I did it in this room and not in a doorway, and I want that entered, because a doorway is where I say things I have decided to say and a room is where I say things I have not."

---

He did not ask her anything. He has not asked her anything in two years and he was not going to start at the seventh hour on the second day of a week, and there was no form in that room to put it on and there was no paper on that table to put it on and there was no pen.

He let it lie where she had put it and she offered nothing further, and between the two of them there was a stretch of about a minute in which neither of them said a word, and it is the longest either of them has gone in the house without a sentence in it, and neither of them has ever been able to work out afterwards who filled it.

He got up and moved the chair.

Not much, and not for any reason he would have given, and not out of the way of anything. He took the chair that has been standing in the middle of that floor since before he had the room and set it down under the corner of the window with no pane in it, where the cold is worst, and where a man sitting down at about the seventh hour would find the cold coming on him from the side.

The chair came off the middle of the floor after two years and it took him about as long as it takes to fold a coat, and there was nobody on the floor to see a man do it and nobody on the ground floor who would have been able to tell whether he had. The window has no pane in any of it and has not had one at any time in the whole of the two years, and nobody has been up here to look at it, and no form anywhere in this empire will give a man a new pane, and he has not wanted one, and the cold is worse where he has just put himself and that is not a mistake.

Then he sat down in it, which he has not done, and the four feet of wall beside that window was bare behind him and the nail in it was about the height of his hand and he did not look at it.

Sitting in it put him in the corner where the frame is worst, and he stayed in it until she had gone and for a while after she had gone, and the room got no warmer while he was in it and nothing in it was lit. The nail was not looking at anything, and had not been for the length of the two years.

---

**She went back down and nothing came up after her, because nothing is sent to her either, and that is the arrangement and it is the reason she can say things on that floor.** He was not thanked. He was not sent for. Nobody came to that room in the second half of that evening, and there was no paper anywhere in it, and a name is not a thing a person can be given either, and the four feet of wall is still bare, and the nail is still in it, and it has been in it longer than he has, and nobody has been asked about it by anybody.
