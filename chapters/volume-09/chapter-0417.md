# Chapter 0417: Four Feet Of Bare Wall, A Woman Up Those Stairs Again, And The Price Of Him Said Out Loud By Somebody Else

The room is rented by the week and it is the same room it was a week ago, with the table in it and the stool and the bed against one wall and a chair standing against none, and about four feet of bare wall beside the window that looks at the back of another house. **There is a nail in that wall about the height of his hand and it is a common nail and it is driven in a little crooked, and nothing has ever hung on it and nothing is going to this week.** There is no fire in that room and there is not going to be one, and the cold comes off the plaster behind the middle chair rather than off the glass.

He is thirty-eight and his name is Marek Kest, and he is not at a stage and is not going to arrive at one. No office, no fund, no list, no leave, no certification, and none of those five is on its way, and he has not accepted one from anybody in this city in about two years. He holds nothing this morning. That is the ordinary condition and it is not a thing anybody has done to him.

There is a leaf in the inside pocket of that coat. Somebody struck a name out of it, in a hand that is not his mother's, and he has carried it about two years, and what belongs at the end of that line is the one thing he has wanted since before the fire, and he has the claim that would go with it no better than he has the name.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the sixth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fifth hour.

---

She came up those stairs with nothing in her hands, which is the third time in about two years and the second time in five weeks, and she did not knock, and she had not been asked to come up.

**She has been at the bottom of that stair about twenty years and she has never once been above the second one and she has never had his name and he has never had hers, and none of that is going to change this week either.**

"You have not been down them once in two years," she said. "I have not been up them three times, and I have counted, and I am not going to pretend I have not."

"You have counted them."

"I have counted everything in that house for about twenty years because that is the whole of what there is to do at the bottom of a stair." She put her hand flat on the table, which she has done twice before. "**And I am going to say one thing and I am going to say it about you and not about that table, and then I am going to go back down, and nobody thanks me for it, and I do not come up about it again.**"

"All right."

---

"**You are the cheapest party in this matter.** I have had that in my head since the third year of standing at that door and I have never had anybody to say it to, and you are the first person in this city it can be said in front of, and I am saying it out loud and I am not asking you one thing about it."

He did not move.

"**A practice that works by being cheap will find you every time without being told to.** That is the whole of the finding and I have had it about nine years and it has never once been a thing I could use, and I want you to know that I know that, and I am not going to insult you by pretending the two of those are different."

"It is not a thing I can use either."

"I know. That is not why I am saying it and you have not heard the rest of it."

---

"**The bill for being unfindable is not yours and has never been yours.**" She said it flatly, the way she says things she has measured rather than felt. "You did not take it. You did not agree to it. Nobody came to you and said here is a price and here is what you get for it. **And I have been paying a piece of it for about twenty years without knowing what it was for, and so has a woman who made herself askable on purpose and came the whole distance to a rented room one night to say one true thing and has not been back, and neither of us was asked either, and I would like that entered in this room in the order it happened.**"

He put his hand on the inside pocket of that coat and did not take the leaf out, and she saw him do it and did not comment on it, and there is a shelf about four hundred yards off with a question on it and a name at the foot of it, and that question does not end, and nobody has asked the woman who wrote it a second thing about it.

---

"You have just said that in a room," he said, "and it is the third true thing anybody has said to me about the price of me, and I have heard two of them and I have never once repeated either of them back to anybody, and I am not going to repeat this one."

"I did not expect you to."

"You expected me to say something about the twenty years. I have watched you decide twice this week whether to say it and you have decided twice not to, and I am not going to give you a third opening, and you are not going to get one out of me this evening." He took his hand out of the pocket. "**I have not asked you what you have been carrying for nine years. I am not going to. And I want it heard that I have not, because you have just handed me the price of a thing I have not got and that is not a thing I am going to spend tonight.**"

She looked at the bare wall for a while.

"Then it stands between us," she said. "Which is where it was this morning."

"Which is where it was this morning."

---

"And there is a road about four hundred and thirty miles long," she said, "and people keep setting out along it on your account, and I have watched one of them not finish, and I am not going to walk it and I am not going to tell you not to, and nobody is going to walk it on your account." She did not raise her voice. "**It is not going to be walked because of you. That is not a kindness to you and I want it said that way and not any other way. There is a girl at the end of it about four hundred and thirty miles up this river who is not being paid and is not going to ask, and that is what is at the end of that road, and it has been what is at the end of it for two years.**"

"You have never been up that road."

"I have never been up that road and I have known about that girl for about nine years and I have not been asked about her and I am not going to start."

---

She went back down and did not come up again that week.

He sat there until about the ninth hour with the leaf on the table under both his hands and did not write anything down, and there is no pen in that room and no paper on that table, and he did not go and look at anything, and he did not take a stage or a list or an office or a wage from anybody, and nobody sent for him and nobody was sent for.

**The four feet of wall is bare. The nail is in it, a common nail, driven in crooked, and nothing has ever hung on it and nothing is going to hang on it this week. He is not thanked, and he holds nothing at the end of that evening that he did not hold at the start of it. The cheapest party in this matter has now been told what he costs by a person who took a rent for twenty years with no name at the foot of his own stair, and the two of them have a thing between them with no form on it, which is the only kind either of them has ever had.**
