# Chapter 0408: The Lock End Of Four Hundred Yards Of Cold Flags, A Rack That Comes Up Light, And The Going Back Up That Nobody Has Ever Asked About

Four hundred yards of cold flags run off a flood terrace at the back of that building. There is a lock at this end of them. At the other end there is a room with a rail standing about four feet off the floor, and the rail is not on the work of that passage and has never been, and it has had nothing on it since before she came. She has been at this end four years. **No person in this building has ever put her a question, summoned her, said thank you to her, or handed her a sheet to take anywhere, and she has not once been counted among the three clerks whose offices are up the stairs.**

What she has instead is a figure she keeps in her head, about how many people are sitting inside a thing nobody is able to be asked about. It is on no paper of any kind and it is not going on one and it is not going to anybody, and it has gone up, and it does not get a heading.

The man in the felt apron owns the whole length of that stone. Four times up, four times down, and on every one of those eight walks there is a single sheet riding on the rack, and it has been a single sheet for longer than either of them has stood here.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the third month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fourth hour, and nothing with a heading on it has come down that passage in a week.

---

He came down with the rack at the fourth hour and there was a woman at the lock, and he did not stop, because in four years not one thing has come up that stone that a person at this end would have had any reason to stop for.

"You are back on it."

"I am back on it."

"You were not on it three weeks ago for three journeys out of four and you have not said why, and I have not wanted it." He went on up with the rack on his hip. "**I am going to set out the arrangement once, and after today I go up and down that stone four times a day until the weather turns and I do not raise it again, and I want the order of it exact, because the first half of what I am going to say is yours.**"

---

"You are the only person at this end. That has been the arrangement for four years and it is going to be the arrangement next week. I go up and I come down and there is one sheet, and I have never wanted that number off the rack and I have never once gone up that passage looking for it, and I am not going to start, and the not starting is not a decision I made this week. It is the trade." The stone turned under the rack. "**A sheet came down that passage once in four years. It had a heading printed over a space on it. It went back up again with the space clean.**"

"I know which sheet."

"You know which sheet. That is a fact and I have had it for four years and you have never asked me one word about it and I have never asked you one word about what you did with it, and I have been up and down that stone four times a day the whole time." He stopped with the rack on his hip, which he has not done in four years. "**It came down and it went back up. Nobody has ever asked you about the going back up.** Not the clerk, not the man at the top of the stairs, not anybody else in that building, not me. And nobody has ever asked you about the coming down either, and the coming down is an ordinary afternoon and everybody in that building has a heading on nearly everything that moves in it. **The going back up is the half that has a person in it, and nobody has asked you about it, and there is no form in which they could.**"

---

"I was not going to ask you."

"I know you were not, and I want that said, because I have spent four years being a man who is not asked things and I have just told you the one true thing I have, and a man who has told a person a thing and been not asked about it afterwards is a man who has put something on that person and cannot take it off again." He went on up the stone. "**And I am telling you the rest of it once. A blank is the only paper in this empire with nothing printed over it, and there is no rule on this passage about one, and the reason there is no rule is that no rule was ever needed. If a sheet with no heading on it ever comes down here, I would not know what to do with it, and neither would you, and neither of us would be able to say so to anybody, and that is the only thing I have to say to anybody in a year.**"

---

She did not ask him where it went back up to. She had not intended to and she did not now, and the not asking was in the room on both sides of that passage, and it was the loudest thing in it.

Then she stood at the lock for all four of the journeys that day, which she has not done since the spring, and there was nothing on the rack on any of them, and the number on the rack was one going up and one coming down, and the stone at the top end of those flags was as cold at the seventh hour on the fourth day of that week as it had been on the first.

**A week in which nothing arrives is not a week in which anything has been withheld.** Those two are so close together that a person standing at a lock can lose a month in between them, and she lost most of one standing in the middle of the stone, and there was nobody at that end to notice that she had lost it and nobody at the far end of the same four hundred yards who would have been allowed to ask.

He did not carry anything past her. He did not stop. The rail at the other end had nothing on it, and it is not hers, and nobody has been in that room this week who had any business there.
