# Chapter 0433: The Same Four Lines For The Ninth Time, A Woman Of Fifty-One Four Hundred Yards Off, And A Book That Takes Nothing In

He is Rennick Adley and he is thirty-eight and he keeps the books of a company that has about nine hundred rooms in it, and the room he keeps them in is a room with a high window and a bench along one wall and a stove that is lit in the sixth month and not in the seventh.

He has been in that room about nine years and he was called to it off a list of clerks, and there is no longer such a list and there has not been one for about six years, and nobody in that room has ever said the word to him and he has not said it either.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the tenth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fourth hour.

---

He wrote four lines out on the ninth of those mornings and they came out the same as the eight before them, in the same words, in the same order, in the same length, and he did not improve them and he did not ruin them and he is not going to.

The four lines go into a book that about nine hundred rooms come and go through over a year, and the book has a name on the cover of it and a date in the corner of every page, and there is nowhere on any of those pages for a thing that is not a sum.

The week before this one was entered the way the ones before it were entered. He had not added a column and had not taken one away, and no heading has ever been printed over a space in it which was not printed over the week before, and rooms come and go through a book of this kind in a year without any of them asking for anything else or being given anything else.

**He has wanted the rest of a sentence for about two years.** A woman of fifty-one began it in a doorway in a week that is gone and got about nine words into it and stopped, and the nine words are on this page in his own hand, and the rest of it has not arrived in about two years and he has not asked and is not going to.

---

He read the nine words again at the fourth hour on the ninth morning and he read them the way he reads the four lines, which is the way a man reads a thing he has decided to go on being able to read.

Nobody in that building knows he has them. There is no list and there is no place in the book for them and he has not asked the woman for the rest of it and he has not written to her and there is no form in this empire in which a book-keeper may put down that he would like a sentence finished.

The clerk who brings that week's returns up to him is about twenty-two and she has been doing it about three years and she has never once been in this room for longer than it takes to put a bundle down.

She put the bundle on the end of the bench and squared it, and she waited about nine seconds, which is longer than she has ever waited.

"That is not a thing anybody is owed," he said, and he said it to her and not to the bundle, and it is the fourth time he has said it and it has not got him anywhere in about two years.

"You have not asked her for the rest of it."

"I have not asked her for the rest of it."

"Then it is not a thing anybody is owed, and I have known that for about three years and you have known it for two, and neither of us is going to be the one who says the other part of it."

She waited about as long as she had waited and then she stopped, and she goes the way she goes, which is down the stairs and out, and nobody else in the building has any part in what she carries up it every week. No thank you was said and none of it was waited for, and neither of them treated the other as though the other had done anything unusual, because neither of them had.

---

**And here is what she is not going to carry for him, and it is the reason he has never asked her for it either.** She has carried a bundle up that bench about three hundred times and she has carried a note and a form and once a coat, and every one of those was going somewhere that had a person at the end of it who could be shown it. Nine words of a sentence that a foreman of fifty-one began in a doorway and never finished are going nowhere. **There is no column for them and there is no heading over them and there is no page of this book they could be written on, and a thing with no place to go is not a lighter thing to carry, it is a heavier one, and she has said that out loud in this room and he did not ask her to.**

"You are not going to write them down for me."

"There is nowhere in that book to write them and you know that better than I do, and I am not going to keep them in my head for a man of nine years who has a page in front of him with four headings over it."

---

**And here is the whole of the cost, and it is in this room and he is not in it, and it has a cost and not a name, and he has never been in a room with the man it belongs to and is never going to be.**

There is a man in this city who has nothing behind him at all. No office, no fund, no list, no leave, no wage owed to anybody, and no company that could be shown a demand against him, and that is not a hardship and it is not a virtue, it is a price, and the price is that a person in that condition can be handed a piece of paper and there is nobody to hand it to.

**He is the only person a book-keeper like this knows of who could be given a thing and not be shown holding it, and he has wanted that for about two years, and he has not found him, and there is nothing to send a letter to and no name to send it to, and not finding him has cost this man the only thing he has wanted out of nine years of a book that takes nothing in.**

---

He put his pen down about a quarter of an hour before the six and looked at the four lines he had just written, and they were the same four lines.

He did not pick the pen up again before she left, and what he had written stayed on the page where he had put it and was not read a second time by anybody. The bundle was still on the bench where she had squared it, and nothing in the room had been moved while the two of them were saying what they said.

"They are the same four lines," he said.

"They are the same four lines every week, and I have been bringing them up here for about three years, and I am not going to be the one who says that out loud twice."

"You have said it out loud once."

"Once is once and I am counting it."

The bundle went down the stairs in her own arms at the hour it goes, and the bench stood empty behind her afterwards, and no two of the four lines had been moved since he wrote them. Nobody came back up to say anything about them and nobody has since, and the writing stays on the page for whoever comes in on the next working morning, which is a sentence about a book and not about anybody.

---

**The four hundred yards up that lane was not walked that week. It was not walked on his account and it was not walked on hers, and a blank is not carried up it, and the reason it is not carried up it is not a rule and there is nobody who made one. A carrier who can be found has stopped being cheap, and a thing that arrives up four hundred yards arrives in a hand somebody can name, and there is no form anywhere for what would be done with a hand after that.**

The two of seventeen at the back of a shed at the end of that lane are not in this chapter and are not named anywhere in this matter. The form on the board by the door of that shed is not in this book and is not in any book and nobody in that lane has standing to ask it to be void and nobody has.

Nobody thanked him. Nobody was sent for. Nobody was forgiven and nothing was resolved and the bill at Lowcross is unpaid and nobody is liable for it and nobody in that room has ever made it the reason for anything.

---

The four lines went in the book in the ninth place out of nine and the book was shut and put back on the shelf at the sixth hour, and the nine words are still in his own hand on a page that has no column for them and no heading over them, and he is going to want them next week and the week after that, and he is not going to ask.
