# Chapter 0431: A Sheet Off The Pallet With Nothing Printed On It, Nine Words Said To A Wall, And A Pen Put Back In The Pot

He signed about two hundred of them between the third hour and the sixth and the hand did the same thing on every one, and it is the same hand it was on the first of them that morning and it is the same hand it has been since he was forty-two.

He cannot read a paragraph. He has never once pretended to, and there is nobody in that building who has been in there long enough to have found out one way or the other, and nobody has arranged that either.

It is the second day of the first week of the tenth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fourth hour.

---

The one that came up about half way through that morning was not square with the others.

The others have all been square for two years because the man who brings them squares them against the sill before he lets go of them, and this one was not square, and it was not square the way a thing is not square when it has been carried in a coat and not in a hand.

**There was nothing printed on the top of it.** He could not have told a man what was or was not on the top of it if a man had stood in that corridor and pointed at it, and he can see that a sheet is not the same width as the others, and he can see that this one was not folded.

---

It had about nine lines of writing on it in somebody else's hand, and it was filled the way a man fills a thing when nobody is standing near him, which is close to the bottom and small, and there was no space left at the foot of it for anything.

That is the whole of what he knows about it and it is everything.

The space at the foot of a sheet is about so long and it is on about nine hundred of them a year and it is on every single one of them whatever else is on the sheet, and the man above him has never once put anything there that wanted doing, and this one wanted nothing at all.

No name. No mark. No reading. No paragraph. No day. Nothing to fill in.

---

He signed it where it lay, the way he signed the other about two hundred that morning, and the hand took about the length of a breath and a half, and then he put the pen back in the pot.

He did not look up while he did it. He has not looked up on one of them since he was forty-two and there is nothing on the page to say he ever did.

---

The man of about twenty-six was at the top of the steps with the empty corner of the bunch in his two hands and he had not gone down yet.

"Nobody filled that one in that room."

"It came off the pallet the same as the others."

"It came off the pallet the same as the others and there is a person's handwriting on it and it is about the width of the boards." He did not put the empty corner down. "**I did not carry that one up. I have carried about four a week up these stairs for two years and I have never once had a thing in my hands that I could not tell the day of.**"

He was right about the day. He was wrong about the room and he was not going to be told he was wrong, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for a man to be entered as the one who was told he was wrong.

---

Then he said it, and he said it to the wall above the sill and not to a man, and it was nine words and none of them was about paper.

"**More of them. That is what I am saying.**"

Then he said the other part, and this one was longer and he said it faster, and the man of about sixty-one did not turn round for either.

"**I have signed nothing in two years that wanted nothing off me and I do not know what happens to a man when one does.**"

---

"**You are not going to tell me what is in it, and I am not going to ask you, because there is no form in this building that I could fill in with the answer.**"

"I have not read it and I cannot read it and you knew that when you put it on that sill."

"I knew it." The man of about twenty-six shifted the empty corner against his hip. "**And I am paid for a corner and not for a page, and I have said that in this corridor before and I am not going to say it again this month, and I am not going to be thanked for saying it either, because there is nobody in this building who could.**"

"Nobody in this building has thanked me for anything since the year I turned forty-two."

"I am not going to start at sixty-one."

---

**And the man of about sixty-one could not have told him whether he had said a true thing, and neither of them found out, and that is not a thing either of them did and is the whole of it.**

A man who carries sheets up a stair for money cannot be shown what he is carrying. That is what he is worth and it is what he is paid for and it is the only reason anybody lets him stand at that sill. He had just said out loud, in a corridor nine feet long, that he did not know what happens to a man when a sheet wants nothing off him, and he had said it in front of the one man in this matter whose hand goes on every one of them, and he had got nothing for it and would get nothing for it and had not wanted anything for it.

---

Then, about the fifth hour, the man of about sixty-one put his palm flat on the sill and took it off again, and he said one thing to the pot, and it is the fourth thing he has ever said to that pot and the first three were his own and none of the three has been answered by anybody.

"Nineteen years, and I never once knew a thing did not want something. **Today one did not, and I found out it is the only work I have ever done that is not asking me for anything.**"

The reason is in it and the first three do not carry this one, because the first three were about what a thing wanted and this one is about what a man does with a morning where nothing does.

---

Nobody said anything else about it for the rest of that day and nobody said anything about it on any other day of that week.

**Nothing was put in a book that week. There are two numbers that man of about sixty-one has carried for years and not one of them has been written down, confirmed, denied or asked for by anybody, and they are not in this chapter and he has not been asked about either of them. The nine miles is walked zero times.**

About four men on the far side of that town cannot get the sense of a paragraph and none of them has ever been asked one thing by anybody. The man of about fifty-five with the chain is nine miles off in a shed and can read a paragraph and has never been one of those four.

---

The basket went down at the seventh hour and it did not come back up, and that sheet went down in it with about two hundred others, and the hook was empty inside a minute.

**Nobody asked him what was in it, and he was never going to be told it and had not the smallest idea how to have gone after it, and that sheet is now in a basket in a building nine miles off with about two hundred ordinary ones and the one that was not like the others has gone into the same place as all of them, and there is nothing anywhere that will ever bring it back out and be able to say which it was.**
