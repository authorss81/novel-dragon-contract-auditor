# Chapter 341: The Bay At The Top Of That Lane Off The Slade, A Gate Shut At About The Fourth Hour, A Bench Longer Than The Bay Is Wide, And One Man Going Up To A Gate Nobody Asked Him About

The bay at the top of the lane off the Slade is about nine foot by eleven and there is a bench in it that is longer than the bay is wide, and the lamp above the middle of that bench is not lit until about the seventh hour and was not lit at the fourth hour either.

She is fifty-one, and her name is Halla Wray, and the foreman's job on that cut came to her because nine families agreed between themselves, in the open, that it would be hers, and that was nine years ago. It is not an office. It is not on a list of offices anywhere and nothing was ever drawn for it.

This is the fourth day of the third week of the tenth month of the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour, and the gate was shut at about the fourth hour by a man of about forty who does not argue.

---

**Nobody asked her about the box this morning and nobody is going to.** She has been inside it since the first week of the seventh month of the year after, and a class put her there, and about nine men in that bay know it, and not one of them has said a word about it since the day it went in, and that is not a courtesy and is not going to be lifted.

The man who keeps the books of the Company four hundred yards up that same lane did not come up it. Nobody brought a word from him. He has wanted the second half of a sentence of hers since the third month of the year after, and the day he asks is the day she stops saying the half she has got, and he has not asked.

The bench runs the length of the room and past it, about nine men sit along it, and it has never been shortened. She has watched it being sat on every working morning for four years and has never once sat down on it herself.

---

The chain man got up off the bench at about the fifth hour.

He is about fifty-five and the chain is on him and it has been on him for years, and he is not one of the four men in that bay who cannot make sense of a paragraph, and he says so himself. **He said it out loud once, about four years ago, and it was not a speech and it was not for anybody**, and he wanted it said that he knew what he was doing.

**He is the only person on that bank that findability does not work on.** He has known that for about four years and he has never once used it.

This morning he went down the lane to the gate.

He came back and sat down, the chain went over the side and hung there, and nobody in the bay said one word about it. She has gone over the walk to the gate and back in her own head four times since and got nowhere with it.

---

She did not ask him where he was going.

That is the whole of it. It took him about four minutes to walk down there and about as long to come back up, and she did not ask him, and he did not tell her, and nobody in that bay looked up off the bench. **There is no form for counting a man out of a room** and none for counting him back into it, and about four men have had the use of that absence for about four years, and this morning is the first time one of them has spent anything on it.

He came and sat down in the place on the bench he had been sitting in, and the chain went over the edge of it and hung there, and he said nothing to anybody about where he had been or what he had done standing at a shut gate, and she said nothing to him about either of those.

**There is no form for owing a man an account of a thing he did on his own feet.** Wanting to know is not asking and is not refused and is not anything at all in this empire, and a foreman of fifty-one who wants to know why a man of fifty-five went out of a bay at about the fifth hour is a woman standing in a doorway with her hands empty and no place to put the wanting.

---

At the back of that bay the two of seventeen are where they have been for weeks, on a written engagement with terms on it and a rate at the foot of it, and one of the four lines of it is the name of the person who set it, and that person has never been in that lane.

**Neither of them was spoken to this morning, and neither of them was thanked, and neither of them was sent for, and neither of them is going to start.** The reader is paid forty-five pence a day and four days a week, and what a day of that comes to is anywhere between fourpence and a shilling according to what gets set on the bench on the day, and that rate exists because a figure was put in a list by somebody who was not in that bay and is not in it now.

The other one is seventeen and nobody in this empire has ever put a word to her about any of it, and there is no paper anywhere that asks a person of seventeen whether she would rather not, and so there is no answer on the record anywhere, and no answer on the record is not the same as no.

Nobody moved them forward and nobody is going to. Four weeks of the two of them standing at the back is four weeks of nothing at all happening, and it is the ordinary condition of a bench, a lamp and a wall, and not anybody's decision.

Nobody moved them to the front of that bay and nobody is going to. The bay has had room at the back for two for weeks, and about nine men are in the front of it, and the two of them are at the back, and that is where they are.

---

The mark in the chalk on the end wall is at the height of the third link of that chain, and the cross means nothing, **and it is nobody's, and it is about four years off being anybody's evidence.**

The chain went back onto the man it is on and the mark stayed on the wall, and **the two of those things are about four feet apart in that bay and neither one of them is going to turn into anything at all.**

She measured a length of wall at about the sixth hour for a man who wanted it measured, and she gave him the figure and he was gone inside a minute, and it is the same answer it has been for about four years and it does not satisfy anybody and nobody has ever stood in that bay and said that it does not.

---

The count of the second gang came up the lane on a slip of paper with a foreman's mark at the top of it and no heading over it, and it is a count of men and hours and not a count of anybody, and the number in it was entered in a hand that does not vary by a man who is not in that lane and has never been in it.

A slip came up the lane with a foreman's mark on it and nothing set over the mark, and a man who has never set foot in the lane wrote a number on it in a hand with no waver in it at all. Rooms get counted on in a morning out of slips like it, and the number of them in a year is about nine hundred.

Putting a man into a class is a thing a person does on paper and no paper in this empire lets him be taken out of it again, and that is the reason about nine hundred rooms a year can be counted on in a morning and not one person standing in any one of them can, and the two of those are one arrangement and have been since before the man with the chain came to that bank.

"Is that chain still on him?" she said it out loud at about the sixth hour, to the bench, about the chain, and the chain was on him, and nothing in this empire lets a chain be said to a person, and a man who is findable is a man who can be served, and being served is a party, and a party is the only person a thing can be told what it is going to be used for.

---

The mark in the chalk is a mark. The gate is shut and will be opened at about the seventh hour by somebody who was not asked to open it and will not be thanked for opening it. The lamp is not lit and will be lit by whoever is still in the lane.

Nobody thanked her and nobody thanked him, and nothing was resolved, and nothing was said out loud in that bay this morning that has not been said out loud in it before, **and the count of things anybody has asked out loud in this matter is the same as it was in the morning and it is going to be the same in ten weeks.**
