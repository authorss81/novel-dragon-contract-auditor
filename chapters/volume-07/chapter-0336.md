# Chapter 336: The Ground-Floor Room Of A Building Off The Old River Road, Eleven Books On A Shelf Under A Window With The Pane Still Out Of It, And The Third Of Three Absences Said Out Loud To A Page

She has had that room to herself for nineteen years and there is a shelf in it, a table, a chair, and a window about two feet wide whose pane has been gone for about four years and has not been put back by anybody.

About four thousand entries are in the eleven books on that shelf and there is not one name in any one of them, which is not carelessness. It is the only shape a minute book has got, and she has got very good at it, and being good at it is the whole of what she is paid for.

This is the second day of the second week of the ninth month of the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fifth hour, and the season is over or going.

---

Under that window, on the shelf, there is the finding of the last ten weeks, and it is still in the form of a question and it is not a sentence.

She wrote it on her own motion, at nobody's request, with the day and the hour on it and her own name at the foot of it. **It is the only thing on that shelf with a name at the foot, and the name is hers.**

She has not gone near it since the middle of the last month. It has not become an answer and it is not going to become one, and a quarter of it was said out loud in this room in the second month by a man who came nine miles on his own two feet, and she entered none of that either.

The question carries a day and an hour and her own name at the foot, and nobody has asked her a second time about it and nothing in this empire is shaped so that a second time could be put to her. **It is the only thing she has ever put her name to in nineteen years.**

Putting a name to it is the whole of what she did and it has not been one inch further since, and she has decided that this is how it is going to stay, and she has not told anybody that she has decided it.

---

Then she said the cost out loud, to the page, at about the fifth hour, because she has found out that nobody will say it for her and nobody can.

"**A man who has said a thing twice is findable by the saying of it.** That is not my finding and I did not arrive at it. He arrived at it himself, about nine miles, in a season, on a day he is not paid for, and he has now said the whole of it out loud twice in nineteen years, in this room on two of his three visits, and not on the first."

"About nine people were in that room the second time. I wrote none of it down and I said out loud, standing at my own table, that writing it down would make it an order. **A minute with an answer in it is an order, and I have not entered one in nineteen years.** Nobody in this building would have stopped me."

"That is the whole of what it cost. Nobody paid anything. Not me, and not the eight others who were in that room with him, and not the woman of about forty who carries a scuttle and is not one of the three clerks and is not in this."

---

**Nothing in this empire makes findability into a thing a man can refuse**, and that is what the saying of it twice bought, and what it bought was not the sheet going back this time. The sheet went in and was entered correctly in a hand that does not vary, and the man took it back and gave it to her and it was the nine hundred and something of them and it is correct.

He has not been told that there is no form. He has not been told anything.

**He knows what he did, and it is findable, and no paper lets a man write that he would rather not be.**

---

Then she said the third of the three, and she said it slowly, and it is the last time in this volume that any of the three gets said out loud in a room.

"**A man of about thirty-four was in this room with him.** He came on his own two feet on a day he is not paid for, and he has no business in this building, and nobody sent him, and there was nothing here for him."

"He is not coming back. Nobody is sending for him and nobody will send for him, and I am not told that he is not coming back, and **there is no form under which a clerk of an office can be told that a person is not coming.**"

"I have said the not asking twice in this room, once when I was working out what asking would cost and once when he stood at my table and did not ask. **The not telling is the same absence, and it is the third of the three, and I will say it once and then the three can stand on their own.**"

"I have been here nineteen years. In that time I have advised nobody and asked nothing and entered about four thousand things with no name in any of them, and I am the only person in this matter anybody could serve a question on in writing, and nobody has served one and nobody is paid anything for the standing ready."

---

A carrier came up the passage at about the sixth hour with a rack on her hip and put four on the shelf by the window and went back down it. She is paid ninepence at a lock and she is on a list and nobody has asked her what she carries in about six years, and the woman of fifty-five has not asked her either, and neither of the two has ever asked the other a thing.

The four went in correctly in about four minutes in a hand that does not vary. That is the whole of what was written down this afternoon.

"Is that page dry?" she said it out loud at about the sixth hour, to the room, about the page, and the page was dry, and nothing in this empire lets a clerk be asked what she is going to do with a question she has not answered and has not withdrawn, and she is not going to answer it and she is not going to withdraw it.

The window is still without its pane. The step at the top of that passage is still worn through, and the rail at the far end of it is still about four feet off the floor.

A man of about sixty-one has stood on the wrong side of that rail three times in nineteen years. He is nine miles off, he does not know it, and he is not going to be told.

Nobody asked her anything. Nobody thanked her and nobody sent for her and nothing was resolved, and about nine hundred sheets a year are still going to go out of that room with a hand at the foot of every one of them.
