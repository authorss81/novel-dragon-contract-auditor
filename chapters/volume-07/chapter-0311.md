# Chapter 311: The Fourth Counter Of A Table In Auremar, Four Flights Of A Stair, And A Copy Somebody Carried Up It

There are four counters in a Table in Auremar and this is the top of them, and the only way up to the top of them is four flights of a stair, and the stair has no door at the foot of it.

Her name is Marn Ottery and she is thirty-four and she has been on this counter eleven years and she is not a lawyer, and behind her on the wall is a board with four things written on it, and not one of them is a time, and about nine people come up that flight in a day.

At the other end of the counter stands a woman of twenty-four who has been there about two years, and a man of about thirty does the copies at the far end, and a woman of about twenty has been in the room a year and is at the back with a shelf of her own and is not in any of this.

There is a received room at the back with four shelves in it, three of them full, and about four hundred bought and unfilled blanks in a box under this counter that has been under this counter for eleven years, **and nothing has ever been written over the top of the box.**

This is the second day of the first week of the third month of the year after the year after the year after next, and the hours at this counter are the second to the sixth, and it is about the fourth hour, and a man of about thirty-four is on the wrong side of the counter with his hand still in his coat.

---

He took a sheet out and put it down on the wood and it was a copy, and he had paid fourpence for it at the far end, and it was folded once along a line somebody had folded it along a long time ago.

"Is this the one you were going to say something about?"

"It is a reason to say it and it is not the thing itself, and I will come to the thing, and I am not asking you anything."

He put his hand back in his coat and left it there.

She said the shape of it first, the way she has said the shape of a thing before she has said the thing about nine hundred times in eleven years and the way she has said it to a person twice.

"I am going to say a thing out loud in this room and it is not in a book and I am not asking you anything, and I would like it noticed that I said the shape of it before I said the thing."

The room went on the way a room goes. The man of thirty at the far end did three copies and a half of a fourth. The woman of twenty-four turned a page over and put it back the way it was.

"There is one form in this empire that has ever been put in front of about nine hundred rooms at once," she said. "It is about two hundred years old. A House wrote it for the households on its own border, and it says that a thing offered to a border household at no charge must be answered, and that the answer must be entered, and that the entering of it must be a form and not a letter."

"That is three things in one sentence."

"It is three things in one sentence and the third of them is the whole of it, and I will come back to the third of them, and I am not going to hurry it, because you have come up four flights for a copy and you can stand there."

---

The first of the three things is that it must be answered, and the second is that the answer must be written down, and **the third of the three is that the writing down must be a form and not a letter.**

A letter can be answered or not answered and nobody is hurt by that. A form has a space in it with a line ruled under the space, and the line is there before anybody has written anything. **A thing with a line ruled under it in advance is a thing this empire knows how to finish.**

"It got an answer in about three weeks," she said. "All at once, and the answer came back in three weeks, and the households refused it, and the refusals are entered because that law says a refusal is entered. **It is the only time in this empire that anything has been answered by that many rooms at once,** and it is also the only time anybody has ever tried."

"And this is a copy of it."

"This is a copy of the fourth one of about four that come down that counter in a year. The other three went to the same three people. **There is no form in this empire that would let a clerk keep a copy of a form about a household,** and I have kept four of them in a drawer with the rest of the paper, and nobody has ever asked me what they are."

---

A woman of about twenty-nine came up the flight at about that hour about a day she had been given for something in the first month, and she said the day had passed and the thing had not been done, and she said out loud that she would like to know which of them had not done it.

She was told at the counter that a day is a day and it goes past and it is not this counter's day to bring back, and the woman went down the stair.

She did not ask who had not done it a second time, and nobody told her, and being told would have been the asking and the asking is the thing this counter does not do.

"I am going to say the rest of it now and it is the part that is actually about you," Marn Ottery said, "and it is about a form and not about a person, and you will find that strange, and it is the only shape the thing has ever had."

"Say it."

"That form is not one of the seven things anybody has asked out loud in this matter, and I want the reason said out loud in a room, **because the reason is the whole of what that form is.**"

"No person was in it. A House put a sheet in front of about nine hundred rooms and every one of those rooms is a household, and **a household is not a person, so there was nobody in any of them for anything to be done to.** I have worked that out standing at this counter and it took about four minutes, and it is the reason it came back in three weeks and it is also the reason nobody in this empire has ever wanted to copy it."

---

"Then what is a form in front of nine hundred rooms," the man of about thirty-four said. "If it is not a person."

"It is a class." She said it flatly, with no weight on it at all, the way you say a word you have looked at a long time. "**A class is the only thing this empire has ever been able to put in front of about nine hundred rooms at once.** A class is a heading, and a heading is a company, and a company is a thing this empire can hold wages against and give letters to and take to law."

"So the one form that worked is the one form that makes about nine hundred rooms into a company, and about two hundred years is a long time for nobody to want that."

"That is the whole of it and it is about nine words long and I have had it since the second month."

---

She said one more thing and she said it to the room and not to him.

"There is no form in this empire that has ever been put in front of about nine hundred rooms and asked a person anything," she said, "and there is no form in this empire that has ever been copied out of one House's border law and used in that many rooms, and **both of those are the same absence,** and I have known it for two years and I have said it out loud twice."

Nobody thanked her for it. Nobody in that room was going to, and there is not one form in this empire that would oblige anybody to, and the man of about thirty-four was not thanked and was not going to be and did not expect to be.

He took his hand out of his coat and put it flat on the copy on the counter and then took it off again.

"The third of the three things," he said. "You have not said what the form is for."

"It is not for anything. It is a shape for putting a thing in so that about nine hundred rooms can be counted answering it." She turned her head about four inches. "**A form is not a question, and a question has a person in it.** That one does not, and that is the reason it works, and it is also the reason there has never been a second of it in this empire."

---

At about the fifth hour a man of about forty came up and wanted three things put back a year and could not have them, and was not told why, and went down the stair.

The fourth thing on the board behind the counter is in a hand that is not one of the hands in this room and has been on that wall longer than she has, and she and two other people have looked at it together on three occasions in eleven years and have not arrived at anything, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for accounting for a line of writing on a wall.

**About four hundred blanks went under that counter in eleven years, and not one of them has ever been accounted for,** and none of them is going to be, and nobody has ever asked her to account for them, and the not asking is the whole of what has happened to them.

The copy went down the four flights with the man who had carried it up them. She did not ask him what he intended to do with it, and she did not ask him why he had come, and he did not tell her either of those things. **The whole of what passed between them in about eleven minutes was fourpence, a sheet of paper, and a thing said out loud in a room,** and it went in a room and not into a book.

Nobody was thanked this morning and nobody was sent and nothing was written down that was not a figure about somebody else's day.
