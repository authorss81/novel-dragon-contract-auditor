# Chapter 349: The Room Over A Chandler's Shop Taken By The Week, Four Steps And A Landing And A Table With Nothing On It, A Window About A Foot Wide Looking At Another House's Back, And A Lamp That Is Not Lit At About The Fifth Hour

The room is taken by the week and it is over a chandler's shop, and the shop keeps tar and rope and lamp oil and the smell of all three of them comes up between the boards all day and is the smell of the whole of the two years he has been in it.

There are four steps and a landing, a table, a chair stood against the wall the bed is against, and a window about a foot wide that looks at the back of another house and has nothing to look at in it but a drainpipe and a wall.

This is the fourth day of the third week of the twelfth month of the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fifth hour, and the lamp in that room is not lit until about the seventh hour and it is not going to be lit now.

---

About four feet of the wall by that window has a nail in it and has had one since he came, and nothing has ever hung on it, and the four steps to his own door are not the four steps at the bottom of which a woman lives and the two sets of steps are about four miles apart.

He put his hand flat on the table. The table has nothing on it. He has not put anything on that table for about a week and there is nothing to put on it and nobody is coming up those steps with anything for it.

He is thirty-four or thereabouts and the hand is a fair one, and what he is paid for is the copying out of other men's figures until the totals agree. Four days a season he stands on somebody's list, and this is not one of the four, and he is not standing on anybody's list this week and did not put himself on one.

---

He is not going to tell her what was said in that room, and she was at the bottom of the four steps at about the sixth hour and did not ask him how it went, and he did not tell her and was not asked and neither of those things is going to trouble either of them tonight.

**There is no form for refusing to be told a thing**, and the other half of that absence is not being entitled to insist on it, and the two are the same absence and it is not a wall between anybody and anybody. The last time he did a thing of that kind he told her six weeks before he did it, out loud, on purpose, without asking her first, and the reason he gave her for that is still the truest thing he has ever said to her. He is not going to give it again and she is not going to ask him for it.

Nobody has ever thanked him. He has never asked to be and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be thanked for saying a thing out loud in a room, or for not saying one, or for walking nine miles and coming back, and there is not going to be one, and the not going to be is not anybody's decision.

---

He has a number in his head and he has had it for about two years and he has never given it away and nobody has ever asked him for it.

It is a small number and it is made up of people and not of sheets, and **it is the number of people who have said a true thing out loud in a room with nobody in authority in it and have not been thanked for it**, and he knows roughly how many of them there are because he has been in about nine rooms in about two years and he has been one of them.

He cannot give it away and he has not given it away, and the reason is not modesty and is not a rule anybody made. **The giving of it would be a list, and a list of persons is a company, and a company can hold wages against and take to law, and the whole of what six years of this has been about is those two things.** A number in a head is a person, and a number on a page is a column, and a column wants a heading.

He will not give it to his wife. He cannot ask her first whether she would mind being told it, and he has never been able to, and that is why he will not give it to her at all. **There is no form for waiting on anybody to come and ask a man for a number he is carrying**, and he has been carrying it for about two years and nobody has come up those four steps about it, and there is no list of the people who might, and the people who might are about nine hundred rooms and every one of the nine hundred is a person.

A man of about thirty-four who stands at the top of four steps every working day and cannot be reached by anybody is not a man anybody is looking for, and that is the price of being one man and not a heading, and he worked it out in about four minutes in a room with nothing on the table about two years ago and told nobody.

---

"Is that window open?" he said it out loud at about the fifth hour, to the window, about the window, and it is not open and it has not been open since before he came, and nothing in this empire lets a window be said to a person, and a window about a foot wide looking at another house's back is the whole of what there is to look at and there is nothing in it that has ever been anybody's.

---

The lamp was lit at about the seventh hour by somebody else. The table had nothing on it and the nail had nothing on it and the chair is still against the wall the bed is against, and nothing was resolved, and nobody was sent for, and nothing was forgiven, **and the count of things anybody has asked out loud in this matter is the same as it was at the fifth hour and it is going to be the same in ten weeks.**
