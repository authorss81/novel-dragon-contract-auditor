# Chapter 327: The Counting Room At The Top Of The Same Lane, A Shutter Half Up Since Before The Frost, And A Piece Of Waste Paper At The Bottom Of A Box

The counting room is at the top of a lane off the Slade and the bay is at the bottom of it, and a man who works at one end of that lane can see the roof of the other end of it on a day with any light in it at all.

There is a bench along one wall and a shutter half up on this side, and it has been half up since before the frost, and about nine men go in and out of that lane in a day.

Four shillings a year is the whole of the Company's money and every one of them is the rent of the store, taken out four times a year at a shilling a quarter, and **there is no money in this building behind that.**

His name is Rennick Adley and he is thirty-eight and he is a book-keeper, and he has kept the Company's books since the second month of next year, and that is not a figure he computes from and nobody has asked him to.

This is the second day of the first week of the seventh month of the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the afternoon, and the shutter on this side is half up and the light in the bay is not on.

---

At the bottom of a box, under a rule that is not the Company's, there is a piece of waste paper.

About nine minutes of writing went into it and the rest of his life has gone into keeping it where it is.

He wrote the whole of it out in a fair hand about four months late, in about nine minutes, after he had had the right of it in his head for about two years. It is the finding, and it is this: **a column with a heading over it is a machine, and a column with no heading over it is a person, and a person is only ever asked one way, out loud in a room.**

He has read it twice and he has not torn it up and he has not shown it to anybody and he is not going to. There is no form in this empire in which a man who has written the right of a matter down on a piece of waste paper can have it received by anybody, and the paper is not the difficulty, and the showing is not the difficulty, and being shown a thing by a man with no office is a thing that two men then owe each other.

---

What the last ten days gave him is not the finding. He had that in the second month, and the paper is the correction of it, and the correction is four months old.

**What the last ten days gave him is that the one man who could answer any of it is about nine miles off a room he has no way into, and that there is no form in this empire for asking a man in a room to wait.**

He has had that in his head since the middle of the fifth month and it has not moved since, and there is no instrument anywhere for it, and asking a man to wait is asking a man to stay findable, and a man who waits is a man who can be served.

A man owed a sentence cannot go after one without turning into a document, and he arrived there in about four minutes and has never once mentioned it to anybody including her, and the not mentioning has been going on about as long as the wanting.

He has not written that on the paper and he is not going to, and the paper is the only page in this building with anything on it that is his own opinion.

---

**Who signs for the fourth one?** He said that out loud to the shutter, at about the fourth hour. The fourth one is the fourth of four lines taken off a fee board about four hundred yards away, and it is in a hand that is not one of the hands in either of the two rooms, and it has been on that board longer than he has had anything to do with it.

There is no form in this empire for accounting for a line of writing on a wall and there has never been one, and he and two other people have looked at it together on three occasions and have not arrived at anything, and he is not going to arrive at anything this afternoon either.

---

The money for nineteen days of a crew is in a weigh-house ledger about half a mile off and it is called *held*, and somebody chose that word in about four minutes out of three others and was not thinking about us.

The sum is three pounds and eleven and threepence, and it is nineteen days out of seventeen shillings and a penny the week, and it is paid in full, and nobody is going to be told what it is held against, and nothing in this empire will take nineteen days of a wage into a book as owed by anybody.

His own wages are held against a roof he put there on purpose, and that one he can account for, and it is the only one on the four hundred yards of that lane he can account for, **and the two of them are not the same thing and he has never once put them in one column.**

---

He wrote the day's figures out at the fifth hour and the totals came even, which is the whole of the work he is paid for and none of the rest of it, and the shutter stayed half up where it has been since before the frost, and the light on the flags came and went with the weather and asked nothing of anybody.

A column in the second of the Company's papers is ruled down both sides like any other and it has nothing in it. Not one name, not one figure, not one day. He ruled it himself in the second month of next year because a name in a column is a thing a person can be served at.

He has never put a heading over it and he is not going to, and **that is the whole of what he has: a column, a piece of waste paper, and a finding nobody has ever been asked to account for.**

A person is the one thing a class cannot be asked about, and that is written on the waste paper in his own hand, and nobody has ever asked him for it, and the not asking is the whole of what has happened to it, and the not asking is the same arrangement that is nine miles off keeping about nine hundred sheets a year correct.

He shut the second of the Company's papers at about the fifth hour and wrote out the day's figures so that they came out even, which is the ordinary part of a day and the whole of what he is paid for.

He ruled the column himself and has left the top of it bare, and about four years of nobody asking him why is everything that has happened to it. Tearing it up has never once occurred to him and letting anybody see it has never once occurred to him either.

Nobody asked him anything this afternoon. Nobody sent for him and nobody thanked him and he did not go down the lane and he did not go into the bay, and a foreman of fifty-one owes him a second sentence and has owed it since the third month of the year after, and he has not asked and is not going to, and the day he asks is the day she stops saying the half she has got.
