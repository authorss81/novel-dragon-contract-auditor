# Chapter 312: The Same Room Over The Chandler's Shop, The Lamp Not Lit, And Three Weeks Set Against Eleven

The same four steps and the same landing, and the same table, and the same chair standing where it has stood since he came, against the wall the bed is against. The window is about a foot wide and it looks at the back of the house across the lane, and about four feet of that wall has nothing on it but a nail.

Nothing is lit in it. The lamp waits for the seventh hour and has waited every week of the two years he has had the room, and there is tar and rope and lamp oil under the boards all day and nobody on that lane has ever said a word about any of it.

He is thirty-four or thereabouts, and what he does for money is write out other men's sums so that they come out even, and there are four days a season he is on somebody's list and this week is not one of the four.

The house with a door on it is four miles off and his wife is at the bottom of the steps of it, and he is on nobody's paper this week and did not put himself on it.

This is the second day of the second week of the third month of the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fifth hour, and the copy is on the table in front of him with the ruled line under the space on it, and there is nothing else on the table at all.

---

The copy had cost the man who gave it to him fourpence and had come out of a drawer in a room four miles off, and the man who gave it to him was about forty-four and had had it about eleven years and had not known what it was for.

He had turned it over on the stair, which he had not meant to do, and on the back of it in a clerk's hand that was not the hand on the front there were three lines.

The copy went into the inside of the book and stayed there for the rest of the walk, and it was still in the inside of the book when he laid it down with the ruled line uppermost, and he did not smooth it out.

**The first line said how long the form had been standing.** The second said what it had got, and it said three weeks. The third said what a House's own table had done with the same question, and it said about eleven, and then it said no.

He read the third line four times and then he read the second one again, and the second one said three weeks.

There is no way of reading the two of them together that makes either of them a lie.

**He got two figures and put them side by side in his own head,** the way he does, and he said them out loud to a room with nobody in it.

"Three weeks, or eleven?"

**The date at the foot of the third line was about a hundred and forty years old.** He put his thumb on it and took it off again and did not go and look at anything, and he has not gone and looked since, and there is no form in this empire that makes a man at a table a person who has been somewhere.

---

He did the arithmetic the way he does arithmetic, which is to say he put three figures side by side and waited.

Three figures side by side is the only arithmetic he trusts, and the third one is not like the other two and never has been, and the not being like them is the whole of what he arrived at before the light went.

About three weeks. About eleven. And a hundred and forty years, which is not a duration of a process but the length of time a thing was lost and nobody put a figure on what losing it cost, because the losing was the answer.

**A person deciding can say no, and this one said no,** and it took about eleven weeks and it cost a hundred and forty years. That is not a slow answer. It is the answer you get when the thing doing the answering is somebody.

**A class cannot be frightened** and cannot be told what a thing is going to be used for and cannot write anything down afterwards, and a class answers in about three weeks because there is nobody in it to be slow.

He wrote none of that on the table, because there is nothing on the table to write it on, and no form anywhere in this empire would let a man write the whole of a thing like that on a sheet of foolscap and have anybody read it back to him. **He is not on anybody's list this week and he did not put himself on it,** and that has been true every week of the two years he has had the room.

---

He went through the rest of it once, which he does about once in four weeks, and it went the way it goes.

He read the second line about four times without setting anything beside it, and every time it said the same, and a figure with nothing to set against it cannot be got out of him by anything he owns.

The two people in their boxes are in them. Nobody has asked either of them anything and he is not going to and there is no form in this empire for putting two people in one room, and the form that put each of them there has never been asked to be void by anybody, because no form in this empire has ever been asked to be void and there is no procedure for it.

About four men in a bay cannot read a paragraph and have never been asked one question about a number in the whole of this. **A girl of seventeen and a reader of seventeen are at the back of that bay on a written engagement,** and neither of them is going to be spoken to or thanked or sent for, and the reason is that nobody has decided anything and nobody is going to decide anything this month.

A bill at Lowcross stands at nineteen pounds three and fourpence and is unpaid and nobody is liable. He did not pay it and he is not the reason it is unpaid.

A guarantee is still standing offered and unanswered with about sixty children under sixteen inside it and no child in it has a name anybody has said out loud.

---

And then the two of them, and they are about four hundred and thirty miles apart, and there is no list of them and no form and no heading and no fund, and nobody in either building knows the other one exists.

He has never walked that road and he is not going to. **There is no form in this empire that would let a man carry a sheet that distance and have somebody in a room answer him for it,** and no form in this empire can keep a man from saying afterwards that he went, and the second of those is the price of the first and it is not a small price and it is the only instrument anybody has ever found for making a person findable.

He has found out that the bill for his being findable is not his and stands on somebody else's account, and he worked that out in about four minutes on a step in another street, and he has told nobody and has not gone and looked.

---

His wife was at the bottom of the four steps at the end of it and she did not ask him how the week had gone, and she has not asked him how a week went in about two years, and she said out loud some weeks ago that she is not going to.

Not one word was said about the sheet lying on the table with its fold in it. He had no asking available and did not try for one, and the evening went by with the copy lying face up and him not looking at it.

He had told her about the second thing he did about six weeks before he did it, out loud, on purpose, without asking her first, and gave her the reason, and the reason was that if he had asked she would have said no or said yes and been frightened and both of those would have been him arranging the room.

He did not ask her this week either. **Nothing in this empire is a form for asking a person whether a thing she is about to be told will be used against her,** and the asking would have made her a party to it, and there was nothing on the table to ask her about and about nine minutes of nothing.

Then the ordinary part of it, and the ordinary part is that the woman of about forty came up the four steps with a scuttle and lit the lamp, as she does twice a week. **She is not in this matter and cannot be asked what she has heard, and she is not going to be,** and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that would oblige anybody to ask her.

---

He put his hand flat on the table at about the seventh hour and the table had the copy on it and the lamp had been lit by somebody else.

About nine hundred rooms a year still get a hand at the foot of what goes into them, and the only instrument that has ever got an answer out of that many rooms at once is a form about a class, and the class is a heading, and the heading is a company.

And about nine hundred rooms a year are going to go on getting a hand at the foot of what goes into them and not one question, and the answer came back in about three weeks the last time anybody tried anything, and nobody has tried since, and nobody went north, and **the lamp is lit and nobody thanked anybody and the seven did not move.**
