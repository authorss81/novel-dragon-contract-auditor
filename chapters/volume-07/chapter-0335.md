# Chapter 335: A Counting Room At The Back Of A Dye End In A Furnace Town, A Coat On A Hook With A Notice In It, And The Two Of Three Women She Cannot Get To

There is a bench along one wall of that room and a window with a broken light in it that has let the same weather in for about four years, and a hook by the door with a coat on it, and the stove behind the bench has not been lit since the summer and gets lit every day of the winter by whoever gets in first.

She is twenty and her name is Nell Kest. Sixteen books are kept in that room and fifteen of them were put there by somebody else, and not one of those fifteen carries an office on the cover of it. The other one is hers by the name at the head of it, and nobody has ever wanted to know what a woman of twenty keeps a book for in a room at the back of a dye end.

This is the second day of the first week of the ninth month of the year after the year after the year after next, and the dye end has been running since about the second hour, and it is about the fourth hour.

---

One woman of the four has been told that she is not paid. That was standing on a step for about nine minutes and the woman has not said one word about it since and has not passed it to anybody, and that is not a promise and it is the best the arrangement has got.

**Three are still to go, and this is the afternoon of the two she cannot get to**, and not the one she told, and the difference is not a matter of liking or not liking anybody.

About three weeks, about nine visits, and the last of them was the shortest. One of the two is at a dye end two doors off and spends the middle of a shift standing in a doorway; the other is a lane away and gets in near the sixth hour.

She has been at both of them in about three weeks, and about nine times in all, and the ninth was the shortest. One works a dye end two doors down and stands at a door in the middle of a shift; the other is at the far end of a lane and comes in at about the sixth hour. Nell Kest has got as far as the doorway of each one and has not said the thing, and the not saying has taken longer each time and is now taking the whole of a lane.

---

**A person who cannot be reached is a person nobody has asked**, and that is not a fault in the person. There has been no asking. That is the whole of it and it took her three weeks and two doorways.

Eleven houses off a dyers' lane keep their numbers in that room. **None of the eleven is a person, and so there is nothing that can be put in front of eleven of them at once and get back an answer anybody is inside.**

A border law of a House got an answer in about three weeks where that House's own table took about eleven and then said no. It got the answer because a border household is not a person either, and the law does not need one, and that is the reason it worked and it has been working for about two hundred years.

Her eleven are not a person. Neither are the four women.

**A number of women is not a woman, and what is available at a number is a class, and a class is a heading, and a heading is a company.** A company can be written to about a wage. It cannot be written to about whether a woman said a thing out loud on a step one evening, and there is no line in it for a person.

That is the whole of what stopped her. She worked it out in about four minutes about three weeks ago and she has been carrying it round the two doorways ever since.

What she has got is not a sentence and not a form, and it runs to four words, and the four of them are that a person is one and a number of persons is not, and she has never once said them in either of the two doorways, and the reason she has not is that a person who said them out loud ends up standing where they are, and there is nowhere to stand there which is not somebody's list.

---

There is a notice in the coat on the hook and it has been in the coat since a foreman off a dye end set it there and told her to write it out and keep it and not send it.

**A notice in a coat says a woman is owed money and there is nothing that can hold one against anybody.** The foreman who brought it is a different man from the ones in this matter, has not been told any of it, and is not going to be. The coat is on the hook and she is not going to send it.

Her own wage has been held since the second week of the third month of the year after, and she cannot say what it is held against, and there is no form for telling a woman what her own money is being kept for, and she has asked nobody about it and is not going to.

She is not going up this river this season. Whether she is ever going to is a thing she has not said out loud to anybody, **and she is the only person who knows that not going this season and not going at all are two different sentences**, and nobody has ever been in a position to establish which it is going to be.

A woman of about thirty offered her money in the third month of the year after next and she refused it, and that was the first time in about three years that she had refused anything from anybody. She gave the reason out loud and the reason was one sentence.

---

"Is that stove out again?" she said it out loud at about the fourth hour, to the room, about the stove, and the stove was out again, and the person who gets in first on a cold morning will light it and not know that anybody said anything about it, and nothing in this empire lets a stove be said to a person.

**There is no form that could reach a woman who has not been asked**, and being reached is not the same as being found, and there is no difference between the two on any paper in this empire.

The dye end had been running since the second hour and by the fifth the whole lane had the smell of it, and she went out into that smell with her sleeves down, and the two doors were on the way to the end of the lane and not past it.

The smell had got into her sleeves by the fifth hour. Both doors stand short of the far end of the lane, and she went out and came back in again without either of them.

She went down the lane at about the fifth hour and did not go to either door. The lane is short and she was at the end of it in about four minutes, and she stood at the end of it, and then she came back.

She got within a few steps of the first one more than once and turned back each time, and the last time was quicker than the first, and about four minutes of the afternoon went on the walk and none of it went on a door.

**About nine hundred entries a year come in under a heading, and about nine of them go out not as the person above them wrote.** The man who knows which nine cannot write one of them down, and he is nine miles off the far end of a river she has never travelled. She worked that out about three weeks ago and it has told her two things: that her own wage is not an unusual shape, and that the shape is not a thing she can take a person to.

She has never been in that building. Nothing in this empire would let anybody in a counting room four hundred and thirty miles up this river write to a clerk in a building off a road nine miles from a different town, and there is not going to be one, and the two of them are kept apart on purpose and she is not the other end of anything.

Nobody thanked her and nobody was sent for and nothing was resolved. Three women she has not told are three women in eleven houses who do not know on this day that there are three of them, and the fourth rule has been in her head for about three weeks and has not been said out loud to anybody yet, and she has been carrying it round the two doorways instead of saying it, and she knows what that is and has not stopped.
