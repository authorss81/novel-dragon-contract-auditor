# Chapter 342: The Counting Room At The Top Of That Same Lane, A Shutter Half Up Since Before The Frost, A Column With Nothing In It And No Heading Over It, And A Man Of About Thirty Who Says A True Thing Out Loud At A Counter And Is Not Asked One Word Back

The counting room at the top of that lane has a shutter half up on this side and it has been half up since before the frost, and the light comes off the flags of the lane and goes out again according to the weather, and the bay at the bottom of it is shut and its lamp is not on.

He is thirty-eight and his name is Rennick Adley, and he came to the books of the Company in the second month of next year and has had them since. He does not compute that from anything and nobody has asked him about it.

This is the fourth day of the fourth week of the tenth month of the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the morning, and the afternoon of the same day is the same room.

---

The man of about thirty from the second gang came up the lane at about the fifth hour and stood in the doorway of that room with his cap in his hand, and he was findable, and everyone in that lane knows he is, and that is the whole of what is known about him by anybody anywhere.

He said a true thing out loud. He said the figure on the paper he was holding is not the figure he was given on the day he took it, that he has had it eleven days, that he has been at nine doors in that lane about it and has not been given the figure at any one of them, and that he did not mean anything by coming up here.

**Adley could have put one question to him at the doorway and there was nothing in that room to stop him and nobody standing behind him to say no.** He did not put it. He asked him to put the paper on the table, and he looked at the figure, and he said the figure is not wrong in this room, which is the answer he gives everybody, and the man took it and went back down the lane at the speed he had come up it.

---

The reason is not a rule and nothing was ever drawn for it and the not putting is not cowardice and is not care.

**A question put to that man in that room would make him a party, and a party is the only person a thing can be told what it is going to be used for.** Then the figure would stop being a figure in a lane and become a matter in a file, and the file would want a date on it, and the date would want two people on it, and one of the two would be the man in the doorway, and he did not come up that lane to be on anything. Adley has thought about putting that one question about four times in about four years and has done it none of the four times, and the not doing it is the whole of what his knowing consists of.

**There is no form for serving a man with the figure he was given**, and Adley has not got that figure and never had it, and the one piece of paper in his hand is a copy of a copy of a number that about nine hundred rooms a year agree on without ever being in the same room as each other.

"There is no form for thanking a man for telling you a true thing either," he said out loud at about the fifth hour, to the shutter, about the man, and the man was not thanked, and he was down the lane by then, and the shutter took it, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that would oblige anybody to.

---

Under a rule at the bottom of a box that does not belong to the Company there is a piece of waste paper and it has been there since before the frost, and he has read it twice, and he has not torn it up and has not shown it to anybody and is not going to.

What is on it is half a sentence that a foreman of fifty-one four hundred yards down that lane has been giving him since the third month of the year after, and what is not on it is the other half, and **the day he goes and asks for the other half is the day she stops giving him this one**, and he worked that out a long time ago in about four minutes and has never mentioned it to anybody including her.

A column in the second of the Company's papers is ruled down both sides like any other and there is nothing in it. He ruled it himself in the second month of next year because a name in a column is a thing a person can be served at, and he has never put a heading over it and is not going to.

**A column with a heading over it is a machine and a column with no heading over it is a person**, and a person is only ever asked one way, which is out loud in a room by a person who wants something, and he has never been in a room with her and is not going to be.

---

A page came up the lane with the gang's count, from the district, listing what the district has not paid, and it went on the board with about nine other pages of the same kind and nobody looked at the third line of it.

That line is a crossing at Lowcross standing at nineteen pounds three and fourpence, unpaid, nobody liable, and there is no line for a bridge in that fund and there has not been one in nineteen years. **The word standing against that figure in that column is not the word that stands against nineteen days of a crew's money in a weigh-house ledger about half a mile from here**, and both of those words were picked by somebody out of three others in about four minutes, thinking about a column and not about anybody. The two of them are not one thing and have never been in one column and are not going to be in one this week.

He does not know who put that page on the board and neither does anybody else in that lane, and the bill has been unpaid since before he kept the books and it will be unpaid after he stops, and nobody in this room is the reason it is unpaid and nobody in this room could pay it.

---

His own wages are held against a roof he put there on purpose, and that one he can account for to the penny, and it is the only sum on that length of lane he can account for.

He entered the day. It came out even, which is the whole of what the day is for and none of what it is. About four men in that bay cannot make sense of a paragraph and not one of the four has ever been asked one question about a number by anybody at all. He has never been in that bay and could not get an answer out of one of them if he went, and being refused is the whole of what he would need and he would take it in an afternoon.

He thought about going down the lane. He thought about it for about as long as it takes to write out a day's figures and then he stopped thinking about it, and the stopping is the discipline and not the indifference.

"Is that shutter coming down?" he said it out loud at about the fifth hour, in the afternoon, to the shutter, about the shutter, and it is not coming down at that hour and never is, and nothing in this empire lets a shutter be said to a person, and the light on the flags of that lane goes out with the weather and comes back with it and is not asked anything either way.

---

Nobody put a question to him this afternoon and nobody has for about four years. Nothing was sent for and nothing was thanked, and the waste paper is at the bottom of the box and the column has no heading over it and the third line of the third page on the board is nineteen pounds three and fourpence that nobody is liable for.

Four hundred yards down that same lane the man of about fifty-five is sitting on a bench with a chain on him, and there is a mark in chalk on the wall at the height of the third link of it, and neither one of those is his and neither one of them is going to be. **The count of things anybody has asked out loud in this matter is the same as it was this morning and it is going to be the same in ten weeks.**
