# Chapter 332: The Inside Of That Bay, About Nine Men In It And A Foreman Of Fifty-One, A Gate Shut At The Fourth Hour, And A Day On Which Nobody Asked Anybody Anything At All

The bay is about nine foot by eleven and there is a bench in it that is longer than the bay is wide, and the lamp above the middle of the bench is not lit until about the seventh hour and was not lit this morning either.

She is fifty-one. Her name is Halla Wray. She has held the foreman's job on that cut for nine years, which is nine years of a thing nine families agreed to between themselves in the open, and it is not an office and it is not on any list of offices anywhere and no form was ever drawn for it.

This is the second day of the second week of the eighth month of the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fifth hour, and the gate was shut at about the fourth hour by a man of about forty who does not argue.

---

She has been inside a box since the first week of the seventh month of the year after. A class put her there, and nobody has ever asked her about it, and that is the whole of it, and it has not been a single morning since.

**Nobody asked her about it today either.** Nobody in that bay put one question to her about the box, and nobody has for about four years, and the not asking is not a courtesy and not a rule anybody made and not going to be lifted.

About nine men in that bay know she is in a box. Not one of them has said a word about it since the day it went in, and she has not said a word to any of them, and there is no arrangement anywhere that would let nine men who have all said nothing be asked whether they agree about it.

**Nine men agreeing to nothing out loud in a shed is not a class and it is not a company either, and that is the whole of what the nine of them have.** There is no heading over the nine, and there is no money behind the nine, and if the whole thing were written down the nine would stop being nine men and become a line in a return, and a line in a return is the thing the class is made of. So the nine say nothing, and she says nothing, and about four years have gone by in a room where nothing has been said by anybody at all.

---

The man who keeps the Company's books four hundred yards up that same lane has wanted the rest of a sentence off her since the third month of the year after and has not asked for it.

She has owed him that half sentence for the same length of time and she has given him the half she has got, and the half she has got is the half she has been giving, and it is not going to turn into the whole of it anybody's asking.

**The day he asks is the day she stops saying the half she has got.** She worked that out in about four minutes a long time back and has never said it out loud to him and is not going to.

He has not asked and he is not going to. **There is no form for wanting a thing out of a person who is owed one**, and wanting is not serving, and is not refused, and is not anything at all in this empire, and a man four hundred yards up a lane wanting a sentence off a foreman he has no office over is a man standing in the weather with his hands in his pockets.

**Being asked is being served, and being served is being a party, and a party is the only person a thing can be told what it is going to be used for.** That is the arrangement and it is nineteen years old in a room nine miles off with a rail at the end of a cold passage in it, and the two of them are the same arrangement and she has never been in that room and never will be.

If he came up that lane on a morning and said the thing out loud, nothing would stop him. **And that is the difficulty and not the comfort of it**: nothing would stop him, and there would be nothing to refuse, and a thing that cannot be refused is not a question, and she would have given him the rest of it inside a minute to be rid of it, and that is the reason she has wanted the lane to stay at four hundred yards for about four years.

---

This was not a near miss. Nothing came near it this week.

He was not at the gate and he did not come up that lane and she did not see him from the door of the bay and nobody brought a word from him. There was no point in the whole of the week at which a question could have arrived and been refused, because a question from him would not have been a question in any instrument at all. It would have been a man saying a thing out loud in a lane, and a thing said out loud in a lane is a thing a number of people hear of inside a week and a clerk writes none of.

**She is not going to be asked about the box by anybody this month**, and the form that put her in it is not going to be asked to be set aside by anybody, and nothing in this empire has ever been asked to be set aside, and there is no procedure for asking.

---

At the back of that bay the two of seventeen are where they were, on their written engagement, and neither of them has been spoken to.

The reader is paid forty-five pence a day and four days a week. **Nobody has put one word to either of them about a number or about anything else**, and neither of them has said a word to her, and the bay has room at the back for two and has had for weeks, and nobody has moved them and nobody is going to.

The man of about fifty-five with the chain is in the bay and is not one of the four who cannot make sense of a paragraph, and he says that himself, and putting him down as one of them would be a way for a list of four to go wrong. She did not ask him anything and he did not ask her anything and the two of them have not asked each other anything in about four years.

**The four are not a route and they are not going to become one**, and the reason is not that they are slow or that they are difficult or that they would say no. The reason is that a question has to be put to a person, and each of those four is a person, and there is no instrument in this empire that will put a question to one of them without putting it into a record, and a record is a thing that follows a man about for the rest of his life and gets him served at. She has known all of that for about four years and has never once been tempted to break it, and she has also never once been in a position to be asked why not.

She is fifty-one and has been in a box for about four years. She has kept nine men saying nothing, and a man with a fair hand four hundred yards off wanting half a sentence, and a foreman in a coat on a different lane saying three things he has said four times, and none of it can be put in front of a person as a question at any hour of the day.

**She has stopped thinking of it as a thing that has happened to her and started thinking of it as the price of the house she lives in**, and the house is a bay about nine foot by eleven with a bench in it that is longer than the bay is wide.

---

A man came through at about the sixth hour to have a length of wall measured and she measured it and gave him the figure and he was gone inside a minute. It is the same answer it has been for about four years and it does not satisfy anybody.

Nobody thanked her. Nobody sent for her. Nothing was resolved and nothing was forgiven, and the count of things anybody has asked out loud in this matter is the same as it was in the morning, and the mark in the chalk on that end wall is a mark and is nobody's and is about four years off being anybody's evidence.

"Is that mark still on the wall?" she said it out loud at about the sixth hour, to nobody, about the mark, and the mark is a mark, and nothing in this empire lets a mark be said to a person, and a mark is not a person, and the chain it stands level with is on a man she is not going to ask about it.
