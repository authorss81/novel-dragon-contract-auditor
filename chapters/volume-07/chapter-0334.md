# Chapter 334: A House With A Door On It Four Miles Off, Four Steps Between The Pavement And The Room, A Fire That Went Out At The Fourth Hour, And The Reason He Gave Out Loud About Six Weeks Before He Did The Thing

The house has a door on it that anybody in the lane can see is a door, and there are four steps between the pavement and the room, and the fire went out at about the fourth hour and neither of them made it up again.

She is not named in this and she has not been named anywhere else in this matter, and she has been at the foot of those four steps for about two years. She works a day's shift and comes in at about the sixth hour. There is a board on the wall inside with the rent written on it and nothing else on it.

This is the fourth day of the fourth week of the eighth month of the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fifth hour.

---

He came in with the wet still on him and did not say where he had been, and she did not ask him, and that is not new.

**She said out loud some weeks ago that she is not going to ask him how it went, and she has not asked since, and the not asking is hers and she did not give it to anybody.** There is a difference between not asking because you have decided not to and not asking because there is nothing in the room to ask into, and she has been careful about which of the two it is for about two years, and she has not managed it every day.

She has never asked him why he goes. He has told her where once, and that once was about six weeks before he did the thing, and he did not ask her first.

**There is no form for ever asking a person why they go**, and there has not been one in this empire for as long as anybody has been keeping records, and she has asked him nothing on about four hundred mornings, and he has not been asked either, and the two of them are the only people in the house and there is no third thing in it to be asked about.

---

That is the part she has never been able to put down flat.

**He told her about six weeks before he did it, out loud, on purpose, without asking her first.** She heard all of it and has not been able to decide since whether it was the right of it.

The reason he gave was a short one. If he had asked her, she would have said no. Or she would have said yes and been frightened, and either of those would have been him arranging the room.

"There is no form for putting such a question to a woman, about whether she will mind being told a thing before she is told it. If I had asked you, whatever you said would have been an answer I had arranged, and I would have gone on and done it whatever the answer was. So I told you first and let it be too late for you to be able to stop me."

That is what he said, in about four sentences, on a step, with the rain going on.

**It is the only time in about two years that he has explained a thing to her before he did it**, and he has not done it since and she has not expected him to. She has thought about the other of it since, on and off, for about six weeks: that a man who tells you first has chosen the moment, and a man who asks you first has chosen nothing, and she has never found out which of the two she would rather be.

About nine people she has never met know some of it. None of them will ever be told any of the rest, and about nine hundred sheets a year go out of a room nine miles off with a hand at the foot of every one of them, and nothing in this empire lets a sheet be said to a person at the foot of four steps in a lane four miles from a counting room.

---

She has not thanked him for that and she is not going to.

There is not one form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be thanked for being told a thing before it is done, and there is not going to be one, and she would have had to be the first person to ask for it, and she has wanted a great many things in two years and that is not one of them.

**What she has not done is decide anything, and a thing that has not been decided is not waiting to be decided; it is only not decided, and it will be like that on a day she has not thought about yet.**

Whether she trusts his judgment is hers, and it has not been decided this month, and it is not going to be decided because he came in wet again, and it is not on his side of the room either. He has not asked her about it and he is not going to, and she is not going to raise it, and the two of them are living in a house with a door on it and neither of them has any idea what the other one is doing about it.

---

He is about to do a third thing. She does not know what it is and has not asked and is not going to.

This is about six weeks before it, and the six weeks are made of three things and he has not told her about any of them, and she has not asked about any of them, and the not asking on her side is the same not asking and not a different one.

The first of the three is a room over a shop that sells tar, taken by the week, with four steps and a landing and a lamp that is not lit until about the seventh hour. The second is a bay about four hundred yards off that room, with a bench longer than the bay is wide and a mark in chalk on a wall at the height of the third link of a chain. The third is two people of seventeen at the back of that bay on a written engagement at the rate a list is set at, and he has been near that bay and has not gone into it.

**He has not said one word about any of it to her and she has not asked one word about any of it**, and about nine people in other buildings know pieces of it and not one of them has said a word either, and that is how it is done, and there is no instrument for it anywhere and there is not going to be one.

---

She put the fire out properly at about the seventh hour, because he lit the lamp instead and the lamp is worse than a fire for money and always has been.

"Do you want the fire?" she said it out loud at about the fifth hour, to the room, about the fire, and the room did not take it, and he said no from the other side without stopping what he was doing.

**Nothing in this empire lets one person be asked about another person's absence**, and the asking would be a party, and a party is the only person a thing can be told what it is going to be used for, and she had worked that out about two years ago in about four minutes and has not needed to work it out since.

She washed the two plates and put them on the board upside down to drip, which is what she does, and the lamp went on being worse than a fire.

Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was sent for and nothing was resolved. She is not going to ask him how it went, and he is not going to ask her whether she minds, and about nine hundred sheets a year are going to keep going out of a room nine miles off that neither of them has ever been in, with a hand at the foot of every one of them.
