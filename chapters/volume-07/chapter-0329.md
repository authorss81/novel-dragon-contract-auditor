# Chapter 329: A Counting Room About Eleven Feet By Fourteen At The Back Of A Dye End, Sixteen Books And A Stove That Goes Out Behind The Bench, And Eleven Houses That Are Not A Person

The counting room is at the back of a dye end in a lane in a furnace town about four hundred and thirty miles up this river, and it is about eleven feet by fourteen. There is a bench along one wall. There is a window with a broken light in it, and that broken light has let the same weather in for about four years and nobody has had it out. There are about forty books on shelves that were made for taller books.

In the corner behind the bench there is a stove that has not been lit since the summer and gets lit every day of the winter by whoever gets in first.

**Eleven houses off a dyers' lane keep their numbers in that room and not one of the eleven has ever asked to see the number.**

She is twenty and her name is Nell Kest, and she keeps sixteen books. Fifteen of the sixteen belong to somebody else and not one of the fifteen has an office on the cover of it. The sixteenth has her own name at the head of it and nobody has ever asked her what it is for.

This is the fourth day of the third week of the seventh month of the year after the year after the year after next, and the dye end has been running since about the second hour, and it is about the fourth hour.

The dye end had been running since about the second hour and by about the fourth the whole of the room smelled of it. The shelves were built for books taller than the ones now on them, and fifteen of the sixteen she keeps belong to somebody else and carry no office on the cover.

---

Her wage has been held since the second week of the third month of the year after.

The man who is holding it is a foreman off a dye end and he is about forty, and he is not in this matter and has never been in it. He has come up those two stairs four times now and said the same three things on each occasion, and the three things are that the ledger is shut, that the entry is not his, and that he would look at it in the spring.

This week he came up the two stairs with a notice in a coat and set it on the bench, and he did not send it, and he told her to write it out and keep it and not send it. She told him out loud what he ought to do instead and gave him a reason she made up in about four seconds. **A notice in a coat on a bench in a counting room says a woman is owed money, and there is nothing in this empire that can hold a notice against anybody at all**, and the notice is still in the coat and the coat is on the hook and she is not going to send it.

She is not thanked for that and nothing anywhere in this empire would thank her for it.

---

**Four women are going to be told that she is not paid. One of the four has been.**

That was in the fourth week of the ninth month of the year after next and it took about nine minutes standing on a step. The woman has not said one word about it since and has not passed it to anybody else, and that is not a promise and it is the best the arrangement has got. Three are still to go. The going is on her own time, there is no fund for it, there is no half-day, and nobody to stand at the bench.

Four women is not one woman, and a number is a class, and the telling costs her nine minutes of an evening each time and comes out of her own hours and not out of anybody else's week.

A woman of about thirty came to her in the third month of the year after next with money in her hand and offered some of it. She refused it, and that was the first time in about three years that she had refused anything from anybody, and she gave the reason out loud and the reason was one sentence.

The reason was that she is not going up this river this season. Whether she is ever going to is a thing she has not said, and she is the only person in this matter who knows that not going this season and not going at all are two different sentences, and nobody has ever been in a position to establish which of them it is going to be.

---

**What the last ten days gave her is the answer to a question she had not thought to ask, and it took her about four minutes, and she has nobody to tell it to.**

The only question anybody in this matter knows about that was ever put in front of about nine hundred rooms at once was put there by a border law of a House, and a border household is not a person, and the law does not need a person, and that is the reason it worked.

**Her eleven houses off a dyers' lane are not a person either.** Nobody in any one of those eleven houses is a household, and nobody in any one of them is a party, and there is therefore nothing in this empire that can be put in front of eleven houses at once and get an answer. There never has been. And if there were one they would answer it the way a class answers, which is all at once and without anybody minding.

She worked that out in about four minutes at about the fourth hour with a pen in her hand, and then had to sit there with the rest of it for a while, and there is no form in this empire in which a thing can be put in front of a number of houses and come back with an answer that anybody is in.

Her own figure has no column to go into in the building and none is going to be made, and anything set down in a book wants a heading over it, and she has worked it through about four times and said it out loud to nobody.

---

She is not on a list. There is no list. There is no heading over either of them and there is no money behind either of them, and the whole of what is between two people in two rooms in two cities is that both of them have said one true thing out loud in a room with nobody in authority in it.

**There is another held wage in this matter about four hundred and thirty miles from this room and the two of them are kept apart on purpose**, and nobody in this building knows it, and nobody in the other building knows this one, and there is no form in this empire that would let anybody join two people who have not asked to be joined.

The foreman in the coat with the notice in it is a different man and he was not told any of it and is not going to be, and there is no form in this empire that has ever put the same question to eleven houses off a dyers' lane twice, and the asking is the only part of it that would ever have cost anybody anything.

A notice sits in a coat on a hook by the door, and about four years of the room has gone without anybody asking her what she keeps the sixteenth book for, and the window has been broken for about as long.

---

A man came in at about the fifth hour about a figure that was wrong in a ledger about half a mile off, and it was wrong in the ledger and not in this room, and nobody could be told so by anybody without a form and no form exists.

"Is that the same figure?"

"It is the same figure and it is wrong in a ledger and not in this room, and I cannot tell you where in it, and I have said that to you twice this season and I am not going to say it a third time differently."

He went away with what he came with and has not come back about it and is not going to, and she put no figure of her own into any of the sixteen.

The stove had gone out behind the bench and the broken light in the window had gone from weather to dark without anything happening in between, and nobody was thanked and nobody was sent, and three women she has not told are three women in eleven houses who do not know on this day that there are three of them.
