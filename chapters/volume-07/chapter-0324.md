# Chapter 324: The Same Room Seven Days Later, The Pane Still Out, And What It Cost To Have Said A Thing Twice In Nineteen Years

The same shelf, the same eleven books, the same table, the same chair, and the same window about two feet wide with the pane out of it that has been out of it for about four years.

Behind the building the passage runs about four hundred yards and the flags of it come up cold underfoot in every month of the year, and the rail is still about four feet high at the far end of it, and the step at the top end is still worn through, and there is still nobody at that door at any hour of the day.

**The season is on and it is going to be on for another about nine weeks.** About nine hundred sheets will go out of this room before the end of it, and there is no way of telling from inside this room how many have gone, and nobody counts them, and there is no form in this empire that would let anybody count them.

This is the second day of the second week of the sixth month of the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fifth hour.

---

**One of the nine hundred will go to the district a man of sixty-one lives in.**

If he signs it, his hand will be at the foot of it, and it will be correct, and the district will be told what the sheet says, and the sheet does not say that a man came about nine miles in a season in about nineteen years and said about nine minutes of it standing up at a rail.

**There is no form in this empire under which a person is told what a season is about to send into his own district**, and there has never been one, and the man who wrote the first of those has been dead a long time and nobody has replaced him.

---

She said the cost out loud at about the fifth hour, to a page, and then she put the pen down and did not pick it up again that afternoon, which she has not done in nineteen years.

"Here is what it cost and I would like it said out loud in a room, because I have found out that nobody will say it for me and nobody can."

"**A man who has said a thing twice is findable by the saying of it.** That is not my finding and I did not arrive at it. He arrived at it, on his own two feet, about nine miles, in a season, on a day he is not paid for. He has now said the whole of it out loud twice in nineteen years, in this room on two of his three visits, and both times somebody told a carrier in a lane where to come, and a carrier in a lane is the only instrument there has ever been, and a man who is findable by a carrier in a lane is a man who can be served, and being served is a party."

"About nine people were in that room. **I wrote none of it down**, and I said out loud that writing it down would make it an order, and I have not entered an order in nineteen years and I am not going to start with a man of sixty-one."

"That is the whole of what it cost. Nobody paid anything. Not me, and not the four of the others who were in it, and not the woman of about forty who carries a scuttle and is not one of the three clerks and is not in this."

---

And there is the other half of it, and she said that too, and it is the older half by about nineteen years.

"A quarter of the question I wrote into the second of the eleven books was answered out loud in this room in the second month of the year after the year after next, by that same man, and **I wrote none of that down either**, and the question is still a question, and it is on that shelf under the window with the pane out of it, and it did not become an answer and it is not going to become one."

"Two things have now been said out loud in this room and neither of them is in a book. That is not a defect in the two of them. **That is the room, and it is the only room either of them has ever had**, and I have been in it nineteen years and I have got very good at it and being good at it is the whole of what I have been paid for."

---

A man of about thirty-four was in that room with him and had been in no other room of this building in his life, and he is not on a list, and he is not in an office, and he does not work here.

Nobody has asked her why he was there. **No form in this empire is shaped so that a clerk of an office could put a question to a man of thirty-four about what he was doing at a rail in her building**, and she is not going to ask him and is not going to ask anybody about him, and he has not come back and there is no reason he would.

**How long a page stands before it is a document?** She said that to the room at about the sixth hour with nobody in it, and then she went back to the sums, and the sums were the sums.

---

A carrier came up the passage with a rack on her hip at about the sixth hour and put four on the shelf and went back down it without stopping, and the woman of fifty-five entered them correctly in a hand that does not vary, and that is the whole of what was written down this afternoon.

The pane is still out of the window. The step is still worn through. The rail is still about four feet high and a man of about sixty-one has stood on the wrong side of it three times in nineteen years and is nine miles off and does not know it and is not going to be told.

Nobody was thanked. Nobody was sent for. Nobody was forgiven and nothing was resolved, and the season is on, and about nine hundred sheets are going to go out of that room before the end of it, and one of them is going to the district he lives in, and there is nothing in this empire that has ever made findability into a thing a man can refuse.
