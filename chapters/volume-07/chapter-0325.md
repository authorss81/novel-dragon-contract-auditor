# Chapter 325: The Bottom End Of About Four Hundred Yards Of Cold Flags, A Rack Coming Off A Carrier's Hip, And A Fact That Is Not A Fourth Question

The passage is about four hundred yards long and it runs off a flood terrace, and the building it belongs to is about as wide as that length divided by nine, and no fire is lit at either end of it in any month.

At the bottom end there is a shelf and a window about two feet wide with a pane gone out of it, and the pane has been gone about four years and nobody has asked her about it either.

At the top end there is a door with a step worn through and a rail inside the room beyond it standing about four feet off the floor, and there is nobody at that door at any hour of the day.

**She is about twenty-six. Four years is how long she has been in that building, and none of those four years are in this matter, and none of the rest of them are going to be.**

This is the fourth day of the third week of the sixth month of the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour, and at the far end of the flags there is a woman of about fifty-five with a page and a hand that does not vary.

---

She counts the racks, and she counts them in her head.

Every working day, at about the third hour, a rack comes off a carrier's hip and is set down on the shelf by the window and nobody touches it, and while it is going she adds it to the other ones. The racks that go down that passage and come back light by one are what she is counting. **She has been at it for about four years and has never once put a hand on a rack.** Nobody knows that she does it at all, and there is no form in this empire that would make a person ask her whether she does.

**The figure has gone up in every year since the seventh month of the year after and she has not said by how much and she is not going to.**

Some time back she worked out that she has been counting the wrong thing. A rack that comes back light by one has a carrier's own in it, and a carrier's own is her own business and is entered nowhere and is seen by nobody, so the count of what comes back light is not a count of who is sitting in a room.

**She is not going to correct it.** She is not going to hand it to anybody in a state she knows to be wrong, and no form in this empire will take a number a woman worked out for herself, and there is no column in this building it could go into and there is not going to be one, and there is no paper she could put it on that would not need a heading over it first.

---

What she has that she did not have a month ago is a fact, and it is the plainest thing she has ever had.

**He came a third time.** About nine miles, in a season, on his own two feet, on a day he is not paid for, and he stood on the wrong side of the rail for about four minutes and then said about nine minutes of it standing up, and about nine people were in that room, and the woman of about fifty-five wrote none of it down.

She was in the building. She is the only other person in this matter who knows what he said at that rail, and she has known it once, and now she knows he has said the whole of it twice, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that will let anybody ask her what she heard in that room, and nobody is going to.

---

**And the fact does not give her a fourth question.**

She has three, and they have been in her own head for a long time, and none of them has been asked and none of them is going to be. **Knowing that he came a third time does not turn three into four**, and it does not turn the three into ones she could put. A woman at the bottom of a cold passage with a figure in her head is not a person anybody in this empire can hand anything to, and there is no form here that would let a question of hers travel the four hundred yards to the other end and be one anybody had to stand inside.

Knowing it does not get her out of a corridor either. **She is not coming up a stair in this season**, nobody has written down that she is going to and nobody is going to, the four hundred yards of that passage is not walked by her on an errand of her own, and there is no form in this empire in which a fact she has is the beginning of a journey.

---

**She worked all of that out in about four minutes**, standing at the bottom end of the passage with the cold coming up at her, and she has been at that end for about four years, and she had it in about four minutes, and she had the same part of it in about four minutes a while back.

She said it out loud to the shelf, and the shelf did not answer, and she went back to counting.

"How many racks?"

Then a carrier came up the passage and put a rack on the shelf and the carrier said nothing and she said nothing and the carrier went back down it and the cold came up after her and the rail stood in it at the other end.

---

Nobody asked her anything this week. Nobody has asked her anything in about four years, and nothing in this empire is shaped so that anybody is obliged to ask her what she counts or what she heard, and the not asking is the ordinary condition of a person at the end of a passage and not a hardship arranged for her.

She is not going to give the figure a heading. She is not going to hand it on. She has not written it down and is not going to, and there is nobody she is going to hand it to, and **no form in this empire has ever made being told something the same as being able to do anything with it.** That is the whole of what she got for standing at that end of the passage about four years.

The pane is still out of the window at the bottom of the passage. The step is still worn through at the top. About nine hundred sheets a year go out of the other end of that building and one man signs all of them, and he has been in that room three times in nineteen years, and she is the only other person who knows it, and she is not going to be asked and she is not going to tell.
