# Chapter 301: Four Steps And A Landing, A Table With Nothing On It, And A Nail In About Four Feet Of Wall

He has a room over a chandler's shop and pays for it by the week, and it has a table in it and a chair, and the chair is against the wall the bed is against because there is nowhere else in it to put a chair. The window is about a foot wide and it looks at the back of the house across the lane, and about four feet of that wall has nothing on it but a nail, and nothing has ever hung on the nail, and he has been in the room long enough to have had the chance.

There are four steps and a landing. The shop underneath keeps tar and rope and lamp oil and the smell of it comes up between the boards all day, and nobody on that lane has ever said a word to him about it.

He is thirty-four or thereabouts and he has a fair hand, and what he does for money is write out other men's sums so that they come out even, and there are four days a season he is on somebody's list and this week is not one of the four.

He has a house with a door on it and a wife at the bottom of four steps. He is not on anybody's paper this week and he did not put himself on it and nobody has asked him to.

This is the fourth day of the third week of the twelfth month of the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour, and the lamp in that room is not lit until about the seventh hour and is not lit before it.

---

**The number of things anybody has asked out loud in this matter is seven.** It has not moved and he has not moved it and there is nothing in this room to move it with.

He goes through the rest of it about once in four weeks and this is the once, and he has the list in his head the way he has a column in his head, and the list is not on a shelf anywhere and **there is no place in this empire it could be put.**

A practice, he has worked out, is a count of things done and not of people.

A count that counted people would be six, and a practice with one person in it is stopped by that person, and a practice with two people in it is stopped by nobody at all, and that is the whole of the arithmetic and it took him about four minutes the first time he did it and it has not needed doing since.

---

He started at the top of the list because the top of the list is the two that are in boxes, and they are still in them.

The form that put a woman of twenty into one of them has not been asked to be void by anybody. The form that put a foreman of fifty-one into the other has not been asked to be void by anybody either. **No form in this empire has ever been asked to be void and there is no procedure for it.** The person who administers one is a clerk who is not a villain and has done about nine hundred of them correctly.

Nobody has asked either of them anything. He has not asked either of them anything. There is nothing in this empire that can be sent to a room, and a room is the only place this matter has ever been anything at all, and **there is no form in this empire for putting two people in one of them.**

---

The question in the second of the eleven books is still a question.

It went in on her own motion, at nobody's request, with the day and the hour on it and her own name at the foot of it, and it is on a shelf in a room on the ground floor of a building off the old river road, under a window about two feet wide with a pane out of it that nobody has put back in about four years.

It has been on that shelf about a year. **It did not become an answer and it is not going to become one**, and a minute with an answer in it is an order, and a clerk of that office has not entered an order in nineteen years.

A quarter of it was answered out loud in that building in the second month, by a man who came nine miles on his own two feet, and it went into a room and not into a book, and she wrote none of it down, and he told nobody what was said, and the two of them are the only two people who know that it was answered at all.

---

About nineteen people are still behind about four hundred and forty foot of bank.

A guarantee is still standing offered and unanswered, and there are about sixty children under sixteen inside it, and it is not in anybody's own words and no child in it has a name anybody has said.

Nobody has asked those about nineteen people a second time and nobody is going to, and a man who has said no twice in the same words with the same reasons is a man who is finished as a subject. **A bill at Lowcross stands at nineteen pounds three and fourpence and is unpaid and nobody is liable.** He did not pay it.

He is not the reason it is unpaid, and it is worth being exact about that, because it is the only figure in the whole of this that he is inside of at all and he is inside it as a man who once owed it and did not pay it and has not said so in a room.

---

About four men in a bay cannot read a paragraph and have never in the whole of this been asked one question about a number.

That is the part of the list that costs the most and it costs nothing to say. A girl of seventeen is at the back of that same bay on a written engagement at the rate a list is set at. **She has not been spoken to and has not been thanked and has not been sent for and is not going to start.** A reader of seventeen is at the back of it in the same condition, and she would say yes and has never been given the chance to say no. **Nobody was thanked anywhere.** There is not one form anywhere in this empire that calls for a person to be thanked for saying a thing out loud in a room, and there never is going to be one, and the not thanking is the ordinary condition of a thing anybody did and is not a cruelty in it.

The two of it are this.

A woman of twenty is in a box about nine miles off a building off the old river road, and a foreman of fifty-one is in a box on a bank about four hundred and thirty miles down this river, and he has got a list of about ten items in his head and no place to put any of them.

He has not written a line of it down in about two years, and there is no form anywhere that would take it if he did. **A written list of the people in this matter is a list of persons, and a list of persons is a company**, and a company is a thing this empire can hold wages against and give letters to and take to law.

---

And then there are the two of them, and they are the practice, and a practice is a count of things done and not of people.

One is him, and he is in a room taken by the week over a shop that sells tar.

The other is a man of thirty-eight with a labour a gate can refuse and a mark about three inches long on the inside of his left wrist that reads nothing and decides nothing and does not know where the ford is, and the wage is the price and not the mark. **They are about four hundred and thirty miles apart, and they have never met, and there is no list of the two of them and no form and no heading and no fund.** Nobody in either building knows that the other one exists, and nothing in this empire is going to put them in a room.

Neither of them has said the other's name out loud.

He knows a fact about the other one that he did not see and that he was told at second hand, and he found out at second hand that the bill for his being findable is not his own bill and stands on somebody else's account, and he worked that out in about four minutes on a step in another street and told nobody and **did not go and look.**

---

**He wrote out one sum before the light went, for a man who cannot pay him until the spring.** It came to eleven shillings and it took about nine minutes.

It was the whole of the work of that day, and the man who sent it will get it in the spring and will not know that it was done in a room where nothing was lit and by a man who went through the whole of it in his head in an afternoon and did not once put any of them to anybody.

Then the ordinary part of the day, which is that the woman of about forty came up the four steps at about the fifth hour with a scuttle and lit the lamp early, as she does twice a week, and she is not in this matter. **There is no form in this empire that would let him ask her whether she minds being told a thing before she is told it**, and he did not ask her and he is not going to.

He went down at about the sixth hour to a house with a door on it, and his wife was at the bottom of the steps of that house, and she did not ask him how the week had gone, and she said out loud some weeks ago that she is not going to, and he did not ask her to change her mind about that either.

There is a woman of about thirty-five next door who has known him about eleven years and who is findable by that, and there is a stair four miles off where a man came up wanting nothing at all and was answered one word, and there is a woman of about forty with a scuttle who is findable by a lamp, and every one of those three is a way of being found and not one of them is on any paper of his own making.

A man who is findable by three people in a lane is a man whose bill for it is not his own, and he worked that out in about four minutes on a step and told nobody.

The four days a season he is on somebody's list are in about the sixth week and they are stone and cutting and a man who cannot read the figures, and this is not one of them and no body has been at this door about them.

There is a table and a chair and a wall with a nail in it and that is the whole of what a man of about thirty-four with a fair hand owns in this empire, and none of it is findable except by the woman who carries the scuttle.

---

He put his hand flat on the table at about the seventh hour and the table had nothing on it, and the lamp was lit and had been lit by somebody else, and about four feet of wall had the nail in it and nothing on the nail.

The seven did not move. The two of them are about four hundred and thirty miles apart and have never met and are not going to be put in a room by anything in this empire. **A bill that is not his is still standing on somebody else's account.** About nine hundred entries a year are still coming in under a heading with a hand at the foot of every one of them, and that hand is a man, and nobody has asked him, and there is no form that would let anybody ask him. And not one of the seven is going to be eight this month.
