# Chapter 346: The Room At The Far End Of About Four Hundred Yards Of Cold Flags, A Rail Standing Four Feet Off The Floor, A Door With A Step Worn Through In Front Of It, And Nine Minutes With A Coat Open And A Hand That Never Went To The Rail

The passage comes off a flood terrace at the back of a building off the old river road and runs about four hundred yards, and the flags of it come up cold underfoot in every month of the year. There is no fire at either end of it.

He came to it on his own two feet, on a day he is not paid for, and nobody sent him, and the cold was still on him when the door at the top end gave, and there is nobody at that door at any hour of the day.

This is the fourth day of the fourth week of the eleventh month of the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the second hour, and the nine minutes are between about the second and about the third.

---

He has been in this room once. His two earlier rooms were the counter at the top of four flights four miles off, and he paid fourpence for a copy in each of them, and neither of those rooms is this one.

The rail is standing about four feet off the floor and there is nothing on it this morning, and it is not a thing anybody is waiting for. **About four other people came with him into that room or were in it before him**, and none of them came for him and none of them knew he was coming.

The man of sixty-one is on the wrong side of that rail and was standing there before the door gave. He has been in this room three times in nineteen years, and one of the two men standing in it walked the nine miles this morning and the other walked it in a month that is gone. **The one that did it this morning is not going to say which of the two of them it was, and there is nobody in that room who is going to tell him.**

---

He has had the question ready since the fourth week of the second month. He has it ready now. It is a single sentence and it takes about four seconds to say, and it has been in his mouth in about nine rooms since the spring, and it is the only one of its kind he has ever been in a position to put to anybody.

A woman of about fifty-five is at a table with her back to the wall and there is a pen on the table and she does not look up when the door gives. A woman of about forty is standing near the rail with a scuttle on her hip and she is not one of the three clerks of that building and is not in this and cannot be asked what she has heard, and nobody has ever asked her and nobody is going to.

"Is that rail the same height it was?" he said it out loud, at the door, to the room, about the rail, and it was, and nothing in this empire lets a rail be said to a person, and he did not go and put a hand on it.

---

Then he said it out loud, standing, in about thirteen sentences, to the man of sixty-one and to the woman of about fifty-five and to about four other people, and it was not a speech, and it took about nine minutes, and it is the whole of what he came nine miles for.

"I am not going to put it, and I have had it ready since the fourth week of the second month, and I have had it ready this morning as well, and it is the only thing I have come here with."

"A question put at that rail goes onto a shelf, and **the shelf is the shelf the sheets go on**, and a sheet with a question written on it does not go back to the man who answered it."

"A question put anywhere else has to be carried to him first, and a man who is found carrying something is a man who can be served with it, and about nine people would have to carry it."

"There are about nine of those sheets a year, out of about nine hundred, that are not what the person above him wrote, and he has put his hand at the foot of every one of them since he was forty-two. If anybody put a question to him about those nine he would tell the truth, and that is the one thing he has wanted to do for nineteen years, **and he has never been asked anything by anybody, and I am not going to be the one who starts it in a room with a pen on a table.**"

---

"There is nowhere in this empire for a man to put a mark beside a sum and call it a disagreement, and that is not an oversight in the making of the page. It is the shape of the page."

"A question in this matter is a party, and a party is a person, and a person is one, and I am one, and he is one, and this room has a clerk in it and a rail and about four other people, and every one of them is one."

"And about nine hundred of these go out of this room in a year, and the whole of what is available at that size is a class."

"And a class is a heading, and a heading is a company, and a company can hold wages against and take to law and never once tell anybody inside it that it has done either. **That is the whole of what is on offer at nine hundred rooms at once**, and it is the same thing that is on offer at one, and I am the one."

"So a question cannot be asked in about nine hundred buildings at once by people who cannot be instructed to ask it, and I have gone at that for two years and the answer is no. **There is no form that carries a no anywhere and no form for undoing an asking once it is in a room**, and that is the half of the reason I have never said out loud before."

---

The man of sixty-one did not answer him.

Nobody in that room put a question to anybody for the whole of the nine minutes and nobody has for about four years, and the woman with the scuttle said nothing at any point in it and said nothing after it, and the woman of about fifty-five did not write any of it down and nobody is going to ask her why she wrote none of it.

Nobody thanked either of them. **Nobody has ever thanked him, and not one form anywhere requires a person to be thanked for saying a true thing out loud in a room with nobody in authority in it**, and he said so himself the last time he stood there, and he is not going to say it again, and the not saying it is not ingratitude.

---

It was in that room and it was in about nine people and it was not in the table and it was not on the shelf under the window at the top end of the passage, and about four thousand entries in eleven books on that shelf have no name in any one of them and none of those is this.

He went out by the door he came in by and down about four hundred yards of cold flags on his own two feet, the same nine miles, and nobody walked with him and nobody was sent for him and nobody will know he went.

**The count of things anybody has asked out loud in this matter is the same as it was when the door gave and it is going to be the same in ten weeks**, and the asking he did not make is in that room and nowhere else, and he is not going to tell anybody what was said in it.
