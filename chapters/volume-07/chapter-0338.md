# Chapter 338: The Same Bay About Nine Foot By Eleven, The Gate Shut At The Fourth Hour And The Lamp Still Unlit At About The Seventh, And The Third Of Three Mornings In Which Nobody Was Spoken To

The bay is at the top of a lane off the Slade and it is about nine foot by eleven, and the bench in it is longer than the bay is wide, and there is a mark in chalk on the end wall at the height of the third link of a chain.

This is the fourth day of the fourth week of the ninth month of the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the morning, and the gate was shut at about the fourth hour by a man of about forty who does not argue.

The morning and the afternoon after it are one room and it is the same one both times. **The foreman of fifty-one has it in the morning and the room itself has it in the afternoon**, and a room has not got a mouth, and this one is given one here for the length of a few words and not for a paragraph.

---

She is fifty-one and her name is Halla Wray and she has held the foreman's job on that cut for nine years, and she has been inside a box since the first week of the seventh month of the year after, and nobody has asked her about it, and that has not changed today either.

Same room in the morning and in the afternoon, about nine foot by eleven both times, and a bench in it running the width of the room and past it, and nobody has ever cut it down.

**She said nothing new this morning.** She has said one sentence about it three times in four years and she is not going to say it a fourth, and the sentence is in nine men and not in a book, and it is about a class and a heading and a company, and it is about four hundred yards and about four hundred and thirty miles, and none of those figures are going to change because she says them again.

The man who keeps the Company's books four hundred yards up the lane did not come up it. She did not see him from the door and nobody brought a word from him, and the day he asks is the day she stops saying the half she has got, and he has not asked, and the not asking was the whole of this morning and will be the whole of this afternoon.

---

The man of about fifty-five with the chain was in it from the fourth hour.

He is not one of the four who cannot make sense of a paragraph and he says so himself, and he has said so out loud about four years now, once, and the saying of it was not a speech and it was not for anybody. **He said nothing this morning, or he said the same thing he has said for about four years, and the two are not different to anybody who was in the room.**

She did not ask him anything. He did not ask her anything. Nobody asked the four about a number and nobody is going to, and the four of them have never once been asked anything by anybody in this matter, and that is about nine years old now.

---

The lamp over the middle of that bench is not lit until about the seventh hour and was not lit at the seventh hour either, because the store was shut and the bay was left with a bench in it and a mark in chalk on a wall.

Nothing was lit in there at all: the gate was shut, the store was shut, and there was a bench, a wall, and a pair at the back of it and nothing else.

"The mark is a mark," somebody said, and nobody answered, and the chain it stands level with is on a man who was not asked about it and is not going to be.

**The cross means nothing and it is nobody's and it is about four years off being anybody's evidence**, and nothing in that lane is going to turn it into anything, and the two people at the back of that bay were not asked about it either.

---

In the afternoon the room is the room.

There is a bench and a lamp and a wall and a door that a person standing at the far end of the bay cannot see out of, and there are two of seventeen at the back of it on a written engagement at the rate a list is set at. **They are there and they have been there for weeks and neither of them is going to start.**

The reader is paid forty-five pence a day and four days a week, and a reader's day is anywhere between four pence and a shilling according to what gets put in front of her on the day. That is the rate and it is not a wage anybody else in this room is paid, and it is a rate that exists because a figure was set at in a list by somebody who was not in this room.

The other one of the two is seventeen and has not been spoken to by anybody in this matter, and not one word of anything has been said to her here, and there is no paper in this empire that asks a person of seventeen whether she would rather not, and so there is no answer on the record, and no answer on the record is not the same as no.

**There is no form for calling two people at the back of a bay to the front of it**, and the bay has had room at the back for two for weeks, and nobody has moved them and nobody is going to, and the not moving is not a decision anybody took in a room. It is a bay with a bench in it and about nine men in the front of it, and the two of them are at the back, and that is where they are.

**There is no form for moving a person at the back of a room to the front of it either**, and the two of them have a written engagement and a rate at the foot of it, and neither of the two of them has ever been asked whether they would rather be at the front.

Nobody has called the two of them to the front of the bay in about four weeks and nobody is going to; the reason there is none is a reason about forms and not about them, and about nine men stand in the front of it every morning and the two of them stand at the back.

The engagement is one sheet with terms on it and a rate at the foot of it, and the rate is a rate and the terms are four lines, and one of the four lines is the name of the person who set it and that person is not in this lane and has never been in it.

"Does that lamp come on by itself?" said the room, out loud, at about the sixth hour, to nobody, about the lamp, and it does not, and it is lit at about the seventh hour by whoever is still in the lane, and nothing in this empire lets a lamp be said to a person.

---

About nine men have known for four years that the foreman of that cut is in a box. None of them has said a word about it and none of them is going to, and about nine people in four other buildings know pieces of other halves of the same thing and have said nothing to anybody either.

For about four years about nine men have known a thing about the foreman which none of them has ever said aloud, and the same fact in other halves is known in about nine other buildings, where it has stayed quiet as well.

**Nobody thanked anybody this morning or this afternoon, and nobody was sent for, and nothing was resolved and nothing was forgiven.** Nothing was said out loud in that room that has not been said out loud in it before.

What the last ten weeks brought to that bay is a price and it is not an event. Nobody was spoken to. Nobody was thanked. Nobody was sent for. A mark in chalk is a mark. Two people of seventeen are at the back of a bay on a written engagement, and the reader is paid forty-five pence a day, and there is a lamp that is not lit until about the seventh hour, and that is the whole of what has happened in that room in ten weeks.
