# Chapter 337: The Same Counting Room About Eleven Feet By Fourteen At The Back Of A Dye End, A Stove Somebody Lit That Morning, And About Four Minutes Of A Rule Said Out Loud To One Woman

The first cold of the season came in the night and whoever got in first lit the stove behind the bench, and the room was warmer than it had any right to be at about the second hour, and the sixteen books were where the sixteen books are.

This is the fourth day of the third week of the ninth month of the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the afternoon, and the dye end had been shut and started again twice since the morning.

She is twenty and her name is Nell Kest, and she has told one woman of the four that she is not paid, and there is a second one in this room now, and it is about four minutes' worth of saying and not one minute more.

---

The woman came in at about the second hour and stayed until the third and had to go back to the shift, and the third one of the three is still at the far end of a lane and comes in at about the sixth hour, and today was not her day and will not be for a week.

She came in from the cold, stayed an hour, and was away again before the middle of it. The third of the three is a lane away and there is a week to wait, and the fourth has not been reached at all.

There was no third person in the room. There has not been a third person in that room in about two years except the person who gets in first on a cold morning, and that is the man who lit the stove, and he went down the lane again before either of them started.

This one is at a dye end two doors down and stands in a doorway in the middle of a shift. **She is not one of the two who cannot read a paragraph**, and that is the only reason she is in this room, and it is a reason and not a favour.

Nell Kest did not ask her anything. She was not asked and she did not ask, and what happened in this room was a woman saying a thing out loud to a woman who had come in out of the cold, and about four minutes of it, and nothing in this empire carries a sentence out of a counting room and sets it in front of a person in a doorway.

---

Nobody else came in. For about two years the only other person through the door has been whoever gets in first when it is cold, and he had gone back down it before either of them began.

"Here is the whole of it and I have nobody else to say it to."

"A person who cannot be read **cannot be asked in writing**, and a person who cannot be asked cannot be told anything at all, and those two are one thing and not two, and I have had both halves of them for about three weeks and neither of them is any use without the other."

"**The arrangement that keeps about four men in a bay out of every book in this empire is the arrangement that loses about nine sheets a year that nobody can account for.** Four men, and a woman at the end of a cold passage, and a man at a rail, and about nine hundred sheets a year, and it is one arrangement and it has been running for nineteen years."

"And I am saying it to you and I am not giving you a method, and I am not giving you a route, and the four are not a route. **It is not a right and it is not a method and it is not going to be used by anybody.**"

---

That is about four minutes, and the woman at the dye end stood in the doorway for all of it, and at the end of it she said the one thing Nell Kest had not expected anybody to say to her in this room in about two years, which was nothing at all, and then she went back to the shift.

**She has not been told that she is not paid.** One of the four has been told that and this is not that, and being told a rule is not being told a figure. About nine people in other buildings have each been told a piece of this and not one of them has been told a wage.

Nell Kest did not say it was about her wage. She did not say it was not about her wage either.

**She said the thing that was true, which is the only kind there is.** The not saying of the other thing is not a lie, and nothing in this empire would have let her say it and have been right.

---

**There is no form for reaching a person who has not been asked**, and she has not written any of it down, and the page she said it on is a sheet from a dyers' firm about four hundred and thirty miles up this river and it is not going anywhere.

There is no form for finding one either, and a thing that cannot be found and cannot be asked is the whole of the price, and the price is paid in people and not in a room.

She put the sheet in the drawer with the rest of what there is.

The sheet went into a drawer along with the other paper. No book was made out of it and nobody was given a key, and no key has ever been offered her.

She did not make a sixteenth book out of what she said and she did not give anybody a key, and nobody has offered her one and she has not asked for one. The sixteenth book still has her own name at the head of it and still has nobody's question against it.

---

The two women she cannot reach are still two women she cannot reach. Saying the rule to a third has not made either of them reachable, and she had known that it would not, and she said it anyway, and about three weeks of standing in doorways is what that cost in minutes, and none of it comes back.

**She is not going up this river this season.** She has not said she is never going to, and she is the only person who knows the difference between those two sentences, and it is still a difference and it is still hers.

There is a woman about nine miles off a building off a road in another town who has told a different woman a different thing, and the two of them have never heard of each other. They are kept apart on purpose and neither of them did the keeping.

---

"Sixteen again?" she said it out loud at about the fourth hour, to the room, about the sixteen books, and it was sixteen again, and a number is the only thing in that room that has never changed and a number cannot be said to a person in this empire, and about four hundred and thirty miles is not walked by anybody on her account.

The dye end shut for the hour and the room went quiet and the stove held for about a quarter of an hour after that before it wanted lighting again, and she let it want.

The hour's shutting took the dye end down and the room went quiet enough to hear the stove. She let it want lighting again and did not light it, and a good while went by with the sixteen books where they are.

Nobody thanked her and nobody sent for her and nothing was resolved. **The four are not a route and the saying is not a method and the woman in the doorway will be told about a wage or she will not, and either way nothing in this empire will have arranged for it**, and the other two are at the far end of a lane and in a dye end and will not hear any of it from her, because being told is a thing that only happens to people somebody asked.
