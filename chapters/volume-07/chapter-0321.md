# Chapter 321: A Room Over A Chandler's Shop Taken By The Week, Four Steps And A Landing, A Nail In About Four Feet Of Bare Wall, And A Finding That Is A Room And Not A Document

The room is over a chandler's shop and it is taken by the week, and there are four steps and a landing to it. There is a table in it and there is a chair, and the chair stands against the wall the bed is against because there is nowhere else in it to put a chair. The window looks at another house's back across the lane and is about a foot wide, and the rest of that wall is bare except for a nail, and nothing has ever hung on that nail.

He is thirty-four or thereabouts. A fair hand is what he has got and the work is to copy out other men's figures in such a way that the totals come out even. Four days a season he stands on somebody's list and this is not one of the four. The house with a door on it is four miles off and his wife is at the foot of its steps, and he is on no list of anybody's this week and did not put himself on one.

This is the fourth day of the third week of the fifth month of the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour, and nothing in that room is lit.

---

He goes through the rest of it about once in four weeks and this is the once, and it is nine years old and about four hundred words long and it is not on a shelf anywhere and there is nowhere in this empire it could be put.

The once in four weeks has not slipped in nine years. He gets to it at the end of a week and not before, and on the whole of the weeks in between it does not occur to him at all, and he has never told anybody either half of that.

They are at the top of the list and they are in their boxes. Nobody has put a question to either of them and nobody is going to, and as for the instrument that put each of them there, nothing in this empire has ever been asked to be set aside and there is no way of asking for it, so it stands and they stay where it put them.

Four men in a bay cannot make sense of a paragraph, and no one has ever put a single question about a number to any of them in the whole of this. A girl of seventeen is at the back of that same bay on a written engagement, and a reader of seventeen is behind her in the same condition, and nobody is going to speak to either of them or thank them or send for them.

At Lowcross there is a bill standing at nineteen pounds three and fourpence, unpaid, and nobody is liable for it. He did not pay it. He is not the reason it is unpaid.

And then the entry that is not a person.

---

**He has been carrying it for about two years as a document.** He found that out about four minutes into the middle of it, stopped, did not start it again, and sat with the table in front of him and the lamp not lit.

It is not a document. A document goes to about nine hundred rooms and a question has to be said in one of them.

**A room is the only kind of thing in this empire that a person can be said in, and there are about nine hundred of them, and the whole of this matter has been trying to get at one man in one of them for about two years without knowing that is what it was doing.**

About nine hundred rooms is a great many rooms, and the one at the end of the cold passage is nine miles off and made of cold stone, and he has traced the two ends of it out on the table with a finger about four times and has never once stood at the far one.

He had it as a hand at the foot of a sheet eight weeks ago, and a hand is the most findable thing about a man there is, and he got that far and stopped there, because the next step out of that is a step out of a room and not a step into one.

There is no way to write to a room. No instrument in this empire carries a question out of one building and sets it in front of a person standing in another, and there is not going to be one. **There is no form in this empire that lets a person be said in anything but a room they came into themselves.**

That is the whole of the finding, and it took about four minutes, and about two years of carrying it in the wrong shape to reach the place where it could be found at all.

---

The other half of it is which room, and he has that answer and it is four words long.

He said it out loud and then did not wait for it, because there is no one in the room to answer it and there has not been for about four minutes.

"How long is that passage?"

It is the room at the far end of a passage about four hundred yards long, running off a flood terrace behind a building off the old river road. The flags of that passage come up cold underfoot in every month of the year. A rail stands in the room standing about four feet off the floor, and a step is worn through in front of the door at the top end of the passage, and there is nobody at that door at any hour of the day.

**He has not been in that room.** A man of about thirty-four in a room over a shop that sells tar has no way into it, no reason to be in it, and no business in it, and all three of those are still true this week.

He has got very good at the carrying of a thing and knows nothing whatever about the thing itself, and being good at one of them has never told him which of them he is.

A woman of about twenty-six is at the other end of that passage and has been there about four years and counts racks, and she is not one of the three clerks in that building and cannot be asked about anything, and no form in this empire has ever been written for putting a question to a person at the bottom of a passage about where anybody else is.

---

And there is one man who has been in that room, and he is the man whose hand is at the foot of about nine hundred sheets a year.

He is sixty-one and has been the second hand since he was forty-two and cannot read a paragraph, and he has been in that room twice in nineteen years, and both of those times it was a season, and both of those times a person told a carrier in a lane where to come.

**A carrier in a lane is the whole of the instrument, and no instrument in this empire stands behind a carrier in a lane.**

He is about nine miles off. He does not know that the room is open, he is not going to be told, and there is no form in this empire in which a man at the end of four hundred yards of cold passage could set a sheet down on a rail and have that be a thing anybody was told about afterwards.

---

And the reason all of it stops there is the reason it stopped the first time.

**A person who is asked is being served, and being served is a party, and a party is the only person a thing can be told what it is going to be used for.**

So the room is where a person can be said in, and the same room is the one place a person can be asked in, and he cannot be asked in the room he signs in, and the season is on, and about nine hundred sheets will go out of that room before the end of it.

One of them goes to the district he lives in, and if he signs it his hand will be at the foot of it, and he will not be told, and there is nothing in this empire that would tell him.

---

He wrote out one sum before the light went, for a man who cannot pay him until the spring. It came to eleven shillings and it took about nine minutes, and that was the whole of the work of that day.

The sum took about nine minutes and the paper is still lying on the table beside the copy, and the man it is for will have it by the spring and will know nothing whatever about the room it was written in or the hour.

His wife was at the foot of the four steps at the end of it. She did not ask him how the week had gone, and she has not asked him that in about two years now, and she told him out loud some weeks back that she is not going to start.

**He did not ask her about any of it.** The reason is the one he gave her the last time he did a thing of this kind, six weeks before he did it, out loud and on purpose and without asking her first, and the reason was that if he had asked she would have said no or said yes and been frightened, and both of those would have been him arranging the room.

The woman of about forty who carries a scuttle came up the four steps at about the seventh hour and lit the lamp, as she does twice a week. She is not in this matter and cannot be asked what she sees on those steps, and he has never once asked her, and **there is no form in this empire that obliges anybody to ask her.**

He did not go and look and he is not going to go and look. The four hundred and thirty miles is not walked and the nine miles is not walked by anybody on his account.

Nobody was sent and nobody was thanked and nothing was resolved, and the count of things anybody has asked out loud in this matter is seven, and he did not move it, and it is not going to be eight this month or the next.

**The finding he has been carrying for two years is a room.** It took him about four minutes to find that out and about two years to get to the place where it could be found, and it is in his head, and there is no place in this empire it could be put.

The lamp was lit by somebody else and the table had nothing on it and about four feet of wall had a nail in it and nothing on the nail, and a man of about sixty-one is about nine miles off a room he has been in twice in nineteen years, and nobody is going to tell him.
