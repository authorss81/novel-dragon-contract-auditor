# Chapter 350: The Same Room A Week Later, The Nail In About Four Feet Of Wall With Nothing Ever Hung On It, And A Man At A Table With A Number In His Head That He Cannot Give Away

The lamp in that room is not lit until about the seventh hour and it is about the fifth hour, and the smell of tar and rope and lamp oil comes up between the boards all day, and about four feet of the wall by the window has a nail in it and nothing has ever hung on it.

The table has nothing on it. He is thirty-four or thereabouts and the hand is a fair one and the work is to copy out other men's figures so that the totals come out even.

This is the fourth day of the fourth week of the twelfth month of the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fifth hour, and the lamp in that room is not lit and is not going to be lit.

---

He put his hand flat on the table and he did not light the lamp, and that is the whole of what he did this week that anybody could have found worth finding, and nobody was in a position to find any of it.

**No form for giving away what a person has been told in a room exists in this empire, and none for handing a number on.** He has had the number for about two years and he has not given it away and he is not going to, and the reason is that the giving of it would be a list, and a list of persons is a company, and a company is a heading, and a heading is what a person is turned into when the thing gets bigger than a room.

---

About nine hundred sheets a year go out of a room about nine miles off with a hand at the foot of every one of them, and the hand is a man's, and he is sixty-one, and he has been the second hand since he was forty-two, and he cannot read a paragraph and has never been asked anything by anybody at all.

About nine of them a year are not what the person above him wrote, and he signed those, and he is the only person alive who knows which ones they were, and he has said the whole of it out loud in a room with a rail in it twice in nineteen years, and a clerk of about fifty-five wrote none of it either time. **There is no form against it for a man to put a mark beside a sum and call it a disagreement.**

There is no column for a no and there never has been one, and the whole of what he wants is about four minutes to say no to about half of them and yes to the rest. About nine people were in that room the second time and nobody thanked him, and there is not one form anywhere that would oblige anybody to.

---

Nothing has moved. A woman of fifty-one is in a box on a bank four hundred yards up a lane off the Slade, and a woman of twenty is in one about four hundred and thirty miles up this river in a furnace town, and the form that put the two of them there has not been asked to be void by anybody. **The nine miles off a building off the old river road is a man of about sixty-one and is neither of the two women**, and the two of them are about four hundred and thirty miles apart and neither one has ever heard of the other one. **Nothing in this empire has ever been asked to be void** and there is no procedure for asking, and the person who administers one is a clerk who is not a villain and has done about nine hundred of them correctly.

The question a clerk of about fifty-five wrote into the second of eleven books on her own motion is on a shelf under a window with the pane out of it, and it is still a question, and it carries no period, **and nobody has asked her about it a second time and nobody is going to.**

A guarantee stands offered and unanswered on about four hundred and forty foot of bank with about sixty children under sixteen inside it. It has been referred to in the words of the people who made it and it has not been printed in its own words, and no child in it has a name anybody has said out loud. About nineteen people are still behind that bank.

**A bill at Lowcross stands at nineteen pounds three and fourpence and is unpaid and nobody is liable for it.** Nothing in these fifty chapters has paid it or funded it or forgiven it, and nobody in this paragraph is the reason it is unpaid.

---

About four men in a bay cannot make sense of a paragraph and not one question about a number has ever been put to any of them. **A man of about fifty-five with a chain is not one of them and says so.** A man of about sixty-one is not one of them either and is nine miles off in a different building. A girl of seventeen and a reader of seventeen are at the back of that bay on a written engagement, and neither has been spoken to, neither has been thanked, neither has been sent for, and neither is going to start.

**A figure at the far end of a cold passage is in a woman's head and has gone up.** It is not being handed on and will not get a heading.

Two people are about four hundred and thirty miles apart. They have never met, neither of them knows the other one exists, and nothing in this empire would let either of them be told. A bundle is shut. Two documents are two documents. Two sealed pages are shut and there is no third refusal of either and nobody has asked to open either. Nobody wrote the substituted wording and nobody wrote the sentence about public necessity, the second half of the whole of this is not asked, and the who is not named.

---

Nobody has been thanked. Nobody has been sent for. Nothing has been forgiven and nothing has been resolved, and the count of things anybody has asked out loud in this matter is the same as it was ten weeks ago and it is going to be the same in ten more, and the figure is not written here.

**He has the number and he cannot give it away and has not given it away**, and the lane between that room and anything else in this is not walked by anybody on his account, and about four hundred and thirty miles is not walked by anybody at all. The next asking is not anybody's yet.

"Is that nail still in the wall?" he said it out loud at about the fifth hour, to the room, about the nail, and it is, and it has been since he came, and nothing has ever hung on it, and nothing in this empire lets a nail be said to a person.

He put his hand flat on the table. The lamp was not lit.
