# Chapter 315: Four Hundred Yards Up The Lane From The Bay, A Column With No Heading Over It, And Nineteen Days Of A Wage

The counting room of the Slade Cut Company is four hundred yards up a lane off the Slade and the bay is at the bottom of the lane and the hired stone store is at the top of it, and a man who works at one end of that lane can see the roof of the other end of it on a day with any light in it at all.

His name is Rennick Adley and he is thirty-eight and he is a book-keeper, and he has kept the Company's books since the second month of next year.

Four shillings a year is the whole of the Company's money and all of it is the rent of the store, taken out four times a year at a shilling a quarter, and there is no money in this building behind that.

This is the second day of the first week of the fourth month of the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the morning, and there is a bench along one wall and a shutter half up and about nine men in and out of the lane in a day.

---

He built a column in the second month of next year and it has been in the second of the Company's books ever since and it is about two hands wide and it is ruled down both sides like any other.

The shutter on the side of the lane has not moved since before the frost, and he has mentioned it twice to the person who rents the room, and nobody is going to mention it a third time.

It has nothing in it. Not one name, not one figure, not one day. He ruled it in the second month of next year because **a name in a column is a thing a person can be served at**, and a person who can be served at is a party, and a party is the only person who can be told what a thing is going to be used for, and he had worked that out at about the fourth hour of an ordinary day and has not needed to work it out since.

**He has never put a heading over it either,** and that is the other half of it, and he only got to that half this week.

He turned it over for two days and got it on the second, and then he wrote it out in a fair hand on a piece of waste paper and put the waste paper at the bottom of the box, and it says this:

*A heading over a column makes it a machine, and a machine is asked things and does not mind about it. Take the heading off and what is left is a person, and a person is only ever asked one way, out loud in a room, and being asked like that makes them a party.*

**He read it twice and did not tear it up, and he has not shown it to anybody,** and he is not going to, and there is no form in this empire that would let a man show a sheet of waste paper to a person and have the showing be of no consequence to either of them.

**A heading is what makes a column a machine, and it is not the names.** He had it the wrong way round for about four months, and the waste paper is the correction, and nobody has seen it.

---

The money for nineteen days of a crew is in a weigh-house ledger about half a mile off and it is called *held* and the word *held* was chosen in about four minutes among three other words by a person who was not thinking about us.

Nineteen days of a crew's money is not a sum anybody in the building can see the inside of. He has looked at it about four times with two other people and has got nowhere, and the word standing against it in a ledger half a mile off was picked out of three others by somebody thinking about a column.

Nineteen days out of seventeen shillings and a penny the week, paid in full, three pounds and eleven and threepence, and nobody is going to be told what it is held against, and **there is no form in this empire that will let nineteen days of a wage be entered as owed by anybody.** He has looked. He has looked twice, and he has looked with two other people.

**His own wages are held against a roof he put there on purpose,** and that one he can account for, and it is the only one on the four hundred yards of this lane he can account for.

---

He shut the second one of the Company's papers at about the fourth hour and went out into the lane, because the shutter wants looking at, and the shutter is not what he went out for.

He shut the paper and out he went into the lane; the light on the flags of it went out and came back with the weather while he was standing there, and about nine men pass up and down it in a day and none of them has ever wanted to know what it is he is about.

The bay is at the bottom of it about nine foot by eleven and the store is cold in every month of the year, and the four shillings a year is the rent of the store, and there is a mark in chalk on a wall of that bay at the height of the third link of a chain, and the chain is on a man.

**The cross means nothing and is nobody's,** and it is about four years off being anybody's evidence, and nothing in that lane is going to convert it, and he did not go in to look at it.

About four men in that bay cannot read a paragraph. They have never been asked one question about a number in nine years and they are not going to be, and he did not ask them one on his way past and he is not going to.

A man of about fifty-five with a chain was in there in the morning and said nothing. **He is not one of the four and says so himself,** and putting him down as one of them would be a way for a list of four to go wrong, and he has known for about four years that findability does not work on him, and he has never once used it, and he has said out loud that he wants it said he knew what he was doing.

At the back of that bay, on a written engagement at the rate a list is set at, there are two of seventeen, a girl and a reader, and **neither of them has been spoken to and neither of them has been thanked and neither of them has been sent for.** He went up the lane past the door without going in and without stopping, and that is the whole of what he did about them.

---

The foreman of fifty-one was in the bay at about the fourth hour and he did not cross the lane to where she was standing.

He owes him a second sentence and has owed it since the third month of the year after, and he wants the rest of it, and he has wanted it since the same month, and he has not asked and is not going to. **The day he asks is the day she stops saying the half she has got.** About nine men in that bay know that she is in a box, and not one of them has said a word about it, and there is no form in this empire that would turn nine mouths into a document, and nobody has asked them and nobody is going to.

He went back up the lane and shut the shutter and wrote the day's figures out in a fair hand so that they came out even, which is the ordinary part of a day and is the whole of what he is paid for.

The day's figures came out even on the paper he shut at the fourth hour, and the waste paper was still at the bottom of the box where he put it, and he has never once copied a figure off one and onto the other, and nobody has ever asked him which of the two is the record.

---

A man of about thirty-four came up the lane at about the fifth hour and said, "Are they working in there?"

"No."

"Then why is the light on?"

"There is no light on and the shutter on this side is half up and it has been half up since before the frost, and I have told the person who rents it twice."

The man went down the lane and did not ask anything else and did not stop, and nobody thanked anybody, and nothing was sent for anybody.

**Not one form in this empire would let a book-keeper refuse a heading in writing and keep the refusal.** That is the reason the second of the Company's papers has a ruled column in it with nothing in it and nothing over it, and no form in this empire can hold a column against a person, and that is the whole of why the column is a person and not a machine.
