# Chapter 326: The North Bay, The South End Of A Hired Store Four Hundred Yards Up The Same Lane, And One Sentence Of Hers Said Out Loud A Second Time

The bay stands at the north end of a lane off the Slade and it is about nine foot by eleven, and it is longer than that where the bench is, and there is a lamp in it that is not lit until about the seventh hour.

The hired stone store is four hundred yards up the same lane. It is cold in every month of the year. There is a bench along one wall, and on a wall of it there is a mark in chalk at the height of the third link of a chain, and the chain is on a man.

She is fifty-one. Her name is Halla Wray. She has had the foreman's job on that cut for nine years, which is nine years of a thing nine families agreed to between them in the open, and it is not an office and it is not on any list of offices anywhere.

**She has been inside a box since the first week of the seventh month of the year after, put there by a class, and nobody has ever asked her about it.** That is the whole of it, and it is about four years old now, and it has not been a single morning since.

This is the fourth day of the fourth week of the sixth month of the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the morning, and the gate was shut at about the fourth hour and the man at it is a man of about forty who does not argue.

---

**Where does the number four come from?** That is what the man of about fifty-five with the chain said, out loud, in the bay, at about the fifth hour, to nobody in particular, and it is the only thing he has said in about four years that was not said to a wall.

He is not one of the four men in that bay who cannot read a paragraph, and he says so himself. Putting him down as one of them would be a way for a list of four to go wrong, and he has known for about four years that findability does not work on him and has never once used it, and he has said out loud that he wants it said that he knew what he was doing.

Nobody answered him. Nobody asked him one thing about a number and nobody is going to, and he did not ask anybody anything either, and the two of them have not asked each other anything in about four years.

Nobody has ever taken the chain off him, and about four years is how long he has gone without putting a question to anybody about a figure or having one put to him about one.

Four men in that bay cannot read a paragraph. Nobody has put a question about a number to any of them in nine years and nobody did this morning and nobody in this room is going to, and the four of them are not a method and are not a route to anything and are not going to be used by anybody in this matter.

---

The thing that reached her that morning reached her at second hand, and it is about a room she has never been inside and is never going to be inside.

A man of about thirty came off a job at about the fourth hour, told her one thing in about nine words, and went down the lane. The thing was that a question cannot be put in the one building that makes the man who signs at the foot of a sheet, and that the arrangement which has kept about nine hundred sheets a year correct for nineteen years is the arrangement which has kept anybody from asking that man one question.

**She said one sentence about it out loud, in the bay, to about nine men, and it is the same sentence she said about herself a long time ago, and this is the second time, and it is not a coincidence and it is not a system.**

"I have been in a box for about four years and nobody has asked me about it, and the reason is that nobody can be asked about a box, and a box is put in by a class, and a class is a heading, and a heading is a company."

"And that is not only mine. It is about nine miles off and it is about four hundred and thirty miles up this river, and it is the same arrangement in both of those places, and it is the arrangement nine of us agreed to in a shed, and it did not ask us either."

Nobody asked her about it. Nobody in that bay said one word back, and nine mouths that have said nothing for about four years said nothing again, and there is no form in this empire that would let nine men be asked whether they agree with what they have all been saying nothing about, and she is not going to ask them.

She said one sentence out loud to all of them, and the saying of it is not a finding and not a document, and it is going to stay a sentence in a room with nobody in authority in it and nothing else.

---

At the back of that bay, on a written engagement at the rate a list is set at, there are two of seventeen, a girl and a reader.

A reader is paid forty-five pence a day and four days a week, and what a reader's day comes to is anywhere between four pence and a shilling according to what is put in front of her.

**Neither of the two has been spoken to this week and neither of them is going to be spoken to by anybody in this matter.** Neither of them is thanked and neither of them is sent for and neither of them is going to start, and there is room at the back of that bay for two and there has been for about four weeks, and nobody says why and nobody is going to.

If the girl of seventeen were asked she would say yes, and nobody has asked her, and so nobody knows that. No paper in this empire asks a person of seventeen whether she would rather not, and there is no other way of finding out, and so the answer nobody has is the answer nobody has.

The lamp in that bay was not lit at about the seventh hour either, because the gate was shut and the store was shut and the bay was left with a bench in it and a mark in chalk on a wall.

**The cross means nothing and is nobody's and is about four years off being anybody's evidence**, and the chain is on a man, and nothing in that lane is going to turn it into anything, and she did not go and look at it and is not going to.

The cross sits about four feet off the ground on a wall, long enough to have been looked at every day by men who cannot make sense of a paragraph. Nobody put it there on any paper and nobody has ever claimed it and nothing in the lane off the Slade is going to.

---

**A second sentence is owed to a book-keeper four hundred yards up that lane, and it has been owed since the third month of the year after.**

He wants the rest of it and has wanted it since the same month, and he has not asked and is not going to, and the day he asks is the day she stops saying the half she has got, and nobody has asked in this volume and nobody is going to.

Four hundred yards up the lane a book-keeper wants the rest of a sentence, has wanted it long enough and means to go on wanting it; there is no gate anywhere in the lane and nothing has ever stopped anybody walking up it, and he has not walked up it.

There is no form in this empire in which a woman who has been in a box for about four years and never asked can put what she knows beside what anybody else knows, and she said the sentence out loud in a bay to about nine men, and it is in nine people and not in a book, and that is the whole of what she got for it.

Nobody thanked anybody this morning and nobody was sent for and nothing was resolved, and nobody has ever asked that the form which put her in the box be set aside, and no form in this empire has ever been asked to be set aside, and there is no procedure for asking.
