# Chapter 333: The Counting Room At The Top Of That Same Lane, A Waste Paper At The Bottom Of A Box, And The Half Of The Finding That Is A Man Turning Into A Piece Of Paper

The counting room at the top of that lane has a shutter half up on this side and it has been half up since before the frost, and the light comes off the flags and goes out again according to the weather.

He is thirty-eight, and his name is Rennick Adley, and he has kept the Company's books since the second month of next year, and that is not a figure he computes from, and nobody has asked him to.

This is the fourth day of the third week of the eighth month of the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the afternoon, and the bay at the bottom of that lane is shut and its lamp is not on.

---

Under a rule at the bottom of a box that does not belong to the Company there is a piece of waste paper, and it has been there since before the frost.

He has read it twice. He has not torn it up, he has not shown it to anybody, and he is not going to, and the whole of the deciding took him about nine minutes the first time and about four the second. The showing is not the difficulty. A man with no office who shows a page to another man hands that man a thing the two of them then owe each other, and nothing in this empire will take that kind of debt into a book.

**There is no form for holding a sentence that a person owes him**, and a fair hand is a fair hand, and a hand is not a hand anybody can be served at.

---

The last ten days gave him the half of it he did not have. He had the rest since the middle of the fifth month: that the one man who could answer any of it is about nine miles off in a room he has no way into, and that there is nothing in this empire that lets anybody ask a man in a room to wait.

The half he has now is worse than that, and it is about himself. **A man who is owed a sentence cannot go and ask for it without becoming a document.**

A document is the only thing this empire has ever been able to put in front of about nine hundred rooms at once, and he has watched it done more times than he can count. A thing goes out on a form, the form goes to a heading, the heading is a company, and the company is nine hundred rooms with a person in each of them who cannot be asked anything whatever. **The whole of the machinery of asking at a scale is the machinery of turning people into companies**, and that is the thing this has been about for six volumes, and it has never once been said out loud in a room that was going to be written down.

**Nine hundred rooms is a scale and a scale wants a class, and a class is a heading, and a heading is a company, and a company can hold wages against and take to law without anybody in it ever being told it has been done.**

If he wrote the sentence down, then he would be a document about one woman. A document about one woman goes to a heading, and the heading would be a foreman of the Slade Cut, and the class would take her side or his, and she would never be asked whether she had ever said it.

**He is not going to write it down.** Not her name, not the sentence, not the month, not the lane, and not a list of any of them.

---

There is a column in the second of the Company's papers ruled down both sides like any other, and there is nothing in it. He ruled it himself in the second month of next year, because a name in a column is a thing a person can be served at, and he has never put a heading over it and he is not going to.

What he has is a column, a piece of waste paper, and a finding nobody has ever been asked to account for. The not asking is the whole of what has happened to all three of them in about four years, and the not asking is the same arrangement that is nine miles off keeping about nine hundred sheets a year correct, and he has known that for a while and has done nothing with it.

**A column with a heading over it is a machine, and a column with no heading over it is a person**, and a person is only ever asked one way, which is out loud in a room, and he is never going to be in a room with the woman of fifty-one.

---

His own wages are held against a roof he put there on purpose. That one he can account for to the penny and it is the only one on that length of lane he can account for.

Nineteen days of a crew's money went out of a building and stopped in a weigh-house ledger about half a mile from here, and the word that stands against it in that ledger is *held*. Somebody picked the word in about four minutes out of three others, thinking about a column and not about anybody.

The sum is three pounds and eleven and threepence. It is nineteen days at seventeen shillings and a penny the week, it is paid in full, and nobody is ever going to be told what it is held against.

**The two of them are not the same thing and he has never once put them in one column, and he is not going to this afternoon either.**

One of the two is a debt he entered himself on a day when the alternative was a worse figure on a roof he had put there. The other is a sum that walked out of a building in seventeen weeks and stopped somewhere between a word and an office, and about four hundred blanks came back with it and none of them filled.

Both of them are called held. **A word does that**: somebody picks it out of three others for a column and in about four years it is the name of a thing nobody can see into, and two people standing in different buildings both say the word and neither of them means the other one.

He thought about the four hundred blanks this afternoon for about as long as it takes to write out a day's figures, and then he stopped thinking about them, and the stopping is the discipline and not the indifference.

---

A man came in at about the fourth hour to have a figure looked at because it was wrong somewhere, and he gave him the answer he gives everybody, which is that the figure is not wrong in this room.

**There is no form for telling a man which of nine hundred rooms a thing went wrong in**, and the man did not press him and did not go and look, and the same thing happened about four times a season, and each of the four walked away with the answer and thought it a stone wall, and it is not a wall, and he has never once been in a position to say so.

Then he wrote out the day's figures so that they came out even, which is the ordinary part of a day and the whole of what he is paid for.

"Is that paper still where I put it?" he said it out loud at about the fifth hour, to the shutter, about the paper, and the paper is at the bottom of a box and nobody has seen it and he is not going to show it to anybody, and nothing in this empire would let a piece of waste paper be received by a person.

Nobody put a question to him this afternoon and nobody has for about four years. Nothing was sent for and nothing was thanked, and the shutter stayed half up and he stayed behind it.

Four hundred yards down that lane a foreman of fifty-one is still carrying the half of a sentence she has been giving him since the third month of the year after, and he has wanted the rest of it for the same length of time, and he did not go down there to say so, and he is not going to. **The day he says it is the day she gives the half back**, and he has worked that out in about four minutes and has never mentioned it to anybody including her.
