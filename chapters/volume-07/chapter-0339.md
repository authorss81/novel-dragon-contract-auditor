# Chapter 339: The Room Over A Chandler's Shop Taken By The Week, A Table And A Chair And About Four Feet Of Wall With A Nail In It, A Lamp Unlit At The Fourth Hour, And A Thing He Does Not Do And Is Not Thanked For

The lamp in that room is not lit until about the seventh hour and it is about the fourth hour now, and this is the third week running he has been in the room at the fourth hour with the lamp off, and the shop underneath keeps tar and rope and lamp oil and the smell of it comes up between the boards all day.

He is thirty-four or thereabouts and the hand is a fair one and the work is to copy out other men's figures so that the totals come out even, and there are four days a season he stands on somebody's list and this is not one of the four.

This is the second day of the first week of the tenth month of the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour.

---

He is not at a stair. Nobody sent him and he is not going up it and nothing in this empire would let anybody send him, and the reason is not a rule anybody made.

The reason is that two instruments require her in any room he is in and they are not one instrument, and the woman of about twenty-four at the other end of that counter is not in this room and is not being sent for and is not going to be told anything. **There is no form for standing in a room and being the reason a thing did not happen, and there never was one, and nobody in this empire has to account for a thing they did not do.**

**There is no form for telling the person it did not happen to, either**, and she is at a counter four miles off and she is not going to be told, and that is the same absence as the not asking and it is the third time in this matter that one absence has turned up wearing a different shape.

She has been at that counter two years. She has wanted to be asked something every working day since about the seventh month of next year, and nobody has asked her.

He is not going up a stair to arrange for it, and she is not told that he has decided not to, and about nine hundred rooms a year have a person in each of them who has wanted to be asked something and has not been.

---

The undertaking of the fourth of the first month is live. It is unretired and it is unamended and it has not been exercised, and this is not the first month it has gone that way and it is not going to be the last, and nobody is going to give it another name or another number this month.

**The reason is the same one it has always been, and it is a fact about her and not about him, and he has no right to it and does not have it.** A person who is not asked has not been given a duty, and she has known that since about the seventh month of next year, and she has never once known that it did not apply to her, and the reason the undertaking sits where it sits is that somebody never put it to her in the first place.

He has thought about going up that stair about four times in about two years. Each time he got as far as the bottom of it and each time he went back down, and nothing happened on any of the four occasions, and nobody was sent and nobody was thanked and no instrument anywhere in this empire records a man deciding not to climb a stair.

---

He did not go and look and he is not going to go and look. The four hundred and thirty miles is not walked and it is not going to be walked by anybody on his account, and the nine miles is not walked by anybody in this room this week.

**He did not light the lamp.** He has not lit it before the seventh hour in about two years and there is no reason he has ever given for that and the reason is that the lamp is a thing a person does at the end of a day and not at the beginning of one, and he has never written that down either.

About four feet of that wall has had a nail in it since he came and nothing has ever hung on it. About nine hundred sheets a year go out of a room nine miles off with a hand at the foot of every one of them, and the hand is a man's, and he is sixty-one, and he has never been asked anything by anybody, including the two of them who have wanted to.

---

"Is that nail for anything?" he said it out loud at about the fifth hour, to the wall, about the nail, and the wall did not answer and it is not going to, and nothing in this empire lets a nail be said to a person.

Before the light went he copied out a column for a man who cannot settle until the spring. It came to eleven shillings and took about nine minutes, and the man will have it when the season turns and will never know the light was off while he did it.

At the end of it there was a woman standing at the bottom of four steps, in the way there is of a person who lives at the bottom of them. She has told him in as many words that she is not going to ask him about his week, and he did not take her up on it, and he was not going to ask her to change her position either.

**Nothing in this empire lets a man put to his wife the question of whether she would mind being told a thing before she is told it.** That absence is why he told her anyway the once, about six weeks before he did the thing, and it is why he will not tell her this.

---

Nobody thanked him. Nobody has ever thanked him and nobody is going to. **Nothing in this empire requires a person to be thanked for saying a thing out loud, or for not saying one, or for standing in a room and going down four steps and not going up a stair**, and there is not going to be one, and this is the cheapest thing anybody in this matter has ever got away with and it is not cheap and it is not going to get cheaper.

About nine people know where he is. Not one of them is going to be told by him that he is not going up a stair, and the four hundred yards of lane between this room and a bay is not walked this week either.

The lamp was lit at about the seventh hour by somebody else. The table had nothing on it and the nail had nothing on it and the count of things anybody has asked out loud in this matter did not move, and it is not going to.
