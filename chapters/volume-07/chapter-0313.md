# Chapter 313: The Other End Of The Same Counter, And A Law Written About Two Hundred Years Ago By People With Better Sense

It is the same Table and the same flight and the same two hours to the sixth, and it is the other end of the same counter, and about nine people come up that stair in a day and not one of the nine has ever asked her anything.

Behind her is the same board with the same four things on it and not one of them a time, and under it a drawer, and in the drawer four things, none of which has been opened.

She is twenty-four and her name is Tamsin Rook, she has been at this counter about two years, and she is not a lawyer.

At the other end of it stands a woman of thirty-four who has been at that counter eleven years, and there is a man doing the copies at the far end, and the woman of about twenty is at the back with her own shelf and has not been in the room a year yet.

This is the fourth day of the third week of the third month of the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fifth hour, and there are about four people in that room, and none of the four has asked her anything.

---

A woman of about thirty-two came up the stair at about that hour about a letter and said, "Is it kept here?"

The same four items stand written on the board behind her, and the fourth item is the one nobody can account for. Two years of looking at it has not got it any clearer and has not got it any worse, and she has looked at it in every week of the two.

"No, and there is no such thing kept and none of it is told, and that is the way of it here and not a favour to you."

She says that to about nine people a week and it is the truth and it is not a kindness, and a question at a counter about a letter is a question about the letter.

Then a man of about fifty wanted a thing put back two years and could not have it and was not told why and went down the stair.

Nobody has asked her anything this week. Nobody has asked her anything this month. A man came up that stair a good while back wanting nothing at all and asked her one question in the plainest words anybody has used in this room in two years, and she did not answer it.

She has wanted to be asked every working day for as long as she has been on this counter, and **a person who is not asked has not been given a duty, and she has known that since about the seventh month of next year**, and she has never once known that it did not apply to her.

---

She said the whole of what she had out loud at about that hour, to the room, to nobody in particular.

"**The reason that form works is that the law does not need a person.** A border household is not a person either, and the law was written by people who had noticed that about two hundred years ago **and had better sense about it than we have.**"

"That is all of it. It does not care in the slightest whether there is a name on the thing or not, because there is not going to be one. There is a class and a class cannot be told what a thing is going to be used for, because a class does not have a use, it has a size, and you cannot serve a size at anybody."

She said that to the room and then she said the other half of it, which is the half that took her two years.

"It asks about a class. **A class is a heading, and a heading is a company,** and a company is a thing this empire can hold wages against and give letters to and take to law. So the one form in this empire that ever worked at anything like a scale is the one form that turns about nine hundred rooms into a company. And it has been standing there about two hundred years and it has been used once, and nobody has copied it, and it is not because nobody noticed."

"Why is it then," the man of about thirty at the far end said, without turning round.

"Because a person did not want about nine hundred rooms to be a company, and a person is the only one of those two who can decide anything, and there was a person in it at the time."

---

**The man at the far end went on with the copies and said nothing else, and he is not a person anybody in that room is obliged to answer for anything.** Fourpence a copy is what he is paid and that is the whole of his part in it.

He is at the far end and does the copies for fourpence each, and nine or so come up the stair in a day, and most of them are gone back down it before he has finished one. He has been in the room about a year and has not been spoken to once.

Fourpence a copy, and a person who brings the same figure up that stair twice is charged twice, and nobody has ever been told that twice is a word in a charge.

**There is no form in this empire that has ever been able to put a person in a room and ask that person a thing,** and the reason is the same one that is in the room, and she has never worked out whether that makes her right or only loud, and she has not asked anybody, and there is no form in this empire for asking a person which of those two it is.

After the counter she went to the room she rents two streets off a street that goes down to the river road, and **the landlord has never once been inside it and has never once asked to.**

---

What is under that table is hers and is not a copy of anything and has no office on it, and there are two things in it.

The first is a line in her own hand recording that a clerk of thirty-four said no on a stair and gave no reason, and she put it there on her own motion at nobody's request, and it is still the only thing about another person in there.

The second is four lines taken off the fee board off a shelf, and the fourth of the four is in a hand that she and two other people at that counter cannot account for.

There is a second page with two boxes drawn on it by hand, and the same name is written in both of them, and she is not going to say in this room what the two boxes are, and nobody has asked her.

**She is not going to give them a heading.** A heading is what a company is made of, and the name in them is a woman of thirty-four who has been at the other end of the same counter for eleven years, and nobody has ever asked her about anything, and a company is a thing this empire can hold wages against and take to law, and she is not going to be the one who puts a woman in one.

There is a third page and it is empty and she is not going to rule a line on it, and **there is no form in this empire that will take a line back out of a page a woman keeps for herself**, so that whatever goes in there stays in there for as long as she is the one holding it.

The third page in her own book has been blank since the day she bought it, and she has looked at it about twice a season, and every time she has put something else on the shelf above it instead.

---

The undertaking of the fourth of the first month is live and unretired and unamended and has not been exercised.

She is not going to make a new reason out of this month. The last reason was about four minutes long and it was that nobody asked her, and nobody has asked her this month either, and there is nothing in a month in which nobody asks you to make a new reason out of.

**There is no form in this empire for telling a clerk that a form about a household was the reason an answer came in three weeks,** and there is never going to be one.

Not one form in this empire was ever written by a person who wanted to be asked something in return for writing it.

The decision about whether she trusts a man's judgment is not made, and it is not in this room, and it is not going to be made this month, and nobody in this empire is going to be told when it is made if it ever is.

---

**She put the lamp out at about the seventh hour, and the board on the wall had the rent on it and nothing else,** and she has not asked the landlord for a second room and is not going to.

She has read the board on the wall more often than she has read anything else in the room, and the lamp went out about an hour before it could be made out at all, and about two years of a wall is what it has been good for.

The four things in the drawer under that counter are where they were. The fourth of the four is the one she has not looked at, and it is not opened, and it is not sent, and the other three have not been either.

Nobody thanked her and nobody thanked the woman of thirty-four at the other end of the counter and nobody sent for anybody, and the two boxes on the second page have got the same name in both of them and no heading over either, and they are going to be like that for as long as she is the one keeping them.
