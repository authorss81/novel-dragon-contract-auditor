# Chapter 60: A Column of None Named

The register of pending duties is a book and a board, and the book has five columns, and she had written four of them nine hundred times.

> *Date. Household. Duty. Next review. A shilling a copy.*

The board above the window is the same five things in chalk and it can be read by anybody who cannot pay a shilling, which is the only argument she has ever been able to make for having two, and the argument is not in the book.

What she could not do for three weeks was the fourth column.

A next review is a date. A date is a thing that happens. A thing that happens has a person standing at it, and that is the sentence she has had on a wall since the second of the seventh month of last year, in a form nobody asked her for: *a review date with no named person is not a review.*

And there was no review. Not one.

The district had not decided whether to run the process next season and had not been asked by her and would not be asked by her. The Cinder Court had escalated the sealed instruction to a full sitting with jurisdiction on its own motion and there was no date on it, because a thing on a list with a date and nobody's name on it is a date and not a review, which is a sentence she has now heard a man in a good coat's building say out loud in a room and did not enjoy. Restitution is not a word this court can use, and she is not going to teach it a new word in the twelfth month, and the magistrate will not be asked, and the flue in the old records room is still booked for the fourteenth of the second month and the fourth cycle's copies are still not made because there is still no fourth clerk.

So she sat down at the window on the fourth of the first month with a new book — not the board, the book, the one with the columns — and entered the thirty-one, one at a time, in nine days, in her own hand, and she entered four of the five columns out of five with the case as plain as a case can be made.

> *Sixth year, third cycle. Return marked and signed, first week of the cycle. The sentence of this court of the twelfth of the ninth month of last year, applied in letters, without a hearing. Duty entered as the term stands: the holder of the said property is joined as guarantor until full review of the matter. Next review: none named.*

Thirty-one times, in her own hand, over nine days, with the door, the name, the word *seen* or the word *told*, and the date of the returning officer's walk against it.

*Next review: none named.*

She put the word down thirty-one times and it took nine days and it is the most honest thing that book has ever held, and a person coming in at the window reads it and thinks the clerk has not bothered, and the clerk has bothered for nine days.

---

The first person to come to the window with one of the thirty-one was on the sixth of the first month, at about the third hour, and she was not a claimant and she did not want to complain, and she is called Iolanthe Rees and she is sixty and she has paid a hundred and forty pounds of ground rent on two strips above the upper lane in twenty years.

"Is it you, then," she said. "The board."

"Yes."

"I have read it and I have read my line. Seventhsomething Rees." She did not have her spectacles and she did not ask for them. "I want to know one thing and then I will go, and I have come at the third hour because I did not want to be seen doing this at the window with a queue behind me, because I have paid ground rent in this district for twenty years and I am not going to stand here being the woman on the board."

"Ask."

"Am I going to be called into a room."

Ivet Sarn looked at the book, and at the board, and at the wall, and there were about nine things she could have said and she said the true one, which is that she did not know, in a form that is on the page.

"I am not going to call you into a room," she said. "Nobody in this building has asked me to and I would have to write your name on a slate with a date on it and I am not going to. And that is not the same as a promise that nobody ever will, because there is a high court with four orders in it and one of the orders says that a person must be heard before anything joins them, and if anybody ever does come for you, I will have to tell you that I did not call you, and I will have to say that in a room, and you are entitled to a shilling's copy of this."

"Will you write it down."

"I will write it down now, and I will read it back, and you may pay a shilling for it, and it is a shilling because everything on this board is a shilling a copy and the day it stops being one is the day nobody in a flood district can prove a thing."

> *Entered at the request of the household, and it was the household that asked and I am glad it is written that way. Iolanthe Rees, of the two strips above the upper lane, of the thirty-one named in the fourth week of the ninth month, asks whether she is to be called into a room. She is not being called. Nobody in this office has asked that she be called, no slate carries her name, and this court has not ordered anything in this matter. If any person is ever to be heard about the duty entered against her household, that person will be named on a slate with a date on it and she may come to this window and buy a shilling's copy of the line. The clerk says now, in the room, to the household: that line will be kept. The clerk cannot promise what any other office or any other court will do and the clerk has been wrong four times in twelve months and says so when asked.
>
> *I. Sarn, clerk of this court, the sixth of the first month, at the third hour, read back twice at the sill.*

Iolanthe Rees paid a shilling, and gave her name for the line, and asked whether anybody had been in her house.

"Three of the four times, by the word on your card, and the fourth is a word that is not a name and it is one of eleven, and I have written the eleven down separately and it is on the wall and it is a worse thing to read than your line."

"Then I have been in it four times and did not know."

"You have been in it four times and did not know, and that is the finding and it is the whole of what I can do about it today, and I have been doing this for nine years and a woman of sixty came in at the third hour so as not to be seen, and that is the second time this month that somebody has told me they came at an hour when nobody could see, and I am putting the number of those on the wall as well."

---

On the eighth of the first month, at the seventh hour, on his way home from the works, she walked up the upper lane.

There is about a mile of it and she walked it at the pace she walks it, which is the pace of a person who has told herself she is going four hundred times. Rennie Sallow's house is the fourth from the top and it is a good house with a gate that does not shut and a woman in it who has been at the window about the seed twice and the second time did not want Ivet Sarn there, and that is his one piece of standing in this district and she was not going to take it off him by standing on his step in working hours with a court clerk's folder.

She had the sheet in her coat. It was a copy of the line she had entered in the register on the fourth of the tenth month of last year, in her own hand, with the reason and the consequence in the same box: an upper-district barn term file of the fourth month of the fifth year, a household called Sallow, four days, and eleven pounds of seed bought over the price because a docket could not be closed and the relief rolls could not be certified.

She had told them all of it, on the thirtieth of the ninth month of last year, at the top of the lane, in her own words, with a man from Auremar standing at the bottom for five hours and not permitted up. It was entered. It is in the register. There is a copy of the register. He could have come to that window any day since the fourth of the tenth month of last year and bought a shilling's worth of the line about his own house.

He had not, because nobody had told him that he could.

And in the seventh month of last year she had written it in the first person in her own book, and told nobody, which is the half of it that was the whole of the reason, and what she had written was that she would walk up this upper lane on the fourteenth of the seventh, on his way home from the works, and say four words and go; and the fourteenth of the seventh had been a day in the seventh month and she had not gone, and the not going was not a decision and she has had six months to find out what it was and it is this: it was an ordinary day in a month with a hearing in it and a copy to be got out of a building, and it was because there were about four other things, and every one of the four other things was in a book, and a man in a book is a thing that can be done in six months.

So she put it on the wall on the second of the eighth month, in the first person, with the day on it, which is a thing she has never done about a promise she made to herself:

> *Nine: I said in the seventh month of last year, in my own book and to nobody, that I would walk up this lane on the fourteenth of the seventh and say four words to Rennie Sallow and go, and I did not go, and I did not write down that I was not going to, and that is the first dishonest thing I have done in nine years and I have got about four of them for company. A rule that is written on a wall is a rule. A rule that is broken in private is a preference, and I have had a preference since the fourth of the tenth month of last year and I called it a drawer. — I.S.*

And on the eighth of the first month she did it.

She did not knock. She said his name, and he came out to the gate because it was a woman he knew, and she put the sheet into his hand and said four words.

"From the window, no charge."

And he stood there.

"I did not know there was a line about us," he said.

"There has been one since the fourth of the tenth month of last year. It is a shilling a copy. I am not going to tell you what is in it and I am not going to read it to you here, and I am not going to come in."

"My Wenna was on the reading table."

"Your Wenna was on the reading table and asked in open lane, in the winter before last, whether a duty was standing against her own family, and she got the true answer instead of a comfortable one, and I have never once told her that I wrote the line that made it possible for her to ask."

Rennie Sallow looked at the sheet for about as long as it takes to look at a piece of paper.

"You will want to come in," he said.

"No. I have a shilling to take back to a man with a tray." She put her hands in her pockets. "I have had eleven shillings in a cloth since the ninth of the seventh month of last year and I have not spent a penny of them and I would rather they went on a thing somebody could use than on nothing, and that is not a joke, Mr Sallow, that is a line item."

And she went down the upper lane at the pace she walks it, and she did not go in, and it is in the register in her own hand that she did not go in, because a clerk of a river court who carries a paper down a lane in working hours is doing two jobs, and the second job has a name and a number and an initial in the margin of a book in Auremar, and it is hers and she would like it written down.

---

The notice for the second month of next year came down the river on the ninth of the first month on the morning packet, addressed to a party, and it was copied four times by Sarn because that is what a clerk does, and it went to the four on the list at their own addresses, and one of them is a woman of forty-eight in the lower district who is on the copy list and who is not on the board.

It is a notice that a man named **Corvin Dray** will be tried at the second hour on the second of the second month in the chamber of the Auremar district court, and it sets out four things attached to it, and she read all four out to the queue at the window because there was a queue, and then she wrote them on the board under the thirty-one because there was nowhere else.

> *One: he is not to be released on his own recognisance before the sitting, and is to remain in the district house.*
> *Two: he is to be given no paper except paper of the court's own, and every sheet accounted for at the door by an officer.*
> *Three: he is not to speak to any of the eleven protected people named in the order of the seventeenth of the ninth month, except through an officer, and a breach of it is to be entered.*
> *Four: the trial is confined to the local forgery charges, to the entries in his own ledger of ninety-one leaves, and to the thirty-eight lines in three districts against which a mark appears, and no count arising out of the sealed instruction, or out of any office, is to be tried with it, and his appearance to be tried is not an admission touching any person or office not named.*

Eleven people, and sixty-one certificates that a registrar suspended on his own authority in a room in the sixth month without hearing an argument, and a mark in a public minute book against thirty-eight lines in three districts which is a mark and not a name and which the court does not know the house of, and a plea that was refused on the eighteenth of the ninth month of last year and read back off the record in the seventh month and not refused again.

And nobody has been named, in eleven months, by a man who has been asked by four separate people, and he is not going to be asked again for one, because a man who cannot be got to name one can be made to say anything by a person who wants him to.

"A mark," said the woman from the lower district, who was forty-eight and who is on the copy list. "You have a mark in a minute book and a man going to be tried in a fortnight and neither of them has a name in it."

"Neither of them," said Ivet Sarn, "and I have written both of them on this board with the date on them, and the thirty-one are above them, and the other number is above the thirty-one, and if anybody ever comes to this window and asks me which of these I would take down first, I will tell them that I would not, and that is the first sentence I have said in nine years that I have written down in advance."
