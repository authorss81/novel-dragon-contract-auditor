# Chapter 97: A Letter Is Not an Instrument

Two things came down the river in the first week of the tenth month, in the same packet, and both of them had five words on the front of them and no office and no name, and both of them were put on the wall above the sill, and the wall now has more on it than it has ever had.

The first is a transcription, in a copyist's hand, of the order of the court sitting in room four of the eighteenth of the seventh month. It came down in about six days, which is fast, and Sarn read it four times before she wrote a word out, and what she wrote out first was not the order.

> *What I am reading. Written before I copy anything, because in nine years somebody will read a transcription and not this page, and in nine years somebody in this room will have a reason for having read the transcription first.*
>
> *I am reading an order that says a person may be heard about an instrument in a room, in a place that recognises the room, and that an instrument with no person's name at the top of it has no operation upon a person until that person has been heard about it, and that the court that made it does not know who gave the instrument and cannot find out and says so in a fourth form, and that a seal is not a person and cannot be served, and that the court entered a thing in this very matter in the fifth month that it cannot serve, and knows it, and has entered that it knows it.*
>
> *And I am reading an order that says a person may be heard. It does not say when.*

She left that last line on the wall as well as in the book, because it is the only part of the transcription that a person in this district can do anything about at all, and because she wanted to see it in chalk beside the figures.

The second thing is a transcription of a certification from the same court, of the fourth week of the eighth month, and it is one side, and it says that the ruling made in this district on the twelfth of the ninth month of the ninth year operates in this district, at the field, the channel, the gate, the bank works and the household named in it, and in no other place whatever.

"Is that us," said a woman who had come in behind her and had been standing at the sill for about a minute, and Sarn said, "Read the second line of it and tell me."

"That is us," said the woman.

"That is us," said Ivet Sarn, "and that is the first piece of paper that has come down that river in about a year that has a place on it, and it names five things and four of them are about eleven miles from this window and one of them is a household I could not name in a room in nine years."

---

The third thing on that part of the wall is older than either of them and it is the only one anybody in this district has ever asked for, and it is a transcription of the answer of the court sitting in room four of the eighth of the third month, in one side, in five points, and it came down the river in about eleven days against an ordinary six weeks, and Sarn read it three times and wrote out what it does before she wrote anything else.

> *What it does, written before anything else, and I am writing it a second time tonight because a woman at my sill on the ninth of the fourth asked me a question I could not answer and I have had six months.*
>
> *One. A scale reading by a person not entered in the register of the Docket of Powers at the time is of no legal effect whatever. That includes the readings in the two entries in that register, and the court named them, and it named nobody else.*
>
> *Two. The record in which such a reading was entered is a record of a court, is not altered and is not disturbed, and the entry stands.*
>
> *Three. Every act done in reliance on it stands, and no person who acted upon it in good faith is to be questioned for having done so.*
>
> *Four. The bar of the applicant upon any audit in any jurisdiction in any office is not discharged by that answer and is not varied by it.*
>
> *Five. The court names no person and says again, in a form with a name at the foot of it, that it does not know and cannot compel the authority under which a certain instruction was given.*
>
> *So: the letters of this court in the sixth year were applied on the authority of a sentence that a court has now said was model wording and not a decision of any court. And the letters are not withdrawn. And the answer says every act done in reliance on it stands, and no person who acted on it in good faith is to be questioned. **The letters of this court in the sixth year are now unchallengeable, and it is not because anybody has lied and it is not because anybody has broken a rule. It is because a court in a city four miles away was honest, correctly, about a thing, and the honest answer has made six weeks of my district's letters into a standing record with a judge's name at the foot of it, and the only line in this empire in which a girl of fifteen is entered as heard is on the same page.***
>
> *I wrote all of that on this wall in the sixth month of this year in the first person, and I have read it in front of a man from the district works and about thirty other people and I sold two copies at a shilling, and it is the most correct thing I have ever written and it is the reason the thirty-one on that board are the thirty-one on that board for ever.*

She said that last sentence out loud at the sill on the tenth of the tenth month, in about nine sentences, to eleven people, because a document that is read is a record and a sentence that is said at a window is a thing that about nine people in a district will be able to repeat, and the whole of the work of a clerk of a court is to work out which of those two is worth doing.

And she said the thing that is the finding of the month, and it took about a minute, and it is this.

"A high court has made the harm permanent instead of beginning to repair it, and it has done it by giving us a sentence and not a supply of hearings, and **the sentence with no supply of hearings in it is the reason a review date with no named person is not a review, and it is going to stay the reason, and about nine hundred rooms in this empire is everything there is.** I have thirty-one names on that board and no way to put one of them in any of them, and I am not going to pretend that a better sentence in a better room has changed the shape of this counter, and I am thirty-eight and I have had this window for nine years and I have about as many left as I have had, and I have told the wall that I would rather be frightening and indispensable at a window with a nail in it than tidy and ignored, and that is still true, and I have added to it, because I am not going to be frightening and indispensable and also honest unless I write down what the counter cannot do."

---

The board is above the sill and the board is not the wall, and the board is where the figures are, and the figures have not moved in about a year and did not move this week.

> **THIRTY-ONE OF ABOUT SIXTY. Eighteen returns did not come back. Six copies were never made, by a clerk who was not asked to and did not, and I have her kitchen and the date and her word for it, and she is sixty-one and has a child and is alive.**

Thirty-one. Twenty-two of them are marked *seen* and nine are marked *told*, and both of those words are on the board in her hand and neither of them is a hearing, and she has never pretended otherwise. Eleven carry a word and no name: *left* four times, *gone* three times, *no such* twice, and twice two letters and a blot. Twelve came back from the post with a stamp on them and nothing else. Fifty-four filed copies for three cycles, thirty-one and eleven and twelve, and six copies in a fourth cycle that was never made, and eleven that came back empty and six that were never copied is eighteen returns that did not come back, and the day anybody adds one of those numbers to another number somebody is going to be hurt and it will not be an error.

**A review date with no named person is not a review.** That is on the wall already, from the sixth month, and it is the sentence of my own life in nine years and I wrote it about a form.

And below it, in the fourth column of the register of pending duties, in her own hand, thirty-one times over nine days in the first month of this year:

*next review: none named*

---

The woman who had come in behind her was about sixty and she had come at the third hour so as not to be seen standing in a queue, which she has done twice before, and she has paid a shilling each time, and she is one of the thirty-one, and she did not go to the board first, which she has also done twice before.

She put the shilling on the sill.

"Two documents on the wall," she said, "and I cannot read either of them, and I have heard there is a man in a city four miles away who reads things out in a yard, and I have thought about that for about a fortnight, and I have not gone, and I want to know if the wall is for me."

That is the third time she has asked, and the first two were *whether I was going to be called into a room*, and then *whether this is a thing I am on*, and this is the third and it is a better question and she did not write it down and I did not ask her to.

"No," said Ivet Sarn, and she took the shilling before she said the rest of it, and she has taken that shilling three times now and has written down why she takes it, and the reason is on the wall in the first person and it is nine words long: *a person who has paid can answer without owing anybody.* A person who has not paid has to be grateful, and gratitude is a debt with no figure on it, and a debt with no figure on it is the kind that gets paid in land.

Then she said the rest of it, at the sill, in about four minutes, and about eleven people heard it, which is more than usually.

"**The sentence from the city protects a person from an instrument.** That is what it does. It says that an instrument — a thing with terms in it, sealed, witnessed, filed — cannot touch a person until that person has been heard about it in a room, in a place that recognises the room.

"And you were not sent an instrument. **You were sent a letter.** And the letter is not an instrument, and there is no instrument in this empire, and there is no sentence in this empire, and there is not one in the file in the city four miles away, and I have looked, and I have had a copyist look with me — and the sentence is addressed to an instrument, and the thing that was done to you was a letter written by a court, and **a court is not addressed by its own sentences about a letter it wrote, and nobody in this empire has ever yet made one answer for a letter.**"

"So no."

"No," said Ivet Sarn. "No, and I am going to say the other half, because you paid, and because in about four years you are going to be asked by somebody with a tray whether you were told, and I want you to be able to say what I said and when.

"**You were sent a letter in the sixth year by the court whose window you are standing at, and there is a court above it that has just said, twice, in two rooms, that a person must be heard before an instrument takes effect upon them, and that court cannot get to you either, and it cannot make this court hold a hearing, because a district court is not an office anybody can instruct. That is not a thing anybody is doing to you. That is the shape of the whole thing.** The court in the city four miles away made a sentence in a room and it binds nobody, and I wrote that on a wall in the sixth month, and there is a second sentence on that wall now that says a person may be heard, and the word I have underlined on it tonight is *when*, and there is no answer to that word in any of the four buildings that have sent anything down this river in a year."

She put her hand flat on the sill, which she does.

"They will offer you a hearing," she said. "They will write and say, come to a room on a day, and there will be a chair, and somebody will read your letter to you, and you will say no, and it will be entered, and about four months afterwards there will be nothing in your house that is different. And if you want it, come and ask at this window and I will put your name down and I will not tell you what to do about it, and I will not be one of the people who tells you what to do about it, and I have said on this wall that I will not, and it is the only promise on this wall that I have kept every time."

---

The other thing about that week is that Ivet Sarn is the clerk of the court that wrote the letters, and she has never put that on the wall, and she put it on the wall on the twelfth of the tenth month at about the seventh hour in the evening, in the first person, with the day on it, in about nine lines, and it is the ninth thing she has written about herself in nine years and it is the shortest.

> *The twelfth of the tenth month. Thirty-one letters of the three cycles were written at this window or above it. I do not know which hands. I was the clerk of this court in the sixth year and I was twenty-nine and I was in the room when the fourth cycle was not copied, and I did not ask why, and I have written on this wall that I did not ask why, which is true, and I have never written the other thing, which is that I was in the room when the third cycle was copied and the flue was booked and nobody said anything.*
>
> *A clerk who fills in a gap in a reason is a clerk who has decided what the reason was. I did not fill in a gap. I am going to write down instead that I did not ask, and that a person who does not ask has not been given a duty, and that the fourth column of my own register is empty thirty-one times for exactly that reason and not because there is no review.*
>
> *The court above has said it does not know and cannot compel, in three forms before this month and a fourth on the eighteenth of the seventh. I am a fifth instance of the same thing at a lower desk, and I have been it since the sixth year, and nobody in this building has ever asked me whether the letters went out, and I have never told anybody, and I am telling a wall, and a wall is the correct place, and I have known that for nine years and it is the only good thing about it.*

And below that, on the same evening, the ninth paragraph, which is the reason she went up the lane on the fourteenth of the seventh and came back, which she had decided to do and did and entered, and the entry is four lines:

> *The fourteenth of the seventh. I walked up the upper lane with Rennie Sallow's own line in my hand at the seventh hour and I said four words and I went, and it was nothing, and I have written that down in the days book with the day, because a rule that is broken in private is a preference and I have had a preference since the fourth of the tenth month of last year and I called it a drawer.*
>
> *And it was nothing, and I want the reason written in the days book and not only on the wall, because a wall is read by strangers and a days book is read by me.* **The reason is that a line in a register is not a duty. He is not anybody's duty. Nobody in this district owes him anything, nobody is going to be examined about him, and a man who has been wronged and is owed nothing and is not owed a hearing is a man who is owed an apology, and an apology is not a thing a court supplies and it is not a thing a register can carry and I have been carrying it in a cloth since the fourth of the tenth month of last year because there is no column for it.**

---

The embankment corridor is where the press is now, and it is dry, and there is about nine feet of wall down there that nobody reads, and Ivet Sarn went and stood in it on the twelfth of the tenth with a lamp, because the flue was cut in that room on the fourteenth of the second month and there is nothing left in the room to lose, and because she has worked out that a person who cannot read a paragraph is not frightened of a document and is frightened of a room with a door.

The bundles are there, in a press, under an oiled cloth, fifty-four filed copies for three cycles, and the fourth cycle's six were never made and cannot be made, because the fourth cycle was never copied, and a copyist in a city four hundred and thirty miles up the river offered in the second month of this year to write the eighteen letters of it and this court refused in four lines, and the four lines are on the wall and the reason is the best reason anybody has given in nine years: *a bundle of eighteen letters in a copyist's hand is a record of this court doing something in the second month, and the whole of what is on this wall is that this court wrote to sixty households and kept nothing whatever.*

**The eighteen are gone for ever and nobody is to be angry with it.** I put that on the wall in the second month and I am not going to soften it now that there is a court order in a city four miles away that says a person may be heard before an instrument takes effect upon them, and I want the two things side by side on the same wall, because a person who stands at this sill in nine years should be able to see that a better sentence arrived in a better room and the eighteen letters did not come back, and **the flue was cut on the fourteenth of the second month and there is still no fourth clerk, and there is not going to be a fourth clerk, and that is not a scandal and it is a fact about a district and I am the clerk of it.**

The last thing she did that week was to write at the foot of the whole wall, under everything, in a hand about half the size of the rest, because it is the only line on it that is an instruction:

> *A wall is a thing that a person looks at. I have been putting things on walls for nine years and this is the first time I have written on one what it cannot do, and it is the first time I have written a sentence for anybody who is not in this room, and there is nobody in this room, and I have noticed that while writing it.*
