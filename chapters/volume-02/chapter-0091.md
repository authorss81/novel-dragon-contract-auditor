# Chapter 91: The Fourth Form

Room four has four days a week on which a person may come and sit in it while an order of the court is read, and the days have no name and no form and no notice, and the reason is that the court is a court of foundational things and does not publish its own workings except when it must. In nine years it has published six things. One of them was a list of offices.

About nine people came on the day in the second week of the eighth month, and they came because a card in a window three corridors from the great stair has said for about a hundred and forty years that the room is open on such days, and nobody has ever put a date on the days.

The registrar wrote their names in a book. He has been in this building twenty-six years and is an ordinary official with a heavy file that has been heavy for years, and the book is a new one, and the heading at the top of the first page is his own, and it reads *came*, and his own name is the fourth line in it, and he has told nobody that and is not going to.

Four people had asked this court what came of a sitting in this matter, in four months, in four buildings, and had been told by four people who did not know. He read their four questions out of the file before he read the order, in the flat voice that room four uses for things that are not orders, and none of the four had been asked in a form, and that is on the page because it is the finding of the morning: **there is no form in this empire for asking a court what came of its own sitting, and there never has been, and a court that publishes nothing cannot be asked for nothing, and the two facts are the same fact.**

Then Sela Vint took the sheet off the desk and read the order.

---

She had sat in room four for nineteen years and had never once had cause to be ashamed of anything in it, and she came out of the sitting on the eighteenth of the seventh with four words in her head that she had not been able to get into a document, and the four words were: *this is the best sentence I have written and it does nothing.*

The room on the eighteenth of the seventh had smelled of heated seal-wax and of the cold off the stair, and had held about forty people, and had been the fourth sitting of a matter that has been alive for fourteen months and has never cost anybody a penny. There was no device on the door of it, because a device does not come to a hearing. There was a seat's device in the register of parties, entered by the registrar in a form this court published, in a column that reads *this respondent to be served at*, and the entry in that column is four words long and says the court cannot serve it.

The applicant was in the room and was not permitted to speak and did not try, which she entered, because a man who does not try in a room is a fact about a room and she has found that about nine of them a year and it is worth more than anything anybody says.

She read the order in the order the entries were made, and each entry has a time against it in the registrar's minute, and she gave the times.

> *I. This court has addressed the question it raised on its own motion on the sixth of the third month, which was what may be done with an instrument sealed with a device, standing upon a file in this matter, of the authority for which this court does not know and cannot compel. The answer is this. **A court may address what an instrument does. A court may not address the mind of the office that issued it.** The instrument is not void, is not invalid, and is not set aside, and the matters touching the instrument of nineteen years back at the crossing at Lowcross remain held.*
>
> *II. It is entered, in the fourth form in which this court has entered it, that this court does not know and cannot compel the authority under which that instrument was given, that it knows of no person at the head of any office whose standing authority gives it, and that the fact that no person can be named is not a defect in this proceeding and does not prevent this court from addressing what the instrument does. This court makes no finding that any record has been altered. This court makes no finding that any person has done wrong. This court names no person and has found nobody at the end of anything.*
>
> *III. It is found that a device is not a person, is not a party, and cannot be served. On the twenty-sixth of the fifth month this court entered a respondent in this matter by its device, in a form this court published in the reign of the third of the Ashfall line, and the registrar wrote in the column for the person served: no person, no address, this court cannot serve it. The court repeats that entry and adopts the reasoning. **Where an instrument bears no person's name, it cannot be produced against a person in any proceeding, because there is no person to produce it against, and it cannot bind a person, and it has no operation upon any person until that person has been heard about it in a room, in a place that recognises the room.***
>
> *IV. And it is ordered, following the third of that, that the office whose instrument this is, and the officers of it, and any person into whose hands the instrument comes, shall not be taken to have done anything in this matter by reason of this order, and shall not be excused from anything in this matter by reason of it, and that this court makes no order touching the instrument of nineteen years back, and does not address its construction.*
>
> *V. It is entered that this court cannot serve the respondent it entered on the twenty-sixth of the fifth month, that it has known that since that day, and that it entered it anyway, and that it will continue to act in this matter with that fact entered, and will not act as though it had not been entered.*

She stopped there, which was where the order stopped, and then she said, "Read the entry of the twenty-sixth of the fifth," and the registrar read it out, because he had it in the same file and because it is the only document in the file that this court produced itself.

> *Entered the twenty-sixth of the fifth month, on the registrar's own motion, asked for by nobody. This court has entered a respondent in the matter of the crossing at Lowcross. The respondent has been entered by its device. No person of or belonging to that respondent is party, cited, or examinable, and no inference of any kind may be drawn from the fact that a person has not been named. The form on which the respondent was entered is the form this court published, and the registrar has no power to decline it, and has said so at the time and not afterwards.*
>
> *The register of parties provides a place at which a respondent is to be served. There is no such place for a device. The order made on the fourteenth of the fourth month holding the matters touching this instrument was not served upon the person named in the eleventh line of the schedule of persons who may be answerable in the matter, was not served upon any other person, and could not have been, because there is no address at which this court can serve it and no person who can receive it.*
>
> *The court enters that it has entered a respondent which it cannot serve, that it has no means of notifying any person that the matters touching this instrument are held, and that it has no power to make that respondent a party, and that the form does not permit it to be made one. The court makes no finding that any person is not liable for anything. The court makes no order.*

The room did not make a sound when the third of those three paragraphs was read, and she had expected it to, and the reason it did not is worth about four seconds of a morning: about nine people had come to that room, and one of them had read that entry before, in a different building, off a copy, and about four of the others had not.

"The form that entry is on," she said, "was drawn in this building in the reign of the third of the Ashfall line, for a case nobody can now find, and it has been used about twice in a decade, and in the sixth month of last year a registrar in this court read the whole of it out loud in open court and it took seventeen minutes. It is the best piece of drafting in this file and I have never admired anything in a file more. And it is the reason that the third entry you have just heard was available to me this morning, and it is also the reason that a seat can be in a room without being a person, and I have never in nineteen years worked out how to be grateful for a form and frightened of one in the same breath, and it turns out that is not a contradiction and it is simply what a form is."

"Now I am going to read out one more thing. It is not part of the order. The registrar is going to write down that I said it, and is going to read it back, and then it goes in the minute with my name at the foot of it, and it is the only part of this morning that anybody will remember in nine years."

Nobody had brought a pen forward, which told her something about the nine people in the room, and she noted it and did not use it.

"This is the fifth thing this court has entered on this file. It is not a grant and it is not counted among the four. A grant gives something to a person. This gives nothing to anybody. Enter that."

The registrar wrote it. It took about eleven seconds and he read it back.

---

Then she said the half of it, and it took about nine minutes, and it is on the page because she said it out loud in a room, and a thing said out loud in a room is the only kind of thing anybody in this building can be got to admit to later.

"There is a paper in this file from the fifteenth of the sixth month, put to this court by a notary of House Oris from a chamber about two hundred miles away. I am not going to read it and I am not going to argue with it, because he was right, and he is a notary, and he read it in four days, and I have had four months.

"He said that our order of the seventeenth of the sixth month is a door with the hinges on the wrong side, and that his House has been short of one sentence since the twelfth of the ninth month of last year, and he asked for that sentence and we did not give it to him, and he asked it the right way, off a form of ours, out of a book, with a person and a witness at both ends of it, and it took us a day and a half of a lawyer's time and he had told us it would."

"And in the third of the entries you have just heard, we have given his House half the sentence he asked for, four months late.

"The half we gave him is the half that says a person may be heard."

She put two fingers on the sheet and did not move them.

"The half we did not give him is the half that says when.

"A person may be heard. In a room. In a place that recognises the room. That is what we said, and it is what we have said in three orders and in a form and in a sentence on a wall four hundred and thirty miles down a river, and it is true, and it does not say one word about the order of the day. It does not say that the hearing is before. It does not say it."

"Somebody else has said it. Nine days after we sat, in a chamber four miles from this building, in a district court, by a magistrate, with her name at the foot of it in her own hand, and neither of us has read the other, and I am not going to stand in a room of nine people and pretend to you that I knew, because I did not. The registrar has had it entered that this court has not been told, and he has had it entered since the sixth of the third month, and it is in the file, and I have read that file four times since, and the sentence about the order of the day was in another file in another building in another room, and I have not seen it, and it is mine and not his and not hers that I have not seen it."

Nobody said anything, and about four of the nine were looking at the floor, and she let them, because a room that looks at the floor for a minute and a half has heard something, and a room that says something at that point is a room that is managing it.

---

"Now the price of it, out loud, in this room, because the room is the only place it will ever be said, and the minute will say the order and not the price.

"The order binds nobody who has not been heard. That is the sentence. That is the whole of it, and it is the best sentence this court has written in about nine years, and I have written four grants and a great many refusals, and none of the grants is as good as that and none of the refusals is as bad as that is good.

"And it binds nobody. Not one person in this empire is bound by it this morning. Not a trade, not a works, not a district, not a landing office, not a person with a paper in his hand. There is no form in this empire for compelling a person to be brought to a room. There is no form for compelling an office to describe anything. And there is a respondent in this very file which this court entered, in a form this court published, and cannot serve, and cannot refuse, and cannot examine, and the respondent is an office, and the office is the only party in the matter, and there is nobody in it.

"So the sentence makes a duty to describe and a right to be heard, and it supplies neither, and every hour of work the sentence has just made necessary will be done by clerks at counters and by women with barrows and by people who are not in this room, and are not paid for it, and have never heard of this court. It will be done badly in about nine hundred places, and it will be done for nothing, and in about four years the only record that it was done at all will be a day and an initial on the back of a piece of paper, and nobody will be able to tell from that whether the person was heard or only spoken to."

She said one more thing and it is the last thing she said in a room in this volume.

"There was an offer of carriage in this matter on the tenth of the fourth month. A seat's office offered in writing to pay the carriage of anybody this court thought should be heard, without asking to know why, and to pay it twice if the person came twice, and it is the only line in that document with a person in it, and my own registrar entered it on the twenty-sixth of the fifth month and said so in a minute. **That offer cannot be worked. It cannot be worked because the district court has never been asked in writing which persons it thinks should be heard, and the notice's own terms require that it be asked, and asking is a day, and the answer is another, and nobody has asked.** It was live for four months and it was inoperable for four months, and a dragon notary found that out from two hundred miles away in four days, and we did not find it out at all.

"So the money is in the offer and the asking is not, and the seat has been holding a thing that does nothing for four months for exactly the reason that the court has been holding a sentence that does nothing, and neither of those two facts can be fixed by the person who wrote either document, and I have not got anybody to send it to. I have wanted somebody to send it to all afternoon."

She sat down, which she does not do in the middle of a morning, and the registrar wrote for about a minute and a half and then read back the four words she had said before she sat down, which are on the page because she said them out loud, and they are the sentence the sitting gave her:

"That is the best sentence I have written and it does nothing."

---

The nine are worth a paragraph each and are not worth nine paragraphs, and this is what they are.

A copyist of about forty from a shop on the Greyfen road with one window and a price painted in it, who came because her own window says that a person may come, and who has a waiting list inside the window and told nobody in this room that she has one. A retired man of about sixty-one who keeps the tally at the head of a crossing in a lower district of this city at a penny a day and who was not asked to come and came, and who sat at the back and did not say anything, and who has been paid a penny a day for nineteen years and has not been paid for this morning and will not be. A woman of about seventy who cannot read a paragraph and who was brought by a granddaughter, and who was read the order aloud in the corridor first, out of an order of the day that nobody outside this building will ever hear of, and who came in anyway.

The rest are clerks, four of them, from four different buildings, and two of them are the sort of person who writes a heading at the top of a book that has never had one.

Nobody from a seat's device, because a device does not come, and nobody from the office whose instrument it is, because that office is not a person and could not be sent for, and nobody from the district court four miles away except the clerk of about two and twenty, who came on the same road he has come on four times to ask this court a question.

She noticed the tally-man at the back during the second entry and decided not to look at him again, because a judge who has noticed a person in the room has to decide what to do about the person in front of the person, and there is no form for that either, and the whole of what the morning was for is that she could not think of a third kind of thing to say.

---

Afterwards the room emptied the way a room empties, which is slowly and in ones and twos, and about four of the nine stayed to ask a question, and three of those four asked it in a form, which is the whole difference between this court and a county court and is everything nineteen years has bought.

The question came from a clerk of about two and twenty from a district court four miles from this building, and it is the same clerk who wrote the four words in a book with no office at the top of it on the evening of the twenty-first of the fifth, and who has asked what came of the eighteenth of the seventh four times since the sixth of the third month, and who did not ask it today because he had come to sit in a room and sit in it.

He asked whether he could put it in a minute.

"Yes," said Sela Vint. "You can put in a minute that this court has made an order on the eighteenth of the seventh month and that your court has regard to it. You can put that in because your court takes a recognition from this one and has to have regard to it, and that is the whole of what you are entitled to and it is about a fifth of a line. You cannot put in that it binds you, because it does not. You cannot put in that the person before you is not affected by it, and you may find in about four years that he is, and then you will have a minute in which you have decided that."

"And my magistrate?"

"Your magistrate found something on the ninth of the eighth," said Sela Vint, "and I do not know what it is, and if you tell me I shall not put it in anything, and I am telling you that so that you can decide whether to say it."

He said thank you and did not say it, and she let him go, and it is on the page because a clerk who is asked a question and is told the answer and then is not asked anything else has been given the whole of the protection this court has.

Then the room was empty except for the two of them and the clerk who had written the book, and the registrar shut the order's file, which he does at the end of every morning whether anything has happened in it or not, and said the thing that he says to a bench about four times a year and never to a person.

"Marginal note, ma'am, and it is the file's and not mine."

"Read it out."

"It is this. This court has, in nine years, granted four things. It has entered five on this file. The fifth entered nothing into anybody and named nobody and is not a grant, and a clerk in a book with a heading on it is going to read the word *entered* on that line and put it in a column with the other four, and in nine years he will add up the grants of this court and get five instead of four, and it will be my fault, because I keep the file and I will have written the word *entered* myself."

"Put it in," said Sela Vint, "in that sentence, with that reason, and let him add it up wrong."
