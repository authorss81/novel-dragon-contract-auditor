# Chapter 99: An Account and Not a Finding

Nobody asked Halvard Riet for it. He has nine years at the Inheritance Desk, which is the desk that a packet comes to first and goes away from last, and on the second of the twelfth month of this year he came in at about the seventh hour, as he does, and did the ordinary thing for about three hours, and then at about the tenth hour he took out the day's book and did not do the ordinary thing.

The book is the desk's own and it has a day to a page and a rule between the days, and a day's book does not have a place in it for what he was about to do, and that is the reason he used it. **A day's book is the only book in this building that a person cannot make a heading in, because a heading in it becomes the first line of a day and a day that begins with a heading is a day that has been organised, and nobody at that desk has ever organised a day in nine years and the book is the better for it.**

He took the Kest box down off the shelf on the first of the ninth month of last year, on the Registrar's own request, in about four minutes, and he asked for the Venn box and not the Kest one, and he was given the Kest one as well, and he has never told anybody that he took it on purpose, and the only record on the page that he chose is a clerk's handwriting on the cover of a day's book and it is in the form the day's book uses for a thing that has no business in it and he wrote it in the margin of the second of the ninth: *took the other one as well. H.R.*

He is not going to be the one who has this box in nine years. He is fifty-one and he does not know that and has never said so, and a man of fifty-one at a desk in a building of four hundred people is the most reliable thing in it, and the whole of his reason for doing an account nobody asked for is this, and he wrote it at the top of the page before he wrote anything else:

> *Second of the twelfth, tenth hour. Nobody has asked for this. I am doing it because the person who gets this box is not going to be me and because a box with a list in it and no account in it is a shape with a hole in it, and I have spent nine years writing the word "reason not known" in the column for a reason, and I am not going to hand a person a box with a hole in it that I have not looked at.*

---

The figures are these, and every one of them is a figure that is already on the page of this matter, and not one of them has been altered, and not one of them has been added to another one.

**One. Four sentences in fourteen months, and not one of them has paid anybody.**

The third of the fourth month, at Mosswake, a question asked and answered and entered as a question and not as a finding. Cost: nothing. Paid: nothing, and the clerk who entered it was a court, and the court was the one that had gone four months without asking.

The second of the seventh, in a district court of the lower district, that a die is a mirror, in four lines, at a bench under a window in a lane. Cost: one pound fourpence and a day out of a fund under a head that does not have a name on it, and a plate-cutter of about thirty-four who was warned on the record before she began and is now a person in a file who can be asked that question for the rest of her working life. Paid: the observation that about nine hundred plates in a hundred years in that city were cut from impressions and nobody had ever looked, and that a true seal can be photographed convincingly enough to pass in every court in the empire that has not got a plate-cutter in it.

The twenty-eighth of the seventh, a count of a bar omits every person who crosses without the bar moving. Cost: a line in a file in a city four miles away about a woman of about seventy with no name in it, which she will never know is there, and a magistrate's day in the rain. Paid: a figure that has been in a works' return for nineteen years and was never anybody's fault, and a padlock that has been broken for longer than anybody living.

The ninth of the eighth, that a precedent may be challenged in a matter in which a person is affected by it, and that the person may be heard before the instrument takes effect upon them. Cost, said out loud in the room by the magistrate who made it: every precedent in the empire is now open to challenge, including the ones that are right, and there is no way to charge that to anybody. Paid: about four hundred and forty people in four offices and one trade, a very little safer, and nothing whatever.

And an order of the court sitting in room four of the eighteenth of the seventh month, which gave a person the right to be heard and did not say when, and which is nine days older than the fourth of the four above and is the other half of it. It cost a carriage offer that could not be worked for four months and that nobody could be compelled to work. It paid nothing and it is the best sentence in the file.

**Two. Two things refused in fourteen months, and both by the same man, and both in a room with a witness.**

A bounded certification, in the fourth month, which would have made a sentence usable in any district and would have required the man to accept a ruling as an emergency precedent and drop the formal request. A standing examiner's seat, in the third month, being a room and a drawer and the standing to which forty-six districts would answer.

What is in the place of the two: a bar, which stood from the third week of the eleventh month until the second week of the ninth and was discharged by one line the man wrote himself; a watch, in one sentence with an eligibility, on the nineteenth of the ninth; an office that does not exist, held by a copyist of the third gallery, with a limit on it that is the only part of it anybody can put a hand on; and a room with a barred door, a stove and eleven feet of shelf.

Not one shilling of the nineteen pounds three and fourpence came out of a bar, a watch, a grant, or a refusal.

**Three. The die.** Forty-four thousand eight hundred and nineteen, cut shallow in the edge of it, written in a certifier's own day-book in his own hand on the nineteenth of the sixth, and it is a number of a book and not a number of the die and not a number of the mark. The book is in a press room off the Crown Kiln stair about a hundred yards from the desk this account was written at. Nobody has read it. The die is in a drawer in the Kiln sheds.

**Four. The plate.** Seven plates in a district court of the lower district, entered as plates, and not one of them identifies a house, a client, a person or an office. A plate-cutter of about thirty-four, a fee of one pound fourpence and the carriage, a warning entered before the work and not after it. A plate-cutter of about sixty in a lane, sent for once, declined in four words, asked one question and answered it, not asked the other, and two visits from a court in the history of that lane, both of them the same officer.

**Five. The gap in the copy.** Ninety-one leaves in the book, numbered consecutively. Ninety-two on the wrapper it was made from, in the hand of the copyist of the sixth year, with the word complete against the ninety-one and no figure against the word. The original was produced to the district court of Auremar on the eighteenth of the tenth month and has ninety-one leaves in it. One leaf is unaccounted for and a court has directed that nobody be asked about it in any proceeding in that court or elsewhere, on its own motion, asked for by nobody. **The only reason in this city that the original was ever ninety-two leaves is a figure in a copyist's hand on a wrapper, in a copy, certified short on purpose by a clerk of nine years against his own gallery.**

**Six. The thirty-one.** Thirty-one of about sixty. Twenty-two marked seen, nine marked told, and neither of those is a hearing. Eleven with a word and no name. Twelve that came back from the post with a stamp on them and nothing else. Fifty-four filed copies for three cycles, and six copies in a fourth cycle that was never made, by a clerk of the sixth year who had a child and eleven weeks of docket and no replacement and who says in her own kitchen that nobody told her not to copy it. Eighteen returns did not come back, and the twelve that came back stamped and the six never made are the same eighteen and nobody is to add them to anything.

A heating flue was cut in that room on the fourteenth of the second month. The press came off the third flagstone into the embankment corridor. The fourth cycle is gone for ever. A copyist four hundred and thirty miles up the river offered in writing in the second month of this year to write the eighteen letters of it and was refused in four lines, and the four lines are on a wall and the reason is the best reason anybody has given in nine years.

The fourth column of the register of pending duties reads **next review: none named**, thirty-one times, in one hand, over nine days in the first month of this year. Restitution is not begun. A court of limited jurisdiction cannot order it. A high court has made the harm permanent instead of beginning to repair it, and it did that by giving the empire a sentence and not a supply of hearings.

**Seven. The crossing.** A bill of **nineteen pounds three and fourpence**, unpaid. A fund holding **six pounds and eightpence** committed for the quarter and about six pounds on the average of the four quarters. **No line for a bridge in that fund and no line in it in nineteen years.** The real cost about a penny a day, out of a box in a kitchen, for nineteen years. About **fifty-seven pounds** of a district's money spent on a tally at the head of that crossing and not one penny of it ever claimed by anybody. **Four hundred and eleven** people on the far side. **Three hundred and eighty-eight** crossings in four weeks, which is a number of a bar and omits every person who crosses without the bar moving, and the two are different figures and are not reconciled by this account or by anybody.

**Eight. The sheet.** Four pence a sheet, about a minute and forty seconds of ruling and a halfpence of foolscap, three and a quarter pence above the cost, out of the wage of the person who rules it, entered in a book with no office on it, in her own hand, with the day, as the first number about herself that has ever been in that book. Four given away at a counter on the eleventh of the fifth month and three out of four, and a column with a heading on it and an initial against every line in it, kept by a person who can be found on purpose.

**Nine. A sheet nailed to a rail at a gate.** The fifth term of a ruling made on the twelfth of the ninth month is a list of works, and the list went onto the rail of the Reed Cut gate under the water office's own flood authority, with the office's device, a memorandum number, a date and a foreman's name on it, and no person's name at the top of it. The crew read it on the day it went up and did the work. **Nobody in this matter has ever sent anybody to read it, and the reason is that it is a schedule, and a schedule is a thing you read when you are about to do the work, and the work was done, and a rail is a rail.**

---

He came to the sum of it at about the fifth hour in the afternoon, and stopped, and made himself do the thing he had told himself in the morning he would do, which was to add it up, and he added it up, and it came to nothing, and then he added it up a second time in a different order in case the first was a mistake, and it came to a different nothing, and he wrote both down and neither is in the account.

> *The figures above do not add. They are not meant to and I am not going to make them. A shilling is the price of a copy and a pound fourpence is a day of a plate-cutter and four pence is a sheet of foolscap and a penny a day is a bridge and nineteen pounds three and fourpence is a bill that nobody is liable for and three hundred and eighty-eight is a number of a bar and four hundred and eleven is a number of people and forty-four thousand eight hundred and nineteen is a number of a book on a shelf in a press room about a hundred yards from where I am sitting.*
>
> **A clerk who adds them makes a person liable. That is the whole of the finding and it took me a day and a half to get to and it is the only thing I have to say that nobody else in this matter has said, and I have said it in the form the day's book uses because in about four years a person will read this page and try to total it, and a person who tries to total it is going to arrive at a number about a district's money and about four hundred and forty people at the same time, and then they are going to write to somebody, and the somebody is going to be a person.**
>
> *An account that reconciles is a lie. Mine does not reconcile and I have not tried to make it and I have written the two attempts down so that nobody can say I did not try.*

Then the six he could not check, with the reason against each, in the form the day's book uses, and the reason in every case is the same shape and the shape is: nobody can ask.

> *One. The number of people in four offices and one trade who have learned what a name in a file is worth. A figure of about forty was given in a room in the fourth month and it is a figure of a room. There is no return of it. There never will be. It is the number of people who have told somebody something, and that is not a number of anybody in particular and I cannot get it and I am not going to.*
> *Two. The number of pages in the four hands of the list. It is thirty-two and there are four pages and the four pages do not agree and one of the four has a surname on it that the other three have never heard of, and there is a woman four hundred and forty miles away with four children and no candle who bought a sheet at four pence in a shop window and told nobody, and there is no way of finding out and Tamsin Rook has written down twice that she is not going to look.*
> *Three. Whether the district of Mosswake will hold a hearing next season. Unknown, and it is not that the district's answer has not come. The district has not been asked, and nobody's job is to ask it, and I have now written that sentence down and this is the ninth time.*
> *Four. The number of sheets of the second instrument in use. Four given at a counter in the fifth month, three of four. Four more given at a counter in the third week of the eighth, and one returned. Nine people in a lane in the fourth month who have a piece of paper with a day on the back of it and no name on the front of it, and a woman with a barrow of hot water who has been putting a word in a box for about four months and does not know what it means, and there is no run on the form and no addressee and no way to send a correction to anybody, and I cannot find the number and I must not send one, because the form has no run on it and the day I put a count in a book is the day it has a distribution.*
> *Five. What the year in the middle of a run is. Nobody has it. A woman of about sixty in a lane has it, and has declined to say, in advance, in a lane, with the officer, and it is in a minute in a form with a name at the foot of the warning, and it is not entered anywhere.*
> *Six. Whether the sentence made in a room in the last week of the seventh month does anything at all. Unknown. It is in a copyist's hand in plain words in a tray in this building and it is four pence a sheet, and I can count the sheets I have seen and the sheets I have not seen and the answer is that a person could have taken one home and used it and I would never know, and that is the correct outcome and it is the first one in this account that I am content with.*

Then he added the eighth item to his own copy of the handover list and nothing to the copy of the six, which is a thing he has done once before with a seventh item and a different reason, and the item is nine words long and it is the last thing in the account.

> *Eight. The rail, and the sheet on it, and the five jobs on it a girl of eleven cannot read. — H.R.*

And then he did the thing he had decided to do at about the sixth hour, which was to write one more line in the day's book, in the form the day's book uses, and to read it back, and not to cross it out, and it is the only line in nine years of that book that is not about a filing.

> *An account is a statement of figures and not a finding. A statement of figures can be checked by anybody who has got the file and about nine years. A finding cannot be checked by anybody, ever, and a clerk who writes a finding into a day's book because nobody asked him to has done the thing this book exists to stop, and I have written "reason not known" twice in nine years and I would write it a third time tomorrow.*
>
> *I am not going to write one line of this that says anybody was right. Not one. There is not a line of it in nine columns and four hundred and ten figures that says a magistrate was right or a registrar was right or a copyist was right or a woman of sixty was right or a man of sixty-four was right or a man in a bad coat was right, and the reason is not modesty and it is not fairness and it is not a form of words.*
>
> **The reason is that in about nine years the person who has to answer for this box is not going to be me, and I have found out today that this is the only reason I have ever been able to write anything true, and it took me nine years and an account to work out, and the man who is going to hold this box is going to have to work it out again, and there is nothing I can do about that except not leave him a line in my own hand telling him he was right.**
