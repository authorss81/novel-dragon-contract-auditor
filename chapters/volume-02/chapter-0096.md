# Chapter 96: The Office That Does Not Exist

The form she had signed on the eleventh of the ninth month of last year is four lines long and has two boxes on it and is a sheet of good commercial paper with nothing at the head of it, and it has been on a shelf in the copying alcove of the third gallery in a tray under about forty other things for about a year, and the four lines on it are these:

*Held by Tamsin Rook, of the third gallery, of the copies of the record of the court sitting in room four, of the dates in that record.*

*The office of the receiving person, if any: `None`.*

*Nobody may take them from her and she may give them to nobody.*

*Given at a counter, at four pence a sheet, to anybody who comes.*

That is the whole instrument and it is eleven months old and it is the reason that the Docket of Powers entered, on a card, in four minutes at a counter in the fifth month: *independent copy exists, not produced, no power.*

And on the nineteenth of the ninth month a court sitting above the Crown Kiln entered that a person whom the Docket of Powers keeps in its register of persons who read **may not be in a room without a person in it who is not from the office that watches him**, and the office is not named, and cannot be named, and the only person in this matter who is not from it is the woman whose name is in the first line of the form above.

She looked at the two pieces of paper on her bench for about eleven minutes on the twenty-first of the ninth, in the alcove, at about the fourth hour, and then she did the only thing she could think of, which was to write out on a sheet of foolscap the reason a form with `None` in the office box was safe, and read it back, and find that it was not a reason any more.

> *The fourteenth of the ninth month of last year, in a copyist's hand, with the day on it. I have carried this form eleven months and I have written the reason on it four times and every time the reason was the same. `None` is not nothing. `None` is a person who cannot be instructed. That is why the holding is safe. The holding is safe because there is nobody anybody can send a thing to.*
>
> *And the reason that stops being the reason today is that in a room in this building there is now a copy of a sentence that says a person who is watched may not be in a room without somebody who is not from the office that watches him, and the person is going to be me, and the reason I am safe is going to be the reason I am useful, and a person who is safe because nobody can instruct her and useful because somebody needs her standing in a room is a person with two boxes filled in on the same form, and the second one is going to tell anybody who reads it exactly what I am worth.*
>
> *I have been a holder for eleven months. I am going to have to be an office, or I am going to have to admit that a court has just made me the instrument of a watch and I have not said so out loud to anybody including myself.*

---

The four lines she wrote on the twenty-second of the ninth are these, and she wrote them at her own bench and read them back twice and did not cross anything out, and they are on the page because a copyist's own book is the only place in this matter where nothing about her has to be true to anybody.

> *The twenty-second of the ninth month, at about the fifth hour, in the alcove of the third gallery, in a book with no office at the top of it. This is not the sheet. The sheet is the sheet with two boxes on it and it is not changed, and it is not sent, and it is not adopted in any jurisdiction, and it is given at a counter at four pence to anybody who comes, and it is not a part of this and it must not be kept with this.*
>
> *I hold the copies of the record of the court sitting in room four. That is the duty. Anybody who comes may have one at four pence and I ask them nothing and I give them no advice. The column headed Given is in this book and it has my hand against every line in it and I am the keeper of it and I can be found on purpose, because a column kept by somebody nobody can find is a column that can be denied.*
>
> *The office of the receiving person, if any: `None`.* **I have written that for the first time on a paper about myself and it is not the same as writing it in a box about a person I am handing a piece of paper to, and the difference took me eleven months and a court order and it is this: in a box on the sheet, `None` is what a person receives the paper into. On this page, `None` is what I am. I am the receiving person and there is no office behind me, and that is not modesty and it is not a privacy and it is not a woman being careful. It is the only sentence in the whole of this matter that I know to be true about the protection I have, and it is true because nobody can send anything to it, and there is nobody to send it to.**
>
> *And the limit, which is the only part of this that anybody can put a hand on:*
>
> *Anybody may ask me to stop. Anybody at all. A person in a lane, a person with a tray, a magistrate, a judge, a man I have never seen, my own mother. I will not be asked why and I will not be able to refuse and I will not be paid for it and nobody has to give a reason to anybody. I will write the reason in this book, under the column headed Given, with the day, and I will send the copies to the four people who hold one page of the list between them — a girl of seventeen at a chandler's counter, a clerk at a river court, a works foreman, and my own colleague's sister, four hands, four pages, and the four pages do not agree and nobody has tidied them and I am not going to.* **And on that day the column goes with them, and there is no office to instruct anybody to take it, and there is no successor, and the four pages of a list that do not agree are what is left. That is the limit. It is the only door on this thing and it opens from the outside and I cannot lock it.**
>
> *— T. Rook.*

She read that back twice and then went and got Marek Kest out of the room with the barred door, and read it to him standing, in about four minutes, and he read it back, and he gave one reason and it was a fact and not a word, and it was the best one he has given anybody in fourteen months.

Before she read it to him she wrote out what an office that does not exist is worth, standing up, at her own bench, and she wrote it under the four lines and she did not cross it out and it is on the page because she copied it into the book with no office on the top of it as well, and a page in two books is a page that can be checked.

> *What it cannot do, and I have gone down the list twice and it is the same list all the way down.*
>
> *It cannot be compelled. Nobody can order me to do anything and nobody can enforce it if I do not. I have been a copyist for two years and I know that a thing a person must do is done badly and done late, and there is no office in this empire that can make me hand out a sheet at four pence, and I would not want one.*
>
> *It cannot be instructed. This is the one I wanted and it is the one that is genuinely mine. There is nobody to send a word to. If the First Seat's administrative wing wanted a person stopped it could enter itself by its device and hold a matter for as long as the registrar of that court was prepared to be the only person in the empire who could say no, and I have read the minute in which he entered that he could not serve it, and I have read the sentence in which a judge above him said that an order which cannot be served upon a person is not served upon that person. **Nobody can serve `None`. Nobody can instruct `None`. Nobody can ask `None` for anything.***
>
> *It cannot be transferred. And that is not a fault in the design, it is the design, and I only worked it out at about the sixth hour on the twenty-second and I have written it down because I nearly wrote the opposite and somebody would have read it in nine years and believed it. **An office that cannot be instructed cannot be instructed to hand itself on, and so there is no successor, and so the only way to stop one is for the person standing at it to stop. That is the whole of what is different about an office and a person, and it is the whole of what is different about this and a chair.***
>
> *It cannot be audited. There is no auditor of it, there is no return, there is no fund, and if I keep a bad book for four years nobody in this empire can look at it and say so, and if I keep a good book for four years nobody in this empire can look at it and say that either. That is the price and it is not a small one and I have had a year to think about it and I have not found a way round it that does not involve a fund, and a fund pays for services and not for persons.*
>
> *It cannot be paid for, it cannot be given a wage, it cannot have a day off, it cannot be given a room, and it cannot die in office. If I stop, it stops. If I am ill for a month, there is no deputy and no locum tenens and nobody at all holding a rail, and the copies sit in a drawer in a locked book in the third gallery of a building and forty-one people hold other copies and nobody has to be found. **It can only stop. That is what an office that does not exist is: a thing with one exit and it is not a locked door, and everybody in this matter has spent a year complaining that doors are locked and this one is a door and it is the only one here that opens.**
>
> *And it is twenty-four and it is a copyist's wage and that is the whole of what is in the box, and I have written it down so that in nine years a person does not find this page and think it was a career.*

"You have put both of these in the same book as the sheet."

"The sheet is in a tray under about forty other things and the sheet is not in this book."

"Then somebody is going to put them in the same book in about four years, because a person who rules a sheet and a person who rules an account at the same bench in about an hour is a person in a room, and rooms get tidied, and the tidying is always innocent. **And the sheet says in its third line that a person is not to be given advice. This page is the only page in this matter that is about what I do to the people who come. If the two are in one hand in one book then in about four years somebody in a lane is going to be handed a piece of paper by a woman and is also going to be, somewhere in the same building, a person who has been told what this office is. That is advice. It is not advice by anybody's intention. It is advice by proximity, which is the ordinary way advice happens in this empire and it is the reason a woman with a barrow of hot water is not a clerk.**"

"I am not changing a word of it."

"I know you are not. I am telling you the reason and I told you I would say reasons out loud and not on a page, and I have said it out loud, and the thing I have just said is a fact and not a word and there is nothing on the page to change. **The sheet gets ruled at my bench and the office is kept in here**, and if you want the whole of the design in one line then it is: a thing a person cannot read a paragraph of and a thing that tells a person what a person cannot read a paragraph of do not live on the same sheet, and one of those two is worth four pence and the other one is worth nothing, and I have spent a year finding out which is which and I am not going to lose it in a tray."

She put the sheet and the account in two places, which took about four seconds, and then she went down the corridor and put the account in the drawer of her own book, which has a lock, and the lock is a thing she has had for about a year and has used twice.

---

She went down the lane off the Kiln road on the twenty-fourth of the ninth, at about the sixth hour, and stood at the end of it for about a quarter of an hour, and said nothing to anybody, and there is a bench under a window in that lane and a woman at it of about sixty who has been cutting plates there for twenty-two years and who is not named in this and is not going to be.

There are three reasons she did not knock on that door and she wrote all three down in about four minutes in her own book when she got back, and the third one is the reason the whole of this chapter is what it is.

*One. If I knock on that door then I am a person in a lane, and a person in a lane is a person who can be described, and I have spent a year making a thing that is about nobody and I am not going to become a woman in a description on the way to becoming an office.*

*Two. There is nothing I could ask her. She is the only person alive who can break a number on a plate into hers and not hers, and I do not want the year in the run and I have not asked for it, and I have written down four times now that I have not asked and I am not going to make a fifth entry of that kind in about four months or a person will start to think there is a reason.*

*Three, and this is the one I did not know until the gate of the lane and have not been able to put down any better than this.* **The woman with the barrow of hot water at the end of Weaver's Cut has written the word `None` in the second box for about four months and does not know what it means, and there is no way to send a correction to her, because there is no run on the form and no addressee and no counter, and a form with a run on it is a model waiting for a district. Tonight I have written `None` on a page about myself and I know exactly what it means and it means a person who cannot be instructed, and if I go up that lane tonight and say the word out loud to a woman who has been using it for four months, then the next person who thinks of it has to hide it. And that is how the four questions got to a lane in Weaver's Cut in eleven weeks, and nobody sent them and everybody arrived, and I have spent a year saying out loud that that is the correct way for anything to travel.**

*And I did not go, and I have written down that I did not go, and the reason is in the same book as the office, which is the only place in this empire where I am allowed to be the reason for something.*

---

The rest of the week was ordinary and it is worth one paragraph because the whole of this case has turned out to be made of ordinary weeks.

Four of the sheets went on the twenty-fourth and two on the twenty-sixth and one came back, which is a return of about one in six and is better than anybody expected. On the twenty-seventh a woman of about thirty came and wanted to know whether the third box on the sheet was for her, and there is no third box, and she said she had thought there were three, and Tamsin Rook told her there were two, and the woman said that was all right, and gave her the fourpence, and put it in her sleeve, and was not asked anything.

On the twenty-eighth the four pages of the list came back into the same drawer, because the four of them had been sending them round, and the four pages still do not agree, and one of the four has a name on it that another of the four has never heard of, and there is a woman in a lane four hundred and forty miles away with four children and no candle whose surname is on that page and who bought a sheet at four pence in a shop window and told nobody, and Tamsin Rook is not going to find out and has written down, again, that she is not going to find out, and has added the date.

And on the last day of the ninth month, at about the seventh hour, she read the four lines out again to a man in a room with a barred door, because a rule made in the fifth month of last year in a corridor by two people says that either of them may put a choice in writing with a date on it and the other must answer in a room in words with a date, and she used it, for the first time in about four months, and it was about whether the word *office* was allowed to appear on the page.

"Yes," said Marek Kest. "Because it is not an office. That is the entire finding and you have had it in your hand since the fourteenth of the ninth month of last year and you have been calling it a custody for eleven months out of not saying it out loud."

"Then it is the only office in this empire," said Tamsin Rook, "that does not exist, and it has a duty, and a limit, and a keeper who can be found, and no one who can instruct it, and no successor, and no way to end it except the one door, and the one door is a person asking."

"Yes."

"And it is worth a shilling a copy and four pence a sheet and about nothing else, and it has been worth that since the ninth of the ninth month of last year, and the only thing that has changed is that there is now a court order that says a room with a certain man in it has to have me in it, and neither of those is the same as an office and both of them are the same as a person standing at a counter."

"Yes," said Marek Kest, "and in about four years somebody is going to come and ask what this is for, and I want to be in the room when they do, and I am not going to be the one who answers, and I have known that since the fifth month and I have not said it out loud until now and I am saying it out loud now, and it is the first thing I have got in this matter in about four months that is worth as much as the sentence it cost."
