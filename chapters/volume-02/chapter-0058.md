# Chapter 58: The Wrong Column

The records room at the end of the embankment corridor has a cold floor and a press in it and a window that does not open, and Tamsin Rook went into it on the second day of the twelfth month with a shilling and twopence of her own money for the boy who keeps the key, and came out at about the sixth hour with three things copied and nothing removed.

She was not there on behalf of anybody. She had a piece of paper from a court that had refused an application and put a date on it, and the court had refused it correctly, and there was nothing in that court to go and look at. What she had was a question, and the question was not a court question. The question was: does the building that sent a form into a yard in the ninth month have a file on the crossing, and what is in it, and is anybody in it.

She asked the young clerk from Auremar to come down again. He came on the same rider, at his own cost again, and did not think twice about it the second time either, and wrote the time in the margin in ink and initialled it, for the sixth time, for a copyist this time, and has still never been thanked for that one.

She did not ask Ivet Sarn to come, and the not asking was deliberate and she wrote down the reason on the train: Sarn is the clerk of the window and this is not a window matter and a district court has no business in a records room, and if it turns out that this court has hurt people in this district in the sixth year then a clerk of this court standing in a cold room with a press is a thing that will be said afterwards in a room, and there will be nobody to say it was a copyist.

The bundles were on the oak shelf in the order they had been put there, which is the order of the quarters, and the quarters are the only label any of them has.

She did not have to look for Lowcross for long, and the reason she did not is worth putting in a book, because it is the whole of the method and it is not clever: the Lowcross correspondence went out in the **third cycle** of the sixth year, because the maintenance requests went out in the third quarter, before the autumn, and a bridge is a thing you write about in the summer. And the third cycle's bundle was the second bundle on the shelf, and it was about four inches thick, and the label said *Returns, third cycle, sixth year* — no, that was Mosswake's, and this was a different building, and this one's label said:

*Outgoing correspondence, lower district court and works, third cycle, sixth year. Eleven items. One bundle.*

Eleven items. The entire private paper life of a dissolved parish and a public bridge for one quarter of one year, in four inches of deal.

She copied three things standing up, leaning over the sill of the window that does not open, with the sheets in place, and she wrote the shelf and the bundle and the item on the back of her own hand and not on anything of the court's. She has done this four times now, in four different rooms, and it has never once produced a document, and it has twice produced the only document in the matter.

The first was the **schedule**.

> *Schedule of persons who may be answerable in the matter of the crossing at Lowcross. Compiled from the parish list of the second year and from returns received. Revised, third cycle, sixth year.*

It is one page and it has eleven lines and every line has a name in the first column, and the second column has a short word in it, and the third column has a date.

> *The parish of Lowcross — body, dissolved sixth year, no successor entered.*
> *The district works of the lower district — office.*
> *The district court of the lower district — office.*
> *The trustees of the low crossing, appointed second year — office, no surviving appointment.*
> *The wharf company of the Weir Cut — office.*
> *The river navigation body of the lower cut — office.*
> *A return of the fourth month of the sixth year — the present holder.*
> *A return of the second quarter, sixth year — the present holder.*
> *A return of the third quarter, sixth year — the present holder.*
> *A return of the first quarter, seventh year — the present holder.*
> *A maintenance form of the eighth month, this year — H. Crane, of the yard, holder in fact.*

Ten of them are offices, or bodies that have been dissolved, or entries that are a return and not a person. Four of the ten are the phrase *the present holder* against no name at all, and they are in the *right* column, in a schedule of persons who may be answerable, and they have been there since the fourth month of the sixth year.

And the eleventh is a woman of sixty-one in a yard in the lower district, and she is in the *wrong* column, under a heading that says *persons from whom a return has been requested*, because she answered a form in the eighth month of this year, and a person who has been asked a question is filed under *asked* and not under *liable*.

Tamsin Rook read the schedule four times and then went and stood in the cold room for a minute and a half without doing anything, which is a thing she does.

The file names everybody the building could imagine being liable. It names the works, and the court, and a parish that does not exist, and two trustees whose appointment died with it, and a company, and a navigation body, and four returns that say *the present holder* and no more. It has one living person in it, and she is in the column for people who have been written to, and she is in it because a young man in a good coat came out into her yard and put his finger on a box.

**The Chancery's file names every possible debtor except the one family that can prove the clause was copied onto it.**

And then she copied the second thing, which is the return of the fourth month of the sixth year, and it is two thirds of a page of a works form with a box on it and a line at the head of the box that reads *holder of the crossing*, and inside the box, in the hand of a clerk in a room over a cobbled yard, in nine minutes, with a hundred and forty returns to do that month, it says:

> *The present holder.*

Six words. And she had seen those six words in three other places and has written about them in a book of her own three times, and every one of the three times she was careful, and this is the fourth time and the carefulness is what she is about to write down.

The first is the substituted line in the Sallow file, in the fourth month of the **fifth year**, prepared in a Mosswake court building.

The second is in twenty-two of thirty-one sheets in the Forms Received rack in the passage behind the third gallery, in a series issued by a Chancery standing office, in a sheet dated the **twenty-second of the ninth month** of last year, printed to run nine hundred and forty and sent to forty-six addresses holding a flood docket.

The third is on a guild's own model endorsement, a box headed *the person who holds*, in an instruction to assessors on the entering of a second instrument, **four years and four months older than the Venn correction**, about tool debts in a dyers' trade, and she proved in the sixth month of last year that it is a fact about a number and a date and is not a finding about anybody.

The fourth is here: a works return in the lower district of Auremar, in the **fourth month of the sixth year**, which is a year *before* the Sallow file and **six years and five months before the sheet of the twenty-second of the ninth**, in an office that is not on the forty-six, in a form that is not in the series, in a district that has never held a flood docket and has never been asked for anything.

She stood in the cold room and worked out the dates twice, out loud, to nobody.

And then she did the thing she has been getting better at for eleven months, which was to write down what she had, and not what she thought it meant.

> *Filed by T. Rook, roll-keeper of the third gallery, on the second of the twelfth month, in my own hand, in a book of my own, with no seal, no office and no authority. Nothing was removed from the bundle. The three sheets copied were copied standing up, with the bundle in place, and the shelf, the bundle and the item are on the back of my own hand and not on the court's.*
>
> *One: the crossing at Lowcross has a file. It has eleven lines. Ten of them are offices, dissolved bodies, or a return with no name. The only living person named anywhere in the file is H. Crane, of the yard, and she is under the heading *persons from whom a return has been requested*, and not under the heading of the schedule, which is *persons who may be answerable in the matter of the crossing at Lowcross*.*
>
> *Two: four lines of that schedule read *the present holder* with nothing after them. They are in the right column. The earliest is the fourth month of the sixth year.*
>
> *Three: the substituted wording also stands in a works return of the lower district of Auremar of the fourth month of the sixth year. That is a year before the Sallow file and six years and five months before the amendment sheet of the twenty-second of the ninth month.*
>
> *And here is the part I have to write down properly because I have caught myself twice this afternoon wanting to write it the other way. These are four appearances of one practice. They are not four documents in a chain, and there is no instrument that joins any two of them, and I have looked for one for eleven weeks in a corridor and I have not found it, and the correct finding is that a person at a desk in a hurry used words that were already in the district's mouth. That is what Sabra Ollett did in four seconds at a landing office three hundred miles down the water and what a clerk in a room over a cobbled yard did in nine minutes in a quarter of the sixth year.*
>
> *If I wrote the four of them as a chain, in one sentence, in that order, with those dates, a person in a room in nine years would read it as a conspiracy with a beginning, and I would have built it, and I would have built it out of four true facts and one invented join, and the invented join is the only part anybody would remember.*
>
> *Four: therefore the file names every possible debtor except the one family that can prove the clause was copied onto it, and the finding that is worth having in a court in the fourth month of next year is not a seam. It is a column.*

She read it back. Then she copied it out three times, and she did three things with the three copies, and the order of the three things is the decision of the day and she wrote the order down before she acted on it.

The first copy went to Hester Crane, in the plain words, in a copyist's hand, on the evening of the second of the twelfth, by a cart, with four pence of carriage in a slot at the counter, addressed to a gate in the lower district and not to a court and not to a works and not to an office.

The second copy went to Silas Sowle, who is not her solicitor and has never heard of her, at his address in the lower district, with a covering line of five words: *for the application in the fourth month. — T. Rook, no office.*

The third copy went down the river to Ivet Sarn, at the Mosswake Roll Court, by the morning packet, and it went second and it went third and that is the part she has to be able to say out loud in a room in nine years, and so she wrote it down the same evening, on the same page, under the heading she used for things that are not oversights.

> *I sent it to the household first, and to Marek Kest second, and the reason is this and not another reason. A file is evidence. Evidence that arrives in a woman's hands ten days before a bar goes across a bridge is not evidence any more, it is a warning, and a warning is worth more to the person it is about than to the person who might use it. And Marek Kest is barred from auditing by a Docket with three powers and one date, and if I had sent it to him first he would have spent a night in a room with a barred door and a cold stove and a shelf, and he would have come to that gate on the fourth day and he would have said something in it, and he would have been the clerk of the Roll Hall saying a thing in a woman's yard about her own file, and I have watched him do that twice in eleven months and I have been the one who paid for it both times.*
>
> *So it went to her first, and to him second, and I have not asked him, and the rule we made in the twelfth month of last year says one of us puts a choice in writing with a date on it and the other answers in a room in words with a date, and I have broken the first half of that rule twice in three months and I have broken it for the same reason each time, which is that I can see further into a decision than he can at about the moment when it is being made, and that is the exact thing he does not trust, and he is right not to trust it, and I am going to have to earn it back out of something other than being right about a corridor.*

At the fourth hour of the second of the twelfth month she came up the stairs of the Roll Hall with a cold hand and put the third copy on the table in the Received Room, which is what the bar on the door is for, and Marek Kest read it standing up because there were two chairs and one of them was under a pile of things nobody wanted.

He read it in about nine minutes and then he read the second copy's covering line and the first copy's, which was on a separate sheet, which said *for the gate, and do not read it to her unless she asks, and she will ask, and when she asks, read all of it.*

"You sent it to her first," he said.

"Second of the twelfth, at about the eighth hour at night, by a cart, with four pence of carriage in a slot, and I put five words on the front and did not put your name on it anywhere on it, and I did not tell the cart man who it was for."

"That was your decision and not an oversight."

"It was my decision and I wrote down the reason before I did it and I have sent you the reason, and it is two paragraphs, and I would like you to answer the second half of it in words, and I have brought you the form." She put the four-line custody form on the table and turned it round so that the second box was facing him. "None. I have written it twice, because I wrote it once and then I thought about whether a person with an office could hold a document in a matter and protect the person it is about, and I worked out that she could not, and that is the whole of the finding. **None** is not nothing. None is a person who cannot be instructed."
