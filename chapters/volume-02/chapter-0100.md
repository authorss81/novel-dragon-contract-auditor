# Chapter 100: The Fifth Term's Schedule

There is a rail at the Reed Cut gate and there has been a rail there for longer than anybody at that gate, and the gate is not the river, it is the frame the water goes through, and a woman who crosses at that gate crosses the water, and the rail is a piece of iron with a curve in it that somebody's grandfather set into the platform so that a boat has something to hold against while it is being worked.

There is a schedule nailed to that rail.

It has the Reed Cut district water office's device at the head of it, which is not a name, and a memorandum number, which is a number of a book, and a date, and at the foot of it a foreman's name, which is the only person's name on the whole sheet. It is on good paper and it is about eleven inches by nine and it has been folded once across the middle, and it is held on by four nails and the holes of an earlier set, and somebody tied it on with a length of oiled cord on the evening it went up and the cord is long gone and the four nails are still in the rail and they will be in the rail after the rail.

Nobody has come to read it in fourteen months.

---

Tavi Venn is seventeen and she has been on that bank since she was old enough to be on it, and she knows that gate linkage better than any clerk in this matter, which is a thing four people have said out loud in about eleven months and one of them was a man in a good coat writing in a book. She was examined for canal work on the twenty-sixth of the fifth month of this year by a guild witness and passed in reading, writing, arithmetic, ditching on the level and the setting of a gate linkage to a maker's gauge, and the fee of two shillings and sixpence came out of a works fund under a head that a man who is not Wendt Sayer will read in a year and have to be argued with, and it was entered on the sixth of the fourth, seven weeks before the examination, as a recruitment cost and not as a kindness.

She can be taken on by a master on a written engagement and not on a verbal one, and nobody has taken her on, and that is a thing in the fourth month of next year and not in the twelfth of this one.

The fourth term of the ruling of the twelfth of the ninth month is a new signature, and a bridgewarden four hundred and thirty miles down a river knows the fourth term by number because it is on a gate she passes, and could not read it if it were not.

The fifth term is the district works. The fifth term is the only one of the five that anybody could ever do anything about, and it is a list.

---

The girl came to the rail at about the seventh hour on the twentieth of the twelfth month, which is the hour the crew changes, and she is about eleven and she is not the only person who waits at that rail, and there were about four people on the platform.

She had a pail.

She stood at the rail the way somebody stands at a rail that has a thing nailed to it, which is to say she looked at it for about a minute and a half, and then she said:

"What is that."

"That is a schedule," said Tavi Venn.

"What is a schedule."

"It is a list of jobs, with a number of days against each, and the water office put it up the evening the work was done, and it is nailed on there because a nail is what you have got."

She read it out then, in plain words, standing at the rail, with the cold coming off the water and about four people on the platform who had not asked to be there and did not go away. She read it twice.

> *Reed Cut district water office. Under the flood authority of the office and the fifth term of the ruling of the twelfth of the ninth month. Memorandum forty-one, of the number the office gives to a thing that is going out of the building.*
>
> *One. Ditch, left bank, the fourth to the sixth. Two men, one boy. Cut to level, spoil on the low side, not on the towpath.*
>
> *Two. Bank, the seventh to the ninth. Three men. The slumping place at the ninth, and the two above it, and nothing else until somebody looks at it.*
>
> *Three. Abutment and kerb, tenth to the twelfth. Two men. The kerb stones go on the bed and not on the mud, and the bed is to be taken down to sound before anything is set.*
>
> *Four. Gate reset and the linkage, thirteenth. Two men and the boy. Set to a maker's gauge and not to the hand, and the gauge goes back to the office the same day.*
>
> *Five. The rail on this platform, new pins, the fourteenth. One man.*
>
> *Given at this office.*

The last line of it was written and not printed, and it was the only hand on the sheet that was not the office's, and there was a name at the foot of it, and there was no person's name at the top of it anywhere.

"That is the whole of it," said Tavi Venn. "There is no price on it."

The girl looked at the rail again. "Why is there no price on it."

"**Because it is not a bill.** It is a list of what a crew was going to do and how long each thing was going to take, and the money is in a book about forty miles from here, and a man reads the money when he is checking what he has paid for and not when he is about to do the work, and the two men in this empire who have been in a position to want that money are a foreman and a clerk and neither of them is you."

"So there is no money on it."

"There is no money on it and there is no household on it and there is no name on it at the top of it, and that is not a fault in the paper. It is the correct order. A person reads a schedule to find out whether they are on it, and there is nothing on that sheet that says anybody is on it except a crew of about nine and a boy, and they have all been paid, and that is the whole of what happened on that bank in the autumn of the year my father's field was saved."

---

The girl nodded at the rail for a while and then said the first of the four questions, and it is the first of the four questions a person of eleven has asked in this matter, and it is the ordinary version of it and not a lawyer's version.

"Is there work in it."

"Yes."

"Good," said the girl. "Then who is it for."

"That is the second one," said Tavi Venn. "And the answer is that there is nobody's name on it, and that is not a trick and it is not a mistake. There is a mark at the top of it, and the mark is the water office's, and a mark is not a person, and a device is not a person, and I have known that for about a year because my brother's case is about it and he has explained it to me at a table about four times and every time he has said it slower."

"And the foreman at the bottom."

"That is the only name on it, and he is the man who nailed it up and he has his mother's house on it, which he has told about nine people and does not have to tell anybody, and that is the ordinary thing about a foreman and a piece of paper."

The girl nodded.

"Then if I stop, whose name answers for it."

"Nobody's," said Tavi Venn. "And that is a real answer and it is not a nice one. **The office's name answers for it, and the office is not a person, and there is nobody in it that you could go and shout at, and if that list of jobs was not done then nothing at all would happen to anybody, it would go on the list again, and a list that goes on a list again is the safest kind of paper in the world and it is also the kind that is easiest to forget.** And it is not a trick either, because the mark at the top of it is how a water office says *this is ours and we will answer for it*, and they will answer for it, and the answering is done by about nine men and none of them has ever met a person it hurt."

"That is the third one," said the girl. "My mother says those are the three that are easy and the fourth one is the one that decides it."

"What is your mother called."

"Nery," said the girl, and then, immediately, and this is the fourth question, and it came out of her at about the speed of a thing she has been carrying a long way: "Who read that to you."

"You did," said Tavi Venn. "You read it to yourself. You can read a bit and you cannot read a paragraph, and you have read the words on that rail about nine times in a minute and a half, and you have read them the way a person reads a thing they are not going on."

"And do they get paid if I sign."

"I am not paid," said Tavi Venn. "I am not paid a penny for this and nobody asked me to be here and nobody has told me to be here, and there is no form of any kind in that office that says a person reads that rail to anybody. **I have not signed anything and you are not going to sign anything and there is nothing on that rail for you to sign, and I want to say the last part out loud, because that is the whole of what that paper is: there is nothing on it for you to do.**"

The girl thought about that for a bit, with her pail on the platform.

"Then who reads it to the ones who cannot read it," she said.

---

Tavi Venn did not answer that one, and the reason she did not answer it is that she is seventeen and she has been on that bank since she was eleven and she has never once in six years had anybody ask her a question she could not answer, and she stood there for about a minute with her hand on the cold iron of the rail, and then she did the thing she does, which is the thing the examination was about, which is to look at the thing instead of at the person.

"Four nails," she said. "And it has been there fourteen months. **Fourteen. I have been at this gate since I was eleven and I know the date on that sheet because my mother knows it, and I can work out a difference, and it is fourteen months.** And in fourteen months I have never once seen anybody stop and read it, and neither has my mother, and there are about nine families on this bank that cross here every day of their lives and not one of them has stopped."

"And that is the whole of it?"

"That is the whole of it, and it is not a mystery and it is not anybody's fault, and I want to say it in the right words because you asked me a proper question. **It is a schedule, and a schedule is a thing you read when you are about to do the work. The work was done. The crew read it on the day it went up, and they read it three times, and a foreman said it was a good sheet, and then it stopped being a schedule and became a thing that had been done, and a thing that has been done does not get read.**"

She took her hand off the rail.

"That is the ordinary way anything works in this empire, and it is the reason you have never been told anything, and it is the reason my mother is out in that ditch this week even though the fourth term of the twelfth of the ninth says in so many words that the household is not to be required to do the field-side, and she knows it, and she has the copy with the five terms on it in a tin, and she is out there anyway, because a woman who has done a thing for nine years does not stop because there is a piece of paper."

---

They stood there, and the water came up under the Reed Cut and went down again, and Tavi Venn said the thing that she has been carrying up and down that bank for about a year, and she said it to a girl of eleven with a pail because there was nobody else on the platform to say it to.

"There is one more thing and I am going to say it and then I am not going to answer it, and if you tell anybody I said it I will deny that I said it and we will both know I am lying.

"**A water office can be nine men and a boy for a fortnight, and then it is nobody for a year, and then in the fourth month it is the water or it is not the water, and everybody in this district knows which of those two is going to happen and so nobody asks.** And a court in a city four hundred and thirty miles from here has said in a room that a person affected by a paper has to be heard about it before it takes effect upon them, and there is a copy of that on a wall in a district where about sixty households were sent a letter by a court in the sixth year and thirty-one of them are on a board, and the fourth column of that board's book says none named thirty-one times, and nobody has put a name in the fourth column because there is nobody to put there.

"So if the water office cannot do it — and it cannot, not always, not in the fourth month — then somebody has to, and the somebody has to be a person, and the person is going to be a household, and the household is going to be a woman with a ditch and a girl with a pail, and **the whole of the argument of my brother's case for fourteen months is that a duty which is handed down to a person who has not been asked about it comes out as a child.**

"That is the thing I cannot answer. Not whether the water office should do it. Whether there is anybody in between. And if there is, then what is the child at the end of it, and how many, and whose, and who gets to say no, and who is standing in the room when it is said no. And there is not one word of law in this empire about that, and there are about nine hundred rooms and about four hundred and thirty miles of river, and I have asked four people and three of them were in this city and did not know the question was a question."

The girl did not answer it either, and picked her pail up, and stood there another moment.

"You have to be able to read it though," she said. "To read it to somebody. You have to be able to read it and then you have to be the one who is standing there when they ask you what it means."

"Yes," said Tavi Venn.

"And you are not paid."

"No."

"Then somebody will come and ask you to do it for nothing," said the girl, "and they will say it is your mother's river."

"They will," said Tavi Venn, "and I have been waiting fourteen months for somebody to say that to me and I did not know that is what I was waiting for, and you have said it to me on a bank in the cold and I would rather it had been a man in a good coat because then I could have been angry."

"You can be angry now," said the girl. "I am not going to tell anybody."

---

The water came up under the Reed Cut at about half past seven and went down again by about nine, which is what it does between one hour and the next, and the gate was worked twice in that time and the linkage was set twice and a boy of about nine went over on his hands on the far side of it, where there is a plank, and a padlock is broken there and has been for longer than anybody living, and nobody at that gate counts him and nobody at that gate has ever counted him.

On the far side of that water there are four hundred and eleven people, according to a schedule in a file in a city four hundred and thirty miles away that a magistrate read out in a chamber and said she could not vouch for.

At the other end of the district there is a bridge of timber over a cut with a bar at the end of a lane, and nobody is liable for it, and the bill on it is nineteen pounds three and fourpence and it is unpaid, and a woman of sixty-one has been putting about a penny a day into a box in a kitchen for nineteen years, and on the ninth of the eighth a magistrate found in a chamber four miles from a chandler's shop that a thing in general use may be challenged in a room by a person it affects, and said out loud in the room that the price of that finding is that every precedent in the empire is now open to challenge, including the ones that are right, and that there is no way to charge it to anybody.

Four hundred and thirty miles up the other way there is a board above a clerk's window with thirty-one names on it in chalk in letters about four inches high, and the other number above it, and a fourth column in a register that says *next review: none named* thirty-one times.

And on the twentieth of the twelfth month of this year, at about the seventh hour, on a bank, in the cold, a girl of seventeen read a list of jobs out loud in plain words, twice, to a girl of eleven who cannot read a paragraph, and the girl of eleven asked four questions and then a fifth, and four of the five were answered and the fifth was not, and the answer to the fourth one — *nobody read it to me, and you are not paid, and there is nothing on it for me to sign* — is the only time in fourteen months that the four questions have been asked in that order by a person who had never heard of them and got better rather than frightened.

---

The five questions are these, and Tavi Venn has got them because a man who is not a lawyer put them on a stone at the top of a lane four hundred and thirty miles down a river in the sixth month of last year and told her she could take them off the board and put them somewhere.

*Is there work in it.*

*Whose name is on it — yours, or a word that walks about, like the holder.*

*If you stop, whose name answers for it.*

*Who read this to me, and do they get paid if I sign.*

And the fifth one is not on the stone, and there are no four words for it, and no four words will do it, and a girl of eleven said it at the seventh hour at a gate on the twentieth of the twelfth month, and it went into the water with the rest of the evening, and it is this:

*And who is it that reads it to the ones who cannot read it.*

Nobody answered it. The schedule stays on the rail. The four nails stay in the rail. The gate is a gate and the river is a river, and neither of them is a court, and neither of them has ever pretended to be.
