# Chapter 95: In One Sentence

The question went up on the fifteenth of the ninth month in a hand of about fifty on a slip of the Docket's own paper, with a number at the top of it and a day under the number, and it went on the third and last of the three powers, and it came back on the nineteenth.

It took a day and a half, and the day and a half is entered in the Docket's own day's book with a reason, and the reason is that a referred question goes into a list at the receiving end and the list is worked in the order it is worked, and a court that sits four days a week on foundations does not put a clerk's question next to a matter of a seat. The day and a half was not anybody's fault. **The day and a half was the master of the Docket of Powers's own afternoon, twice, in a corridor, waiting for a word from a building up a hill, and he entered that in his own book rather than in a fund, because there is no fund, and the reason there is no fund is that nobody in this matter has ever been paid for anything except a day of a plate-cutter.**

The question was one line and he read it out in his own room on the nineteenth, at about the fifth hour, standing, to a man who had been let in and had not sat down, and it is on the page because Marrow reads everything out loud before he does it.

> *Whether a person entered in the register of persons who read, being a clerk of the Received Room, ought to be found eligible for training as a Continuity Auditor, and upon what terms, and whether the eligibility and the terms are to be in one instrument or in two.*

"The last clause is the one that matters and I put it in because I have thought about it for four days and because I have noticed something about this court in the fourteen months I have been referring things to it," said Oswin Marrow. "**In fourteen months that court has been asked twice to move something it has entered, and it has moved it twice, and both of those times somebody had written the two things in two sentences and a person with a tray had come in and taken one of them out.** I am not accusing anybody. I am telling you the shape of it. If the eligibility and the price of it are in two sentences, then in about four years somebody can ask that court to move the price, and it will have a form in front of it where the thing to move is a whole sentence, and it will move it, because it moved it twice."

Then he gave him the answer, which had come back on the same morning in a sealed sheet with a judge's name at the foot of it, and which is one sentence, and the sentence is this:

> *It is found that Marek Kest, clerk, is eligible to be trained as a Continuity Auditor, and it is ordered that he be under watch, and these are in one sentence and this court has put them in one sentence deliberately, because a court which puts them in two may be asked to move one of them, and this court has been asked twice in fourteen months to move a finding and has moved it twice.*
>
> *A training is not a certification. No certification is granted by this instrument and none is asked for and none is granted. A training is a reading of a file, in a room, with a person in that room who is not the person being trained, on a day fixed, out of an afternoon; there is no fund for it in this court or in any other and the person who gives the afternoon is losing an afternoon, and the court has entered that it knows who that person is in nine cases out of ten and does not know in the tenth.*
>
> *The watch is this. A person from an office, in this matter unmentioned, may read anything the watched person writes. The watched person may not be in a room without a person in it who is not from that office. And the office is not named, and cannot be named, and this court declines to name it, and the reason is entered: a person who signs a column headed *by whose authority* is discharging an office, and an office cannot be examined, and an office that is named and cannot be examined cannot be instructed, and an office that cannot be instructed cannot be made to give anything up.*
>
> *The watch attaches from the date of this instrument. The entry in the register of persons who read of the twelfth of the ninth month is not varied by it and is the reason for it.*
>
> *— S. Vint, presiding, room four. The nineteenth of the ninth month, on a question referred by the master of the Docket of Powers under the third of his three powers, which he said out loud in advance and has not referred before in twenty-two years.*

Marek Kest read it twice, standing, and then he asked for it to be read out to somebody who was not him.

---

Tamsin Rook was in the doorway. She has been in doorways in this matter for eleven months and it is a thing she does and he has never once asked her to stop.

She read it once, at about the speed of a person reading a thing she is going to have to be right about in a year, and then she said one sentence and went and got her pen.

"This is the first instrument in fourteen months that has your name at the top of it and you asked to be inside it."

"I did not know I was going to be asked to be inside it."

"You did not," said Tamsin Rook. "You asked for a line with your name at the bottom of it seven days ago and a question went up a hill, and this is what came back, and you are in it, and the whole of the instrument this case has been built on is the reason you are in it, and I want that said in a room and not on a page. **A document with a person's name at the top of it is a document about that person, and you are the only person in this matter who has ever been on the right side of that sentence on purpose.** In about four years a man with a tray is going to a court to ask whether M. Kest keeps records, and he is going to be able to say *there is a court order about him with his name at the top of it, and you may read it*, and the answer is yes, and there is no second question, and about four years ago you were a man nobody could find and you did that on purpose and wrote down that you did it, and now you are a man in a public minute in another building and you did not."

"I did that on purpose as well," said Marek Kest.

"Then it is the third time and I am going to stop counting."

---

The undertaking of the fourth of the first month was exercised the same afternoon, at about the sixth hour, in the Received Room, standing up, and she read the whole of the order to him in a copyist's hand on a sheet of foolscap, and he read it back, and he gave no reason against one word of it, and she wrote *agreed* and the day on it, which is the fourth time and the only time he has given no reason at all.

Then she asked him the one question, and she asked it in the ordinary way, and it took about four seconds.

"Have you read the sentence about the room."

"Yes."

"What is the only person in this matter who is not from the office that is not named."

"There is a woman at a bench in the third gallery," said Marek Kest, "who is a copyist, who has no office, and who is standing in a doorway at this moment."

"Yes," said Tamsin Rook. "That is a sentence with his name in it about me and I have decided to let it stand, and I want it written down somewhere that I decided to, and not that I was surprised by it, because I was not surprised by it, and that is the part I do not like. So here is the part I do not like, and I am going to say it now while there is a rule in force that says I say things out loud and not on a page.

"**For eleven months you have been the only person in this matter who is findable and I have been the only person in this matter who cannot be instructed, and between us we have been an office of two people with no office on it, and neither of us has ever once called it that, and neither of us has ever had to explain to anybody what it is, and that is the best thing either of us has ever stood in.** And now there is a court order that says a room with you in it has to have me in it, in writing, with a name at the foot of it, and I have known for four days that it was coming and I have not been able to work out whether I am frightened of it or whether I am being made use of, and I have decided to say so out loud rather than decide, because deciding is what you do and it is what I have been copying for a year."

Marek Kest did not answer that for about a minute and a half, and she let him, because she has learned what a minute and a half in that room means.

"You are being made use of," he said at last. "Not by anybody. By the fact. A duty with no office behind it cannot be instructed and it cannot be refused and it cannot be transferred, and you have been the holder of forty-one pages under a four-line form with `None` in the office box since the eleventh of the ninth month of last year, and the word in that box is the only word in this matter that means a person who cannot be instructed, and you have never told me what you think it is for, and I have never asked you, and the reason I have never asked you is that I did not want to be told."

"I did not think you had ever noticed it," said Tamsin Rook.

"I noticed it in the fifth month and wrote down that I had."

"Then you are about eleven months behind and I have been ahead of you for eleven months about the one thing I am actually good at, and I would like to be given credit for exactly one of them in my life and today will do."

---

They went up the corridor together to the Docket at about the seventh hour, and Oswin Marrow had the question back on his desk with the day's book shut, and he listened to the whole of it standing, and neither of them told him what the room bit was, and he did not ask, and the reason he did not ask is that he has been the master of a register of persons for twenty-two years and he has never once asked a person what a word in a book was for.

What he said, at the door, was about the office that is not named, and he said it because he had thought about it for four days and had not found a way to ask a court about it and had found a way to say it in a room instead.

"I have had a card in a window for twenty-two years and I have had people in that room who came because somebody with power told them to, and I have had about nine who came because nobody with power had told them anything and they were frightened, and I have had two who came because they wanted to see the book. **And in four days that room has become the one room in this empire where a person is entered, with their name, by a court, and has to have a person in it who is not from the office that is watching them, and I want it entered in my day's book, in my own hand, that a room I have kept for twenty-two years is now a room a person is watched in, and I have not been asked, and I would have said yes.**"

Then he shut the book, which he does at the end of every day whether anything has happened in it or not, and said the thing that is the reason the whole of the last four months happened, and he said it to Marek Kest and not to the woman, because the woman is not the subject of it.

"A bar is a line in a book with a gap in it, and the gap is a column, and about four days ago you closed the gap with a line you wrote yourself. A watch is a line in a book with a disposition against a name, and the disposition is in a different building, and there is no line of yours in it and there never will be. **Those are the same instrument. One is a page that will not let you do a thing until a person writes something, and the other is a page that will not let you do a thing without a person standing in the room watching you not do it, and the difference between them is a column and about four hundred yards, and I have been the master of the first one for twenty-two years and I have just found out what the second one is and I do not think anybody who has not held the first one has any business holding the second.**"

---

He asked the three questions at the door on the way out, standing in the corridor, and he asked them in the order a person asks them and not in the order he wanted them answered, and the man of sixty answered them in the order asked.

"**Is it for a time.** No. There is no date in it. I have looked for the date four times, because I keep the file of what that court has sent me for twenty-two years and there is nothing in the wording of it, and a thing with no date in it in my experience is a thing that somebody expects to last longer than the person who wrote it.

"**Is there anybody who can end it.** Yes. A person, and only a person, and the person has to be willing to say in a room, in words, in front of somebody who is not from the office, that they no longer wish to be watched, and the office that is not named has to accept it, and it cannot be compelled to and it cannot be asked whether it accepts it, because you cannot ask an office a question. **So the only person in this empire who can end it is you, and the only person who can hear you say it is a copyist of the third gallery, and neither of you can do anything at all with the answer.** I have had the same arrangement in a card in a window for twenty-two years and I did not know until this evening that I had it in a different shape.

"**And what it is for.** That is the one I cannot answer and I have stopped trying. I have read the sentence about a person being heard before an instrument takes effect upon them, and I have read the half of it a magistrate wrote nine days later about a document that is put in front of somebody first, and I am not a lawyer and I keep a book of names. What I know is this. There is going to be an instrument in this matter that somebody is going to produce against a household, and it is going to be produced against a person who cannot get to the room, and when it is, the person who is going to have to write down that the person was heard is going to be a person who is not from the office that produced the instrument, because that is the only kind of person there is. **And the person who is going to have to write it down is going to be a copyist who is not paid for it, in a room, and the whole of this machinery is for that one person and she does not know it yet and will be the last person in this matter to be told anything.**

"That," said Marek Kest, "is the whole of the volume and you have just said it in a corridor."

"I have said it four times in twenty-two years to about nine people and three of them thought I was talking about a window," said Oswin Marrow, "and I would like to be in a book about it, and I am not going to ask you to put me in a book, and if you do it I will read it back and you will read it back and we will both be embarrassed, and that will be the whole of my participation."

---

In the Received Room that night, with the stove in and the bar on the inside of the frame, he wrote it out in his own book, and read it back, and did not cross anything out, and this is the last of it.

> *The nineteenth of the ninth month, at about the ninth hour. I am eligible to be trained as a thing I have spent fourteen months refusing to be, and I am watched, and the two are in one sentence, and the reason they are in one sentence is that a man of sixty who is not a lawyer and has never been a clerk looked at a clause and worked out that a court which writes two things in two sentences can be asked to move one of them, and has been twice.*
>
> **And the watch is the price and it is not a qualification, and the whole of the difference between those two words is that a qualification is a thing you get and a price is a thing you pay, and I got one and paid one and they are on the same line of the same paper and I am not going to be able to tell anybody which is which in about four years, and that is the design and it is a good design and I have been on the wrong side of it for a year.**
>
> *Three things and I am going to leave them where they are and not do anything with them tonight.*
>
> *One. The office that watches me is not named and cannot be named, and the reason it cannot be named is the reason a seat's device is tolerable, and that is the thing a registrar said on a great stair in the fifth month: a person who signs a column headed by whose authority is discharging an office, and an office cannot be examined. **I have been reading a device of a seat for fourteen months as a thing that cannot be refused, and in about four days I have discovered that the reason I cannot be refused by is the same reason they cannot refuse me, and the difference is that one of them asked to be where it is and the other one did not.** I do not think that is a fair trade and I am not going to pretend it is.*
>
> *Two. A room with me in it has to have a person in it who is not from that office, and there is one such person in this matter, and she has been in doorways for eleven months, and she told me in a room this afternoon that she had worked out eleven months ago what the word in the second box was for and had not told me, and that she is not frightened of the order and does not like what it says about her, and that she decided to let it stand rather than decide, and I have written down the exact words because I am going to need them in about a year and because they are hers and she said them out loud so that I would have them.*
>
> *Three. Nobody has asked me whether I want to be eligible, and nobody has asked me whether I ought to be watched, and both of those are things I would have asked about, in a room, out loud, of any man who had put my name in a document, and nobody has, and the reason nobody has is that there is no form for asking a person whether they want a thing that a court has just done to them, and I have now been on the receiving end of the absence of that form and I can tell you it is exactly as bad from this side as the absence of a form for telling a magistrate what a higher court did is from the other one.*
>
> *M. Kest, clerk, Received Room, the nineteenth of the ninth month, at about the ninth hour, with the stove in and the bar on the inside of the frame, and the Ashfall bundle on the second shelf four feet away with the working stitch in it going to where it went twelve years ago, and I have not opened it and I have not thought about it tonight.*
