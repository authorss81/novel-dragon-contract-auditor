# Chapter 93: The Same Words the Other Way Round

It came down from the Crown Kiln on the twenty-fourth of the eighth month in an envelope with a seal on it that was not the fire-and-water device, and it was one side, and the clerk who brought it up from the intake desk said that a man in a good coat had put it into his hand at the top of the stair and would not give a name, and that this is the fourth time in fourteen months that a thing has come up that road with a seal on it and no person behind it, and that the clerk of twenty-five has written the time of it in a book with no heading at the top of it and has not been asked to write a heading and is not going to.

She had it read in the intake run at about the third hour, standing, in about eleven seconds, and then she put it in her own bag and carried it down four hundred yards to the chamber and read it twice at her desk before she did anything at all with it.

It is one side. It has a registrar's certification on the top of it, and the certification is in the ordinary form and says what certifications say, and the first sentence of it is the sentence that made her put the paper down for about four minutes and then pick it up again.

> *This is a certification and not a grant. It is not counted among the four things this court has granted in about nine years, and it is not to be entered in any column of grants, and the registrar enters that he has been asked to certify it in that form and has done so.*

She read that four times. Then she read the rest.

> *The ruling of this court made in the district of Mosswake on the twelfth of the ninth month of the ninth year, being the ruling in the matter of the field, the channel, the gate, the bank works and the household named in it, is hereby certified as operating —*
>
> *— in the district of Mosswake, and at the places named in that ruling, being the field, the channel, the Reed Cut gate, the bank works, and the household named in it; and*
>
> *— in no other place whatever.*
>
> *It is certified that that ruling is not a rule for this empire; that it binds no other court, no other office, no other district, no landing office, no trade and no person; that it may be read, quoted, printed, copied and sold by anybody; and that a document in the general wording of that ruling, wherever it is issued and by whatever office, is not the ruling and does not become the ruling by being in the general wording of it.*
>
> *And it is certified that this court does not know and cannot compel the authority under which a sheet of model wording of the twenty-second of the ninth month, in a series of nine hundred and forty upon an ordinary distribution to forty-six addresses, was set in type, and that the certification above does not touch that sheet, and does not affect it, and is not an answer to it, and that the office which prints it is not before this court and no person in it has been named.*
>
> *— S. Vint, presiding, room four. Certified by the registrar of that court, the twenty-second of the eighth month.*

---

She got about nine people into the chamber, which is a district court and does not fill one, and she read it out in about six minutes, and she said what it was about before she read it and who had written it and how it had come, in that order, because she has done that twice now and is not going to stop.

Then she said the thing out loud, and it is the reason the chapter is called what it is called, and she said it in a room with nine people in it and a district engineer at the back who had not been asked to come and a works clerk in a good coat in the fourth bench.

"**This is the same sentence that this case has spent fourteen months trying to get somebody to say, with the direction of the words reversed, and I am going to say that out loud in a room, and then I am going to say that I agree with it, and then I am going to say what it costs.**

"The sentence this case has been trying to get somebody to say is: *the words must be able to travel.* Because the words of the twenty-second of the ninth have been travelling for about a year and every district that has had them has used them against a household that had never heard of them, and about four hundred and forty people in four offices and one trade have been made answerable by a printed model, and the whole of the argument of this matter in fourteen months has been that a form of words is not a place and a person is.

"And what this paper says is: *a ruling travels nowhere, and a place is the whole of it, and if the words go to a district where nobody was heard, then the words are not the ruling and they do not become the ruling.*"

She put it face down on the desk.

"That is the same sentence. I want the room to have heard that from a magistrate and not from a pamphlet, and I have said it out loud in a chamber and I am going to write in my minute that I said it, because in nine years somebody will read this paper and think that a court of the jurisdiction is being praised for something, and it is not being praised, and the reason it is not being praised is the next thing I am going to say.

"I agree with it, and I agree with it because of what I found in this chamber nine days ago, and the reason is this. **What I found on the ninth of the eighth is that a thing in general use may be challenged by a person it affects, in a room, in a place that recognises the room, and that the person may be heard about it before it takes effect upon them.** And that is a thing about *where you are*. It does not say the thing travels and it does not say the thing does not travel. It says that a person standing in the room where the thing is being used is the only person this sentence in this empire was written about.

"And if the ruling of the twelfth of the ninth travels into a district that has never heard of Aldren Venn, then it travels without the one thing that makes it safe, which is that a girl of fifteen was heard in a room before anybody joined her as a guarantor. And the district that has never heard of Aldren Venn will apply it to its own girl of its own, for its own reasons, and it will be a *bad* district for about four years and then a *normal* one, and the girl in it will be as unheard as the girl in this one was. **That is the echo. The echo does not care about the addresses. A form of words is the only thing about this that travels, and a person is the only thing about it that does not.**"

Nobody argued, and she had not expected anybody to, and the district engineer put his pen down, which he does at the end of a sentence, and said one thing: "That is the first thing anybody has said in two years that explains why our returns have got a word in them we did not put there, and I would like to know what to do with it tomorrow morning." "You are not to do anything with it tomorrow morning," said Joane Tranter, "and if anybody in your office tells you to do something with it tomorrow morning you are to write down that you were told and by whom, and I will hear you about it in a room, in words, on a date." And the engineer said the date he could do, and she entered it, and it is in the minute.

---

The certification names five places, and the clerk on the bench wrote them out as she read them, and it is worth about four minutes of the afternoon to say what the five of them are, because a clerk writing them out is the only reason anybody knows.

A field. A channel. A gate. A piece of bank work. A household.

Those are the five things in the ruling of the twelfth of the ninth month, and four hundred and thirty miles away there is a woman who is seventeen who has worked the channel since she was old enough and a man who is dead who signed for it, and a gate with a rail on it, and a bar and an abutment that were rebuilt in a fortnight in the autumn of the year the field was saved.

**And four of the five are things a person can be shown, and the fifth is a list of work.** Joane Tranter said that out loud to the room and made the clerk write it down, because it is the thing in this matter that nobody had ever said in a room and everybody has been working round since the fifth month: *a list of work is the only kind of sentence in this empire that anybody can act on the same day, and a ruling about a person is a sentence that takes four months and a room and a magistrate who goes and looks, and a list of work takes a foreman and a nail.*

"And the fifth term is the list," she said. "It is the only one of the five that anybody could ever do anything about, and it went onto a rail at that gate, and the crew read it on the day it went up, and they did the work, and I have never in fourteen months asked one single person in this case to go and look at it, because it is four hundred and thirty miles away and it is nailed up and there is nothing in this city that would be affected by anything on it."

She looked at the engineer, who has a hundred and forty returns a quarter and nineteen years and a warding tick at the end of every sentence he says aloud.

"That is my finding on the certification and it is not in the minute because it is not a finding, it is a thing I have noticed. **Everything in fourteen months has been about a person and the only part of it that anybody did was a list of work, and nobody has looked at the list, and I have now read out four documents at that gate and none of them is a list of work.**"

---

Then she found the other half of it, and she found it by accident, and it took about four minutes, and it is the reason this chapter is not only a chapter about a good piece of paper.

The clerk had the file of the ninth of the eighth open on the bench behind her, because the file of the ninth of the eighth is the file she uses most in this court, and when she turned round to get the fourth entry she saw the second finding of her own minute, in her own hand, in four lines, and she read it standing up without meaning to:

> *It is found that the order of the court sitting in room four of the seventeenth of the sixth month of last year, being that no guarantor duty attaches to a person who has not been heard and has not signed, does not by itself provide that a person is heard before the instrument takes effect upon them. A person who has been warned about a document is afraid of that document, and a person who is afraid of a document cannot get in front of it.*

And the certification she had just read out, on the twenty-fourth of the eighth, from a court of the jurisdiction, gives a person the right to be heard, and says nothing whatever about when.

"They are the same sentence," she said, to the file. "He gave them the first half four days ago and I gave them the second half nine days ago and neither of us has read the other, and they are four miles apart and I have walked between them in about fifty minutes twice in nine days and spoken to about nine people, and I could have found out in any of those fifty minutes, and I did not, because neither of us asked the other, and asking would have meant saying out loud that we have each got half and that the half I have is the half that works."

She put the certification down and read the fourth entry of her own minute again, and it is in the room, and about four people in the room had heard both halves read out within about eleven minutes of each other, and about four of them understood what they had just been given.

---

> *Entered the twenty-fourth of the eighth month, on the magistrate's own motion, asked for by nobody.*
>
> *This court has received a certification of the court sitting in room four, of the twenty-second of the eighth month, of the ruling made in the district of Mosswake on the twelfth of the ninth month of the ninth year, which certifies that that ruling operates in that district at the places named in it and in no other place, and is not a rule for the empire, and binds no other court, office, district, landing office, trade or person, and names no person. This court has read it aloud in this chamber, and has said aloud, in this chamber, that the words of it are the words this court has itself been trying to get said for fourteen months with the direction reversed, and has said aloud that it agrees with the certification, and why.*
>
> *The finding of this court of the ninth of the eighth is not varied by this certification and nothing in it is. A thing in general use may be challenged by a person it affects, in a room, in a place that recognises the room, before it takes effect upon them. That finding is about where the person is. It does not decide that a ruling travels and it does not decide that a ruling does not travel. It decides that a person standing in the room where the thing is being used is the only person that sentence is about, and that is enough for it to do everything this court has needed it to do and not one thing more.*
>
> *And the court enters, on the same line, that the order of the court sitting in room four of the eighteenth of the seventh month gives a person the right to be heard in a room in a place that recognises the room and does not say when, and that the second finding of this court of the ninth of the eighth does say when, and that the two documents were made nine days apart in two buildings four miles apart, and that until the twenty-fourth of the eighth month neither court had read the other, and that the court sitting in room four said in open court on that day, in a room of nine persons, that it had not known, and this court has not been asked and does not ask, and would like somebody in nine years to say that the reason nobody asked is that there is no form in this empire for one court to ask another what it has found.*
>
> *And the court enters the cost of the certification in its own words, which is that it is worth one place; that the place is a district of about four hundred and sixty households four hundred and thirty miles from this chamber; that the forty-six district courts and the one landing office which have taken a recognition from the court sitting in room four are not bound by it and are not reached by it; that about four hundred and forty people in four offices and one trade are not affected by it and go on this month exactly as they went on last; and that the sheet of the twenty-second of the ninth month is in nine hundred and forty copies on an ordinary distribution and is expressly not touched by it.*
>
> *This court took no part in the making of it and was not asked about it and did not know of it. The movement asked for it, and the asking was public, and the asking was the finding, and if a person in nine years reads this minute and concludes that a certification in a named place is a small thing then this minute will tell them that the persons who asked for it thought it was the whole of the case, and were wrong, and were not dishonest, and got it in about a fortnight in a room.*
>
> *— J. Tranter, the twenty-fourth of the eighth month, at about the fifth hour, in the chamber, with a clerk in the room.*

---

She went down to the yard at about the half past four, which is the fourth time she has walked four hundred yards to do the ordinary thing rather than send a clerk, and the yard was shut and the door was on the latch and the woman was in, and she read it out at arm's length in about four minutes.

Hester Crane let her get about half way through it and then said: "Stop. Is it about me."

"It is about a district called Mosswake," said Joane Tranter. "It is about a field and a channel and a gate and a piece of bank work and a family, four hundred and thirty miles from this yard. You are not in it. Your name is not in it and mine is not in it and there is no person named in it at all, which is the first piece of paper that has come down that road in two years with no person in it anywhere, and I have been waiting fourteen months for one."

"Then what is it for," said Hester Crane.

"It says the ruling holds there and it does not hold anywhere else, and it says that if anybody anywhere uses those words they are not the ruling and they do not become the ruling by being in the same words."

"That is a strange thing to be pleased about."

"It is," said the magistrate. "And I am going to tell you the half of it that is not in the paper, and then I am going to tell you the other half, and then I am going to tell you what it has cost, because I have said four things at that gate now and I do not intend to come down here a fifth time and say something small."

She said all three, in about six minutes, at arm's length, in a yard, with the bar up at the end of the lane and a man of sixty-four in a lean-to at the head of the crossing who came out and stood nine feet away and did not come closer, which is the correct distance and is the only distance there is.

And at the end of it she said the last thing, which is the thing she has said at that gate twice and which is going to be in the minute of this court four times in a row if she keeps doing it.

"The nineteen pounds three and fourpence is not paid. The paper does not touch it. The works asked the district on the nineteenth of the twelfth of last year and the district has not answered and does not know it was asked, and the fund has six pounds and eightpence in it for the quarter and no line for a bridge in it and has not had one in nineteen years, and the crossing is open and nobody is liable for it, and it will be open in the ninth month and the twelfth, and you are sixty-one and the box in your kitchen is the reason it is open and there is nobody in the world to give it to."

"Yes," said Hester Crane. "I know what the box is."

"I know you know. I am saying it at a gate because in nine years a file in a city four miles away is going to say that a bridge dispute was found to be open to challenge, and it is not going to say in the same file that the bill was unpaid, and I am the only person who will be in a position to say both, and I intend to be dead long before anybody reads it, and that is the arrangement and not a complaint."

"Then read it out to somebody while you are still able," said Hester Crane. "That is my grandmother's whole of it and she was not read to either."
