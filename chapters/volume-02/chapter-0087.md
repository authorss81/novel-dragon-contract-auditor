# Chapter 87: A Document About Nobody

The card had been used before anybody offered it to Marek Kest, and the proof of that is not clever and was not looked for. It is this: a certificate impression is a thing people carry about. A sealed sheet is worth nothing on its own and is worth a great deal if it gets to a counter before a person does, and about a hundred and forty years of practice in this city has taught everybody with a tray to know the difference, and the difference is the day.

The card was produced at a door in the chamber of a district court in this city, in a matter with a number, on the twelfth of the sixth month, and it was produced by a person, and about nine people read it, and it came out again four days later and went back where it came from. It sat in a press at the end of that yard for three days after that, and then a man carried it to a room with a barred door, and then it went into a file in the lower district, and then a woman with steel under her thumbs looked at a die cut out of it.

Nobody wrote any of that down except one day of a book of five columns, kept by a clerk of twenty-five, whose sheets are made a day and are about nine years deep. This is the sheet for the twelfth of the sixth month. It is in a press in the same corridor Marek Kest works in, about forty feet from his own door, and it is the only record in the empire of the whole of it, and it was made by a man who has never once been asked what the figures in it are a number of.

---

He went and asked about it as a document and not as a favour, on the ninth of the seventh month, at about the fifth hour in the afternoon, which is the hour the intake desk fills up and the hour a person of twenty-five has his hands full and is most likely to say no.

The young clerk of the intake desk is not named here and he stays unnamed, and the reason is not modesty. It is that he has a sheet of foolscap with his own handwriting on it, and the sheet is a document about him, and if he is a person in this then this is about him, and it is not about him.

"I want to ask you a question about a book," said Marek Kest. "I am not going to see it and I am not going to ask you to write a heading on it. I want to know what a row in it is a number of."

The clerk put his pen down, which nobody had expected.

"No," he said.

"You have not heard the question."

"I heard the question," said the clerk, "and the answer is no, and I have had this book two years and nobody has ever asked. The one other person who came down here about it was on his own time in the third month, stood in a yard for an hour and a half, and came back and told me what a figure in a works' return is a number of, and I wrote it down in a book I made up myself, and I have not shown that book to anybody either."

"Fair. Here is what I am after, and you can say no to that instead, and it is a smaller thing."

The clerk waited.

"There is a card with a seal on it. It was produced at a door in this city on the twelfth of the sixth month, in a chamber in the lower district, in a matter with a number. It went in and it came out. I am not asking you to find the clerk who was at that door. I am asking you to tell me one thing: **does a row in a book like yours, or a row in a return like that one, carry anything about the person who brought a thing in?**"

"No," said the clerk.

"That is what I thought and I wanted it said out loud in a room by somebody who has kept the thing. Thank you."

"I have not been thanked for it in two years," said the clerk, "and you are the first and I am going to think about that for about a week, and I am not going to say anything else now because there is a queue and two of them are from offices."

---

He got the rest of it in about four days and none of it was difficult, and the whole of the difficulty was in the last hour on the eleventh of the seventh month, on a street, in front of a counter, in about nine minutes.

The officer's return from the door of that chamber is a sheet of five columns and the officer at the door was the same officer and the return is a document about him and he knows it. In goes a figure, which is a count of sheets. Then a time. Then a sheet or a bundle. Then where it came from or where it went to. Then an initial. He had been taught the two-column order in the first week of the first month of this year by a copyist at this counter in about eleven words, and he had written it in a book of his own, and the teaching was that **the order a thing arrives in is evidence of something.**

The card is in it. It went in on the twelfth of the sixth month and it came out on the sixteenth, and the four columns give a count, a time, a description, and a place, and the fifth column gives five strokes.

"You are asking me whether those five strokes are a name," said Marek Kest.

"They are what I write when I take a thing in and I have never once had anybody ask me to write the person's name and I could not if I tried in about half the cases because half the people in this building do not put their names to anything."

"And the card came out four days later and went back where it came from."

"It went back where it came from, and where it came from is in the fourth column and the fourth column is the only column anybody ever reads, and I am not going to say the fourth column out loud to you, and I would like it entered that I said no, because I am fairly sure that what you have come down here to ask for is a name and I have read enough of this matter to know that a name is four pence and half an hour."

"It is not that," said Marek Kest. "And I am going to tell you why, and then I am going to ask you one more question and the answer to that one you can give me in about four seconds and it is the only thing I came for."

"Go on."

"A card with a seal on it was left on a hook at a counter in the Kiln sheds and the hook has got about thirty things on it, and the thirty things belong to about eleven firms, and a carrier comes and takes the one with a seal on it because that is the trade and because a man who is paid eleven pence to carry a thing does not ask what is on the face of it." He turned his hands over and looked at them, which he does. "Is that how it is done."

"Yes," said the clerk.

"Then here is what I came for and it is the whole of it." He looked at the book, upside down, from about four feet away, the way a copyist reads a thing. "**The record that exists in this city for the day that card went out of a door is a record of the order in which things arrived and left a door, and the order is evidence of something, and nobody has ever asked what.** And the answer is that it is evidence of a great many things, and it is evidence of nothing whatever about a person, and the five strokes in the fifth column are not a name and there is no form in this empire for turning five strokes back into a man, and the reason there is no form is that a name is the one thing about a person that cannot be got out of him by a process, and a person who is paid eleven pence to carry a sealed card has a name and that is all he has got and it is not on the return and it never will be."

"You have just told me a man with a tray is safe."

"I have just told you a man with a tray is safe because he has not got a book with his name at the top of it," said Marek Kest, "and that is the first good news anybody in this matter has had in four months and it is good news about about forty people in four trades and about four hundred and forty people in four offices and one trade, and it is bad news about me, and I am going to take it in that order because that is the order it happens in."

---

The carrier was found in about a day, and it was the easiest thing in it, and it is here because he is the price of everything else.

He is about thirty and he carries sealed things between offices in this city for eleven pence a piece, and he has a counter at which he signs in, and that counter has a ledger, and the ledger has a hook number in it in about forty hands. He is not a party. He is not named in any count, in any order, or in any minute. He is not examinable by anybody in this matter and would not be if he wanted to be.

He was asked one question and he answered it in about a minute and a half and he did not want it in writing and it was not put in writing.

"You carried a card with a seal on it from a counter in the Kiln sheds to the chamber of a district court in the lower district on the twelfth of the sixth month, and back again four days later on the sixteenth."

"Aye."

"Did you look at it."

"No."

"Would you have."

"No," said the carrier, "and I will tell you why not, and then I want to go, because I have a bag on this counter and about nine things in it and two of them are late already. Eleven pence is not a wage, it is a piece of a wage, and you get a bag of about nine things and you are on the road from the fifth hour to the ninth, and the trade is a man does not look. That is not a rule anybody wrote. That is what the eleven pence is for, and if I looked at the things in my bag then I would not be able to carry them at eleven pence, I would have to charge a great deal more, and then nobody would carry them at all, and about a hundred and forty of us would be out of work by the winter."

"You would carry it again."

"I would carry it again tomorrow," said the carrier, "and I would carry it for the same eleven pence, and if you asked me who left it on the hook I would tell you I do not know and I would be telling you the truth, because there are about eleven firms on that counter and I take what has a seal on it and I do not read the seal and I do not ask, and I have never in nine years had anybody ask me and I am not going to start. You have told me it is false."

"I have told you it is a photograph of a true seal, which is a different thing and it is a worse thing and I am not going to explain it to you in the street."

"Then I will take it on trust," said the carrier, "and I would rather you did not tell me any more, and here is the thing you are all getting wrong and I am the only man in this city who can tell you and I am telling you for nothing because I am about to be late. **I am not a person in this.** I never was. There is nothing in this for me and nothing against me and nothing for anybody to take off me, because I do not know what it is, and the reason I do not know what it is is that the eleven pence is bought for not knowing, and if the day ever comes when a man in a good coat can take a thing out of a bag and read it and then put it back and the carrier does not know that he has done it, then that man is not a thief, and the day he is caught he will say he only looked, and he will be right that he only looked, and every one of you will find that you wish he had been something worse."

He picked up his bag.

"You have not asked me a single question about the thing itself," he said at the door. "Nine months, from what I hear of it. Everybody asks the man who carried it what was in it. You did not."

"No," said Marek Kest. "And I am going to tell you the reason and then you can go, because you are about to be late and because you have just told me the best thing anybody has said in this matter in fourteen months and I owe you ninety seconds.

"I have spent fourteen months learning one sentence. *A document with a person's name at the top of it is a document about that person.* And it is the best instrument anybody in this matter has made and it works on a district works and a provincial court and a man in a good coat, and it does not cross a species, and a judge in a corridor told me that in the fourth month and I have not been able to put it down since.

"And that card has got a mark at the top of it. And a mark is not a name. **A document with a mark at the top of it is a document about nobody at all.** And the first thing you do with a document about nobody is nothing, and the second thing you do with it is nothing, and there is no room in any court in this empire where a document about nobody can be read out and a person can be found to have said something about it. **That is what my sentence is for, and I have been holding it upside down for fourteen months, and a forgery is the first thing in this empire it is the right way up for.**

"And I am not going to go to a counter in the Kiln sheds and read a hook ledger and find out which of about eleven firms left it there. I want you to understand that I know how to. I know the counter, I know the ledger, I know the numbers, and it would take me a morning, and I have written down the reason in my own book and the reason is that the moment I do it, the sentence stops being the thing that stops a room and becomes the thing that starts one, and in nine years a hook in the Kiln sheds is a place where a person is caught, and it will be a place where a person is caught because of me, and with a name at the top of it, which is the whole problem with the empire and is not a problem anybody has ever been able to write a form about."

---

He did not go to the lane off the Kiln road either, and that was said out loud in a room, on the eleventh of the seventh month, at about the ninth hour, in the Received Room, with the stove in and the bar on the inside of the frame, and Tamsin Rook wrote it down because she was there and he asked her to.

> *The eleventh of the seventh month, at about the ninth hour. There is a woman in a lane off the Kiln road who is the only person alive who can break a number on a die into the part that is hers and the part that is not, and she cut about nine hundred plates off that run and the run has a year in the middle of it, and if I go into that lane and ask her one question then the instrument works on her and she is a person in a file for the rest of her life, and she is not worth a tray at the moment and that is her whole protection, and a clerk of the Roll Hall is worth a tray. I am not going. I am not going to go and stand in a lane and be a man in a good coat who is good at questions, and I have said this out loud in a room with somebody in it because in nine years there will be a line in a file about a woman in a lane and I want the reason for the line to be in the same book as the line, and the line is hers and the reason is mine.*
>
> *I am not going to write the other three down in a row, either, and I want that written here while I am writing about things I am not doing. There are three other appearances of a word that walks about, and one of them is in a printed amendment sheet that is in nine hundred and forty copies on an ordinary list to forty-six addresses, and I could put all four on one sheet of paper in about four minutes, and the copyist of the third gallery did exactly that on the tenth of the fifth month, on a sheet of foolscap she had ruled herself at her own bench, and she wrote four names on it and then looked at what she had written and then tore it up and put the pieces in the waste box under her counter, and she told me about it about four minutes afterwards and she told me why, and the reason was that a list of people who can be found is a list of doors, and a man in the Kiln sheds has four doors in a book with no office on it and wrote down in his own hand that the first thing anybody would do with a list like his in nine years is not to find out about a plate. **Because the moment four of them are in a row somebody will draw a line, and a line is a chain, and a chain is an invention, and the whole finding of this matter is that there is nobody at the end of it to draw it to.** Four is a number. A practice is not a person. If I write the four in a row I will have made a road, and there is nobody at the far end of it, and in nine years somebody will walk it and find a house and be extremely pleased with himself, and the house will be there because I put it there.*
>
> *M. Kest, clerk, Received Room, the eleventh of the seventh month, at about the ninth hour.*

Then he did the other thing, which is the thing this whole week was for, and he did it in about four minutes standing up, and Tamsin Rook wrote it out and read it back and it is the only finding she has ever read that she has not found a hole in.

> *A document with a person's name at the top of it is a document about that person. That is the instrument and it has been the instrument of this matter for fourteen months.*
>
> *This is what it does to a document that has nobody's name at the top of it, and it is worth putting in one place because nobody in this building has ever had cause to.*
>
> *It says the document is about nobody.*
>
> *A document about nobody cannot be answered, because answering is a thing persons do. It cannot be refused, because a refusal has to be given by somebody. It cannot be held to be a forgery, because forgery is a thing a person commits. It cannot be produced against a person in any proceeding, because there is no person to produce it against, and that is not a protection for the person — there is no person in it. **It binds nobody who has not been heard, because it has not bound anybody at all.***
>
> *And every document in this matter for fourteen months has had a mark or a series or an office at the top of it, and the office has not been a person, and the series has not been a person, and the mark has not been a person, and I have spent fourteen months being beaten by all of them and being told in a corridor that my sentence does not cross a species. It does not cross a species. **It does not need to. A sentence about a name does not need to cross a species. It is the documents that have no name on them that are the problem, and there has been one forgery in this matter and it is the only one that is not a person, and the instrument stops it, and stopping it took four minutes and cost nothing and made nobody a file.***
>
> *I am going to be careful about the next hour of my life, because I have been in this matter for fourteen months and every single thing I have found has turned out to be a person I was not looking for, and this one is not, and I do not know what to do with a thing that is not a person, and I am not going to go and find out in a hurry. The eighteenth of the seventh is six days away and I am not going to be in that room and nobody in this building has been told anything about it and I am not going to go and ask, and I am going to spend the six days on this and on the Lowcross application and on nothing else.*
>
> *M. Kest, the eleventh of the seventh month.*

He did not find out anything else about the card that week. The hook ledger was in the Kiln sheds and it had about eleven firms on it and it was four minutes' work, and he wrote the number of the street down in his own book and did not go to it, and the number of the street is in his own book in a hand nobody will be able to read, and it is the only place in fourteen months where Marek Kest has written down where something is and not gone and looked.
