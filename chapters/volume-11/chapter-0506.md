# Chapter 0506: Nine Hundred Sheets A Year, About Nine Of Them Signed Wrong, And A Pot That Hands A Pen Back In Either Hand

There is a sill at that end of that room with a pot standing on it that takes a pen and hands it straight back, and about nine hundred sheets a year go out of that building with his mark at the foot of them.

He is about sixty-one and he has been the second hand since he was forty-two, which is nineteen years, and there is not a working sentence in a paragraph, and in all that time he has taken care that nobody standing on that floor ever found out.

About nine of those nine hundred in a year are not what the person above him wrote. He has signed every one of them as well, and he is the only man alive who could tell you which nine, and it is on nothing at all, and it has been in his head since the spring.

There is a window in that room about two feet wide with a pane out of it, and it has been out for about four years, and a question stands on a shelf under it with no full stop at the end of it and her own name at the foot of it, and that is not printed out in any building in this empire and nobody is going to ask her about it a second time.

The four of them in this matter who cannot make sense of a paragraph are four, and a man of about fifty-five with a chain in a shed at the end of a lane is not one of them and never has been, and there is no room in this building in which any of the four can be asked one question about a number, and nobody in this building has ever asked.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the fourth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the second hour and about the fifth.

---

The woman of about fifty-three came to the end of that sill at about the third hour and put the book down on the sill, on the stone, not on the pot side.

She has kept the minutes of that room for nineteen years. She advises nobody in it. She has not put a thing to that room in about four years and she is not going to start this month, and she said so before she spoke, and she wanted it entered that she said so, and there is nowhere standing in that room at which entering anything is done.

"**I am going to say one flat thing about your hands and then I am going to go back and sit down, and I am not asking you anything and you are not going to answer me and I am not going to ask you twice.**"

He put the pen down about four inches to the left of the pot, face down, and picked it up again, and he did not look at her.

"**You stopped going along the top edges of them with your left hand about a fortnight after I came out of that room into the corridor the first time in four years and said a true thing at the end of this sill. You did that about a fortnight after and you have not gone near a top edge with that hand since, and there was a passage of a month and then something else, and whatever the something else was, you did not tell me and I did not ask. You cannot read the reason for it. If I put it in front of you now you could not get a working sentence out of it and you would not be able to tell me whether it was true.**"

He squared the sheet that was on the stone in front of him, which takes about a second and which he does not need to do.

"**And that is the whole of what I came out for. I wanted it said in a room and not worked out on your own, and it is said, and I am not thanking you for hearing it and you are not going to be thanked for anything this month.**"

---

Then she told him the other half of it, to the sill, in the flat voice of a woman reading a line out of a thing she has been in for nineteen years.

"**Put a man at a sill down in any column in this empire as the one who began doing a thing differently on one particular morning and you have to write a word over the top of him, and the word would have to be about the morning, and there is no such word, and that is not because anybody in this building has refused to write one.**"

"There is no such word," he said.

"I am not asking you whether there is. **I am telling you that there is not, and I have been in a room nineteen years and I have watched people want a word over the top of things and I have never once seen one arrive.**"

And she went back and sat down, and she did not say another word that morning, and she is not going to say another word this month, and nobody asked him anything.

---

Here is the whole of what has changed, and it is not much, and nobody in that building has seen any part of it.

The pot takes a pen and hands it straight back. It has done that for nineteen years to a left hand and it does it now to a right hand, and it has never once had an opinion about which, and that is the only fact about the two of them that has held still while everything else about them stopped being the same.

He put the pen down on the stone four inches to the left of the pot and picked it up and signed with the other hand and put it back in the pot and the pot handed it straight back, and he did it about nine hundred times between the second hour and about the fifth, and he did not stop doing it, and he is not going to.

He cannot say why. He has had that for about three weeks now. It has not got better and it has not got worse and it is not going to get better. The thing he cannot account for is going to go on being true whether he accounts for it or not, and that is the whole of the change, and it happened about three weeks ago on a morning that has gone, and nobody in that building has said one word about it and nobody is going to.

Nobody has ever called it a decision. Nobody has ever called it a statement. It is not on anything.

---

About nine of the nine hundred a year are wrong and he signs all of them, and he could not put a mark beside one of them and call it a disagreement, because there is nowhere in this empire that a man who cannot read a paragraph is allowed to put a mark beside a sum. If anybody in that building could read one of them and could see the line above the one he has signed, and if that anybody then went and looked at the same nine and compared them, that anybody would know.

He has wanted about four minutes of a morning like that more than once in nineteen years. He would turn about half of them down on the spot and let the rest go by, and there has not been one morning. And there is nobody to ask, and nobody to be asked, and nobody who is in a position to be told what a number is, and a person who cannot read a paragraph cannot be given a paragraph to read and cannot be given a sum to disagree with and cannot be put behind a line of anybody's without becoming a party to it.

He is owed nothing and he has asked for nothing in nineteen years.

---

The woman of about nineteen came out at about the third hour under an armful and stopped where she always stops, at the end of that sill, and stood there for as long as she was going to stand there.

She has been at that end of that room for about a year and she keeps the minutes under the woman of about fifty-three, and she has not said one word out loud in that room in that time, and she looked at the pot and at the pen on the stone and at the pot again and neither of them was looking at her.

Nine hundred sheets went out of that building between the second hour and about the fifth, one at a time, and the pot handed the pen back every single time it was put into it, and the mark at the foot of every one of them was made by a hand that is not the hand it was made by a month ago.

A woman of about nineteen has seen him do it three mornings running. She has not written it down and she has not said a word about it and her book is shut.
