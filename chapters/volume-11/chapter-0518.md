# Chapter 0518: A Book Open And A Pen Lying In The Middle Of It, An Hour In Which Nothing Was Asked Of Anybody, And A Woman Standing Up And Going Out Of A Room In The Ordinary Way

That counter opens at the second hour and shuts at the sixth. The case is out, the boards are up, and there is nothing on either of them, which is the ordinary condition of that floor and has been for eleven years.

There is a woman of about twenty-nine at the far end of the two joined tables. She has been at that end for four years and she says one true thing in four years, and it was said once, and nobody has taken it up, and nobody ever has and nobody ever is going to.

The book lies open in front of her and the pen lies across the middle of the open page, and it has lain across the middle of that page every working morning since before the woman beside her came to that end of the tables.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the seventh month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the third hour.

The two joined tables make one working top about nine foot long, and a person who has decided to get up out of the room has to get past the whole of it to reach the door and past two women who have not been asked one thing all morning. She had all of it worked out a long while back and had never once put it to either of them as a thing they ought to know.

---

A month ago a woman at the near end of those boards said a true thing out loud in the ordinary way. The woman at the far end of the two joined tables had her face down over that book for the whole of it. Nothing was asked of anybody, and no form anywhere in this empire could enter her as the one who was at that end of the room when it was said or as the one who was not.

Nobody has told her that this happened. She worked it out herself, in about a week, sitting where she sits, and she has not said one word about it to anybody on that floor and she is not going to.

What she worked out is that she has no distance any more. That is the whole of it. There is no way in this empire to stand at the far end of a room on a morning in the sixth month of that year and to be shown that you were somewhere else.

---

Tamsin Rook was at her end with her back to the room and she squared an edge that did not need squaring, and at about the third hour she said the second of the two true things she has said out loud in that room in about three months, and she said it to the boards, in the ordinary way, and she did not lower her voice for anybody.

"**A month ago I said out loud in this room that I had not decided anything, and I have still not decided anything, and I have found out in the last four weeks that the saying of it was not free. It cost the woman at the other end of these tables the only thing she had, and she never agreed to it, and she was never asked, and there is no way in this empire of giving it back, and I am not going to say sorry for it, and I am not going to say it again.**"

Marn Ottery did not turn round. She has put the shape of a thing out loud four times in that room and has said in advance there will not be a fifth, and what she said at the end of that third hour was not a shape of anything.

She went on working the top at the rate she works it at on any morning, and the sound of it is the ordinary sound of the near end of the working top and carries about as far as the far end, and the only person in the room who was in a position to have said one word about the rate of it had her face down over an open book.

---

The clerk of about twenty-nine stood up.

She pushed her chair back about a foot, which nobody in that building has ever seen a person do at that table, and she walked out of that room and down the stair, four flights, at about the third hour of an ordinary morning, and she did not say one word to anybody on the way out.

Four flights of stone with a rail on the open side take a person down at the rate a person goes down them who has decided to be out of a room, and she went at that rate. Halfway down, a window sits at a man's chest and daylight arrives level off the yard and lies right across the treads in one pale stripe, and she went down through the middle of it with her shadow thrown out in front of her onto the wall and then drawn back up over her own feet.

Nobody stopped her. Nobody said one word. The two of them at those boards went on with what they were doing, and the man with the satchel at the far end of the wood did not look up, and the whole of it took about as long as a sheet takes to be squared and then it was a room with two people in it.

---

**Walking out of a room is not turning a thing down.**

That is the whole of what she knows about the four flights she went down, and it is the only thing she knows. A person who walks out of a room cannot be shown to have refused anything, because refusing is not a fact that will sit in a column anywhere in this empire. And a person who stays in it cannot be shown to have accepted anything either, for the same reason and for the same absence of a column. There is no form that takes a woman leaving a floor at the third hour. There is no heading that could go over her for it. There is nothing.

So she has not refused anything, she has not accepted anything, she has not been asked anything, and she is not going to be thanked for anything, and the only thing she has lost is the distance, and the distance is not coming back, and nobody is going to tell her that she has lost it because nobody knows she had it.

---

Marn Ottery said one flat thing to the boards about four minutes after she had gone, and she said it once.

"**Nobody in this building is going to be thanked for standing up and nobody is going to be asked why, and if either of you comes back to me about it I will tell you there is nowhere standing in this room at which it could be put.**"

Tamsin Rook did not come back to her about it.

"You are not going to say one word to her when she comes back up," Marn Ottery said, to the boards.

"I am not going to say one word to her when she comes back up, and you are not going to say one word to her, and the two of us are the only people on this floor who could."

"**And that is the second time in a month that two people in this room have known what the other one was doing and neither of them has said one word about it, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that takes a person for having been in a room with another person in it. That is the whole of what happened on this floor in the last month, and it is not a fifth thing, and I am not going to say it again.**"

Tamsin Rook went on squaring the edge, and neither of them said one word to the other about the woman who had been at that end of the tables since before Tamsin came to that floor, and neither of them said one word about it to anybody that week or after, and nobody put anything to either of them and neither has been thanked and neither is going to be.

---

She came back up the four flights at about the fourth hour and came into that room and sat down at the far end of the two joined tables and opened the book and put the pen down in the middle of it, and nobody said one word to her about where she had been and she did not say one word about it either.

Coming back up four flights of stone takes a person longer than going down them, and the chair had been standing where she pushed it about a foot back from the top of the tables for the better part of an hour, and she came in and sat down in it without moving it, and the room was the room she had left an hour earlier with nothing whatever done to it while she was gone.

There is nothing in that building that says she was in that room, and nothing in it that says she was not, and nothing in it that says she went down four flights and came back up again, and the two of them at those boards have not mentioned it and are not going to.

At the sixth hour the case went in behind the boards and the four items went up onto the wall above it, one to a nail each, and the pen stayed where she had put it, in the middle of an open book at the far end of two joined tables, and the drawer underneath stayed shut with four things in it and the knife is in a different one and she has never said which.
