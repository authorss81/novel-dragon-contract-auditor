# Chapter 0535: A Stair That Did Not Sound, A Woman Two Floors Down Who Said That Was The Last Time And Was Right About It, And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Who Stopped Listening

He takes that room by the week and the landlord has never once been through the door of it. There is a table and a stool and a bed against one wall and a chair standing against nothing at all, and the window looks at the back of another house, and about four feet of bare wall stands beside that window with a nail in it, and the coat is on that nail, and nothing in that room makes heat.

Marek Kest. Thirty-eight. He stands in no register in this city and never has, and he holds nothing that anybody could come for, and he is not at a stage and does not expect to arrive at one.

The two things are in the inside pocket of that coat on opposite sides of a seam, where they have been since a wet morning about two years ago, and neither of them has been out of that pocket in this building or in any other building in this city, and no hand in this city has gone into that coat.

It is the second day of the first week of the twelfth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the morning.

---

The stair did not sound.

She had come up those four flights about a month ago and said one true thing out loud on the other side of his door, and she had told him she was coming up to be in the room when she said it, and she had said that was the last time she was going to and that he was not going to be thanked for it and was not going to be asked what she had said.

He had not opened the door then and he has not opened it since, and the stair has been going up and down that whole of that month without stopping anywhere near his floor, and it did not come this morning either.

He listened for it for the first hour. That is a thing a person does with an ear and not with a decision, and it took him most of that hour to work out that it was not.

The four flights of that house are stone and they are cold, and you hear a person on them the way you hear a person in the next room, which is to say that you stop hearing anything else while it is happening. He had got used to the whole of it going up and coming down, twice a day, every day for eleven years, and about a month of it had not stopped where it used to stop, and he had not decided anything about that and had not known he was waiting.

---

The window looks at the back of another house and has done for about two years.

It looks at a wall about four feet off, and above the wall at a back room, and in that back room there is a person. There is a floor in there that gets swept in the morning and a table with something on it, and about the second hour the light comes in on the far side of it and the person in there gets up and moves about for about an hour, and then there is nobody in that room for the rest of the day.

He had never once gone and stood at that window. He had used it for the light and for the fact of it being a window, and that was all it had been for about two years, and about a month ago he had started noticing that there was a room at the other end of it with somebody in it, and had gone on not going and standing.

This morning he went and stood at it.

There is nothing to be done at a window. He stood at it for about the length of a sheet takes to be signed and then for about four times that, and the whole of what he got out of it was a person in another building sweeping a floor and then not sweeping it.

The want was not the person. He worked that out standing there, and it took him as long as the person took to finish the floor. The want was the room. He wanted to be in a room with a second person in it who was not there because of him and had not come for him and was not going to know he existed, and there is no way in this empire to arrange that, and he has never once tried to arrange anything in his life.

Then he went and sat down on the bed, because that is what he does, and the window stayed what it was.

---

He said it out loud at about the fourth hour, to the plaster of a room with nobody in it, and there was nobody there to say it to and there never has been.

"**She came up those four flights to be in a room when she said a true thing, and she told me that was the only reason, and she was right, and she has not come up again, and I have not opened that door, and neither of those is going to change this month. The whole of what is left on that stair is a woman who is not going to be thanked and a man who is not going to be asked, and there is nobody in this house who is going to be told that either of those two people is the one who was on the other side of a door.**"

It was not a resolution, and there is not one thing in that room that a sentence could settle.

The walls of a room taken by the week are the only party in this matter that cannot be asked anything, and those walls did not answer him.

What he worked out after that, and what cost him something, was not about her and was not about the door.

She had made a decision. A decision is a party, and a party is the only thing in this matter that a person can be given warning in advance of what it is going to be used for. She had made it alone in a room on a floor below his own, with a boiler at the back of it and a wall to say it to, and then she had gone up four flights and put a second person in that decision, and that person had been him, and he had been on the other side of half an inch of wood and had not opened the door.

Which made him a person who had been in a room when a true thing was said, and there is not one column in this empire that will take him for it, and there is none that will take her either, and the two of them are the only two people in that house who know it.

And she is the only person in that house who can say a true thing out loud with a second person in the room. She has used that up. He is the second person. He has not gone and got another one.

He had never once in his life made a decision anybody could be shown having made. That is the whole of the difference between the two of them and it took him about a year to work out, and it has cost him nothing, because a person who cannot be shown to have decided anything cannot be given anything either, and there is not one column in nine hundred buildings that will take a man who has decided to do nothing.

Except that he had been sitting at a window listening for a stair for an hour, and that was a decision, and it was the first one, and nobody in this empire could be shown it, and it was still one.

He thought about that for a while. Then he got up and put his back to the door and faced the wall that is not the wall with the nail in it, and he made the second one on purpose, standing up, in an empty room, where it could not be entered anywhere at all.

He stopped listening for the stair.

The whole of what that cost him is that if she ever comes up those four flights again he will not know about it, and the whole of what it bought is that there is nothing in this empire to put a man against for having done it, and he had about eleven years of a habit to give up, and he gave it up inside one sheet of work.

---

The stair sounded at about the fifth hour, two floors down and going up.

It went up the way it goes up, four flights of it, stone on stone, and it came to a stop on the floor below his own and did not go any further, and then it went on up again and stopped at the top and then it came down and stopped again on the floor below his own, and somebody on that floor took a long time over a landing.

He heard all of it. That was the thing he had not expected. He had worked out that the decision was to stop listening and he had made it standing up in an empty room like a man putting a thing down, and it turned out that listening is not a door that shuts. It is an ear. You can decide once and the ear goes on doing what it was built to do, and what you have actually done is decided that you will not go to the window of it.

He stood in the middle of that floor and let it finish, and he did not go to the door, and the stair came up at the sixth hour and went down at the seventh without stopping on his floor, and neither time did he go to the door.

The top room of that house is let by the week and the landlord has never once been through the door of it.

The only thing anywhere in that house that says anybody has ever slept in it is a number cut into the back of a key to that room, and that cut is going thin at the top of it. The woman at the bottom of that stair has held it under her thumb about as long as it takes to read a page and has never once said to anybody what is on it.

He is at the top of that stair at this hour and does not know any of that, and she is not going to be asked about it, and he is not going to be thanked for not knowing it, and this empire holds no paper of any kind on which either of them could be entered as the one who knew what was cut into the back of that key.

About four feet of bare wall stands beside that window with a nail driven into it at roughly the height of his own hand and a coat on that nail. He did not put his hand on the wall this month. The stool was where the stool is and the table was where the table is and the chair is still standing against nothing at all, and the window looked at the back of another house all morning and will look at the back of another house all afternoon, and the stair went up at the sixth hour and came down at the seventh without stopping on his floor, and he did not hear it.
