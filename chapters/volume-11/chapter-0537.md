# Chapter 0537: A Foreman Putting Her Hand Flat On The Front Of A Bench She Has Not Touched In Eleven Years And Not Taking It Away, A Man Who Is Not Going To Be The Reason For It, And A Reader At The Far End Who Did Not Look Up

She has been at the back of that bench for two months and the man at the front of it has been at the front of it for eleven years, and about four feet of bare wall separates the front of that bench from the back of it, and neither of them has crossed it in that time.

Halla Wray is fifty-one and she is a foreman, and she has been the occupant of a class for eleven years, and the paper behind that class is on a board by that door and has not been filled in since before she came and is not going to be.

She began a sentence in that doorway about two years ago. It has got to the same two words six times and the rest of it has not arrived and nobody in that lane is going to arrive it for her.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the twelfth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour.

---

Here is what she had worked out at the back of that bench and had not said out loud to anybody.

A class puts a person back where they have been, and it does it by doing nothing, and where she has been for a month is the back of that bench.

So the back of that bench is the place she has been, and about ten weeks ago she stood at the front of that bay for one hour, and the two of those are not compatible, and there is no form in this empire in which a foreman is entered as the one who has been at two ends of the same bench in one month.

She had gone back to the back of it because going back was the only move that cost nothing. She had not chosen it. A class does not wait to be chosen.

This morning she chose.

---

She came down the length of that floor at about the fourth hour and stopped at the near end of the bench, and she put her hand flat on the wood in front of the chain, about four inches from where his hand was going along it, and she did not take it away for about as long as a sheet takes to be signed, and then she left it there.

He did not stop the chain and he did not look at her hand.

"**You have not done that in eleven years and I have not asked you to and I am not going to, and I am not going to be the reason for anything you do in your own bay, and you are not going to be thanked for it and you are not going to be asked what it was for.**"

"I have not come to the front of this bench for you."

"I have not said you did. I have said what I am not going to do."

---

"You have said four things in this bay in about a year and you said the last of them with two people in the room and nobody answered it and you did not want anybody to."

"That was about a month ago and I am not going to say it a second time and I am not going to say any part of it again, and the woman of nineteen at the end of that sill is a different room and none of this is hers."

"I know it is not hers. I have never once said a word to her about anything that has been said in this bay and I am not going to start this month, and there is nowhere in this lane standing at which I could ask you anything and I am not going to look for one."

"Then put your hand back on your bench and let a woman work."

---

She put her hand flat on the front of that bench and kept it there until about the sixth hour, and about the fifth of them she started working with the other hand, so that for about an hour she was a woman working at the front of her own bay with one hand on the front of the bench and the rest of her going.

The chain went along under his hand the whole of that hour. At about the fifth hour he put his hand past the third link, about a hand's width further along it than he has ever gone in eleven years, and he did it without stopping and without looking down, and then he brought it back to the third link and stayed there.

Neither of them said one word about either of those two things. Nobody in that shed has ever thanked him for a thing he did at that bench and nobody is going to start this month, and a class cannot put a hand back where it has been, and neither can anybody else.

---

The bundle of that shed's own ordinary work had been worked off the far end of that bench and cleared away in the ordinary way, and what was at that far end now was the two of seventeen and nothing else.

Neither of them was spoken to, thanked or sent for, and the girl of seventeen was not given the chance to say no about anything, and the one with the book carried on with what he reads and did not look up at any part of that hour.

There is no form anywhere in this empire in which a foreman of fifty-one is entered as the one who put her hand on the front of her own bench, and a class can put her back at the back of it without anybody lifting a pen.

She has done this about nine mornings now and it is going to keep being done about nine mornings a month, and nobody in that lane is going to be told that her hand was there and nobody is going to be thanked for it.

And there is no form in this empire in which a man at a front bench is entered as the one who put his hand a hand's width past the third link, so the two of them have both done a thing this month that cannot be entered, and neither of them has been told what the other one did, and neither of them is going to be asked about either.

---

At about the seventh hour the bracket came on and put light into that bay from the wrong end, the way it does when somebody is standing in it, and the man at the front bench got up off that bench in the ordinary way.

The sentence she began in that doorway about two years ago did not arrive this morning and it is not going to arrive this month, and nobody in that lane has ever asked her for the rest of it, and there is no form anywhere in this empire in which a person is entered as the one who has been waiting for the end of it for two years.
