# Chapter 0537: A Foreman Putting Her Hand Flat On The Front Of A Bench She Has Not Touched In Eleven Years And Not Taking It Away, And A Reader At The Far End Who Did Not Look Up

She has been at the back of that bench for two months, and the man at the front of it has been at the front of it for eleven years, and about four feet of bare wall separates the front of that bench from the back of it, and neither of them has crossed it in that time.

Halla Wray is fifty-one. She is a foreman, and the paper behind the class she has been the occupant of for eleven years is on a board by that door and has not been filled in by any hand since before she came to that bay.

She began a sentence in that doorway about two years ago. It has got to the same two words six times and the rest of it has not arrived, and nobody in that lane is going to arrive it for her.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the twelfth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour.

---

The working out was done at the back of that bench over about a month, and she did it standing up, one piece at a time, in between other work.

A class puts a person back where they have been, and it does it by doing nothing. Where she has been for a month is the back of that bench. About ten weeks ago she stood at the front of that bay for one hour, with a man at the front of it who would not say a word while she was standing there, and the two of those are not compatible, and there is no form in this empire in which a foreman is entered as the one who has been at two ends of the same bench in one month.

She had gone back to the back of it because going back was the only move that cost nothing. That was the truth of it and it took her most of a month to get to it. She had not chosen it. A class does not wait to be chosen.

This morning she chose.

---

She came down the length of that floor at about the fourth hour and stopped at the near end of the bench and put her hand flat on the wood in front of the chain, about four inches from where his hand was going along it.

She did not take it away for as long as it took him to work the length of the stone and come back, and then she left it there.

He did not stop the chain and he did not look at her hand.

"**You have not done that in eleven years and I have not asked you to and I am not going to, and I am not going to be the reason for anything you do in your own bay, and you are not going to be thanked for it and you are not going to be asked what it was for.**"

"I have not come to the front of this bench for you."

"I have not said you did. I have said what I am not going to do."

"Then do not do it, and let me get on with my morning."

He did not do it.

---

"You have had a paper on that board by the door for eleven years," she said, "and no hand has gone near a line of it, and you have never once said a word to me about what that is."

"No."

"It is the same as the thing I am in, and I have never once put the two of them together, and I have had about four days this month to do it in. **The class puts me back where I have been without anybody doing anything and nobody lifting a pen, and there has not been a single form in this empire that will take me for a thing I did this morning, and there is not going to be one. That is what it is for. Whatever else a class is, that is what it is for.**"

"I have never said a class did that to you."

"You have never said it did not."

"No." The chain went a little way and came back. "I have been at this bench for eleven years with a paper on a board that nobody has ever filled in, and I have worked out one thing about that paper and I have not got round to saying it, and you have just stood there and told me what your box is for and you have not asked me anything about mine. I am not going to ask you. I am telling you that a paper nobody fills in is not a thing that has been done to you, and I have been in this bay every working day for eleven years and I know the difference between the two."

"Then what is yours."

"That is exactly the thing I have not worked out."

She worked for the rest of that hour with one hand on the front of the bench and the other one doing the work, which is not a thing a person can do well for an hour and which she did badly and on purpose.

It was worse than badly. The sheets go out one at a time and they go out in order, and a sheet has to be turned the right way up and squared along the edge of the bench with the heel of the hand, and she could do all of that one-handed. What she could not do one-handed was the looking, and the looking is the part of a foreman that is not hands: nine pairs of hands in that shed and one pair of eyes that goes along all of them at the end of their work. There is a thing about a sheet that you can see in half a second if you are looking for it and not at all if you are not, and for one hour in that bay she was not looking for it at any of them.

There was a piece of work done at the far end of that bay in that hour that was not right. It was found at about the sixth hour, after she had gone back to the back of the bench with her hand still on the wood in front of it, and it was the same kind of thing as the last one and about the same size, and it sat on that bench until the morning.

Nobody in that shed was told that the day's count had come out one short for the second time in about a month, and nobody was told why it had come out one short, and there is no form anywhere in this empire in which a foreman of fifty-one is entered as the one who put her hand on the front of her own bay for an hour, and a class can put her back at the back of it without anybody lifting a pen.

The chain went along under his hand the whole of that hour. It is a heavy thing and it is worked by a hand that has been working it since before the foreman came to that bay, and when somebody stands four inches off it at the near end of the bench, the sound of it fills the space between them and neither of them has to say anything at all.

At about the fifth hour he put his hand past the third link, about a hand's width further along it than he has ever gone in eleven years, and he did it without stopping and without looking down, and then he brought it back to the third link and stayed there.

It was a small thing, and it was the only thing he did in that hour that was not the chain, and he is not going to be asked about it by anybody, ever, in any month after this one.

Neither of them said one word about either of those two things. Nobody in that shed has ever thanked him for a thing he did at that bench and nobody is starting this month. A class cannot put a hand back where it has been, and neither can anybody else, and this empire has not got a column, or a roll, or a heading, or a form, that would take a man at the front of a bench for the width of one hand past one link.

So the two of them had both done a thing this month that cannot be entered anywhere, and neither of them has been told what the other one did, and neither of them is going to be asked about either.

---

The bundle of that shed's own ordinary work had been worked off the far end of that bench and cleared away in the ordinary way, and what was at that far end now was the two of seventeen and nothing else.

Neither of them was spoken to, thanked or sent for, and the girl of seventeen was not given the chance to say no about anything, and the one with the book carried on with what he reads and did not look up at any part of that hour.

At about the seventh hour the bracket came on and put light into that bay from the wrong end, the way it does when somebody is standing in it, and the man at the front bench got up off that bench in the ordinary way.

The sentence she began in that doorway about two years ago did not arrive this morning and is not arriving this month, and nobody in that lane has ever asked her for the rest of it, and there is nothing in this empire, on paper or off it, that takes a person as the one who has been waiting two years for the end of a sentence.

She had put her hand on the front of that bench about nine mornings now, and it was going to keep being put there about nine mornings a month, and there was nowhere in this empire to put any of it and nobody in that lane who was going to be told.
