# Chapter 0526: A Number Cut Into The Back Of A Key And Going Down Under A Thumb, A Woman Who Came Down A Lane And Said Nothing At All, And A Tin Behind A Boiler Whose Lid Does Not Shut

That ground floor is not set out again here. What is in it this month is a boiler at the back, a tin standing behind that boiler, a stair going up out of the room, and a board by the door with the keys to those three rooms on it.

She has stood at the foot of a stranger's stair for about twenty years and has not gone past the second of those stairs once in that time. A rent comes once a week out of a hand into her own and has never in twenty years been on a piece of paper in that house.

About nine years of one man's rent is in that tin and the lid has not sat down properly since a spring that is gone, and she has never counted it and nothing on earth would make her start.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the ninth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the first hour.

---

There was a coin on the boards that morning and it had come through the gap where that door does not quite meet its frame, and it has come through that gap every week for about two years, and the door stayed as it was and the hand that pushed it went back up the stair.

The gap is at the bottom of the door on the lane side and it is no thicker than a finger, and the boards under it have been swept for twenty years and the sweep does not lift a coin. A coin under a door arrives on a floor and then goes where a person decides it goes, and it took her about a week to start putting it somewhere other than where it landed.

He has never given her his name and she has never given him hers. She has his name in her own mouth and has had it there once, to the plaster behind that boiler, and she is not going to say it again, because the second time a name goes out of a mouth in a passage it is the first time it has become a thing a person does.

Nobody in that house knows that she has it and nobody in that house is going to be told, and he is at the top of that stair at this hour and does not know any of it.

---

She put the coin into the pocket of her apron and not into the tin, and it has been the pocket rather than the tin since about the first hour, and she did that on purpose, and it is the second week she has done it with a coin in her hand.

Nine years in one gap behind a boiler has made the tin lean, and a thing which leans does not stand level, and a floor swept every working morning for twenty years has a swept part and an unswept part with an edge between them where the tin is not. The lid has not sat down properly for a long while. It comes up crooked in a hand and goes down crooked, and nobody in the house has ever looked at what is under it.

Then she went to the board by the door and took down the key to the top room and held it with her thumb over the cut on the back of it, which is the only record of any of those three rooms there has ever been, and which has been cut in by whichever of her hands had the file on the night.

It is going. She had known it was going for about two years and she had not let herself look at it since, and she looked at it now for about as long as it takes a sheet to be squared.

The cut is shallower at the top of it than at the bottom, and the bottom of it is the part a key wears against, and there is no way in this empire to cut a second number into the same place without the two of them becoming one mark, and one mark with two numbers on it is a thing that has to be looked at by somebody. There is nowhere to enter a fresh one. There is no paper, no column, no heading, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for a second mark on the back of a key, and she is not going to rule one.

A key is turned and not pushed, and it does nearly everything it does at the shoulder of it, which is why a cut in the back of one goes thin at the top before it goes thin at the bottom. About two years of twice a week has taken most of what is left up there, and the man turning the lock does not know he is doing it, and there is nobody in the world who could be shown a figure for how much longer there is.

---

Down that lane, at that hour, with the book under her arm, and she stopped where she stops, and she has stopped there for about a year.

She has a girl in the back room of her house into her second season and four rooms turn over by the week, and there are four headings over four columns in that book and there has never been a fifth column ruled in nine years, and she is not ruling one this month and did not rule one in the month before.

She did not say good evening. She said nothing at all, which is a thing she has come down that lane to do, and which is the only reason she is standing on that step at all, and neither of the two of them has ever once put a word on why.

The woman at the bottom of the stair said a true thing out loud to that boiler at about the first hour, in the ordinary way, in a room where nobody has asked anybody anything for twenty years.

"**There is a number cut into the back of the key to the top room and it is the only thing in this house that says anybody has ever slept in any of these three rooms, and it is nearly gone, and I am the only person in this empire who will ever know what was on it. When it is finished there will be nothing to show, and the not being able to show it is what I have been living inside of for twenty years, and I have just found out that it does not take a person to lose that. It takes a key, being turned, twice a week, by a man who does not know it is there.**"

The boiler ticked. The tin behind it did what it does. The door was not quite shut and the woman on the step did not move while it was being said, and she is not going to say whether she heard it, and she is not going to be asked, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that takes a woman standing on a step, and there is none that takes her standing anywhere else either.

---

She did not open the door and she did not go up the stair.

There are four rooms off the stair and she has spent twenty years at the foot of it. A person who stands at a foot for twenty years knows how many steps are in it without ever having counted them, and she has never once gone past the second. Three of those four are on the board by the door at the bottom of it and the fourth is on nothing, and she has never once asked why a room in this house is on nothing.

"You came down here," the woman on the step said, "and I have not come down here to be told anything in all the time I have been coming down it, and I have never once asked you what you were going to do when you got to it."

"I did not say it for you."

"I know you did not. That is what I have come down here for."

The woman with the day-book shifted the book under her arm and looked at the lane and not at the door.

"**A book is a record and a key is a record, and I have got four columns and no fifth and you have got a cut in the back of a key and no second mark, and neither of us is going to be asked about either. You are going to lose the one on the key and I am not going to rule the one under the fifth, and I have worked out this month that those two things are the same thing and I am not going to say it twice.**"

Then she went up the lane and did not stop again.

---

The coin came out of the apron pocket and went into the tin about as long as it takes a person to cross a lane, and the lid came up crooked in her hand and went down crooked, and nobody came to look at it.

The key went back on the board by the door and the woman went up two steps and stopped there, because she has never once been above the second stair in twenty years and is not going to start this month, and she did not go up any further to find out whether the man at the top of them was in.

She did not go up because of the stair. She did not go up because the cut on the back of that key is the only thing in that house which says he has ever slept in it, and it is going.

She is the only person in this empire who will ever know what was on it, and there is nowhere to put what she knows and no form anywhere for what she has lost. She is not going to be thanked for either, and she is not going to be asked about the coin that has been in her apron for a fortnight instead of in the tin.
