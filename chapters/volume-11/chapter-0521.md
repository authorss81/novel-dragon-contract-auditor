# Chapter 0521: A Form By A Door, A Woman Who Went To The Back Of The Room Instead Of The Doorway, And A Year Of A Man's Sentences Going Into A Class

The man at the front bench had the chain out along the stone in front of him and was feeling his way along it with the pad of his thumb, and he was at the third link, and he had been at the third link for about as long as it takes a sheet to be squared.

Halla Wray is fifty-one and she is a foreman and she has been the occupant of a class for eleven years, and the paper behind that class is on a board by the door and has not been filled in by any hand at any time since before she came to that bench, and the only work it has left is the work of going on being there.

She has had a sentence half finished for about two years. She has taken it to that doorway six times since the spring and it has never got past the same two words, and there is a second half of it that has not arrived and is not going to be arrived for by anybody in that lane.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the eighth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour.

---

At the back of that bay there are two of seventeen who have been at the back of it all week on the terms they are on, and one of them has not been asked a thing in about a year and is not going to be asked a thing in the eighth month of that year either, and nobody in that shed has ever offered her the chance to turn anything down, and the other of them reads.

Nobody in that shed has thanked either of them for anything, and nobody is going to send either of them for anything, and that has never once been described to either of the two of them as anything at all, because nobody in that lane has ever said one word to them about it.

Halla Wray had been to that doorway at about the fifth hour on six mornings since the spring and had stopped at the same two words every time, and this morning she went towards it at about the fourth hour instead, a full hour early, which is the first time she has gone at a different hour since she started going.

---

"You are early," the man at the front bench said. He did not turn round and he did not stop his thumb.

"I have never once been late at that door. You have not been watching which hour I go at."

"I have been at this bench eleven years. I do not have to watch anything to know the shape of a day."

That is all he said and he went on along the chain, and the chain went a small way under his hand and came back, and that is a thing a chain does and it is not a thing anybody in that shed has ever put a meaning on.

---

She got as far as the frame.

The lane was out there and it was about the fourth hour of an ordinary morning and it was doing what a lane does, and she stood with the shed behind her and her hand on the frame, and about four hundred yards up the lane off which this shed stands there is a thing that nobody in that shed walks to and nobody in that shed is going to walk to this month.

The sentence came up in her and got as far as it always gets and then stopped where it always stops, and she stood in the frame for about as long as a person stands in a frame waiting for the end of something.

The chain behind her went on being felt along.

"There is no use in that," the man at the front bench said, to the stone. "I have been at this bench eleven years and the sentence has not got a word longer in two years, and I am not going to be the one who finishes it, and I have not asked you for it, and I am not going to."

"You have never asked me for one thing in eleven years."

"I am not going to start at the two hundredth year of my own age."

---

She came back in off the frame and shut nothing, because there was nothing to shut, and she stood in the middle of that floor and worked the rest of it out standing up, in about as long as it takes to cross a bay and come back.

Here is what she had not seen. She had spent two years believing that the doorway was the problem, on the reasonable ground that a person who goes out of a room and stands in a lane has taken herself out of it, and a bay is nine foot by eleven and a lane is as long as it is.

A class is not a room. She has known that for eleven years the way a person knows the height of a stair they go up four times a day.

A person who is in a class can be put back into it without anybody lifting a pen. Nothing has to happen. There is no form and there is no form coming and there is nobody waiting to fill one in, and the paper on that board by the door has been blank since before she came and it is going to be blank after, and it has done the whole of that work with its knees drawn up under it and nobody had to do anything at all.

And a second ear is a person standing in a room. There is a man at the front bench of this one and he has said four things in it in about a year and nobody answered one of them and he never wanted an answer, and he is a man nobody in this empire has anything to enter him under, and he has not been entered.

She said that last part out loud, to the end wall, at about half past the fourth hour, in the ordinary way a person says a thing out loud in a bay where nothing is being asked of anybody.

"**A class is the only thing in this empire that can put a person back where they have been without anybody doing anything, and it has been putting me back in this bay for eleven years, and it has never once been asked to do it. What it has not been asked to do is put him back, and it has done that too, and about a year of what he has said in here has gone into the class and not into the air. I am not going to be thanked for working that out and nobody in this shed is going to be told.**"

The man at the front bench went on along the chain with his thumb and did not look up and said nothing, and he is not going to say whether he heard it, and neither of them is going to mention it again this week or after.

---

The chain came off the stone about the sixth hour and went back across the front of the bench, and he left it there, and she went to the board by the door and looked at the form on it for about as long as she has been looking at it, which is every working morning for eleven years.

Then she did a thing she had not done in that shed since before the girl came to the back of it.

She crossed the floor and went to the back, and she stood at the end of that bench behind the two of seventeen and she put her hand flat on the wood about a foot from the one with the book. She did not say one word to either of them and she did not look at either of them.

There is no form anywhere in this empire that takes a woman standing in a bay with a hand on a bench, and there is no form that takes her standing anywhere else either.

She is the cheapest person in that shed. She has been it for eleven years and it is not a thing anybody did to her and it is not going to be put right this month, and the reason she is it is written on a piece of paper by that door that nobody has filled in since before she came.

She was at the back of that bay from about the sixth hour to about the seventh and neither of the two of seventeen was spoken to and neither of them was thanked and the girl was not given the chance to say no about anything, and she did not say it.

---

About the seventh hour the bracket came on by itself and put light into the far end of the bay, and the man at the front bench got up off that bench in the ordinary way, and nobody said one word to anybody about the class and nobody said one word about the doorway, and the chalk mark on the end wall is still at the height of the third link and is still only a mark in chalk.

The form is on the board by the door and it is not filled in and it is not going to be, and the sentence she began in that frame has not arrived, and she is not going to be thanked for standing at the back of her own bay and she is not going to be asked what it was for, and there is nowhere in that lane standing at which either of those could be put.
