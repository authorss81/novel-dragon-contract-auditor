# Chapter 0546: A Tin That Was Not Behind A Boiler, A Man Of Thirty-Eight Who Came Down Four Flights On Purpose And Is Not Going To Do It Again, And A Woman Who Says She Is Not Going To Be Thanked For It

The tin had been behind that boiler for about a month and it was not behind that boiler any more, and there are two people in that house and both of them knew where it had been and only one of them had carried it.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the second month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the first hour.

A tin that has stood in one place leaves the shape of itself behind it for a while. Behind the boiler there was a gap in the shape of a round tin that had not been there a month ago, and the side of the boiler that had been against it was the side nobody had looked at.

---

*He came down four flights on purpose.*

That is the whole of what that morning was and it took him about as long to work out as it takes a coin to stop rolling. He had come down once in about two years before this and had told himself at the top of that stair that he was not going to do it again, and he had not said why. There is no form anywhere in this empire that would take a man for the number of times he has gone down his own stairs, and a decision is the one thing in this matter that anybody can be given warning of in advance.

He came down on purpose and he knew he was doing it while he was doing it, and that was new, and he had about two years of not knowing he could.

Four flights of stone take a man longer than a man expects on a day he has decided to come down them. He came down them at the rate he would have called a walk and got to the bottom of them having spoken to nobody and been spoken to by nobody, and every bit of the difference between this morning and the one about two years earlier was inside his own head and got him nowhere anybody else could come.

The tin was on the top step of that stair, about two feet up, at the turn, with the lid crooked on the top of it.

The top step at the turn is the first place on the stair where two people can stand level with each other. She had put the tin down there and left it, and had gone back down the two steps she had come up and not one step further, and there is a rail on the landing and her hand had not been on it.

It weighs what nine years of a rent in coin weighs when it has not been counted in six years, and there is nobody in that house who could have watched her do it, and the boiler in the back of that room is still warm at the side a tin had been standing against, and there is no paper anywhere in that building on which it says who carried what up two steps and round a turn.

"The tin has moved," he said.

"It has."

"I did not come down here about the tin. I came down here because I am not going to be able to stop coming down here about a month later, and I have known that since the last time, and I have come down anyway, and I would rather you had that from me than have worked it out yourself."

"I have not worked out anything and I am not going to be thanked for the not working it out and I am not going to be thanked for the tin either, and you have not asked me where I put it."

---

He had worked out on the way down the four flights what he was going to say about it, and by the bottom of them it was a handful of sentences, and every one of them was about a tin and about a hiding and about how many people can come in off a lane. Not one was about her, and he had noticed it on the second flight and had gone on down.

"What you have done is carry nine years of the only record of that house in this empire up two steps and round a turn to a place where anybody coming in off that lane can see it," he said, "and I have come down four flights to tell you that you have made it a smaller hiding than it was, and I am not going to be thanked for the coming down."

"I have made it a different one."

"That is the same thing said to keep a thing out of a room that has nothing in it."

"You are not going to be thanked for that either and I am not going to be asked what I think of it. I have not asked you to come down that stair. I have been at the bottom of it every working morning at about this hour for twenty years and I was here before you were a lodger and I will be here after, and you have come down four flights to tell me I have made a smaller hiding than I had, and there is no form in this empire for a woman who has been at the bottom of a stair for twenty years."

He stood on the turn with his hand on the rail.

The rail is cold the whole year and has a polished place on it about a hand long where a hand goes, and his went onto the polished place before he had decided anything about his own hands, and it stayed there. The tin was on a step at his feet with the lid crooked and neither of them could see the other's face from where the two of them were standing.

"I know there is not. That is why I am standing here and not asking."

"You are not asking."

"I am not asking and I am not going to and I have not come down here to be thanked for either, and I am not going to be here next week."

---

"There is a key to the top room of this house somewhere in this building," she said, to the stair. "It has not been on that board for about eight weeks and it is not in that tin any more. I have not looked for it and I am not going to, and there is a mark cut into the back of it that is the only thing in this house that has ever said anybody slept up there, and that mark is going thin at the top of it."

He did not go up the stair at once.

The coin was still where he had put it when he turned round, and about four foot of the board by the door was still bare with an empty nail in the middle of it, and the tin on the step at the turn had not moved a hand's width in the whole of the talking. Three things in one ground floor and not one of them a record, and every one of them put there by a person who is not going to be asked about any part of it.

"Who has it."

"That is not a thing I am going to put to you and you did not ask me and I would rather it had stayed that way."

"Then somebody in this house has it, and it is not me."

"You have not asked me that either."

"I have not."

---

He was back at the top of that stair inside about half an hour, and he did not stop on any of the flights.

The coin went under the door the way a coin goes under that door every week for about two years, and the stair went up behind him, and the tin went on standing on the top step at the turn with the lid crooked on the top of it, and about four foot of the board by that door went on being bare with an empty nail in the middle of it.

There was nothing between the bottom of the stair and the top of it that either of them could have said a word into. About halfway up there is a window a man could look out of without lifting his chin, and the daylight comes in flat off the yard and lies across the treads and up one wall, and anybody going down past it is out of sight of the top of the house and cannot be helped, because a boot on stone in the middle of a stair carries up all four flights.

There are now two bare things in that house and they are the only two. One of them is a nail in a board in a hall where a coin comes under a door once a week and goes into a tin. The other is a man at the top of that stair who came down them on purpose and has said out loud that there is not going to be a third time, and a tin that has been carried up two steps and round a turn by a woman who has never once been above the second of those stairs and is not going to start now.
