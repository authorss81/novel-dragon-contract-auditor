# Chapter 0545: A Man At A Sill Reaching Into A Pot With The Other Hand For About Two Months And Not Being Able To Say Why, A Woman Of Fifty-Three Who Came Out Of That Room For The Fourth Time In About A Year, And One Line In A Book Nobody Is Going To Be Told About

He has been at that sill nineteen years and he went from being forty-two to being sixty-one at it without anybody on that floor finding out a single thing about it while it happened, and there is not a working sentence in a paragraph in him and has not been one since before a fire other people walked out of, and the whole of the care he has ever added to anything is taking care that nobody finds that out.

Four people in this matter cannot get sense out of a paragraph and he is not one of them and has said so on that floor in terms. Neither is the man with the chain at the end of a lane off the Slade, and neither is the woman of fifty-three who keeps the minutes at the back of that floor, and the girl of nineteen under her is not one of them and is not going to be.

There are about nine hundred of those sheets a year and his mark is at the foot of every one of them, and about nine in a year are not the work of the person whose sheet it is, and one man alive knows which nine and has never told anybody and is not going to be asked.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the second month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the second hour and about the fifth.

---

The pot has two pens in it and they are the same pen and it has never once had an opinion about which of them went in or which came out.

*For about two months he has been reaching into it with his left hand.*

He noticed that the way a man notices a thing about a hand. There is nothing wrong with either of them and nothing has ever been wrong with either of them, and there is not one person on that floor who could tell you which of the two he used on a given morning, and that has been the arrangement of that pot for nineteen years, and about two months ago his arm started going the other way and he let it.

He has not worked out a single morning out of it. He went back to the left hand once and has been going back ever since, and the pot takes it out of him and hands it straight back the same way it takes it out of the other one, and the girl of nineteen has carried an armful past that sill about forty times in each direction in every one of those weeks and has not said one word about either hand and is not going to.

Nobody on that floor has asked him which hand he signs with, and nobody on that floor is going to, and there is no form anywhere in this empire in which a man at a sill is entered as the one who began doing a thing differently on a particular morning, and a heading over that would be a heading over about nine hundred rooms.

---

The door of that room opened at about the third hour and it was the fourth time anybody in that building had seen it open in about a year.

She came out into that corridor and came the length of it and stood at the near end of it, and he did not look up and she did not look across.

"You have come out of that room four times in about a year," he said, to the flags, "and every one of them has cost you something, and there is nothing anywhere that any of them can be entered as, and you are the only person on this floor who could do anything about that and you are not going to, and I am not going to either, and I have not asked you for anything and I am not going to."

"I came out for the window and not for you, and I said that before you said anything the first two times and I am saying it now because it is still true."

"I have never said you came out for me. I have said what I have watched. Those are two different things and you have known that for nineteen years and so have I."

"Then you know I am not going back in yet."

He signed four sheets in the time it took her to turn round and go back down that corridor, and then he put the pen back in a tin he made himself out of a tin, and the door of that room shut.

---

She wrote one line in those minutes at about the fourth hour.

It is nineteen years of minutes and nothing advised in them by anybody but her, and nothing has been put to that room in about four years, and there has not been a line in that book put there on purpose by anybody in about four years.

What is on the line is not printed out anywhere in this empire. It is not his name and it is not the girl's and it is not about a number and it is about nothing that was said out loud in that room in about a year, and it is not a question and it carries no full stop, and the woman who wrote it is fifty-three and has been the only person in that book for nineteen years.

The girl of nineteen carried the armful past that sill about forty times in each direction that day and was in that corridor and in that room, and was not asked one question about a number, and said nothing back. She did not see the line go in and she is not going to be told about it, and she has not been asked about anything and has not wanted to be.

Nobody on that floor is going to be told that a person put a thing in a book on purpose. The pot went on taking the pen back out of him the same way it has for nineteen years and there is not one person alive who could tell you which hand of that man went into it this morning, and nobody on that floor is going to be told one word about the other thing that happened in that building that day either.

Her own question is on a shelf under a window with no pane in it, and has been there about eleven years with her name at the foot of it put there deliberately. It is never going to be written out in any building in this empire, and she is not going to be asked about it a second time.
