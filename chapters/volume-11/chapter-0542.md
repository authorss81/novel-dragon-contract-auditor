# Chapter 0542: Nine Foot Of Stone At The Near End Of A Run Left Unswept For Eight Weeks By A Woman Who Had Swept Everything For Four Years, And A Man Who Swept It Twice And Then Did Not

There was a strip of stone at the near end of that passage about nine foot long that she had not swept since a month that was gone, and it lay in the middle of a floor she swept every working morning of her four years at that end of the run.

It is not a wide strip. It is the width of a man standing still, and it goes from the wall to about a foot and a half out from it, and it is the only piece of that floor in four years that has had dust standing on it, and on the morning of the fourth week of the first month she noticed that somebody had been at it with a broom.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the first month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour.

---

He had come down at about the fourth hour and had stopped at the last nine foot of it, and he had not said one word, and he had looked at the floor for about as long as a man looks at a floor.

Then he had gone up.

The broom was against the wall by the housing. There is one broom at that end of that run and it is hers and it is kept there because it is always needed, and it had been at the wall with its handle leaning the other way from the way she stands it.

She could tell what the stone wanted without looking at it. The dust on it had gone the same colour as the dust on the rest of it, and there is no colour in a floor at the end of a passage, so the only way to know is to put a broom on it. Eight weeks of weather sits into flags and a broom finds it in about three strokes. Four years of knowing what a floor wanted had never before been in question.

She put it back the way she stands it and she swept the rest of the floor, and the nine foot in the middle stayed as it was.

She swept outward from the wall, which is the way she has swept it since the first month of her four years, because the grit comes off the flags in a line and a broom started at the far end only pushes it into a ridge and then has to be taken back up the same length. She was better at it than anybody alive and there was nobody in the building who could have known it, and she was also the only person in it who knew how any of it was done, and that has been the arrangement for four years.

---

She was back on that strip the next working morning before the light was on it, and it had been swept again.

She stood and looked at it for a while, and there was no mark anywhere on that stone saying who had done it, and she went and swept everything else, and the nine foot in the middle stayed as it was.

A broom does not sign. She had worked it out somewhere in those four years and had never once wanted it to be otherwise, and there had never before been any reason to look at it.

The third morning it was as she had left it, and it was as she had left it on the fourth, and on the fifth she took her broom and stood at the edge of it and then turned and went up the near end of the passage instead.

"You have swept that twice."

He was at the last nine foot with the rack riding on his hip and had not been there when she came round, and there was no arrangement of that morning that had put him there in advance and there never had been.

"I have."

"It has been a month since I stopped."

"It has."

The word came out of her flatter than she had meant it to and faster than she had meant it to, and she heard it go nine foot along the stone and arrive at nothing, and she did not take it back, and there was no second half of it to take back, because there has never been a second half of anything in the passage.

"Stop sweeping it."

"No."

---

She did not expect that and it took her about as long as a sheet takes to be signed to get past it.

Four years is a long time to be the person who does the floors. She had worked out the grit and the ridge and the weather and the hour of the day when the housing wants looking at, and about how long a broom takes to go down and come back up a length of flags, and the width of her own two hands, and it had taken her all of that to find out that not one of them ever needed anybody else, and about four minutes to find out that a man sweeping nine foot of stone does.

"You have swept a floor for a man and you are going to tell me you are not going to do it twice a week for a fortnight."

"I have swept that floor twice because there was nine foot of stone in the middle of it with eight weeks of weather on it and I am forty years old and I do not like walking on that. I have not swept it for you and I am not going to, and nobody is going to thank me for the two or ask me about them, and you have not asked me and I am not going to stand in this passage and be the man who works out what a patch of floor is."

"That is the fourth time in a month you have told me what you are not going to do."

"I have been coming down this stone four times a day for forty years and there is not one thing I have wanted out of the people at this end of it and I am not going to start at my age."

---

She went and got her broom.

"You think that is mine. That is not mine. *A woman who is the cheapest thing in a building she is four years in does not get nine foot of her own floor put down as a thing she did*, because a mark in a floor is a thing anybody can be shown, and being shown is the only thing in this empire I have been able to keep out of."

"You have just said all of that to a man about nine foot off your hands."

She had the broom by the middle of its handle and was holding it in front of her at an angle, the way a person holds a thing when the argument has got further along than the hands expected. The bristles were a hand's breadth off the flags and she was not sweeping with it and was not going to start.

"I have said it to a man about nine foot off my hands and I have said it out loud in an empty passage, which is the arrangement of this end of this run and has been for four years."

"Then say the other half of it. Tell me you did not leave it for me."

The broom was in her hand and the strip of stone was between them and he was not going to sweep it again, and she had said more out loud in one morning than she has said in four years and not one word of it had gone anywhere.

"I am not going to tell you that, and you are not going to ask me, and I am not going to be thanked for the not telling you, and there is nowhere in this empire that either of us could put the fact that I did not."

"I have not said I wanted it put."

"You said something else and we both know what it was."

---

The nine foot of it was still there at the end of the month, and at the end of the month after that, and it is the width of a man standing still and it is the only piece of that floor in four years that has had weather standing on it.

A mark in a floor is a thing that can be looked at. It is not a thing that can be read, because there is nothing written in it and nobody in this empire is going to write anything in it, and there is no form anywhere that a woman at a lock could be put in for having left nine foot of her own work undone in the place where a man stands. It is not a form. It is not a refusal. It is a patch of stone with dust on it, and the dust is going to be there in the winter.

Nothing about it was going to be cleaned and nothing about it was going to be explained. Nine foot of flags at the near end of a run nobody walks, with dust standing on them in the cold months, and the dust would be there through the whole of the winter and be there in the spring and be there the next time a man came down the run with nothing on a rack, and there was not one hour in which any part of that could be put anywhere.

And there is no standing at which either of them can be told about it, and neither of them is going to be told, and he is not going to be thanked for the two times he swept it and she is not going to be thanked for the thirty that she did not.

Dust standing up on stone does not look like much in a working passage until a boot is put down in the middle of it, and then it does. Neither of them was going to say one word about any part of that.

On the last morning of that month he stood on the nine foot of it for the whole of the hour without moving his feet, which is the first time a man has stood still in that passage in about four years. The dust stayed on the stone under him the whole time, and her broom was against the wall by the housing with its handle the other way round from the way she stands it, and neither of the two of them said a word about any of it, and they are not going to say one to each other in this empire.
