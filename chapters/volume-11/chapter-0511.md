# Chapter 0511: Twenty Minutes Before The Hour, An Edge Squared Twice, And A Clerk At The Far End Of Two Joined Tables Who Does Not Look Up

The counter at the top of four flights opens between the second hour and the sixth and there is no other way up to it, and about four hundred people a year come down that stair carrying a different object altogether, and not one of them has ever been sent.

Marn Ottery is thirty-four. She has been at the other end of those boards for eleven years and has not once in that time gone out of that room, and nobody else on that floor could say the same of themselves. She has put the shape of a thing out loud four times and told them in advance that a fifth was not coming.

Tamsin Rook is twenty-four and is two years at her end of those boards, and she has been in the room twenty minutes ahead of the hour every working morning since she was twenty-two. She was in early this morning for the reason she was in early yesterday and she has not got a better one and she is not looking for one.

The clerk of about twenty-nine is at the far end of the two joined tables. Four years. One true thing said out loud in the whole of them and nobody has taken it up since and nobody is going to.

It is the second day of the first week of the sixth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the third hour.

---

The boards were up and the case was out and there was nothing on either of them, and that is what that floor looks like on every working morning of the eleven years it has been like it.

Tamsin squared the near edge. It did not need squaring. She squared it again about a minute later and the woman at the other end of them watched her do it and said nothing for about as long as a sheet takes to be signed.

"You are going to do that until the sixth hour," Marn Ottery said.

"I am going to do it until the fifth hour and then I am going to stop for a minute and start again."

"That is not an answer and I did not put one to you and I am not going to."

"You put something to me on the fourth day of the fourth week of the last month and it was not a question and I have been carrying it for a month the way you carry a square edge."

"I put a fact to you. You are the only person on this floor it was true of."

"You put a fact to me about what arriving early means, and you told me not to arrive early for a reason, and I have not, and I am not going to, and there is nothing in the twenty minutes that would let anybody find out which of the two it is."

---

Marn put both hands flat on the boards and left them there.

"**I am not going to help you with that and I am not going to stop you either, and you are not going to be thanked for it, and nobody is going to ask you at the end of the month what the twenty minutes were for. That is not me being careful. That is there being nowhere for me to put an answer if I gave you one.**"

"You said it as though it were a warning."

"I said it as a fact, in the morning, at my end of these boards, and you are the one who decided there was a warning in it, and I have not corrected you and I am not going to."

---

There was a blank at the near end of the boards by then, squared against the wood with the strip along the top of it showing, and the four things set along that strip were the four things they have always been and not one of them is anybody's name.

Tamsin squared it. About four hundred of them go out of that case in a year and she has squared every one of them for two years, and not one person on that floor has ever wanted one single thing out of her for any of it. There is a box under the far end of those boards that holds about four hundred of them in a quantity, bought and never filled in, and the lid has not been off it in six years, and the box is not the case, and she has not put her hand on that lid once.

"You have got your hand on that edge again," Marn Ottery said.

"It is not straight."

"It is as straight as the other one. You squared the other one at the second hour and it has not moved and neither has this."

"Then I will square it and then neither of them will have moved, and that is the whole of what I have done this morning."

---

A bit after the hour Tamsin Rook said the true thing out loud, to the boards, in the ordinary way a person says a thing out loud in a room where nothing is being asked of anybody.

"**I have not decided anything this month and I am not going to decide anything in any of the next four, and I wanted it said out loud in this room once so that nobody on this floor can stand up in a year and tell me I had. That is the whole of it. I am not going to say which morning, and I am not going to thank the two of you for not asking, and if either of you ever wants to be thanked you are going to have to get it somewhere else.**"

She went on squaring the edge, which by then was as square as it was going to get.

Nobody answered her. That is the ordinary condition of that room and it is not a discourtesy between anybody in it and it has not been since before she came to it.

---

The clerk of about twenty-nine kept her face down over those two tables for the whole of it.

That is the whole of what happened on that floor that morning, as far as anybody in the building can put it. Nothing was asked of anybody, the two of them at the boards did not look at her while it was being said, and she did not look up while it was being said, and there is no way in this empire to tell the difference between a person who heard a thing and a person who did not.

What has changed is not something anybody in that room did. It is that there is no longer any distance between that clerk and that sentence. **A person who was in a room when a true thing was said cannot afterwards be shown to have been nowhere near it, and that has always been the only protection anybody in this matter has ever had, and it does not take a decision to lose it, it takes standing still, and standing still is the one thing everybody on that floor has been doing for eleven years.**

There is no form anywhere in this empire in which she can be entered as the one who stood at the far end of those tables that morning. There is none in which she can be entered as the one who did not stand there. Neither of the two can be produced and neither of the two was done to her on purpose and nobody is going to be thanked for it and nobody is going to be told.

---

The two of them at the boards got on with the fourth hour and the fifth, and the case went in behind the boards at the sixth, and the four went up onto the wall above it on their own nails, and the fourth of them is the one nobody on that floor can account for.

The clerk of about twenty-nine put her hand flat on the open page in front of her and left it there for about as long as a sheet takes to be signed, and then she turned the page and carried on, and the pen went back where it goes every working morning of the four years she has been at that end of the room.
