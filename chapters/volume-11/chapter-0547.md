# Chapter 0547: A Woman At A Lock Putting Her Back To Four Hundred Yards Of Cold Flags Six Weeks After She Said One Word Out Loud To A Bar, And A Man Who Is Not Going To Stop Coming Down That Run

Her two hands were on the passage side of that bar for six weeks and this morning they were on the far side of it with her back to the cold, and that is the second time in about a year that she has moved round it, and both of the times were on purpose and neither of them was for him.

She is about twenty-six, and the four years she has stood at that end of that run are the whole of what she is to anybody in that building, and there is a tin of oil standing on the flags about nine foot off the bar that has been there for three weeks. There are about nine foot of stone in the middle of that floor that has not been swept since two months that are gone, and neither of those two things is going to be mentioned by anybody.

It is the second day of the first week of the third month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour.

Both sides of a bar are cold and only one of them is cold in a way a person gets used to, and she had four years of the difference between them in her hands, and this morning she put both of them on the other one. She got there before she had a sentence for it, which is the usual order, and she did not go looking for a better one afterwards.

---

He was at the last nine foot of that run with the empty rack against his leg before the light had finished coming down the flags, and this time there was nothing standing in the light to stop for.

He stood in the middle of nine foot of lit flags with his back to four hundred yards of dark behind him, which is not a thing a man does at that hour unless there is a reason nobody is going to be told about.

"You have gone round the back of that bar again."

"I have."

"You were on the other side of it for six weeks and you did not say one word to me about any part of those six weeks, and I did not say one word to you, and I have not asked you what they were and I am not going to."

"I have not asked you for anything."

"You have never asked me for anything in four years and you told me in a month that is gone that you were not going to start, and I have believed you every working morning of it."

"Then we are where we were."

---

She had her back to him and her hands on the cold side of the bar and she could not see the light coming down that run, and that was the whole of what she had come back for.

From the far side of a bar at the fourth hour of a morning a person cannot see up the run at all. She could see about nine foot of flags behind her in her own shadow and four hundred of them going away from her into the half of the run the light has never reached, and she could hear a boot on stone a long way off and had no way of telling how far, and that is the whole of what a person gets who has put themselves where a second hearer cannot be.

A man coming down that run in good light gets to about two hundred yards and sees a shape, and about a hundred and fifty and sees the back of a coat, and there is nothing in it to look at until he is inside eighty. He is stopped at about ninety every time, and he has been stopped at about ninety for six weeks, four times a day, and that is about a hundred and twenty crossings of a stone with a woman in them, and she is the only second person that run has had in four years, and she has been it on purpose since the third week of a month that is gone.

She has put an end to it this morning, and she did it because of *a word she said to a bar six weeks ago that he is not going to hear about, because he has not heard it and is not going to and is never going to be told.*

And if he had heard it, none of this would be happening, and he is not going to know that either, and the two of them are going to go on standing in the same passage for the rest of their working lives with the whole of this between them and neither of them able to put it anywhere.

What six weeks of standing on the cold side bought her was an answer she could not give without being made a party to the thing, and it had cost her the only protection she had, which was being a person that nothing can be put in front of. She gave it up on purpose inside a minute and did not stand there afterwards to find out what it weighed.

"I am not going to stand in this passage and work out what you have done," he said. "I did it once, in the light, and I told you what it was, and I said it where you could hear it, and I have not said one word about any part of it since and I am not going to start now. I have come down this stone four times a day for forty years and I am not going to begin asking a woman at a lock to account for where she has put her hands."

"I have not asked you to account for anything."

"I have not said you did, and I am not going to. I have said what I am not going to do with my morning, and you have known the difference between those two things for longer than either of us has been standing here."

---

"Then go up that run."

"I am going to."

He went up about nine foot of it and stopped, which he has never once done going up.

Going up the run is the part of his day with nothing in it. There is nothing to decide at the top of it and nothing to check at the foot of it, and a man who stops in the middle of it going up does it for a reason, and he had gone up and down it for forty years and had never once.

"The tin is still on those flags and the nine foot in the middle of that floor is still not swept and I have not said one word to you about either of them and I am not going to, and I have not moved either of them and neither of them is a thing I could be shown having done. If you want the tin gone it is gone in a morning. If you want the nine foot swept it is swept in a morning. You have not told me you want either, and nobody is going to ask you and nobody is going to be thanked for the not asking, and all of that has been said out loud on a stone today and none of it is going to be said again."

"I am not going to tell you what I want with either of them."

"I know. That is why I said it out loud."

And he took the rest of that run up and it was gone out of the passage, and the light lay along about half of those flags with nobody standing in any part of it.

---

She was the cheapest second hearer in this city for six weeks and she has put herself back where a second hearer cannot be, and she did it in about as long as that bar takes to go home, with a man nine foot off her who could not do one thing about it. She is not going to be thanked for the six weeks and she is not going to be asked what they were for. There is no column in this empire that a woman at a lock can be entered as the one who was in a passage, and there is none that can take her as the one who was not.

She stood with her back to four hundred yards and her hands on a bar, and the tin was on the flags behind her where a man could have picked it up in one hand, and neither of those two things is a thing she could be shown having done.

What she has carried in her head since the spring of her fourth year is on no paper, and it went up out of that building once without touching a sheet, and it is not going anywhere this month. One word out loud at a bar in a month that is gone has not moved it and is not going to be handed to anybody. The housing wants oil about four times a year, and it went home dry again that hour for the hundred and thirtieth time in a row.
