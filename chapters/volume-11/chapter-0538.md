# Chapter 0538: A Key Taken Down Off A Board By A Door And Put Into A Tin Behind A Boiler, A Woman Of About Thirty-Five Going Down That Lane Without Stopping On It, And A Lid That Came Down Crooked

The week before, a key hung on that board by the door where anybody coming in off the lane could see it. There is a cut in the back of it that has been going thin at the top of it for about two years, and nobody in that house has ever held it up to the light.

This week it is not on the board. About four feet of that board is bare and it is going to stay bare.

She has stood at the foot of a stranger's stair for about twenty years. In all that time she has never once gone above the second of those stairs, and nobody has ever asked her to and nobody ever is. Behind the boiler at the back of that room there is a tin, and about nine years of one man's rent is in it, and it has been sitting crooked on its rim since a spring that is gone. Nobody has ever counted what is in that tin, and nothing on this earth would make her start.

A rent comes once a week out of a hand into her own, and in twenty years it has gone into that house as coin and nothing else, and there will never be a receipt for any of them.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the twelfth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the first hour.

---

There was a coin on the boards that morning.

That door has never once met its frame and whatever comes under it has been coming under it for about two years now, once a week, and the hand that pushes it goes back up the stair without stopping anywhere. She picked the coin up off the boards at about the first hour, and she did not put it in the tin.

A tin that has stood in one place for nine years takes the shape of the gap behind the boiler, and it leans into it and does not sit level, and a person who sweeps the floor every working morning sweeps round it and has done for so long that the swept part of the floor has an edge in it.

She put it in the pocket of her apron, and that has been the pocket rather than the tin since about the first hour, and this was the third week she had done that with a coin in her hand.

Then she went to the board by the door and took the key to the top room down off it, and she held it with her thumb not on the cut, because she was not looking at the cut and has not looked at the cut since a month that is gone.

---

She stood there with the key in her hand for a while after that. It is a working key and it is not a good one and it turns a lock that has not been a good lock for about two years, and the number cut into the back of it is the only thing in that house that says anybody has ever slept up there.

The key is for a room at the top of a stair going up four flights of stone, and the room has a bed in it and a table and a stool and a chair that has stood against nothing at all for about two years. The whole of what the key is worth in the house is about two years of rent, and the only part of the two years anybody can be shown is the mark cut on the back of it.

Going up that stair would have taken her about a minute. She has known the sound of four rooms on it for twenty years and she has never once put her foot on the third stair, and the reason is not that anybody has ever stopped her, and nobody has ever asked her for a reason, and she has not given one.

That was the arrangement of the whole of that house for two years, and it was a good arrangement, and it worked. She is not a person anybody could show anything about, and that is worth as much to a woman who takes a rent once a week in coin as it is to anybody else in this matter, and there is a girl in the back room of her own house into her second season who has never once heard her mother's landlord mentioned.

---

She went down that lane at about that hour with the book under her arm. She has walked it about once a week for about a year and she did not stop.

The lane is about as long as four houses are deep and it does not go anywhere anybody has to go, and a woman walking it with a book under her arm is a woman on her way to a house she owns, and every door on the lane is a door she has a reason to be at and none of them is a door she has ever knocked on for nothing.

She has four rooms going by the week in her own house and a girl in the back room of it into her second season, and a book under her arm with four headings over four columns in it. A fifth column has not been ruled in that book in nine years, and she did not rule one last month and is not ruling one this month, and she has never said out loud in that lane that she wants to.

"There is a room standing empty in my house since before the spring," she said, walking, "and I have not let it and I am not going to, and I am not going to rule a fifth heading over a room I have not let. That is two things I have said out loud in that lane this morning and neither of them is a true thing, and you were not standing on your step for either of them."

"I was not," the woman at the bottom of the stair said.

"You were not and I came down anyway, and I am going to say one thing about that and then I am going to go up that lane and not stop on it again this month."

"I have a room on my stair that has been let by the week for about two years," the woman at the bottom of the stair said, "and the key to it has hung on that board by the door where anybody coming in can see it, and there is a mark cut into the back of it that nobody in this house can say what is on, because nobody in this house has ever held it up to the light. I have not asked the man who pays that rent one thing in two years and he has not asked me. **I am moving that key off that board and into that tin. A tin nobody has ever counted is the only place in this house where a thing cannot be looked at, and nobody will be told that I have done it, and nobody thanks me for it.**"

She said it to the boiler, and the boiler ticked, and there was a woman in that room who did not move while it was being said and is not going to say whether she heard it and is not going to be asked.

The room is about as wide as the boiler is deep and about as long as a table is wide, and there is a tin behind the boiler and a board by the door and a stair going up out of it, and a person standing at the near end with her back to the door is a person who can be shown standing in a kitchen with a woman in it, and she had put herself at the far end of it on purpose before she opened her mouth.

"You have never said a true thing out loud in this room in the whole of the time I have been coming down that lane," the woman with the day-book said.

"No."

"I did not say that as a question and I am not going to say it as anything else."

"Then I will take the key down."

"You have put it in the one place in this house that you have never counted," the woman with the day-book said, "and you have done that on purpose, and you have never once in twenty years gone above the second stair of it, and I have watched you not do that for a year now."

"Yes."

"That is four things you have said to me this morning and three of them were about a key and one of them was to a boiler, and I am going to say one back and then I am going to go up that lane. You are going to be the only person in this house who can say what is on the back of that key, and there is no way anybody is ever going to make you say it, and I do not think you have thought about how that is going to sit with you at about the first hour of a morning when you are the only one up."

"I have thought about it," the woman at the bottom of the stair said, "and I have been the only one up at about the first hour for twenty years."

---

The key went off that board and into the tin behind the boiler, on top of about nine years of one man's rent, and the lid came up crooked in her hand and went down crooked on the top of it.

A lid that does not sit flat takes a hand about a second to settle and it never settles square, and nine years of coin under it is heavier than a tin wants to be and has to be moved a hand's width before it will move at all, and she moved it, and the coin went on top of the key, and then the coin was under the key, and that was also fine.

The coin came out of the apron pocket and went in on top of that, and the lid came down crooked a second time that morning.

Nobody came to look at it. Nobody came to look at it in any of the four weeks after that either, and the tin went on sitting crooked on its rim behind the boiler, and the lid went on being a lid that does not sit flat, and the thing inside it is the one place in that house where a thing cannot be looked at, which is the whole reason it went in there.

About four feet of that board by the door was bare from that morning and it is bare now, and there is a nail in it with nothing on it, and anybody can come in off that lane and see the bare board as easily as they could see the key.

Nobody in that house is going to be able to show anybody that anybody has ever slept in that top room. The woman at the bottom of that stair is the only person who will ever know what was on the back of that key, and she will not be asked about it and she will not be thanked for having moved it, and the man at the top of that stair is at the top of it this hour and does not know any of it, and he has not come down since the last time that door failed to meet its frame.

The woman with the day-book went up that lane and did not stop on it again, and she has four headings and no fifth, and nobody on that lane is going to be told that she stopped coming.
