# Chapter 0538: A Key To The Top Room Taken Down Off A Board By A Door And Put Into A Tin Behind A Boiler, A Woman Of About Thirty-Five Going Down That Lane Without Stopping On It, And A Lid That Came Down Crooked

The week before, a key hung on that board by the door where anybody coming in off the lane could see it, and there is a cut in the back of it that has been going thin at the top of it for about two years, and nobody in that house has ever held it up to the light.

This week it is not on the board. About four feet of that board is bare and it is going to stay bare.

She has stood at the foot of a stranger's stair for about twenty years. In all that time she has never once gone above the second of those stairs, and nobody has ever asked her to and nobody ever is going to. Behind the boiler at the back of that room there is a tin, and about nine years of one man's rent is in it, and it has been sitting crooked on its rim since a spring that is gone. Nobody has ever counted what is in that tin and nothing on this earth would make her start.

A rent comes once a week out of a hand into her own, and in twenty years it has gone into that house as coin and nothing else, and there is not going to be a receipt for any of them and there never has been.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the twelfth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the first hour.

---

There was a coin on the boards that morning. That door has never once met its frame and whatever comes under it has been coming under it for about two years now, weekly, and the hand that pushed it goes back up the stair again without stopping anywhere.

She put it into the pocket of her apron and not into the tin, on purpose, and it has been the pocket rather than the tin since about the first hour, and that is the third week she has done that with a coin in her hand.

Then she went to the board by the door and took down the key to the top room and held it for about as long as a sheet takes to be squared, with her thumb not on the cut this time, because she was not looking at the cut and has not looked at the cut since the month that is gone.

---

She went down that lane at about that hour with the book under her arm, and she has walked it about once a week for about a year, and she did not stop.

She has four rooms going by the week in her own house and a girl in the back room of it into her second season, and a book under her arm with four headings standing over four columns in it.

A fifth column has not been ruled in that book in nine years and she is not ruling one this month and did not rule one last month, and she has never said out loud in this lane that she wants to, and she is not going to.

"There is a room standing empty in my house since before the spring," she said, walking, "and I have not let it and I am not going to, and I am not going to rule a fifth heading over a room I have not let, and that is two things I have said out loud in that lane this morning and neither of them is a true thing and you were not standing on your step for either of them."

"I was not," the woman at the bottom of the stair said.

"You were not and I came down anyway, and I am going to say one thing about that and then I am going to go up that lane and not stop on it again this month."

---

"I have a room on my stair that has been let by the week for about two years," the woman at the bottom of the stair said, "and the key to it hangs on that board by that door where anybody coming in can see it, and there is a mark cut into the back of it that nobody in this house can say what is on, because nobody in this house has ever held it up to the light. I have not asked the man who pays that rent one thing in two years. I have not been asked by him. I am not going to be. **I am going to move that key off that board and into that tin, and a tin nobody has ever counted is the only place in this house where a thing cannot be looked at, and nobody is going to be told that I have done it and I am not going to be thanked for it.**"

She said it to the boiler and the boiler ticked and there was a woman in the room who did not move while it was being said and is not going to say whether she heard it and is not going to be asked.

"You have never said a true thing out loud in this room in the whole of the time I have been coming down that lane," the woman with the day-book said.

"No."

"I did not say that as a question and I am not going to say it as anything else."

"Then I will take the key down," the woman at the bottom of the stair said.

---

The key went off that board and into the tin behind the boiler, on top of about nine years of one man's rent, and the lid came up crooked in her hand and went down crooked on the top of it, and nobody came to look at it, and nobody came to look at it in any of the four weeks after that.

Nobody in that house is going to be able to show anybody that anybody has ever slept in that top room.

The woman at the bottom of that stair is the only person who will ever know what was on the back of that key, and she is not going to be asked about it and she is not going to be thanked for having moved it, and the man at the top of that stair is at the top of it this hour and does not know any of it.

The woman with the day-book went up that lane and did not stop on it again, and she has four headings and no fifth, and the coin came out of the apron pocket about as long as it takes a person to cross a lane and went into the tin, and the lid came down crooked a second time that morning, and nobody came to look.
