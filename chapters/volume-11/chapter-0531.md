# Chapter 0531: A Foreman At The Front Of Her Own Bay For One Hour, A Man Who Would Not Say A Word With Her Standing There, And A Count One Short

The chain was out along the stone and his thumb was on it at the third link, and it had been at the third link since the bracket came on at about the fourth hour.

Halla Wray had been at the back of that bench for a month. She is fifty-one and she is a foreman, and for eleven years she has been the occupant of a class. The paper behind that class is nailed to a board by that door and no hand has gone near a line of it since before she came to that bench, and the only work left for it now is the work of going on being paper.

At the back of that bay, at the far end of the wood, the two of seventeen sat where they always sat, on a written engagement and on a silence. One of them had a book open and did not turn a page for the first hour of that morning.

The bundle she had put down at that far end a month ago was still on the wood, tied with the same length of cord. About four days of that shed's own ordinary work had been worked off the top of it since, and what was left was the size of a man's two hands put together. It was not a copy of anything, and it was never going to become one.

There are nine pairs of hands in that shed on an ordinary morning and one pair of eyes that is paid to go along them, and the eyes belong to the foreman. That is the whole of what a foreman is in a bay nine foot by eleven: the looking, done slowly, at the end of every hand's work, before the sheets go out to the lane and go down it one at a time.

It is the second day of the first week of the eleventh month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour.

---

She got up off that bench and came the length of her own floor.

She had started at the near end of it in a spring that is gone and she had gone to the far end of it every working morning of the eleven years since, and about a month ago she had stopped and gone to the back of the bench and stood at the end of it behind the two of them. This morning she crossed nine foot by eleven and stopped at the near end, at the front of the bay, about four feet off the chain, with her hands loose at her sides and her weight even on both feet.

The man at the front bench did not lift his thumb off the chain.

"You are in my way."

"I have never once been in your way in eleven years."

"You are in it now."

"Then say the thing you have been not saying, because I have come to the front of it to stand in it, and there is nowhere else in this building for me to stand."

The chain went a little way along the stone and came back. It made that sound whatever the hour was. She had listened to it for eleven years and she had never once been able to say she was used to it.

"I am not going to say anything with a foreman standing at the front of this bay," he said. He said it to the stone, and his hand did not stop. "**A person at the front of a bench is a person somebody could put a hand on. You are inside a class, and a class is the one arrangement in this empire that can set a person back where they have been on its own, with nobody doing a thing and nobody lifting a pen. I have said four things in this bay in about a year and I have wanted an answer to none of them, and I am not handing a fifth one to a room with a woman standing in it in a position to be written down in.**"

"I moved for me."

"I have not said you did. I have said I am not going to speak."

"Then I have come to the front of my own room for nothing, and I would rather you had said that than what you said."

He went on with the chain for the length of one sheet's worth of work. Then he took his hand off it and put the hand in his lap, which is a thing that hand had not done in that bay in the four years she had been watching what it does.

"I have been the safest person in this building for eleven years," she said. "You said so yourself, in the fourth month, with the girl four feet behind me, and you were right, and I have never once said out loud what it is that makes me safe, because I worked it out and it is not a virtue. It is a box. **And you have been at this bench eleven years with a paper on that board and nobody has told you a thing either, and I came across nine foot of floor this morning to find out whether there is any difference between the two of us.**"

"There is a difference and you have worked it out without me, and what you came over here for was to be told it by me, and there is nobody in this building entitled to tell you anything."

"That is not what I did."

"That is what it was."

He put his hand back on the chain at the third link. The mark in chalk on the end wall is at the height of the third link and has been for as long as either of them has been in that bay, and neither of them had ever once looked at it.

"You have been safe and I have been unasked, and there is not one column in this empire that takes either of us, and neither of those is the better thing. I have watched what a class does to a person who spends eleven years inside one, and I would not put one on the girl behind you for the work she does at that bench."

---

The hour was the longest part of it.

Standing still in a working bay is not the same as waiting. There is noise on nine sides of her and none of it is hers, and about twenty minutes in she found she had nothing to do with her hands and put one of them flat on the front of the bench, on the wood, in front of the chain, and then took it off again, and then put it back, and the man at the bench did not look down and did not stop.

At about the half hour the girl of seventeen moved her weight from one foot to the other and put her sleeve back over her hand where it had gone up. Nobody spoke to her. At about the three quarters the reader turned a page without lifting his eyes off the wood, and the sound of it went along the bench and past her and was the only sound in that bay for a while.

She stood the rest of it with her eyes about six feet ahead of her, and she did not stop at anybody's hands, and there were nine pairs of them and one pair of eyes, and the pair of eyes was on a man with a chain.

At about the fifth hour she went back down the floor.

She found it inside the first two lengths of it. It was not a large thing. It was the kind of thing that costs half a morning to put right and a whole morning to find, and it was going to sit on that bench until the morning, because the shed shut at the sixth.

She left it where it was. She put her hand flat on the wood at the back of that bench, about four feet from the two of them, the way she has put it flat on that wood every working morning for a month.

Nobody in that shed was going to be told that the day's count came out one short of the count that came out the day before, and nobody was going to be told why, and the two of seventeen were not going to be asked about it, and the paper on the board by that door has not been filled in by any hand at any time since before she came.

---

"I stood at the front of it for an hour," she said, to the end wall, in the ordinary way a person says a thing out loud in a bay where nothing is being wanted of anybody, "and he did not say a word, and he was right not to, and I am not going to be thanked for the hour and he is not going to be asked why he did not. A class can put me back there without anybody lifting a pen. I have been at the back of that bench for a month and I stood at the front of it this morning, and a person cannot be entered in two places in one month."

The man at the front bench put his hand back on the chain and went along it to the third link. Whether he heard that was not a thing either of them was ever going to establish, and nobody in that shed was going to be told it either way.

She stayed at the back of that bench for the rest of the hour. She did not go to the doorway, and the sentence she has been carrying to that door since a spring that is gone did not get any nearer arriving, and nobody in that lane was ever going to arrive it for her.

---

When the shed shut at the sixth hour that chain came up off the stone and went back across the front of the bench, and his hand was on the third link of it, where it had been at the fourth hour, and the bay went cold in the way it went cold every working day of the year.

The bracket came on at the seventh hour and put light along the far end of that floor, where the two of seventeen were sitting with about a hand's width of wood between them, and neither of them was looking at the other. At the near end there was a foreman's hand flat on the wood about four inches off a chain, and a piece of work on the bench that had not been looked at, and the board by the door with its paper on it, and the man at the front bench going out into the lane in the ordinary way with his hands in his pockets.

The bundle was still tied at the far end where it had been all month, and the cord had gone into the wood a little at the knot the way cord does, and the two of seventeen had learned to work around it and had never once mentioned it to anybody.

Nobody said one word to anybody about any part of that day. Nobody was thanked and nobody was sent for and nothing was resolved, and the arrangement was still standing at the end of it, and there was nowhere in this empire to put any of it.
