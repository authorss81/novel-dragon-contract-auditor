# Chapter 0531: A Foreman At The Front Of Her Own Bay For One Hour In Eleven Years, A Man At A Bench Who Would Not Say A Word With Her Standing There, And A Bundle Worked Down To Two Hands

The chain was out along the stone and his thumb was on it and he was at the third link, and he had been at the third link since the bracket came on at about the fourth hour.

Halla Wray had been at the back of that bench for a month. She is fifty-one and she is a foreman, and she has been the occupant of a class for eleven years, and the paper behind that class is on a board by that door and has not been filled in by any hand at any time since before she came to that bench, and the only work left for it is the work of going on being paper.

At the back of that bay, at the far end of the wood, there were the two of them who have been at the back of it all year on the terms they are on. One of them had a book open.

The other of them is seventeen, and about a year has gone by in which nobody in that lane has put one single question to her. Nobody is going to put one this month. There has never been an offer of anything she could turn down either, and nobody has ever said to her that there was not, and that is the whole of what that girl has been left with.

The bundle she had put down at that far end a month ago was still on the wood, tied with the same length of cord, and about four days of that shed's own ordinary work had been worked off the top of it since and what was left was the size of a man's two hands put together. It was not a copy of anything. It was never going to be a copy of anything.

It is the second day of the first week of the eleventh month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour.

---

Then she got up off that bench and came the length of her own floor.

She had started at the near end of it in a spring that is gone and she had gone to the far end of it every working morning of the eleven years since, and about a month ago she had stopped and gone to the back of the bench and stood at the end of it behind the two of them. This morning she came across nine foot by eleven and stopped at the near end, at the front of the bay, about four feet off the chain with her hands loose at her sides.

"You are in my way," the man at the front bench said, without lifting his thumb.

"I have never once been in your way in eleven years."

"You are in it now."

"Then say the thing you have been not saying, because I have come to the front of it to stand in it and there is nowhere else in this building for me to stand."

---

That was the whole of what she came for and she got it.

He went on with the chain for about as long as it takes a sheet to be signed, and then he took his hand off it and put the hand in his lap, which is a thing that hand has not done in that bay in the four years she has been watching what it does.

"**I am not going to say anything with a foreman standing at the front of this bay.** You are inside a class, and a class is the one arrangement in this empire that can set a person back where they have been on its own, with nobody doing a thing, and a person at the front of a bench is a person somebody could put a hand on. I have said four things in this bay in about a year and I have wanted an answer to none of them and I am not going to hand a fifth one to a room with a woman standing in it in a position to be written down in. You are not going to be thanked for standing there and you are not going to be asked what it was for, and I have not asked you to move and I am not going to."

"I have not moved for you. I moved for me."

"I have not said you did. I have said I am not going to speak."

---

She stayed there until about the fifth hour.

Nobody in that shed said anything else out loud in that hour, and nobody asked anybody anything, and the one with the book turned a page at some point in it without lifting his eyes off the wood, and the girl of seventeen did not move and was not given the chance to say no about anything and will not be given the chance about anything else either.

At about the fifth hour she went back down her own floor and stood at the end of the bench at the back of it, about four feet from the two of them, and she put her hand flat on the wood the way she has put it flat on that wood at the back of that bay every working morning for a month.

"I have been at the back of this bench for a month," she said, to the end wall, in the ordinary way a person says a thing out loud in a bay where nothing is being wanted of anybody. "**A class can put me back there without anybody lifting a pen, and I have been there, and that is the place I have been, and a person cannot be entered in two places in one month. I stood at the front of it for an hour and he did not say a word, and I am not going to be thanked for the hour and he is not going to be asked why he did not, and nobody in this lane is going to be told which end of that bench I was at.**"

The man at the front bench put his hand back on the chain and went along it to the third link, and he is not going to say whether he heard that, and neither of them is going to mention the hour again.

---

Here is what that hour did and what it did not do.

It did not get a sentence said in that bay. It did not get an answer out of anybody, and it did not get a form filled in, and the paper on that board by the door is where it was at the fourth hour and is going to be where it is at the sixth.

It got her to the front of her own room for one hour, and there is no form anywhere in this empire in which a foreman of fifty-one is entered as the one who stood at the front of a bay, and there is no form in which she is entered as the one who did not stand there either.

A class is not a room and has never once been a room, and what put her in this bay eleven years ago has spent every day of the eleven since setting her back in it, and it has done that without a hand moving.

And it got the only person in that lane who could have listened to a fifth thing in that bay to stand in a position to listen to one, for an hour, in a month in which he did not say it.

Neither of those is going anywhere. Neither of them is going to be thanked, and neither of them is going to be asked about, and there is nowhere standing at the end of that lane at which either of those could be put.

---

The chain went back across the front of that bench at about the sixth hour with his hand on the third link where it had been at the fourth.

The bundle at the far end of the wood was the size of two hands and was not going to become anything else, and the bracket came on at the seventh hour and put light into the far end of that bay, and a woman of fifty-one was standing at the near end of a bench with her hand on the front of it and nobody in that shed is going to be told that she was.
