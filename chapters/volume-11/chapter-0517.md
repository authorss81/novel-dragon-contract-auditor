# Chapter 0517: A Coin Through A Gap In A Door That Is Not Quite Shut, A Woman Going Down A Lane Every Week Who Said Nothing At All, And A Tin Behind A Boiler Whose Lid Does Not Shut

The ground floor of that house has been described once and is not described again. What is in it this month is a boiler at the back, a tin behind that boiler whose lid has not shut properly since a spring that is gone, a stair going up out of the room, and a woman at the bottom of that stair who has never once been above the second one.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the seventh month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the first hour.

The woman of about thirty-five who keeps a day-book in her own hand has been coming down that lane once a week for about a year.

---

The coin went through the gap in that door the way it has gone through it every week for about two years, and the door stayed shut, and the hand that put it there went back up.

Not one of the two of them has ever said the other's name out loud. He does not know she has his. She has his name. It came down that lane out of the mouth of the woman with the day-book about two years ago, spoken aloud in the open air to a wall in front of her own house, and this door was not quite shut and the woman at the bottom of the stair got all of it.

She has had it in her own mouth once since then, to the plaster behind that boiler. It is not going to be in her mouth again, and the reason is that a second time would be the start of something a person does.

**He is the only person in this matter she could say that name to, and she is not going to, and he does not know that she has it, and there is nobody in that house who could tell him.**

---

The woman with the day-book came down that lane at about that hour with the book under her arm and stopped at that step, which she has done for about a year.

She did not say good evening. She had said in the last month of that year that she was not going to come back with anything else and she did not, and she has not said one word to that door since and neither has anybody in that house said one word to her.

She stood on that step and she was in the room.

---

The woman at the bottom of the stair said the true thing out loud, to the boiler, at about the first hour, in the ordinary way a person says a thing out loud in a room where nobody has ever said anything.

"**I have taken eight years and something of a rent out of a hand in this house and put it in a tin behind this boiler and I have never written a line of it and I have never asked that man for a receipt, and I worked all of that out at the time and I have worked out the rest of it this month. I did it so that there would be no paper. A thing with no paper on it cannot be tied to a person. And the price of that is that neither of us can be shown to have ever been in this room, and I have had that for eight years, and I have just given it away to a person standing on my own step who did not ask me for it.**"

The boiler ticked. The tin behind it did what it does. The coin was in the apron pocket and not in the tin, where it has been since about the first hour, on purpose.

---

Neither of them said anything for about as long as a person takes to cross a lane.

"You have said a thing out loud to me," the woman with the day-book said, "and I came down this lane with my mouth shut, on purpose, because I said I would not come back with anything else and I did not, and that is the only reason I was standing here when you said it."

"I know you were standing here. I have watched you come down that lane for about a year and I have never once known what you were going to do when you got to it."

"I came down it to be here. That is the whole of what I have been doing for a year, and it did not come to me until about a month ago, and it has not been said to anybody, and I am not going to say it to you twice."

---

Then she said the other thing, and she said it to the step and not to the woman, and it is the reason she is not going up that lane again next week.

"**A person who is in a room when a true thing is said cannot afterwards be shown to have been nowhere near it. You keep a book in your own hand with four headings over four columns and there is no fifth, and I have watched you keep that book for a year. Everything else about you can be looked at and gone and none of it can be produced. And now there is a sentence in that lane with you standing in it, and there is no form anywhere in this empire in which you can be entered as the one who was standing on that step, and there is no form in which you can be entered as the one who was not, and you are not going to be told which one you are, and I am not going to tell you.**"

The woman at the bottom of the stair did not open the door and did not go up the stairs.

"You came down here to say nothing," she said.

"I came down here to say nothing. I have said nothing for a year and I have never once been thanked for it and I was not going to be thanked for it this morning either."

---

The woman with the day-book went up the lane with the book under her arm and she did not stop again, and there is nothing in that book about the first hour of that morning and there is not going to be anything, because a book is a record and a record is the one thing anybody can be shown, and she knows that better than anybody in that lane.

The stair went up after her and nobody came down it. The coin stayed in the apron pocket and the lid of the tin did not shut and nobody came to look at it, and the man at the top of those stairs went up and did not come back down, and nobody was thanked and nobody was sent for and nothing was resolved.
