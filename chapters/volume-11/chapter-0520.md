# Chapter 0520: A Stair That Was Quiet On One Morning Out Of Four, An Hour In Which Nothing Was Wanted Of Anybody, And A Woman Who Did Not Get Up This Time

Nobody in this city gets to that floor except up one stair, and the room is open between the second hour and the sixth. About four hundred people a year go down that stair holding something else altogether, each of them for their own reasons, and nobody in that building has ever sent one of them up it.

Marn Ottery is thirty-four and eleven years at the far end of those boards, and she has not gone out of that room once in the eleven of them. Four times she has put the shape of a thing out loud. Before the first of the four she told the floor there would not be a fifth, and three months of ordinary mornings have not given her one.

Tamsin Rook is twenty-four and has stood at her own end of those boards for two years, and for the whole of the two years she has been in that room twenty minutes ahead of the hour on every working morning of it.

The clerk of about twenty-nine is at the far end of the two joined tables where she has been for four years, with the book open in front of her and the pen in the middle of it.

It is the second day of the second week of the eighth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the sixth hour.

---

Four times a month, that stair. It had been four times a month for eleven years and it was not four times a month on that morning, and nobody on that floor said one word about it then or afterwards.

The strap did not come up. There was no hand on the fourth step from the top and no satchel set down on the wood at that end, and the far end of those boards was empty from the second hour to the sixth, and it is the first time in about eleven years that anybody on that floor has noticed a thing about that end of it.

Nobody put anything to anybody about it. There is nowhere on that floor at which a thing about the far end could be put to a person, and the only person in that building who could have said one word about it out loud was the man who did not come.

---

The three of them were in that room for six hours and nothing at all was wanted of any of them, which is the arrangement and has been the arrangement for eleven years and is the only reason that floor still works.

About halfway through the fifth hour Tamsin Rook said one flat thing out loud, to the boards, in the ordinary way, and it was the third time in three months that anybody on that floor had said a true thing with a second person standing in the room.

"**Nothing has been wanted of anybody on this floor in eleven years and I have worked out this month that that is the only reason any of us is standing here at all, and I have not decided what to do about it, and I am not going to, and nobody is going to be thanked for the not deciding and nobody is going to be asked.**"

The clerk of about twenty-nine had her head down over the two joined tables for the whole of it, as she has on every morning of the four years she has been at that end.

---

She did not get up.

That is the whole of what is different about the last ten weeks of that floor, and it is the whole of what is different about her, and it is not a decision and there is nothing in it that could be described as deciding anything.

A month ago she had stood up and pushed her chair back about a foot and gone down four flights at the third hour of an ordinary morning and come back up at the fourth hour. Nobody stopped her and nobody thanked her and nobody asked her.

The whole of what that was is that **walking out of a room is not turning a thing down, and a person who leaves cannot be shown to have refused anything, and a person who stays cannot be shown to have accepted anything. And there is no form anywhere in this empire that takes either of the two.**

So it was not a refusal. It was not a protest. It was not a thing she could be shown having meant, and there is no column anywhere that a person at that end of two joined tables could be entered in as the one who got up on the fourth day of the fourth week of the seventh month, and there is none for the one who did not get up on the second day of the second week of the eighth month either.

**What she has lost is the distance. There is no way in this empire to put a person back at the far end of a room who was not asked anything and did not agree to anything and did not refuse anything and cannot now be shown to have been anywhere else, and the whole of what that distance was is gone off her this month without anybody doing a single thing to her.**

Nobody is going to tell her that she has lost it. Nobody has told her that she was in a room in the first month of those three and nobody is going to tell her now. She is not going to be thanked for staying in her chair and she is not going to be asked why she went down four flights a month ago, and there is nowhere standing in that room at which either of those could be put.

---

The case went in behind the boards at the sixth hour and the four went up onto the wall above it, one to a nail each. Nobody on that floor can say what the fourth of those four is for. Beneath that wall is a drawer, and it has not been opened in eleven years. There are four things in it. Two of them have never been looked at. The box under the far end has not been opened and has not been moved and it is not the case.

The clerk of about twenty-nine put the pen into the middle of the book and squared the book against the edge of the table, and the two of them at the boards did not look over at her, and she did not look over at either of them.

Nobody was thanked. Nobody was forgiven. Nobody was sent for, and nothing was resolved, and the arrangement is still standing. There is a woman of about thirty-five who keeps a day-book in her own hand who was standing on a step in a lane in the seventh month of that year and told nobody about it. There is a woman of about twenty-six at the end of about four hundred yards of cold flags who was standing at a lock in the sixth month of that year and told nobody about that either. Not one of the three of them in this building has ever been told one word about the other two, and none of the three is going to be.
