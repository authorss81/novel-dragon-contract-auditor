# Chapter 0536: A Man At The Front Bench Of A Shed Saying One Of His Four Out Loud With Two People In The Bay, And Nobody In That Lane Answering Any Part Of It

The chain was out along the stone with his thumb resting on the third link of it, the way that thumb has rested on that link every working morning for about eleven years, and it had been there since the middle of the hour.

He is about fifty-five. He has been at that bench for eleven years and about nine foot by eleven of floor has gone past the front of it every working day of that time, and there is a mark in chalk on the end wall at the height of the third link of that chain, and there is a paper on a board by that door which has not been filled in by any hand at any time since before a foreman of fifty-one came to that bench.

A working sentence out of a paragraph has never once been beyond him in his life, and that is a different thing altogether from the four people in this matter who cannot get sense out of one, and he has said so in that shed in terms, twice. He is not one of those four and never has been.

He is the only person in this matter who could take a line out of a printed strip and say out loud whose it is, and nobody in this city is going to put a sheet in front of him.

It is the second day of the second week of the twelfth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour.

---

She was at the back of that bench and had been for a month, and she had stood at the front of that bay for one hour about ten weeks ago and had been sent back to the back of it by nothing whatever except the length of her own working morning.

A bench longer than its room runs the length of the back wall and there are about ninety foot of open floor between the bay and the wall it stands against, and a person at the near end of it and a person at the far end of it are as far apart as a room allows two people to be and still be in the same working morning. That is the whole of the arrangement of the bay and it has been the arrangement for eleven years.

At the far end of that bench, four feet off her elbow, were the two who are always at the far end of it, on the written engagement and on the silence. Neither of them has been spoken to, thanked or sent for in this month or in any month after it.

The girl has not been asked one thing in about a year. There has never been anything put in front of her for her to turn down, and nobody has ever told her that there was not.

The bundle of that shed's own ordinary work was at the far end where it had been all month, and the two of seventeen had learned to work around it, and what was left of it was about the size of a man's two hands put together.

---

He wanted the fourth hour to be an ordinary hour.

That was all. He wanted the bay to be nine foot by eleven with a bench in it and a chain across the front of the bench and nobody in the bay at all, and he wanted the hour to go along the way that hour had gone along every working day for eleven years, which is to say that he wanted to be a man in a room by himself at about the fourth hour on an ordinary morning.

The boards of the floor carry a boot and carry a chain, and nothing else in the place makes a noise worth the name, which is why eleven years of the bay had taught him that a sound in it means somebody has come in off the lane and has not shut the door behind them properly, and which is also why three people already in it make very little, because three people working do not take four hours to say anything to anybody.

The bay was not that. The bay had had three other people in it since the second hour, and there was no arrangement of that morning going to change that, and he had worked all of it out at about the half hour and had gone on with the chain anyway.

He had told the woman about ten weeks ago, standing in that bay, that he was not going to say a word with a foreman at the front of it. She had been at the front of it that morning and she was at the back of it now, four feet off his left shoulder, and he had not worked out in ten weeks whether that was a thing she had done or a thing a class had done, and he was not going to ask her, and asking her was the one thing in that lane there was no standing at which anybody did.

He had told her something else in the fourth month of that year, in about nine words, to the floor, with a second person in the bay behind her. He had meant it when he said it. He had gone on meaning it every working day since, the way a man goes on meaning a thing he has said once about a man at a bench.

He was at the third link. He went on past it and came back, the way the chain is worked, and the mark in chalk on the end wall was at the height of his own knuckle and had been at the height of his own knuckle for as long as the two of them had been in that bay. He had never once looked at it. Nobody in that shed ever had.

A chain on a man at the front of a stone bench is worked by feel and not by eye, and the third link is as far as a hand has gone every working morning for eleven years because past it the weight of the chain changes, and what a man feels in his forearm at the third link and what he feels past it are not the same thing, and he had never once stopped at the change to find out what it was.

He had said four true things in that bay in about a year. He had wanted an answer to none of them. He had said them the way this one came out, which is to say that they came out at some hour of some ordinary morning while his hands were doing what his hands do, and there was not one of the four that he had sat down and got ready to say.

At about a quarter past, the girl at the far end put her sleeve back over her hand where it had gone up. At about the half hour the reader turned a page, and the sound of it went along the bench and past the back of it and past the front of it, and neither of the two of them said one word, and neither of them is going to be spoken to that day.

Behind him, about four feet off, the foreman of fifty-one stood at the back of that bench with her hand flat on the wood.

He knew all three of them were there the way a man knows how much of a room his own voice is going into. That is not the same as looking and it is not the same as hearing, and it is the only way any of it is ever known in a bay nine foot by eleven, and it is why a working man can say things in a shed for eleven years without once having to check whether anybody is in it.

He got as far as the fourth hour and a half and got no further, and it was not courage that took him the rest of the way, and there was nothing in him anywhere that had decided to do anything.

It took about as long as squaring a sheet takes, which is about as long as he had been waiting without knowing he was waiting, and he stood up out of nothing at all and said it the way he says everything else in the bay, which is to the stone, with his hands still moving while the mouth goes.

At about half past the fourth hour he said it.

He said it to the stone in front of the front bench, in the ordinary way, the way a man says a thing out loud in a bay where nothing is being wanted of anybody, and there was nobody in that bay he was saying it to.

"**Nobody has asked me for one thing in eleven years and I have just said a true thing out loud in this bay with two people in it, and there is nowhere in this empire to put a man who has.**"

Then he put his hand back on the chain at the third link and went on along it.

The rest of that hour was the rest of that hour.

The chain went the length of the stone and came back, and went again. Something was worked off the top of the bundle at the far end of the bench in the ordinary way, and the girl of seventeen carried it away when she had finished with it, and the reader read, and the bracket was not on yet and would not be on until the seventh hour. There was no sound in that bay after about half past the fourth hour except the chain and, now and then, the sound of a boot on a stone floor, and there was not one thing in that shed that had changed since the half hour, and there was not one thing in that shed that was ever going to change because of what had been said in it.

He worked the rest of it at the same rate and it took the same number of pulls whichever way he counted them, and about forty minutes after he had said it he could not have told anybody how much of the hour had gone on being ordinary, and that was the answer he had been given and it was the same answer the bay had given him about the chalk for eleven years.

---

Nobody answered it.

The reader at the far end of that bench had his hand where his hand was. He did not look up. He read on, and when he came to the bottom of the page he put his finger on the corner of it and left it there instead of turning the page, and after a while took his hand off it and carried on with what he reads, and nobody in that shed is going to be asked which page it was or how long it was.

The girl of seventeen did not move and was not given the chance to say no about anything.

The foreman of fifty-one at the back of that bench did not turn round and did not say one word. She is the only person in that bay who could have asked him anything, and she is inside a class, and a class puts a person back where they have been, and it has been doing that to her in that bay for eleven years, and it does it by doing nothing and never has to lift a pen.

She took her hand off the wood a little while after that and put it down at her side, and the taking of it took longer than the doing of it, and the man at the front bench did not hear it and is never going to know that it happened, and she is not going to be told that he said a word and he is never going to be told that she was standing four feet off his shoulder when he said it.

A hand that has been flat on a piece of wood for an hour has an impression of it, and hers did not, because the wood at the back of the bench is not the wood at the front of it and a foreman's hand has known the difference for eleven years.

---

At about the sixth hour the chain was gathered up off the stone and laid back across the front of that bench, and his hand stayed on the third link of it, and the bracket came on by itself at about the seventh hour and put light into the far end of a bay where two people were sitting at the back of a bench about a hand's width apart and neither of them was looking at the other.

At the near end of that bench there was the mark in chalk on the end wall at the height of the third link, and a chain lying across the wood with a hand on it, and a board by that door with a paper on it that had not been filled in by any hand at any time since before the foreman of fifty-one came to that bay.

That was the week. Nobody was thanked and nobody was sent for and nothing was resolved, and the form on the board by that door was where it had been at the fourth hour and it was going to be where it was at the sixth, and there was nowhere in this empire to put any part of what had happened in that shed that morning, and there never had been.
