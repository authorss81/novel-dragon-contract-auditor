# Chapter 0524: A Coat Taken Off A Nail And Put On And Taken Off Again, A Stair That Did Not Sound, And Two Papers Standing Against A Man's Chest For About Two Hours

The room is taken by the week and has been for longer than he has held nothing, and the landlord has never once come through the door of it, and about four feet of bare wall stands beside the window with a nail in it and the coat has been on that nail for about two years.

Marek Kest. Thirty-eight. He stands in no register in this city, and never has, and nobody is going to rule a line for him in any building in this empire.

About a year ago he put in a form of his own writing, one line of it, and a rank of his own was stripped off him in consequence. A rank that was never granted cannot be drawn back off a man afterwards, and a certification that was never issued cannot be recalled, and so there is nothing on him to hand over and nothing on him that anybody could come for.

He has a mark about three inches long on the inside of his left wrist. There is nothing written in it, it settles nothing, and it does not know where the ford is.

It is the second day of the second week of the ninth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the morning.

---

There are two things in the inside pocket of that coat, one on each side of a seam. One of them is a leaf out of a bundle that was folded in two, with a line drawn through whatever stood at the foot of that line, and the crease a third of the way down has gone so thin that a thumb goes through it. The other is a piece of paper with a printed strip along its top edge and four things set along that strip, and not one of those four is the name of anybody, and he paid fourpence for it, out of a case at the top of that stair, on a wet morning about two years ago.

Nobody in this city has been told that he is carrying two things. Nobody has ever put a hand into that coat and nobody ever is going to.

He took it off the nail at about the second hour and put it on.

Two years of a coat on one nail have pressed the shoulder of the wool out around the head of it and left the fold below hollow, and the wool where it goes over the top has taken the colour of the plaster behind, which is the colour of everything else in there. Taking it down is a matter of lifting it off with one hand and not of undoing anything, and there is no fastener on it and there has not been one for two years.

---

He did it the way a man does a thing he has not done in two years, which is badly. The collar went in the wrong way round and he turned the whole of it and started again, and there was nothing in the room to see him do it.

Nothing in the room makes heat and nothing in it is going to. He had taken the coat off its nail and put it on, so that for the length of a morning there was nothing on any part of the plaster at all, and about four feet of bare wall in a cold room is a great deal of nothing to be holding a coat against.

With the coat on, the pocket is against his chest. He stood in the middle of that floor and worked out, for the first time in about two years, that he had been carrying both of those things at arm's length from himself the whole time and had not known what the difference was until about the second hour of a morning in the ninth month.

A crease worn through to the other side, on one side of a seam. Four things printed along a strip, none of them a name, on the other.

Whatever belonged at the foot of that struck line is gone and the hand that went through it was not his mother's, and the claim that left this empire under that name left it the same day and has not been back since. He has one of those two things and has never had the other.

He said none of that out loud. There is no word in this empire for what he would have to say if he ever opened his mouth about that, and a room cannot be asked, and nothing was asked of him this month.

---

At about the third hour he stood at the door with his hand on the latch, and he did not open it, and the latch is not a lock and has never been anything else.

A woman two floors down has a thing about a window in that house and it has been going on for about two months. She came to that door three times in three months and did not knock, and the last time she said one thing out loud on the other side of it and went up the stair, and neither of them said one word about the half inch of wood between them, and neither of them can be shown refusing and neither can be shown accepting, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that takes either of those.

She is not a person anybody in this matter can put a thing to. That has been the case for three months and it is the case now.

The stair did not sound. It had not sounded since the last time and it did not sound this morning, and he stood with his hand on the latch for about as long as a sheet takes to be squared and then he took his hand off it.

Those four flights are stone and they climb the side of the house with the rail on the open side, and anybody going up at the ordinary rate is heard at every floor and heard twice over, once going and once coming. Over eleven years he had got so exact about the whole of it that he knew how long after a door opened anywhere below him the house would give him away for. He stood in the middle of the floor with his ear doing what it had been built to do, and finding out nothing was on it took him most of an hour.

---

He did not go down those stairs and out into the street and buy something anywhere in this city, and he did not go to the top of any stair in it and he did not go to the end of any lane.

There is nothing standing behind him that anybody could come at, and that has been the whole of it for about a year, and it goes back a long way further than any fire does. A man who goes and looks has been shown a thing by somebody, and being shown a thing makes a man a party, and a party is the one thing in this matter anybody can be told in advance what it is going to be used for.

So he stood in the middle of that floor in a coat he had not worn in two years and worked out the other half of it, which is the half that is new, and he said it out loud to the plaster beside that window.

There is nobody in that room to put it to and there never has been anybody to put it to. A room is the only thing in this matter that has never once wanted a thing out of anybody and never will.

"**A man can put a coat on in a room with nobody in it and that is not going anywhere. I have been the cheapest man in this matter for two years and the cheapest thing in it is a person standing in a room, and I have not been one this month and I have not looked for it and I am not going to. Both of those are the same arrangement and I have known it since the spring and I am not going to do the second one because I have done the first one for two years.**"

It was not a resolution and it did not feel like one and it was not going to be one by the sixth hour.

---

He took the coat off at about the fourth hour and hung it back on the nail. That nail was driven in skew about two years ago at roughly the height of his own hand, and it is the same nail and the same angle.

Putting a coat back on a skew nail takes one hand and about a second, and a man who has done it a hundred times does it without looking at it, and there is a patch round the head of the nail where the plaster has been rubbed pale by the wool, and there is no other mark on the wall anywhere and there has not been one since before the coat went up on it.

The two things in the inside pocket of it were where they have been since a wet morning about two years ago, one on each side of a seam, and neither of them has been out of that pocket in this building or in any other, and nobody has looked in there and nobody ever is going to and nobody is going to be told what is at the foot of that struck line or whose hand struck it.

Four feet of bare wall is beside that window and there is nothing on any part of it. The stair went up at about the sixth hour without anybody on it that had come down as far as his door, and he heard it and did not open, and that is the whole of what the day was and it is not going to be in any record anywhere in this empire.
