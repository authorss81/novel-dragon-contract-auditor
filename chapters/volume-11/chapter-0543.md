# Chapter 0543: A Man Of Thirty-Eight Coming Down Four Flights For The First Time In About Two Years, And A Board By A Door With Four Foot Of It Bare

He came down four flights at about the first hour, which he had not done in about two years, and he had not come down them to tell anybody anything and there was nothing on the stair that told him to.

Marek Kest. Thirty-eight. There is no book in this city with his name in it, and there has not been one since about a year ago, when a single line of his own writing took the last thing he had a heading for off him. A heading that was never granted cannot be drawn back off a man afterwards, and what is left is a man with nothing on him that any building in this empire could come for.

The two things are in the inside pocket of a coat on a nail in a room at the top of that house, one on each side of a seam, and neither of them has come out of that pocket in about two years, and no hand in this city has been into that coat.

It is the second day of the first week of the second month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the first hour.

---

He put the week's coin under the door, the way he has put one under that door every week for about two years, and then he did not go up the stair.

The door is not quite shut. It has not been quite shut for the whole of the two years, and the gap it does not close is at the bottom, and a week's money goes through the same gap every week without either of them ever arranging it. He put it there with one hand and took the hand away and stood, and the stair is stone and cold and takes a boot a long way, and he had come down all four flights of it a little while before and had not hurried and had not gone slow.

There is a board by that door with the keys to those three rooms on it, and there are two keys on it, and there has been a third nail on that board with nothing on it for about five weeks, and the four foot of board around that nail is bare, and it is the only thing in that hall he had come down to look at.

Two keys and a nail. The two keys have been on the board since before he ever had the top room and they sit side by side and do not move, and the third nail is at the other end of the same piece of board with nothing on it and has been empty since a morning about five weeks off.

Four foot of bare wood is not dust. Dust would be on the whole board and in the corners of the room and in the groove of a keyhole and on the floor beneath it. Four foot of bare wood in a line in the middle of a board is four foot of board a hand has gone along and taken something off, and the rest of the board has not been touched since the morning it happened, because there was nothing else on it to touch.

She was at the back of the room with the boiler and the tin behind it.

"There is a nail on that board," he said, "with nothing on it, and it has had nothing on it since about five weeks ago, and I have been at the top of that stair for the whole of those five weeks."

"Yes."

"I am not going to ask you where it is and I am not going to be thanked for the not asking."

"I am not going to tell you and I am not going to be thanked for the not telling you. You have not asked me and I would rather it had stayed that way, and I have said that in about four years of standing in this room on the other side of a door from you and I have not said it before this morning, and you are not going to be thanked for the hour."

He looked at the board for a while. There is nothing to be done with a board by a door at the first hour of a morning and both of them knew it.

"I have taken that room by the week for about two years," he said, "and the whole of what that house has ever said about anybody living in it was a mark cut into the back of the key to the top room, and that key is not on that board, and *there is now nothing in that building that says I have ever slept in it.* I am not going to ask you about the mark and I am not going to be thanked for the not asking, and I am telling you I have noticed because nobody else in that house is going to notice it for me."

"That is four things you have said to me since you came down that stair and I have answered one of them."

"I have noticed you have answered one of them."

---

He did not go up the stair.

An hour in a room with a boiler in it is not an hour anybody would call empty. The thing ticks, and about every so often it takes a bigger breath and goes again, and the tin behind it answers with a rattle a little different from the tick, and the dust off it comes down onto a floor a woman has swept every working morning for the last twenty years. There was a sound in the house for the whole of the hour and neither of them needed to raise anything to be heard over it.

He stayed in that room with a second person in it for about an hour, and nothing was said in it, and that was the arrangement. He had put a sentence about wanting to a room with nobody in it, more than once, and it had never once been a sentence about anybody in particular, and that was the shape of it. A shape does not come the other side of a door.

She was there because she is at the bottom of that stair every working morning at about the first hour and has been for twenty years. She had not come for him. She was not going to know he existed by the sixth hour, and she would not be told one word about any part of it, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that takes a man for having been in a room with a woman for an hour while nothing whatever was said in it.

He looked at the boiler twice. The tin is behind the boiler, and it is a tin that does not sit flat on its rim, and the lid comes down crooked, and about nine years of a rent in coin is in it and there has never been a piece of paper in that house and there never is going to be one. Somebody moved the key off that board and into it about five weeks ago and neither of them was going to say so.

Nine years of a rent in coin is heavier than a tin wants to be. It does not sit flat and it leans into the gap behind the boiler and has taken the shape of it, and a round tin of nine years of coin is a thing that has to be shifted a hand's width before it can be shifted at all. He looked at the top of it and then at the floor on the far side of it, and then he did not look at it again for a while.

He did not look in the tin. He stood about two feet off it for a while and looked at the lid, and the lid is not a thing you can see into from where he was standing, and he did not go round, and she watched him not go round, and neither of them said one word about it then or afterwards.

---

Here is the whole of what that hour is.

Two people were in a room in that house and nothing was said in it. The two of them cannot be shown having agreed to anything and cannot be shown having refused anything. There is nowhere in this empire that a man at the top of a stair and a woman at the bottom of it can be entered as the two who were in a room together, and that is the arrangement, and it was the arrangement before he came down and it is going to be the arrangement after he has gone up again.

Two people can be in a room for an hour and one of them can know exactly how long it has been and the other have no idea at all, and both can be true at the same time in one room, and neither of them was going to use either.

A rent comes once a week out of a hand into her own and has never been on a piece of paper in that house, and a man who is in a top room of it is a man whose whole relation to that building is a coin under a door and a nail on a board, and a nail with nothing on it is a nail with nothing on it, and it is the only piece of furniture in that house that has changed in five weeks.

He worked out, standing two feet off a tin he did not open, that he had wanted for about two years to be in a room with somebody in it who had not come for him. The way to do that, he worked out, is to go and stand in a room where somebody is going to be anyway, four times a month, for a year. That is not a thing anybody in this empire can be stopped from, and it is not a thing anybody in this empire can be shown having done either.

Then he worked out the other half of it, which is that he has not got about four years of weeks, and that a man who goes down a stair once is a man who went down a stair once, and that he is not going to go down it again, and that this is not the second half of a sentence and there is no second half of it and nobody in that house is going to be told that he said anything at all.

---

The boiler went through what it goes through. The tin behind it went on standing crooked on its rim with the lid crooked on the top of it, and about four foot of board by that door went on being bare with a nail in the middle of it and nothing on the nail.

The stair is stone and it is cold, and a person on it can be heard from the floor below the way a person can be heard in the next room. He went up all four flights of it at the rate a person goes up when they are not going to stop, and his hand did not go onto the rail anywhere on the way.

He was back at the top of that stair inside about half an hour and did not stop on any of the flights, and the woman at the bottom of that stair did not go up two steps, because she has not been above the second of those stairs once in twenty years and is not going to start, and she did not go up them to find out whether he was in.

The coin went into the tin behind that boiler and the lid came down crooked on the top of it, the way it has come down crooked twice in about five weeks, and the woman at the bottom of that stair has not been above the second of those steps in twenty years and did not go up them to find out whether he was in, and there is no paper in that house on which either of them could be put.
