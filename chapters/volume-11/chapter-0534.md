# Chapter 0534: A Man At A Sill Working Out That Stopping Is A Decision And Not Stopping, A Woman Of Fifty-Three Out Of A Room For The Third Time In About A Year, And A Girl Of Nineteen Carrying An Armful Past Neither Of Them

Nine hundred sheets a year go out of that building, one at a time, and the mark at the foot of every one of them was made by one of two hands of the same man. The pot on that sill takes a pen and hands it straight back and has never once had an opinion about which hand put it in. None of that is being put in front of anybody this month.

This month is about the third time that the door of the room at the back of that floor has opened since a spring that is gone, and about nineteen years, and a man who went from being forty-two to being sixty-one at that sill without anybody standing on that floor finding out one single thing about him.

There is not a working sentence in a paragraph in him and there has not been one of those since before a fire that other people went out of. The care he takes over it is the only thing he has ever added to anything at all.

There are four people in this matter who cannot get sense out of a paragraph and he has not been one of them at any hour of his life, and he has said as much on that floor.

There is no room in that building in which any of those four has been asked one question about a number, and there has not been one asked of him in nineteen years either, which is a different fact and not a better one. The man of about fifty-five with a chain at the end of a lane off the Slade is not one of them either and has said so in terms twice. The woman of fifty-three keeps the minutes in the room at the back of that floor and is not one of them, and the reader of seventeen is not one of them and is not going to be.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the eleventh month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the second hour and about the fifth.

---

The woman of fifty-three came out of her room at about the third hour.

Nineteen years of minutes, and nothing advised in them by anybody but her, and nothing put to that room in about four years. She came out into the corridor twice in a spring that is gone, the first time in four years, and that is twice, and this was the third time anybody in that building had seen that door open since the spring.

She went down the corridor to the end of that sill and stopped there, where a person stands when a person stands there, and neither of them was looking at the other.

"You are not going to put anything in front of me," she said, before he had said anything, and she said it flatly and to the sill.

"I am not going to put anything in front of you and I am not going to tell you what I have not already said, and you have not asked me for anything and I have not asked you for anything, and that has been the arrangement of this room for nineteen years and it is not changing this month."

"I am not here for what you have not already said. I came out of that room for the window and not for you, and I would like it noticed that I said that before you said anything, and nobody in this building is going to be thanked for it."

---

**Then he said it out loud, to the sill, with a second person standing in that corridor.**

"**About nine of about nine hundred a year go out of this room and I sign them the same as the rest, and there is no mark I could stand beside one of them that would not be a heading over it, and I have worked out this month that putting one there would be a decision, and a decision is the one thing in this that anybody can be given warning of before it happens. I am not going to stop signing them. I have not decided to go on signing them either, and those are not the same thing, and nobody on this floor is going to be told which of the two it is.**"

He signed four sheets in the time it took her to walk back down that corridor and go through that door, and the pot took the pen back out of his hand exactly as it takes it back out of the other one, and the door of that room shut.

She had been in that corridor when he said it. She was not going to be told that he had said it in front of her, and he was not going to be told that she had been there, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that takes a man at a sill as the one a thing was said to, and there is none that takes a woman as the one who came out of a room she had not come out of in four years.

---

The girl of about nineteen carried the armful past that sill about forty times in each direction and put it down on the stone at the end of every one of those, and she had been in that room when the pot took the pen back about nine hundred times between the second hour and about the fifth and about nine of those were not what the person above him had written.

She has said one true thing out loud in a corridor off that room about four months ago and nobody answered it, and the only thing she had that was hers was not being asked, and she let that go in a month that is gone and it has not come back, and no form in this empire puts a person back where they were not asked.

She is not going to be asked one question about a number. Neither of them has said one word to her about the nine and neither of them is going to, and she is not going to say one word about either of them, and she is nineteen.

The number went out at the end of that day at the end of that floor, the way it goes out every working day of that year, and nobody in that building has ever thanked her for the number and nobody ever is going to.

---

About nine hundred sheets left that building that day, one at a time, and two hands anywhere in this empire could have put the mark at the foot of every one of them.

On a shelf under a window about two feet wide with the pane out of it stands a question with no full stop at the end of it and her own name written at the foot of it, put there on purpose about eleven years ago.

It is never going to be written out in any building in this empire and nobody is going to put her a second one of anything.

The window it stands under has no pane in it this month and did not have one last month, and has not had one for about four years.
