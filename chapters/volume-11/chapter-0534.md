# Chapter 0534: A Man At A Sill Working Out That Stopping Is A Decision And Not Stopping, A Woman Of Fifty-Three Out Of That Room For The Third Time In About A Year, And One Of The Nine

Nine hundred sheets a year go out of that building, one at a time, and the mark at the foot of every one of them was made by one of two hands of the same man. The pot on that sill takes a pen and hands it straight back out again and has never once had an opinion about which hand put it in.

He is about sixty-one. He went from being forty-two to being sixty-one at that sill and nobody on that floor found out one single thing about him while it happened. There is not a working sentence in a paragraph in him and there has not been one since before a fire that other people went out of, and the care he takes over it is the only thing he has ever added to anything at all.

There are four people in this matter who cannot get sense out of a paragraph and he has not been one of them at any hour of his life, and he has said as much on that floor. The man of about fifty-five with a chain at the end of a lane off the Slade is not one of them either and has said so in terms twice. The woman of fifty-three keeps the minutes in the room at the back of that floor and is not one of them, and the reader of seventeen is not one of them and never has been.

There is no room in that building in which any of those four has been asked one question about a number. There has not been one asked of him in nineteen years either, which is a different fact and not a better one.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the eleventh month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the second hour and about the fifth.

---

The woman of fifty-three came out of her room at about the third hour.

Nineteen years of minutes, and nothing advised in them by anybody but her, and nothing put to that room in about four years. She came out into that corridor twice in a spring that is gone, the first time in four years, and that is twice, and this was the third time anybody in that building had seen that door open since the spring.

She went down the corridor to the end of that sill and stopped there, where a person stands when a person stands there, and neither of them was looking at the other.

"You are not going to put anything in front of me," she said, before he had said anything, and she said it flatly and to the sill.

"I am not going to put anything in front of you, and I am not going to tell you what I have not already said, and you have not asked me for anything and I have not asked you for anything, and that has been the arrangement of this room for nineteen years and it is not changing this month."

"I am not here for what you have not already said. I came out of that room for the window and not for you, and I would like it noticed that I said that before you said anything, and nobody in this building is going to be thanked for it."

He went on with the sheet he was on. The pot is on that sill at his right hand and it has two pens in it, and they are the same pen, and he takes out whichever one is nearest and gives it back, and that has been the arrangement of the pot for nineteen years.

**"About nine of about nine hundred a year go out of this room and I sign them the same as the rest," he said, out loud, to the sill, with a second person standing in that corridor. "There is no mark I could stand beside one of them that would not be a heading over it, and a heading is the thing that makes a thing about nine into a thing about a person, and I have not got that and I will not have it either. Putting one there would be a decision. A decision is the one thing in this that anybody can be given warning of before it happens. I am not going to stop signing them. I have not decided to go on signing them either, and those are not the same thing, and nobody on this floor is going to be told which of the two it is."**

He signed four sheets in the time it took her to walk back down that corridor and go through that door, and the pot took the pen back out of his hand exactly as it takes it back out of the other one, and the door of that room shut.

She had been in that corridor when he said it. Nobody is ever going to tell her that he said it in front of her, and he will never be told that she was there, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that takes a man at a sill as the one a thing was said to, and there is none that takes a woman as the one who came out of a room she had not come out of in four years.

---

The pot is a thing he made himself out of a tin in a winter before he came to that floor, and it is the only object in that room that anybody has ever repaired. The two pens in it are the same pen. He has never once known which of them he was going to take out, and he has never once cared, and that has been the arrangement of that pot for nineteen years, and there is not one person alive who could tell you which pen he signs with because there has never been a difference to tell.

What he does do, and does every working day of the year without once being asked for it and without once being thanked for it, is read the top of the sheet before he signs the foot of it. He reads the head, and the date at the head, and the line at the top, and the number. Then he turns the pen round to him and signs underneath. It is not a difficult thing to do nine hundred times a year. It is a difficult thing to do nine hundred times a year and never once find anything, and about nine times a year he finds something.

He had been at that sill nineteen years without anybody on that floor finding out one single thing about him, and that was not carelessness. It was the only arrangement he had ever found that worked.

---

The girl of about nineteen carried the armful past that sill about forty times in each direction and put it down on the stone at the end of every one of them, and she had been in that room when the pot took the pen back about nine hundred times between the second hour and about the fifth, and about nine of those were not what the person above him had written.

She has said one true thing out loud in a corridor off that room about four months ago and nobody answered it, and the only thing she had that was hers was not being asked, and she let that go in a month that is gone and it has not come back, and no form in this empire puts a person back where they were not asked.

She is nineteen. She is not going to be asked one question about a number, and neither of the two people at that sill said one word to her about the nine or about the two hands, and she said nothing back, and she carried the next armful past at about the same speed as the last one.

---

The ninth of them came down that sill at about the fourth hour.

He knew it before the girl put it down, because he reads the top of every sheet before he signs the foot of any of them, and he has read the top of every sheet for nineteen years, and that is the whole of the care he has ever taken with anything.

The writing above the foot of that sheet was not the writing of the person whose sheet it was. It was the writing of somebody about four inches taller and it came at the line from the left instead of from the right, and the number at the head of it was right and the date at the head of it was right, and everything between the head and the foot was a different hand's work.

The pot was at his right hand. There were two pens in it and he has never once known which of the two he was going to take and has never once cared, and that has been the arrangement of that pot for nineteen years.

He put his hand in and he took one out and it was the one his hand went to, and it was the one it has always gone to, and he signed the foot of that sheet with the hand that signs nine hundred feet a year, and the sheet went onto the armful, and the armful went down the corridor and out of that building one sheet at a time in the ordinary way.

It took no longer than the signing of a sheet.

He had made a decision. There is not one column in nine hundred buildings that will take him for it and there never is going to be one, and no form anywhere in this empire asks a man at a sill whether the ninth sheet of a year was the one he chose, and the girl of nineteen did not see him do it, and she will not be told and she will not be asked. The number went out at the end of that day at the end of that floor, the way it goes out every working day of that year, and it said nothing about any of it.

The difference between that and the day before was that before the fourth hour of that day he had not known he could, and after the fourth hour of that day he did.

---

About nine hundred sheets left that building that day, one at a time, and two hands anywhere in this empire could have put the mark at the foot of every one of them.

On a shelf under a window about two feet wide with the pane out of it stands a question with no full stop at the end of it, and her own name written at the foot of it, put there on purpose about eleven years ago. It is never going to be written out in any building in this empire and nobody is going to put her a second one of anything.

The window it stands under has no pane in it this month and did not have one last month, and has not had one for about four years. The sill of it takes the weather straight on and the paper on the shelf above it has gone the colour of the stone, and the person who wrote on it is fifty-three and keeps the minutes in the room at the back of that floor and came out of that room three times in about a year, and this was the third.

Nobody on that floor knows that she looked out of that window this morning, and she is not going to be told that anybody knows and she is not going to ask, and there is nothing on the shelf under it but the question and her name.
