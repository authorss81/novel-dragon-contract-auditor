# Chapter 0525: An Armful Put Down On A Stone And Not Picked Up Until A Man Had Finished Saying Something To The Flags, A Door That Did Not Open All Morning, And A Pen That Came Back Out Of A Pot In The Hand It Had Not Been Using

The pen goes into that pot and comes out of it and the pot has never once had an opinion about which hand put it in.

He is about sixty-one. Nineteen years is how long he has been the second hand at that sill, going back to when he was forty-two, and there is not a working sentence in a paragraph, and there has not been one since before a fire that other people went out of. For the whole of that time he has taken care that nobody standing on that floor ever found out, and that taking care is the only thing he has ever added to anything at all.

About nine hundred sheets go out of that building in a year with his mark at the foot of every one of them, and about nine of them in a year are not what the person above him wrote, and he signs those the same as the rest, and there is one man alive who knows which nine of them they are, and the knowing is on nothing at all and has been since the spring.

He is not one of the four in this matter who cannot get sense out of a paragraph and never has been, and that is a different thing from what is wrong with him and it is the only thing about the two that anybody on that floor could ever be shown.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the ninth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the second hour and about the fifth.

---

The door of that room did not open all morning.

The door opens on a corridor, and one side of it has a run of stone along the edge at about the height of a hand with the lane on the other side of the stone and nothing in between. Nineteen years of the same strip of stone have settled which side of it a person walks on without anybody ever settling it, and the girl of nineteen has never once been on the wet side of it in about a year.

The woman of about fifty-three keeps the minutes in there and has kept them for nineteen years and advises nobody in them and has not put a thing to that room in about four years. She came out into the corridor the first time in four years in a spring that is gone, and she has been out of it twice since, and this was the week she was not out of it at all, and there was nobody in that building who noticed the difference except a girl of nineteen with an armful in her arms.

---

The armful went past that sill about forty times in each direction and the girl of nineteen carried every one of them. She has been doing it for about a year and she says the number out loud at the end of every working day and nobody in that building has ever thanked her for the number.

She had not said one word out loud in that room in the year. She said one true thing in a corridor off it about four months ago and nobody answered her. The only thing she had that was hers had been not being asked, and she let that go in a month that is gone, and it has not come back. No form in this empire puts a person back where they were not asked.

---

She put the armful down on the stone at about the third hour and left it there.

An armful is a great many sheets squared and held against one side, and it goes down on the sill because a sill takes the weight of it flat and nothing else in the place does. It goes down and it stays where it goes down until somebody picks it up, and nobody in the place would notice it there except the person who put it down.

She had done it twice before, both times in the last three months, and both times she had picked it up again inside a minute and carried it back with her book shut against her side.

This time she left it on the stone and stood with her hands empty and her book shut under her arm and did not pick it up.

The man at the sill signed four sheets in that time and put the pen back in the pot after every one of them, and the pot handed it straight back, and he did not look at the armful and he did not look at her, and he has never once looked at either.

---

He said it at about half past the third hour, to the flags, at nobody, the way anything gets said out loud in a building where nothing is ever wanted of anybody.

The weather comes onto the sill whatever the season is doing outside it, and it has been arriving straight on the stone for about four years, and it comes onto the shelf beside the sill as well, and nothing has ever been fitted to the opening to stop it doing either. A man working in it for four years stops noticing the cold about a quarter of an hour in, the way anybody stops noticing anything they cannot get away from.

"**I went back to the left hand once about a fortnight ago and I have not been able to work out which morning it was, and the pot took it back off me the same way it takes it back off the other, and I have settled that I am going to keep going back.**"

It was the sixth thing he has said out loud in that building in nineteen years. The fifth of the six was about these two hands and the four before that were not sentences about anything at all, and he was not going to say any of them again and nobody on that floor was going to be thanked for the not asking.

The girl of about nineteen gave him nothing back. The armful was on the stone where she had left it, her own book was shut against her, and the pen was on the sill within reach of her hand, and there was a stretch of that hour in which she might have put something on the page. She did not, and nobody on that floor is ever going to know either way.

---

"You have not written it down," she said, after a while, and it was not a question and she did not make it one.

"It has nowhere to go. To enter a man at a sill anywhere in this empire as the one who started doing a thing differently on a particular morning, somebody would first have to invent a heading to go over him, and any such heading could only be about that morning, and none exists. It has not been refused by anybody in this building. It is simply not there."

"Somebody wrote it in about four months ago. The last time she came out of that room she said there was no such word and she said it to you and you said it after her and then neither of you said one word about it again."

"She has not come out of that room this week and I am not going to say a thing to a shut door, and I have not asked her for it and I am not going to, and you are not going to tell me she was in the corridor in a spring and neither is anybody else in this building."

---

She lifted the armful off the stone and took it back into that room with her book shut against her side, and the pot went on taking the pen back out of it exactly as it had done all morning.

A thing which takes a pen and gives it straight back is a thing a person can learn in an afternoon, and it took him a winter and a second tin to make one, and he has not moved it since, and there is nothing about the arrangement anybody ever needed to think about twice.

The number went out at the end of that day at the end of that floor and nobody thanked her for it, and nobody ever has, and she said it anyway.

On the shelf under that window with the pane out of it a question stands, with no full stop at the end of it and her own name written at the foot of it. It is not going to be copied out in any building in this empire, and nobody is going to put a second question to the woman of about fifty-three about it. The girl of nineteen did not say one word about any of that either and is not going to.
