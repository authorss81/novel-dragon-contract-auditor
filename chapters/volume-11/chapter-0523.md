# Chapter 0523: A Pen Taken Out Of The Middle Of An Open Book And Put Down Beside It, A Woman Who Came In Twenty Minutes Early And Said Nothing, And The First Morning In Four Years That Nothing On That Floor Was Worth Wanting

Work on that floor is done between the second hour and the sixth and at no other time. Plenty of people go down those flights with something in their hands that has nothing whatever to do with the business of that room, and no one in that building has ever sent any of them up.

Marn Ottery is thirty-four and has been at the far end of the boards for eleven years and has not gone out of that room once in the eleven of them, and she has put the shape of a thing out loud in it four times and said before the first of them that there would not be a fifth.

Tamsin Rook is twenty-four and has stood at her own end for two years, and for the whole of those two years she has been in that room a full twenty minutes before the hour on every working morning of them.

At the far end of those two joined tables there is a clerk of about twenty-nine who has been at that end of the room for four years with a book lying open in front of her, and she has said one true thing out loud in the whole of them, standing, to somebody beside her, and went back to her book and has not gone back to it.

It is the second day of the first week of the ninth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the third hour.

---

She came in early the way she comes in early, and she went to her end of the boards, and she squared an edge that did not need squaring, and about as long as a sheet takes to be squared after that she squared it again.

Marn Ottery watched her do it and said nothing for about as long as the first one had taken.

The clerk at the far end of the two joined tables had her face down over the book, which is where her face is on every working morning of the four years, and the pen lay in the middle of the open page, and it has lain in that same place on that page every working morning since long before the woman beside her came to that floor.

Nobody on that floor waited on anybody. That is the arrangement and it has been the arrangement for eleven years and it is the only reason that floor still works.

---

Tamsin Rook did not say it.

She has said three true things out loud in that room in about three months, to the boards, in the ordinary way, on an ordinary morning, and this morning was the fourth of those mornings and she got to about the middle of the fourth hour and she went on with the boards instead.

The clerk of about twenty-nine was the only person on that floor who could have told the difference. She has been looking at the back of the other one's shoulders for four years and she knows what a morning in that room sounds like, and this morning was quieter than the three before it and quieter than the three before those.

Nobody was told. Nobody asked. There is nowhere on that floor at which the difference between a woman who said something and a woman who did not could be put to anybody, and there never has been.

---

"You have gone quiet," Marn Ottery said, to the boards.

"I have not said anything out loud this month and I am not going to, and I have not decided anything, and you are not going to be thanked for the not deciding and you are not going to be asked about it."

"I did not say anything about the deciding."

"You said the word quiet at me and that is the whole of the thing, and it is the first sentence anybody has said to me this month and it is a sentence about me and not about anything I have done, and I would rather it had been about the boards."

Marn Ottery set both hands down flat on the boards and kept them where they were.

"I am not going to help you with that and I am not going to tell you to leave off either, and I have put the shape of a thing out loud in this room four times and there is not going to be a fifth, and what I have just said to you is not a shape of anything and I am not going to be thanked for it."

---

The clerk of about twenty-nine wrote the day's line.

She wrote it the way she has written it every working morning for four years: the date, then the hour, then how many sheets came out of the case. It takes about a minute, and afterwards the pen goes back into the middle of the open page and lies across it until the end of the hour.

A book is a record. A record is the one thing in that room that other people can be shown. Everything else on that floor can be looked at and gone and none of it can be produced, and eleven years of nobody wanting anything out of anybody is worth exactly nothing to anybody, and that is the arrangement.

She had been at the far end of two joined tables for four years writing down, every working morning, that she had been at the far end of two joined tables at that hour. She had not once thought of it as a thing she was doing about herself, because it was never a thing she was doing about anything. It was a line in a book and the book was the work.

It is the only place in this matter where she is on the record every single morning and cannot be put anywhere else at all, and it is a record she keeps with her own hand, and it does not care in the least whether a true thing was said on the floor it is written down about.

---

She thought about it for about as long as a sheet takes to be signed.

Then she put down what she was doing and picked the pen up out of the middle of the open page, and she put it down on the wood beside the book instead, squared along the edge of the table, and she squared that too, and it was straight, and she left it there.

A pen lying in the middle of an open page is a pen that is not going to be used. She has known that for four years without ever having had to look at one.

---

The two of them at the boards went on with the fourth hour and the fifth and neither of them looked over at the far end of the two joined tables, and the clerk of about twenty-nine went on with the morning with her hands on the wood on either side of a book and a pen lying beside it where anybody could reach it.

Nobody said one word to her about it that day. She came in the next working morning twenty minutes before the hour of a morning when nothing had been said at that floor the day before, and the pen was where she had left it, and she did not put it back in the middle of the page.

---

At the sixth hour that floor was shut. The case went in behind the boards and four things went up onto the wall above it, one to a nail each, and the fourth of those four has been in the same hand for about four years and is the one none of the three of them at those boards can give an account of.

Underneath that wall is a drawer, shut since before the woman at the near end came to that floor, with four things in it, and two of the four have never once been taken out and looked at by anybody. The box under the far end of those boards is not the case and has not been opened and has not shifted an inch.

The pen stayed beside the book and not in it, and nothing in this empire has a form for a person who set a pen down beside a book rather than across the page of it, and nothing in it has a form for a person who left it lying there.
