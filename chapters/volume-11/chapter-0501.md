# Chapter 0501: A True Thing Said To A Set Of Boards, A Man With A Satchel At The Far End Of Them, And One Woman Who Saw

The second hour of a morning at that counter, with the case out and the boards up, and the hours there running from the second to the sixth. About four hundred of the people who come down that one stair in a year are carrying a different object altogether.

Marn Ottery. Thirty-four. Eleven years at the other end of those boards, not a lawyer, the oldest of the three, and the only one of the three who has never once left that floor. She has said the shape of a thing out loud in that room four times and nobody on that floor has put one thing back to her about any of them, and she said before the first of the four that she was not going to say a fifth, and nothing since has given her a reason to say it.

Behind her the wall board carries four items and not one of them a time. The fourth of the four is in her own hand and it has been on that board about four years, and none of the three of them can account for it, and she put it there herself, and she has not asked herself about it and is not going to. Under that board a drawer has been shut for eleven years with four things in it, two of which have never once been opened by anybody. The knife that is in this room is in a different drawer, and she has never said which, and nobody has ever come up here to find out.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the third month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the second hour.

---

The man with the fair hand is on the list four days a season and he is on no floor this week, and the list is the only thing in this building that anybody could enter a man on, and it has held the same four days since before she came to this end of it.

That was the morning's difficulty. She had got as far as the middle of the fourth hour with nothing at all wanted of anybody, which is the ordinary condition of that room and has been the whole of the arrangement of it for eleven years, and this is the only room in this matter where that can be said at all.

At the far end of the two joined tables the clerk of about twenty-nine had a book open in front of her and a pen lying in the middle of it, where it lies every working morning of the four years she has been at that end. She had said one true thing out loud in those four years and it was said to a person standing beside her, and nobody has taken it up since and nobody is going to.

It stands where it stands, and it is worth the standing: **a room with one person in it is not a room nobody waits in. It is a room one person waits in.** Neither of them has said that in this room. The clerk said it once, standing, to somebody who was beside her, and then went back to her book and never went back to it.

Marn stood at the boards for about as long as a sheet takes to be squared and worked the rest of it out standing up.

The number of people who go up that stair in a week is the same number as the number of people who come down it having not been spoken to, and it is that number every week, and it has been for as long as she has been on that floor. About four hundred of them come down those flights in a year, and not one of them has been told anything on any of the four floors and not one of them could produce a line that said so. **The two numbers are the same number for a reason and they are not the same number by accident, and the reason is that this is the only building in this matter where nothing is wanted of anybody, and a thing nothing is wanted of cannot be wanted twice.**

She did not say it out loud. She has four shapes of things she has said out loud in that room and there is not going to be a fifth one, and this was not a fifth one anyway, because it was a fact about a stair and not about a person.

"You have it backwards," Tamsin Rook said, from her own end, without looking up. "**A room where nobody waits is not a room people come to. It is a room people come to and cannot put anything down in. The waiting is the only reason the number is the same twice.**"

"I did not say it out loud."

"You said it with your hands. I have been at this end of these boards for two years and I know what your hands do, and you have not looked at that stair once this morning. I have not said one word about it and I am not going to, and nobody is going to ask me what I saw, and nobody is going to thank me for having seen it either, and now go back to being a person nobody wants anything out of."

Marn went back to being a person nobody wanted anything out of. She did not put the sentence down and she did not pick it up, and it is still there, and Tamsin Rook has not repeated a word of it and is not going to.

---

He came up the last flight at about the fourth hour and stopped on the fourth step from the top, where he stops, and then came the rest of it and set the satchel down on the counter at the far end of those boards.

Eleven years of four times a week. Nobody has ever told him what is in it and he has never asked and it has never once been a courtesy between him and anybody in this building. He stood with his back half turned to the middle of that floor and squared the strap on his shoulder the way he does, and the strap went into the shoulder it goes in, and Marn went on with the boards and did not look at him and did not stop.

There was a piece of paper on the boards at that end. There is a printed strip along the top of every blank that comes out of that case and the same four things are set along that strip, and not one of the four is a name, and about four hundred of those go out of that case in a year in a hand that is not doing anything anybody asked it to do. The box under the far end of those boards holds about four hundred of them in total, bought in a quantity, never filled in by anybody, the lid not off it in six years, and the box is not the case and nobody in this room has ever been in a position to say why that needs saying.

---

Tamsin Rook was at her end of the boards and she said a thing out loud, to the boards, in the ordinary way, the way a person says a thing out loud in a room at about the middle of a morning when nothing is being asked of anybody.

Tamsin Rook is twenty-four and has stood at that end for two years, and in two years nobody on that floor has ever wanted one single thing out of her.

"**About four hundred of these go down those stairs in a year, and not one person who is carrying one of them knows what is printed along the top of it. I have stood at this end of these boards for two years and I have not told one of them and I am not going to start. There is nothing on any form in this building that would let me tell them, and there is nothing anywhere else either.**"

She said it to the boards and she went on squaring the edge of them, and that was all of it, and it did not sound like a thing that had cost her anything, because it had not, at the time.

---

Marn saw it.

The clerk of about twenty-nine had her head down over the two joined tables and was not waiting on anybody, because nobody on that floor has ever waited on her and that is the whole of why she has got away with it. The man with the satchel was standing at the far end of the boards with his shoulder to the room and his hands on the strap. And Tamsin had said a true thing out loud, in the ordinary way, with a second person in the room.

Marn was the only one of the three of them who had both of those in the same field of view, and she had it for about as long as a sheet takes to be squared, and she did not say one word to anybody about it and she is not going to.

Noticing is not a form. Noticing is not a party. Noticing is not on anything, and there is no column anywhere in this empire in which a person is entered as the one who noticed that two people were in a room, and the reason there is not one is not an oversight.

She got on with the fourth hour and the fifth.

Nobody was thanked. Nobody was forgiven. Nobody was sent for, and nothing was resolved, and the four items were still on that board at the end of the day and the fourth of them is still the one none of the three of them can account for.

---

When the sixth hour came the case was put away behind the boards, and the four of them went up onto the wall above it on their own nails, and the door was shut behind that.

The man with the satchel went down four flights with a thing in it that nobody in this empire has ever told him about. He did not pause anywhere on the way, and nobody went after him and nobody was sent, and he is not going to be found.

A true thing was said out loud in that room this morning and a second person was standing in it, and neither of those two has said one word to the other about it, and neither of them knows that the other one was there. There is no form anywhere in this empire in which either of them could be entered, and there is nowhere for any of it to be kept.
