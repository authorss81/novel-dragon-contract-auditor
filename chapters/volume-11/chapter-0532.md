# Chapter 0532: A Woman Of Thirty-Four Going Out Of A Room She Has Not Gone Out Of In Eleven Years At About The Fifth Hour, A Woman Of Twenty-Four At The Near End Of Those Boards, And A Floor That Was Shut At The Sixth Without Her

That floor opens at the second hour and shuts at the sixth, and about four hundred people a year come down those four flights with something in their hands that has nothing whatever to do with the business of that room.

Not one of them has ever been sent up them by anybody in that building and not one of them is going to be this month.

Marn Ottery is thirty-four and she has not gone out of that room once in eleven years. Four times in that time she has said the shape of a thing out loud in it, and standing up, before the first of the four, she told that floor another one was not coming.

Tamsin Rook is twenty-four and has been at her own end of those boards for two years, and she is in that room a full twenty minutes ahead of the hour on every working morning of it. She has not been able to work out one morning in two years on which she began doing that, and she is not going to.

At the far end of the two joined tables a clerk of about twenty-nine has been sitting for four years, and the pen has been lying on the wood beside her book for about two months now, squared to the edge of the table, where anybody could reach it.

It is the second day of the second week of the eleventh month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fifth hour.

---

He came up at about the fourth, four times a week for eleven years, and this time he did not put the bag down on the wood at the far end of the boards. He stood with his shoulder to the room and both hands on the strap and he went down again at about a quarter past, and nobody on that floor said one word to him either way, and the strap was not squared and the strap has not been squared every time for about two months.

Tamsin Rook noticed the strap and is not going to say so out loud to anybody, and she has not decided to notice it and she is not going to decide that either, and nobody is going to be thanked for the not deciding and nobody is going to be asked about it at the end of anything.

---

At about the five o'clock she put both hands flat on the boards.

They were flat on them for about as long as a sheet takes to be squared, and then she took them off, and she came round the end of the boards, and she went out of that room at about the fifth hour of an ordinary morning and down four flights and out of that building, and she was not in that room at the sixth hour, and the other two of them did the sixth hour without her.

She has never told anybody in that building where she went and nobody in that building is going to be told, and she has not asked anybody for anything on the way past them and nobody has put anything in front of her, and there is no form anywhere in this empire in which a woman of thirty-four is entered as the one who left a room she had not left in eleven years.

"You told this floor, standing up, before the first of them, that there would not be another."

"There has not been one in about a year and there is not going to be one now."

"You have not come back into this room for anything."

"I have not come back into this room for anything, and if you had asked me where I had gone I would not have told you, and that would not have been a refusal, and you are not going to ask me and I am not going to want you to. **The case goes in behind those boards at the sixth hour and those four things go up onto the wall above it at the same time every working day, and I have watched that happen about four thousand times from the inside of that room, and this morning I got as far as the door and went out of it, and nobody in this building is going to be told which way I turned at the bottom of those four flights.**"

"I have not asked you," Tamsin Rook said, "and I have not done anything for you, and I would like it noticed that those are two separate things and that you are about to say thank you for the first of them on the strength of the second."

They looked at each other across eleven feet of boards for about as long as it takes a pen to be picked up.

"I was not going to," Marn Ottery said. "Four of those are on record in this room and not one of them was ever thanked for, and I am not going to be thanked for eleven paces down a stair either. That is the whole of what I came down for."

---

The clerk of about twenty-nine had her face down over the two joined tables for the whole of that hour and she did not look up when the door of that room opened, and she has not looked up on any working morning of the four years she has been at that end of it.

What changed at the far end of those tables was that her hands stopped for about as long as a sheet takes to be signed, once, and then went on.

She is on the record every single working morning of the four years, in a line she writes with her own hand, and that line says the date and the hour and the number of sheets that came out of the case, and it says nothing whatever about who was standing at the boards, and it is going to say the same thing on the next working morning of that year whether the woman who owns the room is in it or not.

Nobody is going to tell her that the door of that room opened, and nobody is going to tell the woman of twenty-four either, and the two of them did not say one word about it that day or the next, and there is nowhere on that floor at which a thing about a door could be put to a person.

---

The door of that room stood open from about the fifth hour to about the sixth and nobody in that building walked past it and looked in.

The case went in behind the boards at the sixth hour and four things went up onto the wall above it, one to a nail each, and the lowest of those four has been in one hand for about four years and is the one none of the three of them at those boards can give an account of. Underneath that wall is a drawer and it has been shut for eleven years and there are four things in it, and two of the four have never once been taken out and looked at by anybody, and the knife is in some other drawer and not one of the three of them has ever said which.

The pen was lying on the wood beside the book and not in it, and it was lying there at the sixth hour, and nobody on that floor is going to be told which way a woman of thirty-four turned at the bottom of four flights.
