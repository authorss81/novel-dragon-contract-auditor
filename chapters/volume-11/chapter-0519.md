# Chapter 0519: A Question On A Shelf Under A Window With No Pane In It, A Book That Is A Record, And An Armful Put Down On A Stone And Picked Up Again

The woman of about fifty-three has kept the minutes of that room since she was thirty-four. In nineteen years she has not advised anybody in it, and for about four years she has put nothing to anybody in it at all. She announced that before she spoke the last time she came out into the corridor, and she wanted it on the record that she announced it, and that room has no standing at which anything is put on a record.

She is fifty-three and not fifty-five. There are four people in this matter who cannot get sense out of a paragraph and she has never been one of them, and the man at that sill has not, and a man of about fifty-five with a chain at the end of a lane has not, and the girl who keeps the minutes under her has not either and is not going to.

Her own question is on the shelf under that window. It is still a question. It still carries no full stop, and her own name is at the foot of it, and it is never going to be written out in any building in this empire, and nobody is going to be asked about it a second time.

It is the second day of the first week of the eighth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the second hour.

---

She came out into the corridor at about the second hour and stopped at the end of that sill, and about nine hundred sheets a year were going out of that building with his mark at the foot of them, and neither of the two people at that sill was looking at her.

The girl of about nineteen came out under an armful at about the same moment and stopped where she always stops.

They had not been in that corridor together at that hour for about a month, and the last time they had been, the woman of fifty-three had said a true thing about a book and the girl had not answered it.

---

"**I have worked out why nobody has ever come to me," the woman of about fifty-three said, "and I worked it out about a week ago, at that table, and I am saying it out loud now because there is a second person standing in this corridor and neither of us is going to do anything about that. Nineteen years of nobody wanting a thing out of me, and I put a question on that shelf on purpose so that somebody would want one, and nobody came, and I have spent eleven years calling that a protection. It is not a protection. It is the same thing it is in every other room in this matter, and a person nobody can want anything from is a person a thing can be put in front of, and the day somebody works that out I am the cheapest person in this building and I have been it since I was thirty-four.**"

She said it to the sill and not to the girl and she said it once and she did not say it again.

The girl of about nineteen did not answer it.

She stood at the end of that sill with the armful on the stone in front of her and the book shut under her arm and she looked at the pen and the pot and the pen again, and then she did the thing she has not done in about three months, and she put the armful down on the stone and left it there.

"The armful is heavy," she said. "That is all I have got to say about it and I am going to take it back into the room."

"You are not going to say anything about what she has just said."

"I am not going to say anything about what she has just said, and she is not going to say it again, and I am going to say the armful is heavy and that is the whole of the hour."

---

She lifted the armful off the stone again and went back into the room with her book shut against her side, and the pot went on handing the pen back to a man who was not looking at her.

"You came out of that room at the third hour four times in a month in the spring," the girl said, from the doorway. "I counted them on the armfuls and I have never written the number down and I am not going to."

"I did not know you counted them and I am not going to be thanked for the four of them and I am not going to be asked about them."

"I know what you are not going to be asked about. I said the armful is heavy and that is the whole of the hour."

---

Nobody wrote it down.

**What has happened to that girl in the last two months is that she has been in a room when a true thing was said, twice, and neither time was she asked anything, and there is no form anywhere in this empire in which a person who carries an armful between a room and a sill can be entered as the one who was standing at the end of it, and there is no form in which she can be entered as the one who was not. She gave up the only thing she had that was hers, which was not being asked, in a month that is gone, and what she has got now instead of that is that nobody on that floor can put a distance between her and either morning. That is not a thing anybody did to her. It is a thing she did by carrying an armful forty times a day.**

---

The woman of about fifty-three went back into the room and sat down and did not come out into that corridor again that morning, and she is not going to be asked about the shelf by anybody, and the question on it is still carrying no full stop and it is still not going to be printed out in any building.

Nine hundred sheets left that building that day, one at a time, and every one of them had a mark at the foot of it that two hands in this empire could have made. The armful went past that sill about forty times in each direction. At the end of the day she said the number out loud at the end of that floor, and nobody thanked her for saying it, and nobody ever has.
