# Chapter 0539: A Chair That Has Stood Against Nothing At All For About Two Years Dragged Into The Middle Of A Room And Turned Round To Face Four Feet Of Bare Plaster, And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Standing Beside It And Not Sitting Down In It

It is a room taken by the week at the top of a house, and the landlord has never once been through the door of it in about two years, and has not sent anybody, and does not ask. The window looks at the back of another house and has done for the whole of that time, and about four feet of plaster stands beside that window with nothing on any part of it and one nail driven into it, and his coat is on that nail.

Marek Kest. Thirty-eight. He stands in no register in this city, and a rank of his own stopped standing on him about a year ago on one line of paper he wrote himself, and there is not one column in any building in this empire that will take a word over the top of his name.

In the inside pocket of that coat, one on each side of a seam, there are two things. In about two years neither has come out, and no hand in this city has been into that coat, and it is not a pocket anybody has ever had a reason to go into.

It is the second day of the first week of the first month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the third hour.

---

The chair had been standing against nothing at all for about two years.

That is the whole of what it is for. There is a table and the table is a table. There is a stool and he has used the stool. There is a bed against one wall and he sleeps in the bed. And there is a chair with its back to about four feet of plaster that has nothing on any part of it, and in about two years he has not once sat down in it, and he has not been able to work out one morning when he decided that.

He got it at about the third hour.

It dragged on the boards. It is a heavy thing with one leg shorter than the other and it does that, and he let it drag, and the noise of it went across a floor that has never had a noise like that on it in about two years. He brought it into the middle of that floor and turned it round about ninety degrees on its own leg, and the boards complained under it, so that its back was to the window and its face was to the plaster.

The boards in a room where nothing makes heat are the loudest thing in the room, and a chair with one leg shorter than another puts a hitch into a floor that has had nothing but a boot on it, and the hitch came three times in about ninety degrees and he let all three of them happen without stopping the chair.

The boards under it were not the colour of the boards round it. Two years of dust goes on a floor in a room where nothing is made heat, and the shape that chair had been standing in was clean underneath, a rectangle about as long as a man and a foot and a half across, and it stayed on the boards when the chair came off them, and it is the second mark in that room and he had not known it was there.

Then he stood beside it and did not sit down in it.

---

A thing with a use in a room can be produced.

That is the whole of what he has. A table can be produced and a bed can be produced and a stool can be produced, and anybody who wanted to know anything about a man in this city could be shown a table and a bed and a stool and say that a man slept here, and it would not matter in the slightest that they would not be able to say anything else, because they would not have to.

A room can be produced too, and a room is easier to produce than a man, because a room does not have to have a person in it to be shown and a man has to be in it and agree, and there has not been one hour in about two years in which anybody could have been shown a man in the room and had him be a party to being shown.

A thing with no use in it cannot be produced. There is no way in this empire to put a man into a column as the one who owned a chair he had never sat in, and there is no form that would take a man for turning a piece of furniture round at three in the afternoon, and a piece of furniture is not a person, and the whole of what a person can be warned about is a person.

That chair was the only thing in that room nobody could ever be shown anything about. He had had it for about two years. He had never once done anything with it. He did not know until that morning that it was the thing he had.

A name stood once at the foot of a leaf and a hand that was not his mother's went through it, and what belonged underneath that line is gone out of this empire, and the claim that went out of it under that name went the same way on the same day. He has one of those two things and has never had the other. Neither of them can go on four feet of plaster. He was not going to put anything on that plaster, and he had told himself for about two years that he was not going to stand a chair in front of it either, and he had been wrong about the second of those, and it took him about two minutes to do it.

A chair standing against a wall is furniture. A chair standing ninety degrees out in the middle of a floor with its face on a bare wall is a decision, and a decision has a person in it, and about two minutes of a working morning is what it takes to shift furniture in a cold room with nobody in it to see it done.

---

He said it out loud at about the fourth hour, to a room with nobody in it, and the room took it the way it has taken everything else he has ever said in it, which is by not being there.

Plaster at the height of a man's mouth takes a voice and gives back about as much of it as the thickness of the plaster can carry, which on a wall about four feet wide is a room with a chest in it, and there is nobody standing at the far side of it, and that has been the arrangement of the fourth hour in the room for about two years.

"**There is a chair in this room that has no use in it and there is about four feet of wall that has nothing on it, and a thing with no use in it cannot be shown to anybody, and that is the only kind of thing I have ever had. I have turned it round to face it and I am not going to sit down in it and I am not going to put it back.**"

It was not a resolution and it was not going to be one by the sixth hour, and he was not going to say it a second time either, and there is nobody in this house he could say it to.

He stood beside that chair until the light on the plaster had moved a hand's width. Then he went over to the bed and sat down on it, which is what a man in that room does at that hour, and he looked at the chair from there for the rest of that hour and did not get up again.

He had turned the chair with its back to the window.

He noticed that at about the fourth hour and a half, and he noticed what it was, and it took him a moment to be honest about why he had not noticed it at the third hour. The window looks at the back of another house, and over the wall at the back of that house there is a back room, and there is a person in that room for about an hour in the morning and then there is nobody in it at all for the rest of the day.

He had gone and stood at that window a fortnight ago for about four times as long as he meant to, and it was the only thing he has wanted in about two years, and this morning he had dragged a chair across the floor of that room and turned it round so that he would not be looking at the window any more.

He sat on the bed with his back to the glass and looked at about four feet of plaster instead, and the plaster has nothing on it, and there is a nail in it with a coat on it, and he had chosen the plaster.

---

A woman who lives two floors down in this house came up those four flights about two months ago, said one true thing out loud on the other side of his door, and told him that was the last of it. He did not open the door then and he has not opened it since, and that stair has not stopped on his floor once in two months, and he has not gone down it, and nobody in that house is going to be told one word about any part of it.

There was a whole arrangement there for about three months. It cost her a true thing said to a shut door and it cost him a half inch of wood with his hand flat on the inside of it, and neither of them was thanked for any part of it and neither of them can be shown having agreed to it, and it has stopped now because she said it would and she was right, and there is nowhere in this empire that either of them could put the fact of it.

What he had not got to, in that hour, was the other half of it, and the other half came at about the fifth hour and it was this.

Every other object in that room is a thing you could be shown. And a chair in the middle of a floor, turned round to face a wall, on its own leg, with a clean rectangle of boards behind it and a drag mark going out from it in a line, is not an object in a room. That is a thing a person did. He had been living for about two years in a room that could be shown to anybody and could be said nothing about, and at about the third hour of that morning he put the first mark in it, and there is nobody in this empire who can enter him for it, and the mark is still in the room.

He was not going to put the chair back. That was the decision, and it was the whole of what the morning did, and he made it about five times over, and it cost him the only kind of room he had ever had.

He did not go out of that door and along that street and buy anything anywhere in this city. He did not go up a stair, he did not go to the end of a lane, he did not send for anybody, nobody sent for him, and nobody told him one thing at all this month.

The stair went up at about the sixth hour and came down again at about the seventh. It did not stop on his floor and it did not go along it either; nobody on it came down as far as his door, and he heard the whole of it go, and he stayed where he was.

The chair is standing in the middle of that floor with its back to the window and its face to the plaster, and the bed is where it has always been, and the table is against the wall under nothing, and there is no fire in that room and there is not going to be one.
