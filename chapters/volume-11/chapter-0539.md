# Chapter 0539: A Chair That Has Stood Against Nothing At All For About Two Years Dragged Into The Middle Of A Room And Turned Round To Face Four Feet Of Bare Wall, And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Standing Beside It And Not Sitting Down In It

He takes that room by the week and the landlord has never once been through the door of it. The window looks at the back of another house and has done for about two years, and about four feet of plaster stands beside that window with nothing on any part of it and one nail driven into it, and his coat is on that nail.

Marek Kest. Thirty-eight. He stands in no register in this city, and a rank of his own stopped standing on him about a year ago on one line of paper he wrote himself, and there is not one column in any building in this empire that will take a word over the top of his name.

In the inside pocket of that coat there are two things, one on each side of a seam, and neither of them has ever been out of that pocket in this building or in any other building in this city, and no hand in this city has been into that coat and no hand is going to.

It is the second day of the first week of the first month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the third hour.

---

The chair had been standing against nothing at all for about two years.

That is the whole of what it is for. There is a table and the table is a table. There is a stool and he has used the stool. There is a bed against one wall and he sleeps in the bed. And there is a chair with its back to about four feet of plaster that has nothing on any part of it, and in about two years he has not once sat down in it, and he has not been able to work out one morning when he decided that.

He got it at about the third hour and it dragged on the boards, and he let it drag, and he brought it into the middle of that floor and turned it round about ninety degrees on its own leg so that its back was to the window and its face was to the plaster.

Then he stood beside it and did not sit down in it.

---

What he had worked out about that chair, standing up, in the ordinary way a person works a thing out in a room with nobody in it, is this.

A thing with a use in a room can be produced. A table can be produced and a bed can be produced and a stool can be produced, and anybody who wanted to know anything about a man in this city could be shown a table and a bed and a stool and say that a man slept here. A thing with no use in it cannot be produced, because there is no way in this empire to put a man into a column as the one who owned a chair he had never sat in, and there is no form that would take a man for turning a piece of furniture round at three in the afternoon.

That chair is the only thing in this room that nobody could ever be shown anything about, and he has had it for about two years, and he has never once done anything with it, and he did not know until this morning that it was the thing he had.

A name stood once at the foot of a leaf and a hand that was not his mother's went through it, and what belonged underneath that line is gone out of this empire, and the claim that went out of it under that name went the same way on the same day. He has one of those two things and has never had the other. Neither of them can go on four feet of plaster, and he is not going to put anything on that plaster, and he is not going to stand a chair in front of it either.

---

He said it out loud at about the fourth hour, to a room with nobody in it, and the room took it the way it has taken everything else he has ever said in it, which is by not being there.

"**There is a chair in this room that has no use in it and there is about four feet of wall that has nothing on it, and a thing with no use in it cannot be shown to anybody, and that is the only kind of thing I have ever had. I have turned it round to face it and I am not going to sit down in it and I am not going to put it back.**"

It was not a resolution and it was not going to be one by the sixth hour, and he is not going to say it a second time either, and there is nobody in this house he could say it to.

---

He stood beside that chair for about as long as a sheet takes to be signed and then he went and sat down on the bed, because that is what he does, and he looked at the chair from there for the rest of that hour without getting up again.

A woman who lives two floors down in this house came up those four flights about two months ago, said one true thing out loud on the other side of his door, and told him that was the last of it. He did not open the door then and he has not opened it since, and that stair has not stopped on his floor once in two months, and he has not gone down it, and nobody in that house is going to be told one word about any part of it.

There was a whole arrangement there for about three months. It cost her a true thing said to a shut door and it cost him a half inch of wood with his hand flat on the inside of it, and neither of them was thanked for any part of it and neither of them can be shown having agreed to it, and it has stopped now because she said it would and she was right, and there is nowhere in this empire that either of them could put the fact of it.

---

He did not go out of that door and along that street and buy anything anywhere in this city. He did not go to the top of any stair in it and he did not go to the end of any lane and he did not send for anybody and nobody sent for him and nobody told him one thing at all this month.

The stair went up at about the sixth hour and came down again at about the seventh without stopping on his floor, and there was nobody on it who had come down as far as his door, and he heard it go, and he did not open the door.

The chair is standing in the middle of that floor with its back to the window and its face to the plaster, and the bed is where it has always been, and the table is against the wall under nothing, and there is no fire in that room and there is not going to be one.
