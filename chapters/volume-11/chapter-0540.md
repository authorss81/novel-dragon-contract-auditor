# Chapter 0540: A Chain At The Front Of A Bench That Nobody Put A Hand On For About A Month, A Foreman Whose Hand Was Flat On The Wood Four Inches Away From It, And A Lamp That Came On By Itself Over Two People At The Back Of A Shed

The chain was across the front of that bench where it goes every working day of the year, and it had been across the front of that bench every working day of the eleven years he has been sitting behind it.

His hands were in his lap.

It is the second day of the second week of the first month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour.

---

Halla Wray had her hand flat on the wood at the near end of that bench, about four inches off the chain, and she had been putting it there about nine mornings a month for two months, and a class can put her back at the back of that bench without anybody lifting a pen, and she has never once asked anybody to stop her doing it and nobody ever offered.

Nine mornings a month is about four working days out of six, and a foreman who has worked out which days those are has worked out something nobody asked her for, and she had done it on purpose at the back of a bench over two months and she had not got as far as deciding which of the two ends of the bench she would do it at until the second morning.

"That chain has had your hand on it every working morning for eleven years," she said, to the end wall, in the ordinary way. "It has had your hand on it up to the third link and no further, and I have watched that from the back of that bench for two months, and this month it has not had it at all. I have not asked you about it and I am not, and I would like it noticed that I noticed."

"I have not stopped touching it because of anything you have done and I am not going to tell you what I have stopped touching it for, and it is not going to be touched this month."

"You are not going to say what it is for."

"I am not going to say what it is for. You are not going to be thanked for noticing and you are not going to be asked about it, and there is nowhere standing at the end of this lane at which either of those could be put, and there never has been."

"You have gone a month with your hands in your lap."

"I have."

"That is not a thing a man does at a bench."

"No," he said. "It is not."

"Then what is it a thing a man does."

"I have told you what I am not going to tell you and you have not asked me for it, and I have not worked out how to do it, and those are two different things and neither of them changes this month."

"In the fourth month of that year you worked out something about this bench in about nine words and you said it to the floor with a girl four feet behind me, and I have never said one word about it since and I am not going to now. You have said four of those in about a year and I have never once answered one of them, and I have said two true things out loud in that bay in about two years, and the second of them was in that fourth month, and this month I have not said one at all."

"You have counted them."

"I have worked in a shed for eleven years. You do not count them."

---

The chain is a job.

That is the whole of what it is and it took him about a month to find out that he had known it for eleven years without ever having had to think about it. It goes the length of the stone and comes back, and it is worked with a hand, and the hand knows how far is how far without being told, and a man can do that for eleven years and never once think about what he is doing, and then not do it, and find the whole of the morning standing there empty with his hands in his lap.

A pair of hands in a lap is not a thing a man can hold still at for a working morning. They want about nine minutes, and then they want something to be resting on, and then they want to be put somewhere the body can get at them without the body having to decide to get at them, and a lap is the last of those and he tried it for about an hour and worked his way down through the two above it and got to the edge of the stone and stopped there.

He had nothing else. There was nothing else in that bay that was his to do. The sheets that go out go out on other people's hands and the looking is hers and the bundle at the far end had been cleared away weeks ago, and so he sat at the front of a bench in a shed about nine foot by eleven for a whole ordinary day and did not do one thing.

A working morning with nothing in it is about the third hour long and the rest of it is waiting, and waiting in a shed is not standing still, because there is about nine people working and one of them is the man who is not. He got through the second half of it by getting up twice, once for the water and once to look at the far end of the stone the way anybody does about the fourth hour, and he came back to the front of the bench both times in about nine seconds.

The two of seventeen worked. The foreman worked at the far end with one hand on the wood. The lane went on outside the door. About the fifth hour somebody's boots went past and did not stop. The bracket was not on and would not be on until the seventh hour.

He was, for one working morning, a person in a room rather than a man at a bench. There is not one form in this empire in which a foreman is entered as the one who put her hand on the front of her own bay, and not one in which a man at the front of a bench is entered as the one who sat at it with his hands in his lap for a day. A class cannot put either of them back where they have been, because neither of them has been anywhere else.

---

At the far end of that bench there were two of seventeen, and they were on the terms they are on, and neither of them has been spoken to by anybody in that lane in a long time and neither of them is this month.

The girl of seventeen has not been asked one thing in about a year and is not being asked anything and is not being given the chance to turn anything down, and nobody in that shed is ever going to be thanked for that either.

The one with the book turned a page. That is the whole of what he did in that bay that day.

---

At about the fifth hour she went to that doorway and stood in it.

She has not been near it in about two months, on the ground that a doorway is the one place in a bay where a foreman can be shown standing, and a bench in her own room is not, and she had worked that out at the back of that bench and had said it out loud to nobody.

The door of the shed stands about a foot open all day on the work alone, and a person standing in it is a person standing where the light from the lane comes in and not where the light off the roof reaches, and she went and stood in the gap because the gap was the other thing available and not because she had chosen it.

She got as far as the same two words. It has got to the same two words six times since the spring before last, and there is a second half of it that has not arrived in about two years and did not arrive this morning either, and the lane did what a lane does, and she stood in that frame for about as long as a person stands in a frame.

She came back in and put her hand flat on the front of that bench again, at the near end of it, about four inches off the chain, and she did not say one word about having gone to the door, and the man at the front bench did not say one word about it either, and neither of them is going to.

Nobody in that lane has ever asked her for the end of that sentence and nobody ever is, and the man at that bench has told her twice in eleven years that he is not going to be the one who finishes it, and this was the second time this month.

At about the sixth hour the chain was lifted off the stone and laid back across the front of the bench, and his hands stayed in his lap when it did.

His hands had been in his lap for the whole of the day with nothing in them.

Nobody in this empire could put him in front of anybody as the one who did not touch a chain he has touched every working morning for eleven years, and there is nobody in that bay who is going to be told that he did not, and he will not be asked about it, and the one person in that bay who could have asked him anything has just stood in a doorway for a minute and come back again and is not.

The lamp came on by itself at about the seventh hour and put light the length of that bay, and the man at the front bench got up and went out of it in the ordinary way.

The mark in chalk on that end wall is at the height of the third link of a chain that is lying on the wood with nothing on it, and it is going to be there in the winter.
