# Chapter 0508: A Pen In A Pot, A Book That Is A Record, And A Thing Left Out Of It On Purpose

There is a room with a sill at one end of it and a window about two feet wide with a pane out of it, and the pane has been out for about four years.

She keeps the minutes under the woman of about fifty-three, and the woman of about fifty-three keeps the minutes of that room and advises nobody in it and has not put a thing to that room in about four years, and there is no way in that room of putting one to anybody, and both of them have worked that out and neither of them has said it.

It is the second day of the second week of the fifth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the second hour.

---

She was nineteen and she had been in that room about a year, and in that year she had said one true thing out loud in it, in a corridor off it, about a month ago, and it was the only true thing and it was not answered, and nobody has answered her since and nobody is going to.

She has not said it out loud a second time and she is not going to, and this chapter is not going to say it either, because a sentence a person has said once in a corridor and has not said since is not a thing that gets said again for the sake of a reader, and she did not say it for a reader.

**The not being asked was the only thing she had that was hers. She gave it up in a month that is gone and it does not come back, and there is no form anywhere in this empire in which not being asked is put back, and that is not a thing anybody has taken off her, because nobody took it and nobody gave it and it went on its own.**

She carries an armful between that room and the sill and back about forty times a day, and at the end of every working day she says the number out loud at the end of the floor, and nobody has ever thanked her for the number, and she says it anyway, and she is not going to stop saying it, and nobody in that building is going to thank her for it this month.

---

The woman of about fifty-three put her pen down and looked at the book that was shut on the table in front of the girl.

"**I am going to say one thing about that book and then I am going to stop, and it is a fact and not a thing I am putting to you, and you are not going to be asked anything and you are not going to be thanked for anything.**"

The girl did not open it.

"**A book is a record. A record is the one thing in this building that other people can be shown. Everything else in here can be looked at and gone, and none of it can be produced, and that has been the whole of what this room has for nineteen years. And what you did at the end of that sill last week and the week before it is now the only decision anybody under me has made since I came in here, and I am not going to be one of the people who pretends that was nothing.**"

"I did not do anything."

"**You stood at the end of that sill and you saw him make a mark at the foot of a sheet with the other hand and you had a book open under your arm, and you did not put it down. That is the whole of what happened and you know it better than I do.**"

---

"**And here is what that is, and it is the part I came in here to say. Everything a person leaves out of a book is out of it. That is the whole use of a book. And a person who has left a thing out on purpose has made a choice, and a choice can be looked at from the outside by anybody who ever gets hold of what is in the book, and there is no column in this empire that takes the fact that a clerk left a thing out. Put it in a column and you would have to write a word over the top of it, and the word would have to be about wanting to, and there is no such word.**"

The girl put her hand flat on the shut book.

"The not being in it," she said, "was the whole of what I had."

"Yes. And that is what I came in here to take it off you, and I am sorry about it in a way that is worth nothing to you, and I am not going to say I am sorry again, and nobody is going to thank me for having said it."

---

She stood at the end of that sill for about as long as it takes a sheet to be signed.

The pot was on the stone and the pen was in the pot. She had the book shut under her arm and the armful was on the stone in front of her and the two of them were both in her hands, and there was about a minute in which the whole of what that room has ever been for was sitting on a table in front of her with a pen four inches away from it.

She did not put anything down.

Then she picked the armful up off the stone and carried it back into the room with the book shut against her side, and the pot took nothing back that it had not taken back all morning.

---

Here is the part that changed, and it changed on a morning about a month ago and it has not gone back.

Not writing a thing down is not a thing. It is what a person does when there is nothing to write. **It became a thing about three weeks ago, and the whole difference is that she did it on purpose, and on purpose is the entire difference between a person who was not there and a person who was there and chose to be, and nobody in this empire can be entered as the second one.**

There is nowhere for a person's purpose to go. It is not a column and it is not a form and it is not a heading over anything, and it cannot be got hold of, and that is exactly what she wanted for about a year of her life and it has stopped being what she wanted this month, and she has not said that to anybody either.

She is nineteen. She has not said one word about any of this and she is not going to.

---

The woman of about fifty-three came out of that room into the corridor for the first time in four years in a month that is gone, and said a true thing at the end of that sill, and was not answered, and went back in and wrote one word of it in her line and nothing else. She is fifty-three and she is not fifty-five, and she is not one of the four in this matter who cannot make sense of a paragraph, and neither is the man of about sixty-one at that sill and neither is the man of about fifty-five with a chain at the end of a lane and neither is the reader at the back of a shed who is seventeen and is not going to be one of anything.

On the shelf under the window with no pane in it there is a question that carries no full stop, and her own name is at the foot of it, and she put it there on purpose about eleven years ago so that she would be a door in that room somebody could walk through. Nobody has gone to her. Nothing has been set down after it. It is not going to be printed out in any building in this empire and it is not going to be picked up this month.

---

The book went back into the room shut and the armful went back on the sill and the number was said out loud at the end of that floor at the end of the day and nobody thanked her for it.

There is a thing that has happened in that building this month that two people know about and that is written down nowhere. Neither of the two has said one word to the other about it. The minute clerk has not noticed that either of them has not.
