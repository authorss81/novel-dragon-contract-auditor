# Chapter 0527: A Line In A Book With A Date And An Hour And A Number Of Sheets On It And Nothing Else, A Man With A Satchel Who Came Up That Stair For The Eleventh Year, And The Only Person On That Floor Who Had Ever Wanted To Put Anything Down About Anybody

The hours on that floor run from the second to the sixth. About four hundred people come down those four flights in a year and every one of them is carrying something that is not paper, and not one of them has ever been sent up them by anybody in that building.

Marn Ottery is thirty-four and has been at the far end of those boards for eleven years and has put the shape of a thing out loud in that room four times and has said in advance that there will not be a fifth.

Tamsin Rook is twenty-four and has been at her own end for two years and has put three true things out loud into that room in about three months, and has not said one this month and has not said one the month before.

There is a clerk of about twenty-nine at the far end of the two joined tables, and four years is how long she has been at that end of the room.

It is the second day of the first week of the tenth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour.

---

He was four steps short of the top of that stair before either of them heard him. He came on up and put the bag down on the wood at the far end of the boards, and stood with his shoulder to the room.

He stops on the fourth step from the top on the way up and the way he does it has not changed in eleven years, and anybody used to hearing a stair knows a thing is coming up it about the time the door at the bottom opens. There was a noise of a bag being set down on wood, and then there was nothing, and then there was the sound of somebody settling his weight on one foot and staying there.

Four times that month. Four times a week for eleven years. No one in that building has ever told him what is in that bag, and asking has never once been a courtesy between him and anybody on that floor, and it is not becoming one this month either.

He stood half turned away from the room with both hands on the strap, and he did not square the strap, which he does about half the time, and the clerk of about twenty-nine noticed it and has told nobody that she noticed it.

---

She wrote the day's line.

The date, the hour, and how many sheets came out of the case, in that order, the way she has written it every working morning for four years, and then she put the pen down beside the book where she has been putting it since a month that is gone instead of in the middle of the open page, and she squared it against the edge of the table and left it there.

The strap went up over his shoulder and came off again and went up again. Tamsin Rook squared an edge at the other end of those boards. Marn Ottery did not look over.

A working floor makes a noise like a working floor and nothing in it is addressed to anybody. Four people on about nine foot of wood and two joined tables produce about the sound of nine, and the three women had been in the room long enough to hear a difference in it the way a person hears a difference in a room they sleep in.

---

The man with the satchel said it at about half past the fourth hour, to the boards, in the ordinary way, and there were three other people on that floor.

"**Eleven years, four times a week, and nobody has ever told me what is in this and I have never asked, and I have stopped noticing that the not asking is something I do and started noticing that it is something I have never not done, and those are two different things and I have not got to the end of the difference yet. I am not going to get there in this hour and I am not going to say it a second time and nobody is going to be thanked for it.**"

He said it to the boards and he did not look up and he did not say who he was talking to, and Marn Ottery did not turn round, and the two of them at the boards did not look over at each other, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that takes a man at a satchel as the one who was on that floor when a thing was said, and there is none that takes him as the one who was not.

---

She had her hand on the book.

Four years at that end of the room, and the hand had been on the book every single working morning of them, and it was not a hand which fidgets, and it lay flat with the fingers along the edge of the page and it had never once been anything else in anybody's field of view.

It was the first time in four years that she had wanted to write something down about a person rather than about a floor. It had been there since about the middle of the fourth hour and it was the only thing she had wanted in four years, and it was not a name. It was a mark on a line that would say that a man had been on that floor at that hour with a satchel, and that is the smallest possible entry and it would fit in the middle of a line with the number of sheets on it, and the book is a record, and the one thing in that room anybody can ever be shown is a record.

She did not write it.

"You are not going to put anything down about that," Marn Ottery said, to the boards, about four minutes after it, and she said it once.

"No."

"Four times in eleven years I have put the shape of a thing out loud in that room, and I announced in advance that the fifth was not coming, and it is not coming now, and it is not coming about what you have just done at that end of those tables. What is coming is this. A line in a book about a man who came up a stair is the one thing on this floor that cannot go in that book. I will not help you with it and I will not tell you to stop, and nobody is going to be thanked for either."

"I have not asked you."

"You have not asked me and I am not going to make you, and there is nowhere in this room standing at which asking anybody anything is done."

---

Tamsin Rook did not come back to her about it, which is what she said before the middle of the hour and then did not do, and neither of them said one word to the other about a man with a satchel that day or after.

The clerk of about twenty-nine went on with the fifth hour with her hands flat on the table either side of the book and the pen lying alongside it where it could be picked up by anybody who wanted to pick it up. She did not say one word to him either, and she is not going to, and he is not going to say whether he knew, and nobody in that building is going to be told one word about it by anybody.

---

At the sixth hour that floor was shut and there was no more of that day in it. The case went away behind the boards. Four things went up onto the wall above it, each on its own nail, and the lowest of those four has been in one hand for about four years, and not one of the three of them at those boards has ever accounted for it. Underneath that wall is a drawer, and it has been shut since long before any of the three of them came to that floor, and there are four things in it, and nobody has ever taken two of those four out and looked at them, and the knife is in some other drawer and not one of the three of them has ever said which.

The line in the book for that morning says the date and the hour and the number of sheets, and the number of sheets is the same number it has been every working morning of the eleven years that book has been kept in that room, and there is nothing on that page about the strap, and the pen lay beside the book and not in it, and it lay there when he came up the stair the next time as well.
