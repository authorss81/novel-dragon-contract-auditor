# Chapter 0379: The Same Counter At The Top Of Four Flights, Fourpence On The Wood, And A Copy

The rain is coming off the landing window and running along the flags, and there is no door at the bottom of those stairs, and the hours at that counter are the second to the sixth.

He is thirty-eight. **His name is Marek Kest, and he has no office and no fund and no list and no leave, and there is a page in his coat that has a line struck out of it in a hand that is not his mother's, and the name that ought to stand at the end of that line is the only thing he has wanted for about two years.** He has not told anybody he was coming up those four flights and nobody has sent for him.

He put fourpence on the wood and did not say what for.

"A copy," said Marn Ottery, without looking at the money. "I will make the copy of what is on your counter, and I am telling you now that I have not got the whole morning, because you are the second one up those stairs today and the first one bought nothing at all."

"That is all right. I have the thing with me."

---

"You have the thing with you and you have not put it on the counter," she said, "and I have been at this end of it eleven years and that is a thing a person does when the thing on the counter is not what is in his hand."

He put it on the counter. It was a page out of a bundle, folded twice, and it had been folded and unfolded about four hundred times and the fold at the third of it had gone through to a hole.

"Then there is no form for buying a copy of a thing that is not on it," he said. "**Fourpence buys me a second piece of paper with the same four things printed along the top of it, and I have not been at a counter for fourpence in about two years and I have come four flights in the rain to do it.**"

"You have, and I have watched you not put your hand in your pocket for about a minute, which is the part I would have noticed if I were going to notice anything."

---

"I said the shape of a thing out loud in this room twice," Marn Ottery said, "and I have been asked one thing back both times, and I have said it a second time to the same man, which is how a person becomes a thing with a shape."

"I know. I have been told about it by a woman at the other end of four hundred yards of flags who should not have been told and who is not going to be asked about it by anybody either."

"That is twice now that a person has come up those stairs and known something about that room that I have said in it." She squared the copy on the wood. "**There is no form for an hour of a clerk's time, and there is no form for a copy of a thing that is not on the thing, and both of those come down to the counter and neither of them is going anywhere.**"

"You are the third thing I have had to deal with this week and you are not going to be asked one thing about it."

"I did not say I was going to ask you one. I said you were the third, and you are, and I am not going to ask you anything, and the not asking is mine and not yours and I would like that noticed by nobody in particular."

---

She made the copy in about ninety seconds. There is a printed strip along the top of every blank that counter has ever sold, and the strip is the same strip, and the four things on it are the same four things, and there is a knife in the drawer that takes about four seconds to cut a line into it if a person wants one cut.

He read the strip on the copy twice with the window behind it and the light going, and he said one thing out loud, to the window and to the rain and to nobody at all.

**"None of those four is a name."**

"They are all words," said Marn Ottery. "All four of them, and the fourth has been on that board longer than I have, and two clerks have told me in eleven years that they do not know what it is for either, and I have never worked out whether that makes it a word or a name."

"You have worked out the strip and not the word."

"I have worked out the strip and not the word, and I have never said that part out loud in this room before, and I am saying it now at about the third hour of a wet morning to a man who has not put his hand in his pocket."

---

Nobody thanked him for the copy, and nobody at that counter said one word back about the copy. He put it inside his coat on the side the page was not on, and he went down the four flights, and a man coming down four flights with a wet coat on goes down them faster than a man goes up them, and he has never established why, and there is a landing at the bottom with a man standing on it who moved over without being asked to.

Nobody asked him one thing back. The strip at the top of it is the same strip, and the fourth thing on it is the one nobody in that building can account for, and he has not written the number of things anybody has asked out loud in this matter anywhere at all.
