# Chapter 0380: The End Of Four Hundred Yards Of Cold Flags, A Lock, And A Count Going Up

About four hundred yards of cold flags run out of the back of that building from a flood terrace, and the cold of them comes up off the stone whatever the season is, and no fire is lit at either end of them. A lock is at this end. At the other end there is a room, and a rail in it standing about four feet off the floor, and nobody has ever put the passage's work through that room and there is no column anywhere that would say so.

She is twenty-six. Four years of that building are behind her and she has never been one of the three clerks in it, and she is not in this and never has been. **There is no form for a number of persons under nothing**, and there never has been one, and that is not because anybody decided there should not be. She keeps a count in her head of how many people are sitting inside a thing nobody can be asked about. **It has gone up since the seventh month of the year after, and it has gone up again since the turn of the year, and it is not written down and it is not going to be handed to anybody this month or in any month after it.**

Nobody has ever asked her anything. In four years she has not been sent for, has not been handed a sheet, and has not been thanked, and she has never once put a hand on a rack.

---

The man in the felt apron went down at the second hour with a rack and came back along the flags at about the fourth with one sheet on it, which is the number it has been coming back with for as long as either of them has been at this end.

"They are light again," he said, before he had got the rack off his hip.

"They have been light for four years."

"Not once, in four years, has there been a second one come up behind that first one, and not once has a rack gone down off that end full, and I could put the hour of the one morning there was different to within the hour, if there was a place in this building to write a thing down."

"There is no place in this building to write a thing down."

"There is a lock and a wall and four hundred yards of stone," he said, "and I have said the hour out loud to a woman at a lock in wet weather, which is the same as saying it to nobody, and it is the reason I do not mind this end as much as a person would."

---

"You have had a hand on this lock for about a year and you have never once let go of it," he said. "That is not a complaint. I have watched you not let go of it every day for four years and I have never worked out what you are holding."

"Nothing that is on a rack."

"There is nothing that is on a rack. That is what I mean." He took his hand off the rack. "**A person with a figure about them is a person somebody can hand a thing to, and I have watched this building find figures for people for four years, and the one time this empire was tidy about a number of that kind it cost about four hundred people a season and nobody was told why.** I am not saying that about you. I am saying it about the shape of the thing, because you have the shape of it and you are the only one at this end of the flags who has."

"I have not told you the figure."

"You have not told me the figure," he agreed, "and I have not said that you have one, and I have said that somebody at this end of the passage has, and if that somebody is a man I have been talking to a lock again for four years and I would rather not have done it."

---

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the eighth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fourth hour, and the flags at the far end of them were carrying the noise of nobody in them.

She said the rest of it standing at the lock with her back to the wall, out loud, once, to the passage, because a passage is the only thing in that building that cannot be served with anything.

"It has gone up. I am not telling you by how much and I am not going to, and I have three questions for a man of about sixty-one who puts his hand at the foot of the sheets at the other end of these flags, and I have asked him none of them, and the two of those facts are going nowhere together and they are going nowhere apart. **There is no form for a heading over that figure, and I have worked out that any heading over it would come down on every room in the thing at once, and I am not going to be the one who builds the heading.**"

The man in the felt apron did not answer that and did not go down the flags to repeat it, and he has not repeated one word of it and there is no form in this empire for repeating a thing said at a lock, and a rack goes down and comes back and the number on it is one.

There is still a rail in the room at the far end of those flags about four feet off the floor, and nothing on it, and she has looked at that door from four hundred yards off more than once in four years and has not gone up it. The last rack of that day came up light by one.
