# Chapter 0378: Two Doors Four Feet Apart, A Bill With No Figure, And A Man Of Thirty-Four

The lane is the width of two doors and there has been water coming off both eaves of it since the second hour, and by the sixth hour the four feet between the two houses is the only dry ground on either side.

Thirty-four, give or take, and paid for a fair hand: he copies other men's figures until two sets of them agree, four days a season, and only when somebody's list has him on it. **He is the one person in this whole matter that a question could be put to, and not one has been.** A number lives in his head that he has never written down and has never passed on, and the reason is that a number in a second head becomes a column, and a column gets a heading over it, and a heading over that would stand over him the same as over everybody else.

He has been at a bar of iron at the end of a yard six days this week. Three shillings a day, on a wall that is not on a district's list, and the man who set that figure is not stood at the bar and does not come out in this.

---

The woman at the foot of the four steps was on her own step before he came round the corner, which told him how long she had been standing there.

"You have been on that road since the second hour," she said, "and I am not asking you where, because I have not asked you where in two years and I am not starting at the end of a wet week."

"I know that."

"I am going to say the other half of it and then I am going in." She had the cloth over her arm and she did not take it off. "**I have stood on this step four nights running waiting to find out one thing, and I have had no way of finding out whether you were at a wall or a bar or a yard, and the not-asking has not cost me anything, and the standing has.**"

"It has not cost you anything because you have not been told what it is."

"I know exactly what it is. I have known for about two years and I have known it the whole of the time you have been going up that lane, and I have never worked out whether knowing it is a thing I did or a thing that was done to me."

---

The woman of about thirty-five next door put her head out at about the same hour, came down one of her own two steps and stayed there, and from four feet away she had taken in the whole of the two of them and had missed none of it.

"There is a bill at your door," she said, across four feet of lane. "It came on the second of the two days and it has been in your hall since, and it has your name at the head of it."

"There is a bill at my door."

"Four days it has lain in that hall, and it stands against somebody other than you, and you are carrying it, and I am putting it to you as a fact and not as a kindness, because I have had eleven years next door and I have watched what you carry." She did not come down her step. "**Being findable is a bill. It is the only part of being findable that anybody in this lane has ever actually had to hold.**"

---

He did not go in and pick it up. She did not tell him to and neither of them mentioned it again that evening, and the bill was in the hall until the middle of the next week, and nobody is liable for it and it is not paid and the figure on it was not read out in that lane and is not read out here.

"There is no form for a bill on somebody else's account," he said, at about the eighth hour, to the two doors and neither of the two women. "There is no form for paying for a copy of a thing either, and I have paid fourpence for one twice in about two years and the thing I went for was not on either of them."

"You have never once said that out loud in this lane in two years," said the woman at the foot of the four steps.

"I have never once had somebody standing on a step in the rain while I said it."

---

It is the second day of the second week of the eighth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and the two of them went in at about the eighth hour, and the woman of about thirty-five next door went in at about the ninth and put her own door on the latch behind her the way she has every night for eleven years.

The bill was still on the hall floor at the end of it, and the lane was still four feet wide, and neither of the two women said one word about the man who had just gone in, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for a person to say a thing like that out loud to a person.
