# Chapter 0388: The Room Behind The Door, Four Seconds With A Pen Out, And A Sheet Not Signed

**There is a corridor outside that door about nine feet long, and at the end of it there is a hook in the wall with a basket on it,** and the basket is where the sheets go when they have been through the second hand and do not need to come back up. There is a pen pot on the sill, and it is the only thing in that corridor that is not the colour of the wall. The bed is against the far wall and has not been made.

He is about sixty-one, and he has been the second hand on a sheet since the year he was forty-two. **He cannot read a paragraph and has never once pretended to.** A figure comes through his hands, and a name at the top of a sheet comes through, and enough of the small print that holds one to the other comes through for him to know they are both there. After that the sheet stops being words and becomes a weight, and the weight is in his hands, and his hands are what a sheet is for. His hand goes at the foot of about nine hundred of them a year.

**He has wanted about four minutes for about nineteen years.** Four minutes is what it would take to go through the ones he can remember and put no to about half and yes to the rest. There is nobody in that building he could say that to, there has not been a person on that road in nineteen years who thought of offering them, and there is no form anywhere for a person to put four minutes on a table and be the person who put them there.

---

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the tenth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fourth hour, and the corridor outside the door was empty the way it is most of the day.

A young man of about twenty-six has been carrying sheets down the nine feet outside the door for the length of two years, and everything he has picked up over those two years amounts to a list of everything nobody has ever said to him. and he has put the list nowhere, because putting it down anywhere at all would turn it into a page and there is no form for a page. A man paid by the corner and not by a page is paid for what he carries and not one word for what is on it, and about nine hundred of them go down the corridor in a year, and he could carry every one of them twice over and be paid the same for both.

The bed is against the far wall and it has not been made, and the corridor is about nine feet long, and a man who works in it for nineteen years has the whole of his trade inside nine feet of wall. Nobody has ever put a question to him at the end of a working day, and no form anywhere covers a person putting one, and the arrangement is not being asked, and it is not a complaint, and he has never once said out loud that he would rather be asked and get nothing back.

The young man who carries the sheets away came up with four of them and put three of them on the sill and kept the fourth, and then he put it down in front of the second hand instead of keeping it, which is not what he does.

"This one wants you," he said. "It has not had a hand at the bottom of it and it is going back down in this basket whether it gets one or not."

---

Three sheets go onto the sill at the fourth hour and stay there until the seventh, and for about three hours they belong to neither the second hand nor the man who brought them nor to any other person in that nine feet, and no form anywhere covers a sheet lying on a sill in between one man's hands and the hands of the man after him. Nine feet of corridor, a pot, a hook, a basket that does not come back up, and three sheets lying on the sill in the middle of all that, and the man of about sixty-one knows what they weigh and which way up they go and what is printed along the head of them and nothing whatever about the three themselves.

The man of about sixty-one looked at it for about four seconds with the pen out of the pot and not down.

He did not read it. He looked at the slant of it, which came through, and at the weight of the paper, which came through, and at the place where the man above him had signed and left the pen down on the blotter before the ink was dry, which is the one thing about a sheet that comes through a man's hands when nothing else does.

It was the fourth of the four, and he knew it the way a man knows his own hearth from a doorway in the dark, and there was no reading in that and there was nothing in the world he could have done with knowing it.

---

The young man took it back off the sill and looked at the clock at the end of the corridor, and he said it out loud, in the corridor, at a man who had said nothing.

"I am paid for a corner. That is the whole of what I am paid for and I have never once been paid for a page, and if I say a word about what is on a page to a man whose hand goes at the foot of nine hundred a year then I am a man who has had a page, and there is no form for a page and I do not want one." He put the sheet flat on the sill again. "**And there is no form for a basket that does not come back up.** You had the pen out for four seconds just then. I timed it, because there is a clock at the end of this corridor. Four seconds, and I have carried sheets for two years, and in two years a man at the foot of a sheet has had the pen out for four seconds a small number of times, and he has not signed one of them, and I have carried the basket down that corridor on every one of them, and I have never once seen an asking happen."

A pen pot on a sill in a corridor nine feet long is the only object in the whole of it anybody has ever put anything back into, and the man of about sixty-one puts the pen back the way he does everything, which is without looking, and the young man has watched him do it for the length of two years and has never once got used to it. He has got used to the pot itself, and to the man, and to nothing else in nine feet of corridor. No form anywhere covers getting used to a pen pot, and none covers a young man who is going to be gone in a year having got used to one.

The man of about sixty-one put the pen back in the pot. He did it the way a man does a thing he has done ten thousand times, which is without looking.

---

"He took it down the corridor," the man of about sixty-one said, "and it has not come back up and it is not going to, because it does not have to. That is how the basket works and I have known that since I was forty-two."

"You want to tell me it was wrong."

"I have never told anybody that I know which ones are wrong." He said it to the floor, and then he lifted his head, because he had heard himself say it in a corridor with a man of twenty-six in it. "**I have said one at a time, more than once in nineteen years, and I have never once said that there was a way of telling, and this is a man who is paid by the corner and who will be gone in a year.** And it is not the four minutes, and saying it here does not turn into the four minutes, and there is no form in this empire for four minutes, and there is not one for a thing said in a corridor either."

---

At the seventh hour the young man came back up the corridor for the three sheets that were on the sill, signed for them off the rail, put them in the basket and took the basket down, and the hook was empty inside a minute. The pen went back into the pot on the sill and has not been out of it since the fourth hour. The basket does not come back up.
