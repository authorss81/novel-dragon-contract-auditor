# Chapter 0385: A Sheet Down Four Hundred Yards Of Cold Flags And A Woman Who Sends It Back

Four hundred yards of cold flags run out of the back of that building, the same four hundred as last week and the week before, and the cold comes up off the stone whatever the season is, and no fire is lit at either end of them. There is a lock at this end. The rail is four hundred yards off at the other one and there is nothing on it and nothing has ever been on it. Everything else about that passage is the same as it was the week before that, and it will be the same as it was the week after, and she has two years of knowing that to go on.

She is twenty-six, and she has been in that building four years, and she has never been one of the three clerks who work in it. **In all that time nobody has put a question to her, sent for her, thanked her, or handed her a sheet to carry, and her hands have never once been on a rack.** She keeps a count in her head, and what the count is, is how many people are sitting inside a thing nobody can be asked about. It is the only figure in that building belonging to nobody else, and it has gone up again since the turn of the year. **There is no form for a figure nobody can be made to give up**, and there has not been one in four years, and the count is not written down anywhere: not on a rack, not on a sheet, and not going to be handed to anybody this month or in any month after it.

The man in the felt apron has had that end of the passage for longer than she has stood at it. He brings one sheet back on a rack at about the fourth hour and the number on the rack has been one for as long as either of them has been here.

---

The sheet came down at the second hour in a hand that was not the apron.

It was the first one in a month that had come down with a ruled space on it, and the space was about an inch and a half deep and a hand's width across, and there was a heading printed over it in a hand that was not hers, and the heading was one anybody could use. He held it out to her the way a man holds out a thing he has been told to hold out, and he did not say one word about it, and he did not have to.

She took it and turned it so that the space was under her own eyes and read the heading. Then she read it again. **The ruled space is the size of a count and it is not the size of a name**, and a count is a figure and a name is a thing, and the two of them do not go into the same width of paper in any building in this empire, and somebody had printed a heading over the width of a figure and handed it to the only person at this end of the passage who had one.

She handed it back without a mark on it.

---

"I am not going to tell you not to write in it," the man in the felt apron said. He took the sheet and looked at the space on it and then at her hand, which was not on it. "I want that said first, before anything else, so that it is not in a room somewhere later on that somebody said you were told not to."

"I have not written in it."

"I can see that you have not written in it. I am talking about the other thing." He turned the sheet over and back again. "**A number under a heading is a number that belongs to whoever printed the heading.** That is not a threat and it is not a rule, it is how a piece of paper works. The heading is on it before you ever see the space, and the space is ruled to the heading and not to you, and whoever comes back for that space in six months is going to come back with the heading in their hand and not with any interest in what you thought the figure was."

"It would not help if I did not know what the number was."

"Then you write a dash in it and the dash is a figure as well, and a dash in a ruled space under a heading is a figure that is wrong, and wrong is a thing a person can be asked about afterwards by somebody who has never met you." He put the sheet under his arm. "**And you have been the only person at this end of these flags that could be made to give one up, and I have watched the racks go up and down four hundred yards for four years and there has not been one other at this end that anybody could have asked a number off.**"

---

"You are not going to say anything about it," she said, "and I am not going to deny that there is one, and both of those are true and neither of them is any use to either of us."

"**There is no form for a heading with a number under it**, and that is not because nobody decided there should not be one." He shifted the rack off his hip. "I have never once wanted you to tell me anything. I have wanted you to write in about four spaces a week for two years and you have not and I have not asked, and that is the whole of what has happened between us at this end of this passage. What I do not want is for you to be the one who filled in the first one. That is all. It is not about the number. It is about who is standing at the top of it when it comes back."

"It would be a different thing if it were you that filled one in."

"It would be me, and I have not got a room to be served in, and I would be a man at a lock who filled in a space with a heading he did not print." He went to the top end of the flags. "It is a different thing. That is all it is."

---

It is the second day of the first week of the tenth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the second hour, and the cold at the one end of those flags had not gone up or down by a degree since the turn of the year.

Four hundred yards twice a day is work a body stops having an opinion about, and the cold of the flags comes up through the soles in every month there is, and the arrangement amounts to a lock at one end and a rail at the other and nothing whatever in between belonging to either. A person who walks the length can be interrupted anywhere along it and cannot tell in advance where, and nobody at any point along it could stop her, and it is nothing whatever to do with the privacy of a passage. It is that there is nobody on it.

She said the other part of it out loud, once, with her back to the wall at this end of them, and a passage is the one thing in that building that cannot be served with anything, which is why a passage is where a person says a thing like that. "**There is a room at the far end of these flags with a rail in it and nothing on it, and a man of thirty-eight has been in this building and has not been in that room, and those two things are not going anywhere together, and I am not going to be the one who walks them down there and joins them up.**"

At the second hour the passage is the same as it is at the sixth and the light on the flags does not reach the far end of the run, so the last hundred yards of it are a thing she knows rather than a thing she sees. She has come about as far as a person can come on foot and stopped at the place where the light gives out, every time, for four years, and nothing is at the place where she stops and nothing on the wall above it, and the not going on has never once been a decision she had to hold her hand to, because a person standing at a lock is doing the whole of her job and a person who went further would be doing a different one entirely.

The apron he wears has kept its colour for every day either of them has stood at the end of the flags where it is, and he has never made a single attempt to keep it off, and there is nowhere along the run to wipe it, and she has never said a word about its colour either, and the two of them have worked a passage for four years without either of them ever making a remark about what the other happens to be wearing. No form covers a remark like that and no arrangement would require one, and about half of what two people say to each other in four years is not about either of them.

The man in the felt apron did not answer that and did not carry it up the flags to repeat it, and a rack went down and came back and the number on it was one.

He took the sheet up with him at the fourth hour with the ruled space as clean as it came off the press, and she stood at the lock with her hands empty and watched about four hundred yards of stone flags take him off it, and that is the only time in four years a sheet has gone up that passage with a heading printed over a space that nobody at this end had filled in.
