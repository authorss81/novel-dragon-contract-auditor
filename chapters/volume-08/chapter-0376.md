# Chapter 0376: The Same Ground-Floor Room, Four Sheets In Early, And A Woman Of Nineteen

Four sheets came up the flags this morning and all four of them were entered before the fourth hour, which is earlier than they were entered last week and a good deal earlier than they were entered in the spring. The shelf of eleven books is under the window about two feet wide and the window still has no pane in it and has not had one for about four years, and the cold comes in at the one corner of it and stands there.

She is about fifty-five, and nineteen years of that room are behind her, and what she does in it is minutes. She advises nobody and she asks nothing, and about four thousand entries have gone into those eleven books and not one of them carries a name.

At the far end of the room there is a woman of about nineteen who has been in the job eight weeks and who keeps her coat on all day, and who has worked out in eight weeks that there is a thing on the shelf under the window that is not in the minutes and has been there longer than she has been here.

---

"You have gone out of the room three times today," the woman of nineteen said. "Before the last of the four was in. It is not the fourth hour yet."

"It is inside the fourth hour."

"You went at half past two and at half past three and the light has not changed since one o'clock." She had her hands flat on the end of the table and she was not writing anything. "**There is no form for a clerk's own motion kept on a shelf, and there has not been one since before either of us, and I have been here eight weeks and I can see it from where I am sitting and you have been here nineteen years and I do not think you can see it any more.**"

"I can see it."

"Then say what it is, because I have worked out what it is and I would rather have it off the shelf of my own head."

"It is a note of mine, made in this room, in this hand, with the day and the hour on it, and it has no full stop at the end of it, and it is not a minute and it was never going to be a minute."

---

"Nineteen years," said the woman of nineteen. "I have been counting the sheets as they go in. I have been here eight weeks and I have not seen one name at the foot of anything in this room except yours, and I do not think that is a coincidence, and I think it is the opposite of one."

"You are right. It is not a coincidence."

"Then a person whose name is the only name is a person a question can be put to. That is how the rest of this building works and I have watched it work for eight weeks from a desk at the end of a room with no pane in the window." She did not sit down. "There is a man at the other end of these flags whose hand goes at the foot of nine hundred sheets a year and who cannot read a paragraph, and I have had that from three people in eight weeks without one of them being asked for it by me. If your name is the only name in this room then the room has a person in it that a question can be put to, and it does not have one in it at the moment, because you have never once let anybody put one."

---

"Yes," said the clerk of about fifty-five, and carried on down the column. "I have never once let anybody put one and I have spent nineteen years being proud of that in a way I could not defend if I were asked to, and you have done it in eight weeks with a sheet of paper you did not have to count."

"I have not asked you anything."

"I know that. I would not have been able to stand in this room for a minute of it."

She wrote for a minute. The numbers went in the way they have gone in since the spring, in a hand that has not varied, and the figure in the middle column of the third sheet was one she could not account for and did not account for, because a clerk who alters a figure is a clerk who has begun to have an opinion about a thing somebody else did.

"I have started going out of the room before the last of the four comes in," she said. "Three times today and twice yesterday and I have not told you why and I do not know why. **I have told nineteen years of people in this room that a person who is not asked has not been given a duty, and I have been going out of the room four times in two days to find out whether that holds for me.**"

---

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the seventh month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and the four that came up the flags were in the books before the fourth hour, and the fifth did not come up at all, which happens.

The room was the room it had been at the start of the day with four sheets the fewer in it, and the difference was in the light and not in the work. What came through the window had gone off the shelf and was up the wall above it now, and everything in the room that had to be read had been read and everything in it that had to be written had been written, and there was nothing left in the middle of the day for either of them to do. The clerk was in the chair and the young woman was still standing at the other end of the room with her coat on, and the two of them had got to it by themselves without ever once arranging it, and there was no second chair in the room for either of them to have done anything else about.

A note of hers is on the shelf under the window with a day and an hour on it and no full stop anywhere on the end of it, and it is the only thing on that whole shelf that anybody's name is at the bottom of. **There is no form for a question with no full stop**, and there has not been one in nineteen years, and the woman of nineteen at the far end of the room has not thrown it out and has not asked her about it and has not told anybody in that building that it is there.

The door out of the room opens on the flags and the flags go a long way in both directions and neither direction is anywhere she has ever gone. She has been out of the room three times in two days and has stood at the door of it each time, and on the first of the three she had gone as far as the first turn in the flags and had stood there, and on the second she had not gone at all, and she has not told the young woman which of the three was which and is not going to. There is nothing in the room to bring back in from a turn in the flags, and a person who goes out of a room to find out a thing and comes back in with nothing has not been anywhere, and the building has no place to put an answer to it if there is one.

**The window about two feet wide has gone on without a pane for about four years.** A quarter of a finding of hers was answered out loud in the second month, at the cold end of her own passage, by a man who came the whole of that distance on his own two feet and was paid nothing for it. She entered none of it, and there is no column in any of the eleven books for a quarter of an answer, and she went out of the room at half past three, before the last of the four, and the young woman at the far end of it watched her go and said nothing about it, and the count of things anybody has asked out loud in this matter was the same before and after, and it is seven.
