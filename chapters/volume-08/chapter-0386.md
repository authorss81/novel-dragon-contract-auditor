# Chapter 0386: The Shed At The Slade End, A Form Nobody Has Found Void, And A Second Reason

The lamp above the middle of that bench does not get lit until about the seventh hour. On the end wall there is a mark in chalk, and it is at the height of the third link of the chain that a man at the front bench is wearing, and neither one of them means anything to anybody in this building. The shed is about nine foot by eleven and the bench is longer than that.

She is fifty-one. **Nine families settled the foreman's job of that shed on a cut about eleven years ago, in a room behind the second one of these, and it took them an hour and a half, and she was not in that room and nobody went and got her.** Since the first week of the seventh month of the year after she has been in a box, put in by a class, and nobody in this building or in the lane behind it has ever asked her a thing about it.

At the back of that shed, on a bench of their own, the two of seventeen are sitting. They are furniture of the room the way a bench is furniture, and they have no answer in it, and they are not given one.

---

The form came round the front of the shed on the second of the two days, on a piece of paper the size of a man's palm, and it went on the board where anybody coming in could read it. It is the size of a form that puts a person somewhere else. It has two ruled spaces on it and a heading over each of them, and the first heading takes a class and the second heading takes a place, and there is a date in the middle.

There is no asking in it anywhere. **Nobody has asked either of the two at the back what they make of it, and nobody at the front of that bench has said one word to either of them about it, and no one in this lane has ever asked a person of seventeen whether she would rather not be where she is, because the form has no space for that and nothing else in this lane has one either.**

"There is no form for a form that has not been found void," said Halla Wray, more to the board than to the shed, and she got as far as that before the man of about twenty-eight put his hand on the front bench.

---

"No," he said. "And I am going to stop you, and you are going to notice that it is not the same reason as last time, and I would rather you noticed that than that you did not."

"That is not the reason you gave me a month ago."

"It is not, and I have thought about it on the way in, and here it is." He did not take his hand off the wood. "**A form nobody has found void is a form that is still there next week**, and this one is still here, and the second you say out loud what it does to those two, it stops being a form on a board and becomes a form a foreman has explained, and an explained form is a form somebody has acted on. After that there is nobody in this lane who can put it back on the board with nothing over it."

"Nobody would find it void."

"I know nobody would." He turned round and looked down the shed at the bench at the back, and he looked at it for as long as a person looks at a bench. "I have worked out that there is nobody in this lane with the standing to find anything void. I have worked that out at the front of this bench over seven years, and it is the most useful thing I know, and it has never once been any use to me."

---

Halla Wray put her hand flat on the end of the bench and left it there.

**No form for saying a paper out loud in a shed**, and there is not one in this lane, and the sentence about the class and the place stopped where it had got to, and the lamp stayed unlit, and the two at the back were not spoken to again that day and were not spoken to on the third day either.

**The man of about fifty-five with the chain came down off his own end of the front bench and put his hand flat on the form.** He said his four sentences, and they took him as long as turning a hand over, and they were about the weight of a chain, and nobody in that shed stopped him or told him to take his hand off.

About four men in that shed cannot make a paragraph out of anything, and not one question about a number has ever been put to any of them. The man at the front read the form out loud in the ordinary way with the chain man's hand still lying on it, and the reading did not require a paragraph, and nobody at the front of that bench noticed anything about it at all, and that was the week.

Four hundred yards up the same lane there is a room where a man of thirty-eight keeps the books of that Company, and since the third month of the year after he has wanted the rest of a sentence of hers, and she gave him half of one in a doorway in a month that is gone. The half she did not give him did not arrive in this week either, and nobody has sent for it.

---

It is the second day of the second week of the tenth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fifth hour, and the lamp above the middle of that bench was not lit.

The form went back on the board when the sheet it came in with was cleared away, and the gate at the bottom of that lane was shut all week. At the seventh hour the lamp was lit over the middle of that bench, and the two of them were still on the bench at the back with the form about nine foot in front of them in plain sight, and it will be on that board again on the second of the two days next week.
