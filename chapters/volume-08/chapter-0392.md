# Chapter 0392: The Cold At One Corner Of A Window With No Pane, And A True Fact About The Weather

She has been in this room nineteen years and the window in it is about two feet wide with no pane in it, and for a month the cold has come in at the one corner of that window and stood there, and this morning it is not there. The shelf of eleven books is under that window, and a table and one chair are the whole of the furniture in the place, and about four thousand entries stand in those volumes and not one of them has anybody's name against it. What she wrote herself is on that shelf, with a day and an hour at the top of it. **Her name is at the foot of it and there is no full stop on the end of it**, and she is the only name in nineteen years that has appeared at the foot of anything in this room. There is no form for a fact said in a room that is not on a sheet, nobody has asked her about it, and nobody is going to put her a second question about it.

She is about fifty-five. Her work in that room is minutes, and nobody in that building gets advice out of her and nothing comes out of her as a question, and a fortnight ago she came the whole of the distance from that room to a rented room at the other end of it, and she said one thing out loud in that room, and nobody asked her for it, and she has not been asked about it since and is not going to be. **For nineteen years the one thing she had was that nobody asked her.** A person nobody asks cannot be served, and that was worth having, and she has now spent it, and there is no form anywhere for getting a position back once it has been used.

At the far end of that room the woman of about nineteen has been working since the spring and keeps her coat on all day, and she is the only person in that building who says out loud what she sees.

---

The four who come up the flags in a morning are entered before the fourth hour, because the light is going, and the hand they are entered in has not varied in nineteen years, and this morning the woman of nineteen had the back door open at half past one, which is forty minutes earlier than the half past two that the cold has been giving her since the turn of the year.

"You have opened that door early."

"I have opened that door early because there is no cold coming off that stone this morning, and I have been standing at it for two days working it out." The young woman had her sleeves turned back and a pen in her hand with no ink on the nib. "**There is no form for a person telling what the weather is doing**, and I have been telling it to myself in that corner for two days like a woman talking to a wall, and you are the other person in this room and I am going to say it out loud to you and then I am going to be a person who has said a true thing out loud in a room, and I would like that noticed."

"I have noticed it."

"You have not asked me a single thing about it, and I want both of those said in the same breath." The young woman put the pen down on the table, which she does not do. "**A person who has told somebody a thing out loud in a room has to go on being the one who tells.** And there is nobody in that room she is going to be able to stop telling. You did it at the end of last week and you came back on the working morning after, and the telling is not a thing that gets put down in a building because the woman who said it has gone out of a door and come in again."

---

"I am not going to answer that," the clerk said.

"You do not have to answer it and I did not put it as a question and there is no question mark on it anywhere in it." The young woman turned her head towards the window. "**The not being asked has now cost you the only thing you had, which was not being asked.** You had that for nineteen years and nobody could serve a paper on a woman nobody asks, and you have spent it, and you did not get anything for it that a person could put in a book, and I have watched you not get anything for it, and I am the only one in this building old enough to have been here when you were not being asked."

She went on with the line she was in and carried it up to where the line was, and her hand did not change, and after a while she said the other part of it, which is not an answer to what the young woman had said and is about a window.

---

"The wind has gone round to the other side of this building," she said. "It has been round the north side since before the turn of the year and it has come in at that corner every working morning since. It is in at the west side today and the west side of this room is a wall. **The cold in that corner has gone up off the plaster and has not come down.** That is a fact about the weather and not about a sheet, and it is the first thing I have said out loud in this room in nineteen years that is not on a piece of paper. I would like it entered that I said it and did not write it down, because the two of those are the arrangement."

The woman of nineteen did not write it down. She had the pen in her hand again and the nib down in the ink, and she looked along the shelf of the eleven books, and she did not open one of them and she did not take a leaf out of the pad on the corner of that table, and she said nothing whatever about it, **and she has said nothing about it since.**

"It is not going to last," the young woman said at last. "It will be back by the middle of next week and you will be standing in that corner with your hands inside your sleeves."

"I know that. I have watched it four times this winter and it is not a thing I needed anybody to tell me." She squared the last sheet she had done and put it on the pile. "**The four that come up those flags will be at that back door at half past two this afternoon instead of at a quarter to.** If you have opened it early for nothing then that is one afternoon. If I am wrong about it then that is a day I have said a true thing out loud about, and I have been in this room nineteen years and I would take that trade this morning with both hands."

---

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the eleventh month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the second hour, and that corner of that window has not been cold since the middle of the night.

About four hundred yards of stone run out of the back of that building. **She has not walked ten of them this week.** A man of thirty-eight walked that whole distance with nothing but his own two feet in a month that is gone, and has not been in that building since. What she said out loud at the other end of it cannot be said again or not said again by anybody, and there is no form in this matter for a woman taking one of the two back.

She went out of the room at half past three, which is the fourth time in two days, and the corner of that window was warm when she came back in, and she did not put her hands inside her sleeves at the door, which is a thing she has done every morning for a month.
