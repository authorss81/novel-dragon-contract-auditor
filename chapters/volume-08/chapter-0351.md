# Chapter 351: A Room Over A Chandler's Shop Taken By The Week, Four Steps And A Landing, About Four Feet Of Wall With A Nail In It And Nothing Ever Hung On It, A Table With A Sheet On It This Morning, And A Lamp That Waits For The Seventh Hour

The room is over a chandler's shop and the shopkeeper lets it by the week, and the shop keeps tar and rope and lamp oil, and all three of them come up through the boards by the middle of the morning and stay there.

Four steps up from the street and then a landing, and a table on the landing, and a chair stood over against the wall the bed is against, and a window a foot wide or thereabouts that looks at the back of another house and at nothing else at all. **The wall beside that window is about four feet of bare plaster with one nail driven into it**, and the nail was in it before he came and it has not moved since and nothing has ever hung on it.

He is thirty-four or thereabouts and the hand is a fair one, and the fair hand is what he is paid for, and what it is paid for is the copying out of other men's figures until the two sets of them agree. A man has to stand on somebody's list for that, and he stands on one four days a season, and this is not one of the four, and he did not put himself on this.

This is the second day of the first week of the first month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fifth hour, and the lamp in that room is not lit and is not going to be.

---

Last week he put his hand flat on that table and there was nothing on it.

This week there is a sheet. A man of forty came up the four steps on the first morning with a column of figures he wants set out in a hand that is not his, and it is eight hours of fair-hand work at a rate that is a rate and not a kindness, and the sheet has been on that table since the second hour.

The column does not agree with his own. The second line does not agree with the first and the fifth does not agree with anything, and he has been at it since the second hour and he has found where it goes wrong, and he is not the man who is to say so out loud to anybody today.

**He has a number in his head. He has had it about two years, and he has never written it, and he has never said it, and nobody has ever come up those four steps and asked him for it.**

The reason is not that he is shy about it. Handing the number over would be a list. A list of persons is a company, and a company is a heading, and a heading is what a man turns into the moment the thing outgrows one room. A figure that stood over all the rooms at once would have to stand over him too, and there is no version of him that is still him underneath it.

---

There is no form for putting yourself on a list, and there is no form for being reached at a counter in a room you have left, and the two of those are one absence and not two.

He came into this room off a counter at the top of four flights four miles off, and he paid fourpence for a copy there, and before that he paid fourpence for a copy in a third room, and neither of those two rooms is this one, and a person who has been findable in three rooms in about two years is findable in none of them this week.

**Nothing has ever come up these four steps for a copy and the reason is not that nobody wants one.** It is that the people who might have wanted one have about nine hundred rooms between them and him, and a person is in exactly one of them, and no paper in this empire says which.

He is still owed the bill for being findable, and the bill is not his and does not stand against his wages and is on somebody else's account, and he has known that for about two years and has not found out whose account it is and is not going to.

---

Three other things are owed to him, and he has stopped keeping them in order.

The first is the thing nobody has thanked him for, which is standing still while another man's figures get read out of him. **There is not one form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be thanked for being findable**, and he has never asked for the thanks and has never once decided that he wanted it.

The second is a form for asking a person whether they will mind being told a thing before they are told it. He has looked for one for about two years, in the places a man looks, and what is in those places is a form for telling and a form for saying that you have not been told.

The third is a woman at the foot of about four steps and four miles off, who is not thanked either, and who has said out loud that she is not going to ask him about his week, and who is not thanked by him about anything.

---

The count of things anybody has asked out loud in this matter is seven.

It was seven ten weeks ago, and it was seven the last time a man said a true thing standing up in a room with a rail in it, and it is seven now, and it will be seven in a month.

**He is the only person in all of this that anybody could put a question to, and he is the one nobody has put one to, and those are not two facts.** They are one fact and they have been one fact for about two years, and the reason they are one fact is the one above: putting a question to him would be the beginning of a document.

He has had a question ready for about two years. It takes about four seconds to say and it has been ready every week since, and he is not going to say it, and nobody has asked him to say it, and he is not going to be the eighth.

**The next asking is not his to make.** It is nobody's at the moment, and it is not going to become his because a week went by in a room over a shop, and the not making of it is the arrangement and not a decision he took.

---

He finished the column at about the sixth hour and copied it out again clean, and the man of forty will come back for it in about ten days and will not know his name, and the sheet is his own paper and not the shop's.

"Has anything ever hung on that nail?" he said it out loud at about the fifth hour, to the wall, about the nail. It is in the wall and it has been in the wall since the week he came, and nothing in this empire will let a nail be said to a person, and a man that nobody has asked anything is a man that nobody has asked anything about, and the count of things anybody has asked out loud in this matter is the same as it was when he got up and it is going to be the same in ten weeks.
