# Chapter 351: Four Steps And A Landing Over A Chandler's Shop, A Sheet On The Table From The Second Hour, A Nail In Four Feet Of Wall Nothing Has Ever Hung On, And A Column That Will Not Agree With Itself

The room is over a chandler's shop and the shopkeeper lets it by the week. The shop keeps tar and rope and lamp oil, and all three of them come up through the boards by the middle of the morning and stay there.

Four steps up from the street and then a landing, and a table on the landing, and a chair stood over against the wall the bed is against, and a window a foot wide that looks at the back of another house and at nothing else at all. Beside that window is four feet of bare plaster with one nail driven into it. The nail was in the wall before he came and it has not moved since, and nothing has ever hung on it.

He is thirty-four or thereabouts and the hand is a fair one. The fair hand is what he is paid for, and what it is paid for is the copying out of other men's figures until the two sets of them agree. A man has to stand on somebody's list for that. He stands on one four days a season, and this is not one of the four, and he did not put himself on this.

This is the second day of the first week of the first month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fifth hour, and the lamp in that room is not lit and is not going to be.

---

The man of forty came up the four steps on the first morning with the column.

He had it rolled round a stick and he unrolled it on the landing table and put his own book beside it, and the book was the same column three months back, and there was a figure at the foot of the fourth page he put his thumb on.

"The fifth line is the one I am afraid of," he said. "Everything above it is a copy of a copy. That line is mine."

He said it the way men say a thing they have already said to themselves in the lane on the way up. The clerk read the line. He read it twice, because a fair hand reads a thing slowly the first time and cannot help reading it faster the second.

"It is a figure you have written larger than the rest of you," he said, "and it is a figure that does not come to the same thing twice. Line two says one thing the week it covers. Line five says another, and the two cannot both be a week."

"You will tell me which of them is wrong."

"I will copy them as they are."

"That is not what I came for."

"No," said the clerk. "It is what you get for the fourpence."

The man of forty rolled the stick and put it under his arm and went down the four steps and did not say anything else, and the door at the bottom of them let a little of the tar up into the room, and after a while the tar went down again.

---

This week there is a sheet. It is eight hours of fair-hand work at a rate that is a rate and not a kindness, and it has been on that table since the second hour, and it is the same column the man of forty unrolled on the first morning.

The column does not agree with itself. The second line does not agree with the first, and the fifth does not agree with anything, and he has been at it since the second hour and he has found where it goes wrong, and he is not the man who is to say so out loud to anybody today.

He has a number in his head. He has had it two years, and he has never written it, and he has never said it, and nobody has ever come up those four steps and asked him for it.

The reason is not that he is shy it. Handing the number over would be a list. A list of persons is a company, and a company is a heading, and a heading is what a man turns into the moment the thing outgrows one room. A figure that stood over all the rooms at once would have to stand over him too, and there is no version of him that is still him underneath it.

---

There is no form for putting yourself on a list, and there is no form for being reached at a counter in a room you have left, and those are one absence and not two.

He came into this room off a counter at the top of four flights four miles off, where he paid fourpence for a copy, and before that he paid fourpence in a third room. Neither of those rooms is this one. A person who has been findable in three rooms in two years is findable in none of them this week.

**Nothing has ever come up these four steps for a copy, and the reason is not that nobody wants one.** The people who might have wanted one have about nine hundred rooms between them and him, and a person is in exactly one of them, and no paper in this empire says which.

He is still owed the bill for being findable. The bill is not his and does not stand against his wages and is on somebody else's account, and he has known that for two years, and he has not found out whose account it is, and he is not going to.

---

Three other things are owed to him, and he has stopped keeping them in order.

The first is the thing nobody has thanked him for, which is standing still while another man's figures get read out of him. **There is not one form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be thanked for being findable**, and he has never asked for the thanks and has never once decided that he wanted it.

The second is a form for asking a person whether they will mind being told a thing before they are told it. He has looked for one for two years in the places a man looks, and what is in those places is a form for telling and a form for saying that you have not been told.

The third is a woman at the foot of those four steps, four miles off, who is not thanked either, and who has said out loud that she is not going to ask him his week, and who is not thanked by him anything.

---

The count of things anybody has asked out loud in this matter is seven.

It was seven ten weeks ago, and it was seven the last time a man said a true thing standing up in a room with a rail in it, and it is seven now, and it will be seven in a month.

**He is the only person in all of this that anybody could put a question to, and he is the one nobody has put one to, and those are not two facts.** They are one fact and they have been one fact for two years, and the reason they are one fact is the one above: putting a question to him would be the beginning of a document.

He has had a question ready for two years. It takes about four seconds to say, and it has been ready every week since, and nobody has asked him to say it, and he is not going to be the eighth.

**The next asking is not his to make.** It is nobody's at the moment, and a week went by in a room over a shop, and the not making of it is the arrangement and not a decision he took.

---

He finished the column at the sixth hour and copied it out again clean, and the fifth line is in it exactly as the man of forty wrote it, and the fourth page is the fourth page of a book three months old with one figure in it a size the rest of the man is not.

The man of forty will come back for it in ten days and will not know his name, and the sheet is his own paper and not the shop's.

He turned the sleeve back on his left hand and put his thumb against the head of the nail in the wall, the way a man touches a thing he has decided not to do anything about, and then he went down the four steps and out past the shop, and the lamp in the room stayed unlit behind him.
