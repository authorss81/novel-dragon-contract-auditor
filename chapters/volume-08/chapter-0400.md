# Chapter 0400: The Counter At The Top Of Four Flights, A Woman Who Is Ready, And A Colleague Who Tells Her So

**It is the counter at the top of those four flights**, with the board on the wall behind it and the drawer under that board, and a hundred feet of floor and a stair between the near end of those boards and the street. There are four things in that drawer and not one of the four has ever been opened. **It has not been locked in eleven years and nobody at that counter has ever had a reason to offer a key to it.** The fourth of the four came out onto that counter at the fourth hour four weeks ago and went back in unopened, and every foot of that floor between the stair and the boards knows it was out, and the two women at that counter have not said one word about it to each other and are not going to.

**Tamsin Rook is her name and she is twenty-four**, and she has stood at that counter two years and is not a lawyer. **No one has ever put a question to her in that time.** The undertaking of the fourth of the first month is live and has never been retired and has never been exercised, and the four weeks since the fourth of the four things came out onto the wood have not touched it.

Under the far end of those boards there is a box holding about four hundred blanks that were bought and never filled in, and it has not been opened in six years, and it stands a quarter of an inch further forward than it stood three months ago, and nobody at that counter has moved it since the day it moved.

There is no form for a person being ready. Wanting something nobody has agreed to give her does not make her a person anybody can be called on. **That is true and it is not the same as being asked for.**

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the first month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the sixth hour, and the counter shuts at the sixth hour.

---

Marn Ottery had come up at the fifth hour and had not said anything for about a quarter of an hour, and then she had said it, once, and she was not going to say it again.

"You have been here twenty minutes early every working day for two years."

"I have."

"So have I, and mine is a habit, and yours is not a habit, and I have watched the difference in that room for about two years and I have not said a word about it." Marn Ottery did not touch the boards. "**A person who has been ready for two years has made herself the only thing in that room that can be said something to.** And I am saying something to you, and I am saying it as a woman telling a colleague and not as somebody with any standing to do one thing about it, and I want both halves of that heard together."

---

"You have said a third thing out loud to me in about a month and I have not asked you one question about any of the three."

"You have not, and you are not going to, and I have known that about you for two years." Marn Ottery turned the second chair out from the counter and did not sit on it. "**There is no form for readiness.** It cannot be entered on anything, it cannot be put in a column, and **the day somebody at the top of those four flights finds out that you are ready is the day you get used**, and being used is a thing you cannot be talked out of afterwards, because by then there is a form with your name at the top of it."

"I know what being used is."

"I know you know. That is the part I have been putting off saying for a month and I have said it now and I am not going to say it again." She left the chair where it was. "I have been in a box entered by a class for about a year and I know what the day is. I am saying it as one woman to another at the same counter, and not as somebody who can move anything, and I want that entered as well."

---

Tamsin Rook did not exercise the undertaking and did not retire it, and she did not say the date out loud, and she has not said it out loud in two years.

What she did was smaller than that. She looked at the clock at the end of that floor and it was a quarter past the fourth hour, and she had been on that floor since twenty minutes before the fourth hour for about two years.

She put her hand flat on the boards and left it there for a moment. Then she took her apron off the hook behind the counter and hung it up again, because she had come in early and there had been no need to.

"That is all I am going to do about it," she said.

"**That is not nothing,**" Marn Ottery said. "I want it entered that I have said it is not nothing, and I am not going to say it a second time, and you are not going to thank me for it, and if either of us starts on that I will go back to the other end of these boards and stay there."

---

Nobody has put a question to her at that counter in two years and nobody did this week and nobody is going to.

**It is live, it is unretired, and it has never been exercised.** The fourth of the four things is in the drawer where it has been for eleven years, and there are two of the four she has never opened and has never told anybody in that building that she has never opened them.

At the sixth hour that counter shut. Marn Ottery put the box back under the far end of it and did not open it, and the drawer was shut, and Tamsin Rook took her coat off the hook and went down four flights with her hand on the rail and there was nobody on any of them.

At the bottom the door was open and the lane had the six o'clock noise in it, and she went out into that with her hands empty. Nothing is asked of anybody, and the arrangement is still standing when she gets to the bottom of the steps.
