# Chapter 0384: Four Rooms Let, A Sheet Put Down On A Counter And Taken Off It, And Two Women

The house lets about four rooms by the week and all four are let this week, which is the best week the shelf has had in a long while. There is a counter at the bottom of the stair and a shelf behind it and the day-book stands on that shelf with its spine flush to the front edge.

She is about thirty-five and she has kept that book about nine years in her own hand. **There is a heading over every column in it — room, and week, and name, and what was paid — and there has never been a fifth column, and she has never once ruled one.** Two years ago a woman came nine miles to that step and stood on it and was not let in, and this woman heard the whole of it from behind this counter, and she has not written one word of it down anywhere, and nobody has asked her about it, and nobody is going to.

The girl of twenty-two is in the back room for her second season and pays by the week like the other three.

---

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the ninth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the sixth hour, and the afternoon had gone the way an afternoon in that house goes.

A clerk who keeps a book with its spine showing can see who has had it out without touching it, and that is the whole of the reason for the book standing so, and in about nine years of keeping it she has never once needed the reason and has never once found the shelf wrong. Four rooms a week go up the stair and the whole of what the house knows about itself is on one shelf behind the counter at the bottom of it, and a week when all four are let goes into the book in the same four lines as a week when none of them is, because a heading over a column does not take the state of a house into it.

There is one front door to the house and it is not a door anybody stands behind, and everything that is said at the counter at the bottom of the stair is said in the open air of a passage, and about four people a week come in off the lane and pay at the counter and go up, and not one of the four has ever been asked to say a word in the house on any afternoon since the book was bought. The arrangement amounts to one shelf and one counter and a stair, and of the three the stair is the only one where a person can be quiet, and the girl of twenty-two is the only one who uses it for anything except going up and coming down again.

The young man came in off the lane with a sheet of his own in his hand. He was not wet, which meant he had come a way that had cost him thought rather than weather. There is a day and a month at the head of it in a hand that is not his, and there is nothing else on it that he seemed to be able to read, and he put it down on the wood about a foot from her hand and then put both of his own hands flat on the counter either side of it and stood there.

**He did not say anything.** He looked at the shelf, and then he looked at it again, and the second time he looked he looked at the spine and not at the cover, and the woman of thirty-five understood all of that in about four seconds and did not move.

---

"You are not going to turn it round," said the woman of twenty-two from the back of the house.

"No."

"He has come in here to have you turn it round. That is what a person comes to a counter for."

"Then he has come in here to have me turn it round, and I am not going to, and neither of you is going to tell him I said that." She put her own hand flat on the day-book, which was still up on the shelf, and did not take it down. "**No form for a book turned round to face a person**, and there has not been one since the day the book was bought, and a book on that shelf has its own people's weeks in it and their names and what they paid, and turning it round on a stranger is handing a stranger the whole of this house for as long as it takes him to read one line."

"Then he is a person who wants something out of that book that has got his business on it."

"He is a person who has a day and a month at the top of a sheet and does not know what the day and the month are for, and there is no form in this empire for a woman behind a counter saying out loud what a day and a month are for, and I have not said it and I am not going to say it." She took her hand off the shelf. "And no form for turning a book twice. **A book that has been turned round once is a book that gets turned round twice**, and the second one will not be for the man who brought the sheet. The second one will be for me, and it will be for the woman in the back room, and it will be about a coming of somebody's, and I am not doing that to her and I am not doing it to me."

---

The young man stood at the counter for the length of about four minutes and she did not say one word, and at the end of it he picked the sheet up off the wood himself and put it inside his coat and said nothing at all about why he had come, and she did not say one word about why she had not turned the book round.

He went out. The door came to behind him and the girl of twenty-two came out of the back room and stood at the other end of the counter with her arms folded.

A season in the house is not a quarter and is not a figure anybody keeps, it is how long a person has been in one room paying one rate, and the day-book has her in it as a name under a week for every week she has been in the back room and would have her in it for every week she stays in it. Nobody in the house has ever put to her what she means to do about it, and she has never once said aloud that she has noticed how it is arranged, and the woman of thirty-five has not said so either and is not going to, and the two of them have got two seasons of a thing which has gone into neither pair of mouths and never will.

"He will be back," the girl said.

"I do not know that and neither do you, and I am not going to find out, and if he comes back he can come back and I will be behind this counter and the book will be where it is." She reached up and took the day-book off the shelf and set it down on the wood, open, at the front, with the spine to the front edge, and she squared it. "**It has been on that shelf for nine years and it is going to sit on this counter for the rest of today, and I have not read a line of it and neither have you, and that is the arrangement now**, and I would rather it was out where a person can see it than up there where only the two of us know it is."

---

The four headings and the ruled spaces under them were where they have always been and they did not move all afternoon. Nobody wrote anything in that book that was not a room and a week and a name and what was paid, and there was still no fifth column under any of them, and the woman of thirty-five did not rule one, and the girl of twenty-two did not ask her to.

A woman keeping a book nine years without once being asked what is in it has nine years of other people's weeks in front of her, and not one week of her own anywhere in the nine of them, and not a line of her own in the whole of the book, because a clerk's own week is the one week that does not go in. She has never ruled a space for one in nine years and she will not rule one this week either, and a full week at the top of a stair changes nothing about the woman behind the counter, who was there before the four of them came and will be there after they go.

The young man went up the lane at about the seventh hour with the sheet still in his coat and he did not turn round at the corner, and the woman of thirty-five stood behind her own counter afterwards with that book open on the wood in front of her, reading nothing at all in it.
