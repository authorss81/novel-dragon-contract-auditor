# Chapter 358: A Passage About Four Hundred Yards Long Off A Flood Terrace At The Back Of A Building Off The Old River Road, Racks Going Down It And Coming Back Light By One, A Lock At One End And A Room With A Rail At The Other, And A Woman Of About Twenty-Six Who Counts In Her Head

Off a flood terrace at the back of a building off the old river road there runs a passage about four hundred yards long, and the flags of it come up cold underfoot whatever month of the year it is, and no fire is lit at either end of it.

At the end she stands at there is a lock. At the other end there is a room with a rail in it standing about four feet off the floor, and that rail is not on the passage's work and has never been on it.

She is about twenty-six and she has been in that building about four years, and she is not one of the three clerks in it, and she is not in this and never has been.

This is the second day of the first week of the third month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour, and the racks go down all day and come back.

---

A rack went down at about the second hour and came back light by one sheet, and one is the usual number, and one is all it has been for the whole of the time she has stood at this end of the passage.

**She keeps the count in her head and has never once put a hand on a rack.** It has been about four years since she touched one, and nobody ever asked her to, and nobody has ever asked her anything at all.

**Her figure has gone up since the seventh month of the year after, and she has not said by how much and is not going to.**

She worked that out in about four minutes, standing at a lock, one time. **The figure is the only one this empire keeps on how many people are sitting inside a thing nobody can be asked about.** Everything else anybody counts here is a count of hands, or hours, or yards, or stone, or days, or persons under a heading. Hers is a count of persons under nothing.

---

Put a number in a book and you have made a column, and every column in this empire wants a heading standing over it, and a heading over that particular figure would be a heading over about nine hundred rooms at once.

**A person cannot be under a heading like that and stay a person**, and she is not going to be the one who puts it there, and it is not that a heading would be wrong. A heading is how this empire does its arithmetic, and it is the right instrument for most of what gets counted, and it is the one thing on earth that would take the last of the air out of about four hundred yards of cold flags.

So the figure stays where it is, which is in her, and it is not written down, and it is not going to be handed to anybody this month or in any month after it.

"There is no form for answering three questions a person has not asked, and I have three of them for a man of about sixty-one who stands on the wrong side of a rail at the far end of these flags, and he has been in that room and I have not been in that room, and I am not going to be."

"There is no form for repeating what was said in a room at the end of these flags, and I am not going to repeat it, and the not repeating is not loyalty to anybody and is not a thing I have been asked to do."

---

**Nothing that comes down those flags goes into that room, and nothing that comes out of that room comes up them.**

There is no list that reaches it and no rack that stops at it, and there is no person whose work takes her to that door. A person carrying things for a living has no reason to stop at a door and has nothing to carry back out of it, and that is the whole of the reason a thing said out loud in that room stays in that room.

**It is not because anybody is careful.**

The rail is about four feet off the floor and there is nothing on it this week, and it is not a thing anybody is standing ready for, and she has looked at it from four hundred yards off twice in four years and has not gone up.

---

**She is the only other person in this matter who knows what was said in that room.**

She was not in the room when it was said and she has never been in that room. About nine people were in it the last time anything was said in it, and people come out of a room like that and stand about four hundred yards apart in the cold and say things to each other, and she is at one end of that and is one of the people they say them to, and she has never once repeated any of it to anybody.

There is no form anywhere in this empire that would let anybody put to her the question of what she heard. That is the whole of what her knowing is worth, and it is worth nothing, and she would not give it away if there were a form for giving it away.

She has three questions for him, and the fact that he came at all gave her no fourth. It has never once given her a question of any kind, and she is not a party to anything, and no paper about any of it has ever been served on her.

"Is that rail still four feet?" she said it out loud at about the sixth hour, to the flags, about the rail. It is, and nothing in this empire will let a rail be said to a person, and a woman who keeps a count in her head and has never touched a rack is not somebody anybody has a use for, and the count of things anybody has asked out loud in this matter is the same as it was when the lock was still open and it is going to be the same in ten weeks.
