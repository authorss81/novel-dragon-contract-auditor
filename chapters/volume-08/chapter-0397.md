# Chapter 0397: Two Doors About Four Feet Apart, A Hall With A Door On It, And A Woman Who Is Not Going To Knock

There is one piece of paper in that hall that is not there now. It came through the letter slot in a week that is gone and it was on the floor of that hall through the whole of the week after, and it went in the middle of that week, and not one person in that lane knows who lifted it or where it has gone. One of those two houses is four steps up off the pavement and the other is one, and the second of the two has a door with two coats of paint on it that were done by somebody else, and the width of what is left between them is about four feet.

She has lived in that next house eleven years. She is about thirty-five and she keeps a day-book in her own hand. **There is a printed heading over every column in it, and the four of those headings are room, and week, and name, and what was paid.** No fifth column has ever been ruled in it and she has never once drawn the line that would make one, and for about nine years that book has stood on a shelf behind her own counter without a question of hers in it.

**She has knocked on that door twice in eleven years.** The first of the two was a stove that went out about nine years ago and she did not go in. The second was a piece of paper on a hall floor, and she said out loud that she would not be thanked for it and would rather he did not open his mouth about it, and she was not thanked.

**There is no form for the person who knocks.** It is not in the next house, it is not in this one, and it is nowhere in that lane at all, and a person who knocks once and says out loud that she will not knock twice has put herself somewhere she cannot get out of by standing still.

She has never once been inside that hall. She knows the shape of the steps and the landing and the door from her own step and from the sound that door makes, and in eleven years the inside of it has stayed a room in a house she lives four feet off.

It is the second day of the first week of the first month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the third hour.

Her own door was four steps off his and the gap between the two of them was about four feet of lane, and by the third hour the lane was doing what it does with everybody else in it going to work, and she had come out onto her own step with her hands empty and had not gone down it. There is a thing a person can do with a step they have not gone down of, and she had got a good deal of use out of it in the last eight weeks, and it is not the same thing as knocking. Of the two, it is the only one she can reach.

---

Two in eleven years is not a rate of anything. She had done both of them without deciding to and had not told anybody about either, and the first had cost a walk to a door she did not knock on and the second had cost a paper picked up off a floor and a sentence said out loud on a step in the rain, and the two of them together came to about nine years and eight weeks, and she had got through a working life on the strength of it, and nobody in the lane had any idea a rate existed.

He came down his four steps with his coat on and the lane was doing what it does at that hour, and he stopped at the bottom of them, which he does not do.

He had his coat on, which is a thing a man does at the third hour when he is going out and not a thing he does when he has come in, and the coat was the first half of a sentence she was not going to hear the end of. He stopped at the bottom of his four steps, which he does not do, and stood there with his face turned up the lane at an angle which had nothing whatever to do with her door and everything to do with the light.

"You are not going to knock," he said.

"I said in the rain that I would not knock twice and I have not."

"I know you have not and I have thought about it for eight weeks, and I have decided I would rather say it than not say it, and I have thought about the order of that." He did not come the four feet. "**I am not going to forget that you knocked on my door.** You knocked twice in eleven years and one of those was a stove and this one was not a stove, and I have had eight weeks of it. **There is no form for the second knock of the two, and a thing with no form on it does not get put down.**"

She had known all of it before he said it and had said it to herself in the rain at the time. What she had not known was that he had been carrying it for eight weeks, and a man who has carried a thing for eight weeks and then come down his own steps to hand it back is a man who has spent eight weeks deciding, and she had spent the same eight weeks deciding the other way, and not one word about what either of them was deciding had ever been said to a third party.

---

"I did not do it for you. I did it because a paper was lying on your floor and I could not get on with a thing at my own table."

"I know that and I am not thanking you and I am not going to." He had his hands in his coat pockets. "**The thing I am telling you is that you have made yourself responsible.** You said out loud on that step that you were not going to knock again, and saying a thing like that out loud in a lane is not the same as not doing it. **There is now a thing in this lane that you are not doing.** If you stop not doing it then something has changed, and I am the only person who would know, and I would know it in about four minutes and have no way of saying so to anybody."

"You would have the paper."

"I have not got the paper. Somebody in this lane has it and I do not know who and it is not on my account and it is not on anybody's account that either of us knows of." He took his hands out of his pockets and put them back. "I have not filled anything in and I am not going to, and there is a figure of mine that has never been on paper and it is staying that way, and I am telling you that so that you know the responsibility is the whole of what I am putting on you and not a part of it."

That was the half of it she had not expected to get. She had come out onto the step braced for a man who wanted the paper, and had got a man who wanted her to carry a thing he could not put anywhere, and the two of those do not weigh the same, and the one she got was the heavier and could not be set down in front of anybody.

---

"Then you have put it on the wrong person and I am not going to carry it."

"I know. I have thought about that as well and I have decided to put it on you anyway, and that is a thing I have done and I am not going to pretend I have not." He looked at the four feet of lane between them and did not step into it. "I am not able to do anything with it. There is nobody I could hand that to and there is nothing I could fill in, and if it is going anywhere it is going to sit on a person, and you knocked, and so it is you."

They stood exactly so until the light came off the wall opposite, and not one word passed between them about where he had been on his four days, and he had not been going to and she had not been going to, and there was about a yard and a half of lane between the bottom of his steps and the top of hers and neither of them put a foot into it, and what the hour was is not in any book either.

She stood on her own step and looked at the four feet and did not go into them, and he stood at the bottom of his four steps and did not come further. The two of them were in that lane at about a yard and a half of each other for as long as it took the light to change off the wall opposite. **Neither of them said one word about where he had been on his four days, and he had not been going to.**

---

**She did not knock.** She has a book on a shelf in that next house with four headings over it and no fifth, and it came down off that shelf in a week that is gone and stayed out on her own counter for a day and was not read, and it has been back on the shelf since, and nobody in that lane knows what is in it or what is not.

She went in and shut her own door, which is not the same as answering anybody, and stood in her own hall with her hands on the back of a chair. Her own hall had the other one in it the way a room has a thing in it that nobody has looked at, and she never went and looked, and there was nothing in her own hall she could have said one word about.

There is no form for not knocking twice, and she has not knocked twice, and the not knocking is now a thing she is answerable for, and she has said out loud once this week that she is not going to be thanked for it, and nobody has thanked her.

At the fourth hour that afternoon she went out of her own door and down her own step and across the four feet of that lane as far as the bottom of his four steps, and stood there, and did not go up them.

The light in that hall was on and the door with two coats of paint on it was shut. **She was at the bottom of those steps when somebody inside that hall moved a chair, and she went back the four feet and shut her own door, and there is nothing in this lane that anybody can be served with.**
