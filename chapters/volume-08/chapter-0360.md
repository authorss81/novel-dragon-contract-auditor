# Chapter 360: The Same Room On The Ground Floor, The Same Shelf Of Eleven Books Under A Window With The Pane Still Out Of It, About Four Thousand Entries And Not One Name In Any Of Them, And An Afternoon In Which Nothing Is Asked Of Anybody

The room is the same room and the shelf is the same shelf, and the window about two feet wide still has no pane in it and has not had one for about four years, and the books on that shelf are the same eleven.

She is about fifty-five and she has been in that room nineteen years, and she writes the minutes, and she advises nobody, and she asks nothing.

This is the fourth day of the third week of the third month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fifth hour, and there is nothing in that room to write down this afternoon.

---

**The arrangement that has kept about nine hundred sheets a year out of every book in this empire for nineteen years is the arrangement that has kept anybody from asking the man at the foot of them one question, and those are one fact and not two.**

She got to that in about four minutes, spread over about six years, in this room, with a pen on the table and nobody to say it to. It is not a discovery anybody could act on. It is a discovery about the shape of a thing.

A question put in that building is a question put into a record. The record is the shelf. The shelf is what the sheet goes in. And the last time a thing of that shape happened to about four hundred people, their sheet went back to them and they did not get an answer and nobody was punished and nothing was wrong with anybody's work.

She has never been told about that. She has read about four thousand entries that came off a bad season somewhere and she has entered them correctly, and she is not going to guess what those four hundred people were told, because guessing would be the first thing she has ever put in that room that was not hers to put there.

---

**A minute with an answer in it is an order.**

That is the whole of the second half of it, and it has held for nineteen years and there is no form that lets her enter one. A minute that records that a room stood empty is a minute. A minute that records what a man said standing up in front of about nine people, and what the man of sixty-one did with his face while it was being said, is not a minute. It is an instruction, and an instruction has to be carried out by somebody, and the somebody would have a name on it, and a name on it is a party.

So she has not entered one in nineteen years, and not entering one is not a rule anybody made, and it is the price of the only thing anybody has ever asked her to do, which is to be right.

Nobody has thanked her for that either. Nothing anywhere in this empire calls for thanks to a person for doing a thing properly, and the going without is nobody's decision and she has never once taken it for one.

---

Under that window, on that shelf, there is the finding of about six years of her own motion, and it is still in the form of a question, and it has not been turned into a sentence, and there is no full stop on it anywhere.

She wrote it with nobody's request behind it, and the day and the hour are on it, and her own name is at the foot, and it is the only thing on that shelf with a name at the foot. **She is the only person in this matter anybody could serve a question on in writing**, and she is paid nothing for standing ready to have it served.

A quarter of it was answered out loud in a room at the far end of her own passage, in the second month, by a man who walked nine miles to get there on his own two feet, and she entered none of that either.

Nobody is going to ask her about it a second time. She wrote her own question and she is not going to be asked it back, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for a person to ask themselves whether they would still have written it, and she has not asked, and the not asking is not modesty.

---

**She is not told anything about what a man said in a room at the far end of her own passage, and she wrote none of it down, and nobody is going to ask her why she wrote none of it.**

There is nothing in this empire that would let a clerk of an office be told that a person is coming, and there is nothing in it that would let one be told that a person is not.

So what she said out loud in this room, standing at her own table, is exactly as it stands, and **that is the price of the standing ready, and it is not a punishment and nobody laid it out, and she has never had the chance to find out whether she would do it again.**

Nobody in this matter could tell her which month either of the two occasions fell in, and she has not set the two occasions side by side, and setting them side by side would be a thing she did on her own account and she has not done it.

---

Four things went into the books between the fourth hour and the fifth hour this afternoon, and they went in correctly in a hand that does not vary, and the eleven books on that shelf stand at about four thousand entries and there is not one name in any of them.

That is not carelessness. A minute book has one shape and no other, and she is very good at that shape, and being good at it is the whole of what she is paid for.

"Is that pen still going?" she said it out loud at about the fifth hour, to the room, about the pen. It is, and it has been for nineteen years, and nothing in this empire will let a pen be said to a person, and the woman who stands ready at that shelf is not told what she is standing ready for, and the count of things anybody has asked out loud in this matter is the same as it was at the fourth hour and it is going to be the same in ten weeks.
