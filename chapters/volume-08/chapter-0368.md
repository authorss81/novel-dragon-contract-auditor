# Chapter 368: A Ground-Floor Room, A Shelf Of Eleven Books, And A Sheet That Is Not What The Man Above Wrote

It is a ground-floor room in a building off the old river road. A shelf of eleven books stands under a window about two feet across that has been open to the weather for about four years, and there is a table, and there is a chair, and some four thousand entries have gone into those books and not a name appears in one of them.

He is about sixty-one. Since he was forty-two his hand has been the second one on every sheet, and it goes at the foot of about nine hundred of them a year. **A paragraph is a shape to him. A figure, a name and a handful of short words come through, and the rest of a sheet is a thing he can feel the weight of and not read.**

This is the fourth day of the fourth week of the fifth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the second hour, and the four that came up the flags were entered by the fourth.

---

**About nine of those sheets a year are not what the man above him wrote, and he signed all of them.** He signed them because he was not asked. Nobody else alive has any idea which ones those were, and he cannot put one of them in writing, and he is not going to count them out loud either, because a count is a number another person could carry.

There was one in front of him this week and it was not what the man above wrote it, and he could not have told anybody how he knew, and nobody asked him, and he put his hand at the foot of it.

There is no form for disagreeing with the person above in your own hand, and there is none anywhere that would let a man who cannot read a paragraph set his mark beside a figure and call the two of them a quarrel. No form for being told that a sheet was not as written either, and none for the man who has been told and has to go on signing it, and a thing said across a basket at the fourth hour is not a record of anything except that somebody said it. He has known that since the fourth year of it. **What he wants is about four minutes: to go through the ones he can remember and say no to about half and yes to the rest, and there is nobody to ask.**

---

The young man of about twenty-six who takes the sheets away came at about the fourth hour with a basket and initialled the corner of each one in a small hand, and he has been doing it two years and he has never read one of them.

"That one is not what the man above wrote it," the man of about sixty-one said, out loud, to a room with a window in it.

The young man did not look up. "I have initialled the corner of about nine hundred of these a year for two years and I have not read one and I am not going to start this week."

"I am telling you it is wrong."

"I know you are telling me. **You have told me a thing about a page and I am paid for a corner and not for a page.** If you had said that to a clerk I should have had to have written it somewhere, and there is nowhere on this basket to write it."

"You are the person I am talking to."

"You are talking to the only person in this room, which is not the same thing, and that is not me being hard, that is me being the shape of the job." He put the sheet in the basket with the others and squared it. "It goes down that corridor and does not come back up, and I initialled the corner of it."

---

He reads the little words and signs the rest, which is the whole of the trade of that hand.

About nine hundred of those go out of that room in a year and nobody has ever counted how many, which is a different thing from nobody having counted. A carrier came up the flags at about the fourth hour with a rack on her hip, set four sheets down on the shelf under the window and went back down the flags. She is on a list and she is paid ninepence at the lock. In six years nobody has asked her what she carries, and he has not asked her either, and in all that time neither of them has ever put a question to the other.

He carried the basket out and the door at the far end of the passage was open and the flags at the top end of it were loud for a minute and then were not.

Those four were entered before the fourth hour, all four of them, in a hand that has not varied in nineteen years. **One of them is in a basket going down a corridor and is not coming back up to that shelf.**

He has spoken a whole thing out loud in that room more than once, and this week he put one sentence of it into a young man paid for a corner. **Nobody has thanked him, and he knows both halves of that, and he has never put the two halves together in his life.**

A person who is not asked has not been given a duty, and he has known that for nineteen years, and there was a sheet in front of him this week that would have taken him four minutes and there was nobody in the building to give it to, and the pane is still out of that window and nobody has put it back.
