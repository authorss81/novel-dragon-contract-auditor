# Chapter 366: The Counting Room At The Back Of A Dye End, A Stove Let Go Out, And A Rule Said Out Loud A Second Time

This is the other end of the same dye end, and the counting room behind it is the same eleven feet by fourteen, with the bench down one wall, and a window with a broken light in it, and some forty volumes standing on shelving that a taller book would have suited. Whoever got in first lit the stove behind the bench and went down the lane again, and it is out.

**She is twenty and her name is Nell Kest, and sixteen volumes came into that room and settled on her, and fifteen of them are on loan from elsewhere**, and the sixteenth has her own name standing at the head of it and has never had a question set against it.

This is the second day of the second week of the fifth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and the stove has been out since before she came in, and it is about the second hour.

---

**One of the four came through that door with her shawl over her ears, and she is the one Nell told.** The third of the four is a mile off on a lane of her own and the fourth is in a district, and neither of them has been spoken to this month.

"You have not said it again," the second woman said, and set the day's numbers on the bench where they are set.

"No."

"I have been carrying four of my own since the morning you said it and I have had two of them stop, and I have not got the second one back, and I did not have it before you told me either."

"Then it went nowhere then and it will go nowhere now."

"It went nowhere because there was one of us in it." The second woman turned round and leaned on the bench with both arms. "I am not asking you to tell me something new. **I am asking you to say a true thing a second time, and there is not one form anywhere that lets a person ask for that.**"

---

"I am not going to say it again," Nell Kest said, "and the reason is about you and not about me."

"Go on."

"A rule said once to one person is a thing one person is carrying. **A rule said twice to the same person is a person somebody can be served with.** I am not going to be the reason one of the four of yours becomes a person a question could be put to."

"I have been carrying it since a morning you are not going to date, and about four of mine stopped the same week and I never knew which of the four it was, and that is four days of a woman's work I have had to do twice." She did not move off the bench. "You are telling me you will not say a true thing twice because of what it makes me. I have four numbers in my head I cannot account for and no form anywhere to put a mark beside, and I would rather have it twice and be in trouble than carry it once and be alone with it."

---

So she said it over again. It took about four minutes, the stove was dead the whole of it, and not one word came out differently the second time than it had the first. The second woman took out nothing to write with, because **that room has never had a place ruled for a rule and does not have one now.**

"It is in two heads in one lane now instead of one," the second woman said, at the end of it. "**That is what your four minutes bought.** I am not going to say it to a third one and you are not going to either, and a person who says a rule twice is a person who says rules."

"I am not going to say it to a third one."

"I know. That is why I am telling you that I heard it the second time and not the first."

---

The eleven houses off that lane send their numbers in on the first of the two days and get eleven numbers back on the second, and she writes all of it in a hand that has not varied in three years. There is a figure in the fourth column she cannot account for and has never touched, and she will not touch it, because the day a counting room starts correcting figures is the day it starts having opinions.

Her pay stopped arriving in the second week of the third month of the year after and has not come since. It is nineteen days of a crew's money, and it is sitting in a coat four miles off, and the man holding it cannot say what it is sitting against.

**She is not going to ask him what it is sitting against, and she decided that before this week.** A question would be answered, and the two of them would then be parties, and an answer is a record, and records go into sheets. She is not going up this river this season, and this season is not never, and she has not used the word never and is not going to.

**Not one person has ever come up those stairs for that book, and nobody has sent for it, and nobody ever has.** She took it down at about the fourth hour, shut it again without opening it, set it back in with the other fifteen, and she is not going to stand ready for somebody who does not come.
