# Chapter 357: A Room On The Ground Floor Of A Building Off The Old River Road, A Shelf Of Eleven Books Under A Window About Two Feet Wide With A Pane Out Of It, A Table, A Chair, And A Man Of About Sixty-One Signing At The Foot Of Every Sheet

The room is on the ground floor of a building off the old river road and it has a shelf in it with eleven books on the shelf, and a table, and a chair, and a window about two feet wide that has been without a pane for about four years and has not been put back by anybody.

He is about sixty-one and he signs at the foot of every sheet that goes into those books, and he has been the second hand since he was forty-two.

This is the fourth day of the fourth week of the second month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the second hour, and the four that came up the flags this morning were entered by about the fourth hour.

---

**He cannot read a paragraph.** He can read a figure and he can read a name and he can read most of the short words that hold a sheet together, and everything else in front of him is shape, and he has been working out which is which for nineteen years and has got it about as right as anybody could expect a man to get it.

He reads what he can and he signs the rest. That is the whole of what his hand does and it is done about nine hundred times a year.

He signs for people who cannot write and he signs for people who are not in the room. **Nobody standing at a counter has ever asked him whether the person named above his own hand is in the building, and nobody could, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that lets anybody ask.**

---

There is a figure in his head about how many times he has come to that room in nineteen years.

It is a figure about times and not about distance, and the distance is about nine miles, and he knows the distance to about a yard because he has walked it more than once and has never once been driven.

He has never written the figure down and he is not going to. He has said the whole of a thing out loud in that room more than once and a clerk of about fifty-five was in the room and wrote none of it, and **what is said in that room is not in the eleven books and about four thousand entries in those books have no name in any one of them and none of those is this.**

**Nobody is going to ask him what he meant by any of it, and he is not going to be asked, and the not being asked is the arrangement and not a thing that has happened to him by an accident.**

---

About nine of the sheets a year are not what the person above him wrote.

In about nineteen years of putting his hand at the foot of a thing he cannot read, about nine of them a year have not been what the person above him wrote, and he signed every one of those, and he signed them because he was not asked and was given nothing to sign against.

**He is the only person alive who knows which ones they were and he cannot write one of them down and has never told anybody.**

"There is no form for believing a thing you cannot read, and I have not got one and I have not been asked for one, and the nine of them are in my hand and not on any paper anywhere and that is the whole of my life as far as this goes."

"There is no form for marking a sheet as not being what the person above me wrote it to be. I could put my mark beside a sum. I could put my mark on the back of my own hand. **There is nowhere in this empire for a man to put a mark beside a sum and call it a disagreement**, and it is not an oversight in the making of the page, and I have known that since about the fourth year of doing it."

---

What he wants is about four minutes.

In about four minutes he could go through the ones of that year that he can remember and say no to about half of them and yes to the rest, and the yeses are as important as the noes and both of them are about nine minutes of a man's life once every week or so for nineteen years.

**And there is nobody to ask.**

On every one of about nine hundred sheets a year there are three things that can be asked about it. Two of them are a clerk and an office. Neither of those is a person anybody can put a question to, and both of them are the only two of the three that are in the room at the time.

**That leaves him, and he is the one nobody has asked anything in about nineteen years.**

He is not in a bay four hundred yards off any lane and he is not one of about four men in one who cannot make sense of a paragraph. He cannot read a paragraph and he is nine miles off in a different building, and a list that had him in it with those four would be a list of five and there is no such list in this empire and there is not going to be one.

---

Nobody has ever thanked him.

He has never asked to be thanked and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be thanked for saying a true thing out loud in a room with nobody in authority in it.

**He knows both halves of that and has never put them in one sentence**, and there is no form for putting two halves of a thing together, and the not putting them together is not modesty and is not bitterness and is the only piece of administration he has ever been able to do.

---

A carrier came up the flags at about the fourth hour with a rack on her hip and put four on the shelf under the window and went back down. She is paid ninepence at the lock and she is on a list, and nobody has asked her what she carries in about six years, and this man has not asked her either, and the two of them have never once asked each other a thing in all that time.

He put his hand at the foot of four sheets between the second hour and the fourth hour and the four went into the books and about four thousand entries in eleven books on that shelf have not one name in any of them.

"Is that pane still out?" he said it out loud at about the fourth hour, to the window, about the pane, and it is out, and it has been for about four years, and nothing would let him ask a glazier to put one in on a district's account, and he is a man nobody has asked anything in about nineteen years, and the count of things anybody has asked out loud in this matter is the same as it was at the second hour and it is going to be the same in ten weeks.
