# Chapter 357: A Room On The Ground Floor Of A Building Off The Old River Road, A Shelf Of Eleven Books Under A Window About Two Feet Wide With A Pane Out Of It, A Table, A Chair, And A Man Of About Sixty-One Who Signs At The Foot Of Every Sheet

The room is on the ground floor of a building off the old river road. There is a shelf in it, and eleven books stand on that shelf, and there is a table, and a chair, and a window about two feet wide that has been without a pane for about four years and that nobody has put back.

He is about sixty-one, and he signs at the foot of every sheet that goes into those books, and he has been the second hand since he was forty-two.

This is the fourth day of the fourth week of the second month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the second hour, and the four that came up the flags this morning were entered by about the fourth hour.

---

**He cannot read a paragraph.** He can read a figure and a name and most of the short words that hold a sheet together, and everything else in front of him is shape, and he has been working out which is which for nineteen years and has got about as far as anybody could expect a man to get.

He reads what he can and signs the rest, and that is the whole of what his hand does, and it does it about nine hundred times a year.

He signs for people who cannot write and he signs for people who are not in the room. **Nobody standing at a counter has ever asked him whether the person named above his own hand is in the building, and nobody could, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that lets anybody ask.**

---

There is a figure in his head about how many times he has come to that room in nineteen years.

It is a figure about times and not about distance, and the distance is about nine miles, and he knows it to about a yard because he has walked it more than once and has never once been driven.

He has never written the figure down and he is not going to. He has said the whole of a thing out loud in that room more than once. On the one occasion anybody has written down, he was in a room with about nine people in it and a clerk of about fifty-five with a pen on a table, and she wrote none of it. **None of it is in the eleven books at the top end of that passage, and about four thousand entries in those books have no name in any one of them, and none of those is this.**

Nobody is going to ask him what he meant by any of it, and he is not going to be asked, and the not being asked is the arrangement and not a thing that has happened to him by accident.

---

About nine of the sheets a year are not what the person above him wrote.

In about nineteen years of putting his hand at the foot of a thing he cannot read, about nine of them a year have not been what the person above him wrote, and he signed every one of those, and he signed them because he was not asked and was given nothing to sign against.

**He is the only person alive who knows which ones they were, and he cannot write one of them down, and he has never told anybody.**

"There is no form for believing a thing you cannot read, and I have not got one and nobody has ever asked me for one, and those nine are in my hand and on no paper anywhere, and that is the whole of my life as far as this goes."

"I could put my mark on the back of my own hand, and there is nowhere in this empire to put one beside a sum and call it a disagreement. **There is no form for marking a sheet as not being what the man above me wrote it to be**, and it is not an oversight in the making of the page, and I have known that since about the fourth year of doing it."

---

What he wants is about four minutes.

In about four minutes he could go through the ones of that year he can remember and say no to about half of them and yes to the rest, and the yeses matter as much as the noes, and the two of them together are about nine minutes of a man's life once a week or so for nineteen years.

**And there is nobody to ask.**

On every one of about nine hundred sheets a year there are three things that can be asked about it. Two of them are a clerk and an office. Neither of those is a person anybody can put a question to, and both of them are the only two of the three that are in the room at the time.

**That leaves him, and he is the one nobody has asked anything in about nineteen years.**

He is not in a bay four hundred yards off any lane and he is not one of about four men in one who cannot make sense of a paragraph. He cannot read a paragraph and he is nine miles off in a different building, and a list with him in it alongside those four would be a list of five, and there is no such list in this empire and there is not going to be one.

---

Nobody has ever thanked him.

He has never asked to be thanked, and nothing anywhere in this empire calls for thanks to a person who says a true thing out loud in a room that has nobody in authority standing in it.

**He knows both halves of that and has never put them in one sentence**, and there is no form for putting two halves of a thing together, and the not putting them together is not modesty and is not bitterness and is the only piece of administration he has ever been able to do.

---

A carrier came up the flags at about the fourth hour with a rack on her hip, put four on the shelf under the window and went back down. She is paid ninepence at the lock and she is on a list, and in about six years nobody has asked her what she carries. This man has not asked her either, and neither of the two of them has ever put a question to the other one in all that time.

He put his hand at the foot of four sheets between the second hour and the fourth hour, and the four went into the books, and about four thousand entries in eleven books on that shelf have not one name in any of them.

"Is that pane still out?" he said it out loud at about the fourth hour, to the window, about the pane. It is out, and it has been for about four years, and nothing would let him ask a glazier to put one in on a district's account, and he is a man nobody has asked anything in about nineteen years, and the count of things anybody has asked out loud in this matter is the same as it was at the second hour and it is going to be the same in ten weeks.
