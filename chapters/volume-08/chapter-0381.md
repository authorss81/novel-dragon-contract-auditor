# Chapter 0381: A Column With Nothing Over It, And A Clerk Who Has Said It Once

The third one is the one he writes at. A sheet of the Company's fool's-cap is on the table in front of him with a line drawn down it three times in his own hand, and there is nothing printed over any of the three, and it is not a page out of any of that Company's books and it is not a page out of anything.

**There is no form for a column with nothing printed over it**, and there never has been one, and a sheet with three lines on it and nothing over the lines is not a page of anything in the whole of this empire, and the ruling of it took him the length of one evening in a room by himself.

He is thirty-eight. **His name is Rennick Adley, and he has kept the books of that Company since the second month of next year, and there is no office under him and no fund and no list and no leave.** He is the only person in that room whose figures are answerable to somebody, and he is answerable to nobody in the room, and the arrangement has not troubled him for two years, which is a fact about him and not about the arrangement.

The room is at the top of a lane off the Slade. Two tables stand pushed together under a window that looks straight at the wall of the house across the lane, so the light in there is borrowed and comes in sideways and has gone by the fifth hour. At the other end of the joined tables there is a clerk of about twenty-nine who came into that room in the spring and who is quick at a column and slower at anything that is not one.

He has wanted the rest of one sentence since the third month of the year after. He has never asked for it and is not going to, and there is a woman of fifty-one at the Slade end who is in a box that a class put her in and who has not been asked a thing since, and who gave him half of one of them in a doorway in a month that is gone. The half came out in under two seconds and it has been two seconds ever since.

**Four lines of it, in his own hand, at the top of the third column, and under the four lines there is nothing at all.** Not a rule and not a figure and not a mark, and the ruled space below runs to the bottom of the page and is about two inches deep and is there for nothing.

---

"You are at the third one again," said the clerk at the other end.

"I am at the third one."

"You are at the top of the third one. There is a difference, and I have watched the top of it get an inch further down every week since the spring." She had not got up from her end and she turned her head to look at the sheet across the joined tables. "**A column with nothing printed over it is a thing a person can be handed, and anybody who comes into this room can be handed that sheet, and whoever hands it over does not have to say a word while they do it.**"

"Then nobody hands it over."

"Nobody hands it over because you have not put it anywhere anybody else can reach, and that is a decision you have made, and it is not a decision this room asked you for." She went back to her column. "I have worked out that a sheet with nothing over the top of it is a sheet with a thing in it that has a person in it, and that is the only reason anybody would rule a line and leave the space above it empty. A ledger's got a line and a name. A counter's got a line and a day. What you have got is a line and nothing, and I have been at this end of these tables since the spring and I have not once seen the like."

---

He put the nib down in the groove it belongs in, which is a thing he does and which takes about a second and which he has never once done in front of anybody.

"I have put a woman's words at the top of it," he said. "That is the whole of what is on there."

"I know what is on there. I have read it upside down from this end since the spring and I am not going to tell you what I have made of it, and you can put that in the same place as the rest." She squared her own sheet, which did not need squaring. "Here is the part I am going to say, and then I am not going to say it again. There is no form for the rest of a sentence, and there has not been one in this room since before either of us, and I have not looked for one and neither have you. You have been writing at the top of that column for two years. You have never once said out loud, in this room, to me or to anybody, whether the half you have got is the whole of what she said to you. And I am not asking you that, because a question in this room is a record, and a record is what the sheet goes in. **A not saying that goes on for two years is a thing two people know about.**"

---

He did not answer it, and the room went on being a room with two tables in it under a window that looked at a wall.

"Then that is the arrangement," the clerk said, "and I want it said out loud once, so that neither of us is doing it by accident. **I have said one thing this week and the not carrying is mine and not yours**, in the same way that the not asking is yours and not mine. I am not going to say a second thing about that sheet in this room, and I am not going to carry it out of here to the yard or to the lane or to my own kitchen, and I would like you to hold the two halves of that sentence and not mix them up."

"I understand it."

"I do not think you do, and it does not matter, and I am not going to spend the fifth hour of the day on it." She drew the last of her column up to the line and did not look at him again. "You have got about a month of that column left and then the sheet is full and you will rule another one, and I have thought about that more than I have thought about the figures."

---

It is the second day of the first week of the ninth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and the third hour had not got into the room yet, and the two of them had said perhaps ninety words to each other in the whole of it.

No form anywhere covers a sheet a person rules for himself and shows to nobody, and two years is not long enough to get used to the amount of room the ruling leaves under four lines. A column with nothing printed over it is a place a figure could go if a figure were ever going to be put there, and no figure has been put there since the spring, and nobody has given a reason out loud for it, him included, and nobody has ever stood inside the room with the standing to ask him for one.

The wall opposite gives back what little light there is, and it comes in flat along the line where one table stopped and the other began, and it gives out about halfway across the floor, and it had been doing precisely this from the spring onwards without either of them saying one word about it. A room where the light gives out across the boards is a room in which a person can tell how much of the day is left without looking at anything. He put a thumb along the edge of the sheet to keep it flat and took it off again, which he had done more than once in the hour, and the writing at the head of the third column did not move when he did it.

She finishes her column the same way every day, which is to draw the last line up to the line and then go back over the three above it and then stop, and he has watched her do it every day since she came into the room in the spring and has never once mentioned it. Two people at one set of tables for two years is something that could have been arranged and was not, and neither has ever put the not arranging into words. A person entitled to ask could move a clerk to the far side of a room, and nobody in the Company is entitled to ask it, and nothing in this matter has a form of any kind for what the two of them have instead.

There are about four hundred yards between the top of the lane and the box at the Slade end, and he has been to the foot of them more than once, and the whole of what a person can do with four hundred yards in this matter is walk them. Being there is being served, and a person who is served is a party, and a party is the only kind of person who can be told what a thing is going to be used for, and so the yards are the easy part, and he has known it for two years, and it has not once made him go.

He folded the sheet once, which put the crease through the four lines, and then he opened it out again and smoothed it flat with the flat of his hand from the bottom up, and he put it in the inside of his coat on the side the other paper is not on.

The clerk at the other end of the joined tables wrote the last figure in her column, set the pen in its groove, and did not turn round. He put it away and shut the lid of the desk, and the clerk at the far end of the joined tables went on counting under her breath, and there are two people in that Company tonight who know whose words are at the top of that third column, and one of them did not know that this morning.
