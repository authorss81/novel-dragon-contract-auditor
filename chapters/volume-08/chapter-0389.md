# Chapter 0389: Two Doors About Four Feet Apart, A Form On A Hall Floor, And A Woman Who Knocks

There is one piece of paper in that hall and it came through the letter slot that morning. The house has four steps up off the pavement to a landing and a door at the top with two coats of paint on it, both done by somebody else, and the next house is about four feet off with a step of its own, and the lane between them is the width of two doors.

Thirty-four, give or take, and paid for a fair hand: he copies other men's figures until two sets of them agree, four days a season, and only when somebody's list has his name on it. He has been on that list four days this month and came back on the second of the two days. **He is the one person anybody in this matter could put a question to, and not one has been put to him.** What he has is a figure of his own that he has never written and never handed on, and it stays his because the moment a second head holds it, it is a column, and a column wants a heading, and a heading over that would come down on him the same as on everybody else.

The form is the size of a sheet of writing paper. It has a ruled space in the middle of it about an inch deep and a heading printed over the space in type, and there is a line at the foot for a day, and there is nothing at the top of it at all.

---

He read the heading and then he read the three words that are not in the heading and understood that whoever set the type had had a choice of four and had taken a few minutes over it.

**The heading is a word anybody could use.** It is the kind of word a person picks out of three others in a few minutes while thinking about a column and not about anybody, and it has been set in type at somebody's cost, and the space under it is ruled to the width of a figure and not to the width of a name, and the figure he has in his head is the only thing in this empire that would go in it.

He put it back down on the hall floor where it had come through the slot.

"There is no form for a word picked out of three others," he said, to the hall, on his knees. "**And there is no form for a figure of my own on a piece of paper with somebody else's heading over it**, because the day that space is filled in it stops being a thing about me and becomes a thing about a column, and I have had that figure in one head for two years and I am not going to be the reason there are two."

---

She knocked at the third hour, which is a thing she has not done in eleven years.

The woman of about thirty-five who lives in the next house stood on his step in the rain with her hand still up and did not come the last step, and the four feet of wet lane was behind her, and the woman at the foot of his own four steps was inside with the door on the latch and stayed inside.

"I have knocked twice in eleven years on that door," she said, "and the other time was a stove that went out. I am not going to stand here and pretend that this is a stove."

"I know what it is and you know I know, and that is the whole of why you have knocked."

"It is a piece of paper on your hall floor and you have not picked it up in three hours, and I have not been able to get on with a thing at my own table." She put her hand down. "**No form for a form left where a person can find it.** That is not in the next house and it is not in this one and it is not in this lane, and I have found that out from standing on this step in the rain three times over the last month. A form left in a hall is a form somebody will come back for. They do not come back for the figure. They come back for the paper, and they look at the paper, and whatever is or is not on the paper is a fact about you by then, and you filled it in or you did not, and either way it is your fact and it is on a sheet with a heading over it."

"I have not filled it in."

"I can see that you have not filled it in, and I could see it from my own step, and you are not the first man on this lane to leave something in a hall and I am not asking you about the other one." She did not step back. "**I have been next door eleven years and I have watched you not do things, and I have never once said a word about any of it, and I have said a word about this one, and I am not going to come back next week and say a second one.**"

---

He did not tell her where he had been, and she did not ask, and neither of them said one word about the four days or the list or the figures. The not asking was the older of the two arrangements and it came first.

"You have knocked on that door twice in eleven years," he said.

"Twice in eleven years, and the other one was nine years ago, and it was a stove and I did not come in." She turned about on the step. "I am not doing that again, and I am not doing it every week, and I would like it understood that this is a thing I have done once and am not going to be a person who does it twice. And I am not going to be thanked for it, and I would rather you did not open your mouth about it, because a thank you is a debt and I have not come here to be owed one."

He shut the door and did not open it again that week, and the piece of paper stayed on the hall floor of that house where it had come through the slot.

---

It is the second day of the first week of the eleventh month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the third hour, and the weather is coming off the eaves of both those doors.

He put his shoes away and his coat on the hook and went down the lane at the fourth hour with his hands empty. The paper was still on that floor on the second day and it was gone by the middle of the week after, and nobody in that lane knows who took it or where it went. Four months from now somebody turns that sheet over at a desk. The space under the heading is blank, the heading does not say whose it was, and the man who could have written in it will be at a different counter that day.
