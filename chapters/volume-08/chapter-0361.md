# Chapter 361: The Counter At The Top Of Four Flights, A Man Standing At The Rail, And A Week Of Bad Weather

This counter is the top of four in a Table in Auremar, and four flights of a stair are the whole of the way anybody gets to it. There is no door at the foot of that stair and there has not been one for longer than the building has had a name anybody uses. Behind the counter there is a board on the wall, and four things are written on that board, and a clock is not one of the four. The hours are the second to the sixth.

He is thirty-four or thereabouts. The hand is a fair one and the work is copying out other men's figures until two sets agree, four days a season, on somebody's list, and this is not one of the four days. No list anywhere has him on it this week, and he did not go looking for one.

**He is the only person here that a question could ever be put to, and no question has ever been put to him.**

There is a figure in his head that he has never written down and has never said out loud to a person. Handing it over would turn it into a heading, and a heading would stand over him as well, and that is the whole of the reason he keeps it.

This is the second day of the first week of the fourth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and the weather is against the people who come up, and it is about the third hour.

---

He came up those four with his hand in his coat and nothing in the other one. He has stood at that rail twice in his life and paid fourpence for a copy on each occasion, and roughly two years have gone by since the second of them, and the price of a copy has not moved.

Marn Ottery is at the other end of that counter. She is thirty-four and she has been on it eleven years and she is not a lawyer, and she looked at him for the length of a breath and went on with what she was doing.

"You have come up four flights in this with nothing to buy," she said. "**I said the shape of a thing out loud in this room twice, and I have been asked one thing back.**"

"I have not come for that."

"I know you have not. That is what makes it worse." She squared a sheet on the wood with the flat of her hand. "A thing said once costs me nothing. A thing said twice to the same man is a thing with a shape."

"Then I will not be one."

"You cannot help it. You have been in the room, and being in the room is being in it." She put the sheet down. "**There is no form for occupying a counter, and a man can stand at this rail for eleven years and nobody in this empire has to account for it.**"

---

She said that out loud to him and not to the room, and then she went on with the counter, and he stayed where he was.

He had not come for a shape. He had come because a counter is the only room in this empire where a man can stand for a week and be in it and be spoken to and not be asked one thing, and because the weather would keep him off a lane for an hour and would not keep him off four flights.

He did not tell her that and she did not ask him for it, and those are two different facts and only one of them is an arrangement.

---

"About nine people come up that stair on an ordinary day," Marn Ottery said. "That is not a boast and it is not a trade. It is what a stair with no door at the foot of it is for."

"And today."

"Today I have had four, and one of the four wanted nothing and left." She said it to the counter. "The ones who do not come up on a day like this are not deciding anything about the weather. They are the ones who have furthest to come. There is a woman who comes off the flood side and she does not come in this, and one of the two off the lane that goes down to the river road is at the bottom of it in the wet, deciding."

"You could send for them."

"**There is no form for sending for a person, and no form for a woman who did not come up, and I have not said one word to any of the four.**"

He had not asked her to do anything. That was the thing he noticed, standing at that rail with the rain coming off the landing window and running along the flags. **She had told him he was standing in the place where four people decide, and she had said it in a room, and nobody in the room had to do a single thing about it.** The four of them were still at the bottom of their own lanes.

---

On the third day a woman of about fifty came up the four flights in it and paid fourpence for a copy of a form about a week of wages. Marn Ottery made the copy in a minute and a half and read it out to her, and the woman said that was right and put the money on the wood and went down.

That is the whole of the third day. The man at the rail bought nothing and was not served and was not asked for anything.

On the fourth day the weather got worse. Two people came up the four flights, and one of them was a boy of fifteen who stood at the wrong end of the counter with a copy in his hand and read it and bought nothing and went down again.

**He has a wife at the foot of four steps about four miles off, and she has not asked him where he has been this week.**

No form in this empire gives a man a way of putting to his wife the one question of whether she would rather hear a thing first, and he has looked for one for about two years, and it is one absence and not two, and it is why he is standing at a rail on a wet morning instead of on a wall at the sixth hour.

---

The counter shut early on the fifth day, at about the fifth hour, which is an hour earlier than the board says and is not on the board. Marn Ottery shut it because of the state of the stairs and told the two people at the rail that she had shut it, and neither of them said one word back about that.

He went down the four flights with his hand in his coat. There is a landing at the bottom of them and the stair has no door at the foot of it, and a man standing at the bottom of a stair with no door is standing where anybody coming up has to go round him.

He stood there for the length of a coat being put on and then he was not standing there.

**The stair has no door at the foot of it, and there is nowhere at the bottom of it for a man to stand that is not in the way of somebody going up.**
