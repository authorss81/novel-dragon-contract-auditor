# Chapter 369: Two Doors About Four Feet Apart On A Lane, A Woman At The Foot Of The Steps, And Nothing Said Between Them

The house with a door on it is four miles off, and four steps come up off the pavement to a landing, and the door at the top has had two coats of paint on it since he moved in and both of them were done by somebody else. The next house is about four feet off with a step of its own, and the lane between them is the width of two doors and always has been.

Thirty-four or thereabouts, a fair hand, and a trade in copying out other men's figures so that two sets come out even. There is no list this week with his name against it, and he has not gone asking after one. The woman at the foot of the four steps is his wife. The woman of about thirty-five who lives in the next house is a different person and has been next door eleven years.

This is the second day of the first week of the sixth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and the weather is coming off the eaves of both of those doors, and it is about the fifth hour.

---

**Nothing is decided between those two doors, and the price of two households about four feet apart is that neither of them can be the one who starts.**

**He has not told her where he has been this week and he is not going to tell her**, and there is a figure in his head he has never written down and has never given to anybody. The figure is the reason he does not tell her, and the not telling is the reason she does not ask.

"I have not asked you and I am not going to," she said, from the bottom of the four steps. "I have said that out loud before and I am saying it again because of what I am about to say next, and you should know that the next part is mine and not yours."

"Say it."

"**Not asking is not the same as not minding, and I have known that difference since a kitchen.** You have known it longer than I have." She had a cloth over her arm and she did not take it off. "You did one thing about six weeks before a room and you told me you were going to and you did not ask me, and you gave me a reason for that which I have thought about every week since, and I have never once said whether I would have said no."

"You have not."

"No. And I am not going to start, and if you have been waiting about two years for me to start then you have been waiting for a thing that is not going to happen, and that is not me being hard on you, that is me telling you the truth about a door that is shut."

---

**Nobody in this empire has a form that lets a man put to a woman the single question of whether she would rather know first**, and he has gone at it for about two years and it is not there. The not telling and the not asking are one absence and not two.

That absence is why he told her the once. **It is why he is not going to tell her where he has been this week, or the week before it, or in any of the weeks in which he was on nobody's list and went and came back.**

"Then we are two people about four feet apart with the same arrangement," she said, "working it in opposite directions, and I never knew there were two sides of it until you stood on that step and told me a reason instead of asking me for a minute."

"You knew there were two sides."

"I knew there was a me in it. I did not know there was a you in it that had done the arithmetic." She went in, and the door was not shut behind her in a way that meant anything, and he stood on the step for the length of a coat being taken off.

---

The woman of about thirty-five next door came out on her own step at the same hour and stood on it, the way a person stands on a step in weather.

"The delivery has been late four days running," she said, across four feet of lane.

"It has been late four days running," said the woman at the bottom of the four steps, "and it came at about the fourth hour yesterday, and I have not told anybody that because nobody had asked me."

"I am not asking you. I am telling you I have been standing on this step at this hour four days running waiting to find out whether it was the road or the man."

"Then it was the road."

"Then that is two of us who have been standing at a door in the same weather and neither of us is going to say thank you for it."

---

Neither of them said thank you for it. The two of them stood at their own doors with about four feet of wet lane between them and nine feet of air, and neither of them said one word about the man who had just gone inside, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for two women saying a thing out loud about a man, and neither of them wanted to.

**That is the arrangement in the next house worked in the other direction.** The two of them have never once compared notes about it, and both of them would be inside four feet of the other one inside a minute if either said one word out loud, and neither of them did.
