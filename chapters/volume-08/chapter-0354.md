# Chapter 354: The Same Counter Two Weeks Later And The Other End Of It, The Last Hour Before It Shuts, A Drawer With Four Things In It And The Fourth Not Opened, And A Woman Nobody Has Asked Anything In About Two Years

The counter is the same counter at the top of the same stair, and she is at the other end of it, and the fee board is on the wall behind her with its four items on it and not one of the four being time.

She is twenty-four. Her name is Tamsin Rook, and she has been at that counter two years.

This is the fourth day of the fourth week of the first month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fifth hour, and the stair is empty and the counter shuts in an hour.

---

**Nobody has asked her anything in two years, and that is a choice and not a fault**, and the whole of what that costs is what this is: the only person in a room who is never asked anything, wanting to be asked every working day since the seventh month of next year.

**A person who is not asked has not been given a duty**, and she has known that since the seventh month of next year, and she has never once known it to be untrue of her, and nothing at that counter was ever put to her to begin with.

The counter is open four hours and she is at one end of it for them, and the number of people who come up to the counter in those four hours is a number she has never once kept, and there has never been a week in two years in which keeping it would have changed anything she did with it.

Wanting is not owing and owing is not asking. She has gone two years with this sitting nearer a hunger than a grievance, and the distance between those two is fourpence a copy and nothing else at all, and nobody has ever put a figure on it either.

---

A man came up that stair once, in the second week of the tenth month, wanting nothing whatever from that counter, and he put one question into the air and did not wait to find out what came back, and she gave him nothing.

That is the last time anybody has put a question to her. It is a fortnight out of about two years ago, and she has not been able to decide since whether she wishes she had answered. **She is answering nothing this week either, and nobody is putting anything to her this week**, and those two facts are not going to be set side by side by anybody except her, and she has not set them side by side.

---

"There is no form for objecting to a thing nobody has ever asked of you, and I have wanted to object to this for two years and there is nowhere to put the wanting," she said out loud at the fifth hour, to the fee board, about the undertaking. The board has four items on it and none of the four is an undertaking.

"That was not to me," said the woman at the other end of the counter, and she did not look up.

"It was to the board."

The pen was still not moving. She put the point of it down on the column without pressing and left it there, and the woman opposite went on with her own work, and the two of them got about a minute past with nothing put into the room at all.

"You are at my counter, and I have been at this counter eleven years, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire for a clerk to say to a person that a thing was said to a board, and if there were one you would be in a worse position this evening than you were at the second hour."

"You have not asked me what I meant by it."

"I have not asked you what you had for dinner, either," said Marn Ottery, "and you did not hold that against me at the time."

Tamsin Rook wrote a figure in the column she was working on, and the figure was right, and she went on writing after the woman at the other end of the counter had gone back to her own work, and neither of them said anything for a quarter of an hour, and it was not cold.

"You told a man on that stair the whole of a rule, four times, out loud, in about four minutes each," Tamsin Rook said at last. "He came four times and never once put anything back at you."

"He came four times," said Marn Ottery, "and I have never known that to the fourth time, and it is the only one of those mornings I would call a waste."

"He did not thank you."

"He has never thanked me and I have never wanted him to, and those two are one arrangement and not two, and I would like the person at this end of the counter to stop sitting two years over a want of that shape when there is a woman four feet away who has been at the same table for eleven years and has been thanked by nobody either, including me."

The woman at the other end of the counter put her pen down and turned her head and looked at the board on the wall behind Tamsin Rook, at the four items on it, and her eyes went to the fourth of them and stayed there a moment, and then she picked the pen up again.

Both of them knew what the other one had looked at and not one word was said about it by either of them, and the woman opposite went back to the column in front of her, and the room went on being the room it had been all the afternoon. That is how two people at one table are, and it has been how they were for most of the two years.

"I wrote the fourth one," she said. "About four years ago. In my own hand. And there is a clerk at this counter who is not a fool, and I have never said so, and I am not saying it now. I am saying it because you have said a thing out loud to a board and I have been in this building for eleven years and I am tired of the two of us sitting at one table with our faces turned away from each other."

Tamsin Rook did not answer that. There is no form for answering a thing like that, and there is no form for not answering it either, and she put her pen in the drawer at her end of the counter, which is the drawer with four things in it, and shut it, and the fourth of the four stayed where it has been.

---

The undertaking of the fourth of the first month has not been retired and has not been amended and has never been exercised. It has now gone two years that way, it is not going to be different this week, and a correct non-use is worth as much as a use and is entered as one.

The reason she gives for not exercising it is not one of the reasons she gave before. She has given three others, and the third of those was about the room and the fourth was about the season, and this one is that **nobody asked her, and nobody has asked her since, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for asking a person whether they will mind being told a thing before they are told it.** The reason there is no form is that there is no form.

She has been looking for one for two years. She looked in about nine places where one would have been kept, and what she found in every one of them was a column with a heading over it, and a column with a heading over it is a machine, and a machine is the thing that never asks anybody anything.

---

In a book of her own, on a page she has ruled by hand, there are two boxes with the same name written in both of them, and neither of the two has been merged into anything.

At the head of a line she put there is the name of a woman who has stood at that counter eleven years and who once put a coin back on it instead of taking it. She worked that out in a room of her own, on her own time, and what she worked out was that the line is a window and not a wall.

Nothing in this empire can be served out of a line in a person's own book when that person's own name is at the head of it. It turns into a wall the moment she is asked whether she minds its being there, and she is not going to be asked, and she is not going to ask, and nothing anywhere has a column for either of those two things.

The box of about four hundred blanks under that counter is not hers and she has never had it open and nobody has ever told her it was there to be opened.

---

There are four things in a drawer at her end of the counter. **The fourth of them has not been opened and has not been sent and has not been touched, and it is where it has been, with three others, and no key to that drawer has ever been offered to her and she has never asked for one.**

The drawer runs in under the work at her own end of the counter and it takes most of a forearm to get at. She knows where in it the fourth of them is without looking, which is not the same as knowing what is in it, and she has never once gone in for the other three without going in for all four.

She has not ruled a third line this week and is not going to. The decision about whether she trusts a man's judgment is unmade, has not been made in any of the years before this one, and is not going to be made in a dead hour at the other end of a counter with a board on the wall behind her.

There is an hour between that and the sixth and she got through it with the column in front of her, and the figure in it was finished a good while before the ink was dry. She did not turn round to the drawer once in that hour and she did not go over to the other end of the counter, and she had her own book in the room and did not open it.

At the sixth hour the stair was still empty, the way it had been since the second. The woman at the other end of the counter took her own shawl off the back of her chair and put it on, and going past she said out loud that the fourth item on that board was the only one of the four she had ever been able to read in a hurry. Tamsin Rook said that she had not been able to read it at all. Neither of them said anything else, and the counter shut at the sixth hour with the box still under it and the fourth of the four still in the drawer.
