# Chapter 356: A Bay At The Top Of A Lane Off The Slade And The South End Of A Hired Store Four Hundred Yards Off It, A Bench Longer Than The Bay Is Wide, A Lamp Waiting For About The Seventh Hour, A Mark In Chalk At The Height Of The Third Link Of A Chain, And A Woman In A Box Nobody Has Asked Her About

The bay at the top of that lane is about nine foot by eleven and there is a bench in it that is longer than the bay is wide, and the lamp above the middle of that bench is not lit until about the seventh hour, and it was not lit this afternoon either.

She is fifty-one. Her name is Halla Wray, and the foreman's job on that cut came to her because nine families agreed between themselves that it would be hers, and that was about nine years ago, and the nine of them met in a shed behind the second bay to agree it and agreed it in about an hour and a half.

This is the fourth day of the third week of the second month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the afternoon, and the gate at the bottom of the lane is shut.

---

**She has been inside that box since the first week of the seventh month of the year after, entered by a class, and nobody has asked her about it and nobody is going to.**

Not this week. Not in about two years. The men in the front of that bay have known it since the day it went in and not one of them has said a word about it since, and that is not a courtesy and is not going to be lifted and is not a thing anybody decided.

"**Being in a box and not being asked about it are the same arrangement, and the arrangement was made in that shed by nine families and not by me.** I have had about two years to work out a way of saying that as though it were two things and it is not two things and there is not going to be a way."

"The entries that put me where I am were about a class. Not about me. About a class, and I am in it, and the class is a heading, and a heading is how a person turns into a company, and a company is a thing this empire can hold wages against and give letters to and take to law."

"There is no form for disputing a class you have been entered in, and I have looked for one the way a person looks for a door in a wall they did not build, and the wall is about two years old and it is not going to come down."

---

She said the rest of it once, in the second month of last year, in a room with about nine people in it, and she is not going to say it again.

What she said then was that the form was not asking her whether she would represent anybody. It was asking who she represents. **There is a column for the answer and there is no column for a refusal, and a person who writes nothing in it has not refused and has not agreed and is still in the class**, and that is the whole of what she has to say about it and she has said it.

Nobody thanked her for saying it. There is not one form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be thanked for saying a true thing out loud in a room with nobody in authority in it, and the not being thanked is not anybody's decision and is not going to become one.

---

At the back of that bay the two of seventeen are where they have been for weeks, on a written engagement with terms on it and a rate at the foot of it, and one of the four lines of it is the name of a person who has never been in that lane.

**Neither of them was spoken to this afternoon, and neither of them was thanked, and neither of them was sent for, and neither of them is going to start.** The reader is paid forty-five pence a day and four days a week, and what a day of that comes to is anywhere between four pence and a shilling according to what gets set on the bench on the day, and that rate exists because a figure was put in a list by somebody who has not been in that bay and is not in it now.

The other one is seventeen and nobody in this empire has ever put a word to her about any of it, and there is no paper anywhere that asks a person of seventeen whether she would rather not, and so there is no answer on the record anywhere, and no answer on the record is not the same as no.

Nobody moved them to the front of that bay. The bay has had room at the back for two for weeks, and about nine men are in the front of it, and the two of them are at the back, and that is where they are, and this chapter is not going to say why and does not know.

---

**About four men in that bay cannot make sense of a paragraph, and not one question about a number has ever been put to any of them, and none of them was put one this afternoon.**

The man of about fifty-five with the chain is on the front bench and the chain is on him and it has been on him for years. He is not one of the four and says so out loud about once every few months, to nobody in particular, and nobody has ever put him in a list of them and nobody is going to.

He has known for about four years that findability does not work on him. He has never once used it. Nobody knows that he knows it and he has never told anybody, and that is his business and not a thing anybody in that bay has standing to ask about.

---

The man who keeps the books of that Company is four hundred yards up the same lane and he has wanted the second half of a sentence of hers since the third month of the year after.

He has not asked. He is not going to. **The day he asks is the day she stops saying the half she has got**, and she has been giving him that half in a doorway about once a fortnight for about two years, and he has never asked her for the rest of it and he has never once stood in a position where he could.

**There is no form for owning up to a thing you have only half said**, and a half is not a debt and is not a confession and is not an answer, and it is the only thing she has that anybody wants, and it is the shape of about half of everything in this matter.

She is not going up the lane to say the rest of it and she is not going to make him come down it.

---

At about the fifth hour she went down the lane and across to the south end of the hired store and stood in the doorway of it for a while, and there is nothing in that doorway that is hers and nothing in it that is anybody's.

The mark in the chalk on the end wall of the bay is at the height of the third link of that chain, and the cross means nothing. It is nobody's and it is about four years off being anybody's evidence, and the chain it stands level with is on a man, and **nothing in that lane is going to turn either one of them into anything at all**, and she is not going to be the one who scrapes it off to see what is under it.

"Is that bench still longer than the bay?" she said it out loud at about the sixth hour, to the bench, about the bench, and it is, and nothing in this empire lets a bench be said to a person, and a foreman of fifty-one in a box that nobody has asked her about is not a person anybody is looking for, and the count of things anybody has asked out loud in this matter is the same as it was when the gate was shut and it is going to be the same in ten weeks.
