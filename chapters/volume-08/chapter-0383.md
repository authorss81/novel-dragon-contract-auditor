# Chapter 0383: The Counter At The Top Of Four Flights, A Thing Out Of The Drawer, And Two Women

The board on that wall has four things on it and not one of them is a time, and it has not had a time on it in two years. The drawer is under the board. There are four things in it and none of them has been opened, and the drawer is not locked, and it has not been locked in eleven years.

She is twenty-four. **Her name is Tamsin Rook, she is at that counter for the second year, and she is not a lawyer, and nobody has asked her anything in about two years.** She has gone over that more than once on slow afternoons with the woman at the other end of the same counter, and she has never been able to make it anything other than a choice that was made for her and not by her. Wanting is not owing, and owing is not asking, and there is no form in this empire for the wanting.

**The fourth of the four things in that drawer has a date at the top of it and nobody at that counter has said that date out loud in two years.** The date is the fourth of the first month. It is live, and it has never been retired, and it has never been exercised, and the two of them at those two ends of that counter have between them never once said it to each other, least of all this week.

---

At the fourth hour it was on the wood.

"You have had that drawer open," said Tamsin Rook.

"I have not had it open. It was open when I came back from the end and it was on the counter, and I have not touched it, and I do not know whose it is." Marn Ottery had both hands flat on the boards either side of it. "**Out on the wood?** At the fourth hour, in front of whoever came up."

"I know whose it is."

"Then you will want to be careful about how that is said, because I have not asked, and because I put it down and picked it up again and I did not know, and I want that on the record in the only way anything gets on the record in this room, which is by me having said it out loud and you having heard it."

"It does not need to go on anything." Tamsin Rook did not put her hand out. "It is a piece of paper. It has been in a drawer for longer than I have been at this counter. Put it back."

---

"Out on the wood is not the same as in a drawer," Marn Ottery said. "I have watched you not open that drawer for two years and I have said nothing about it for two years, and I am not going to say that part again, and I have thought about how I am going to avoid it all week."

"You said it a month ago."

"I said it a month ago and I have been waiting a month to see whether it changed anything, and it has not changed anything, and this week it is on the counter." She straightened and did not touch the paper. "**There is no form for a fact two people share about a building**, and there has not been one since before either of us, and what the two of us have now is not a secret between us and not a confidence. It is a fact about the floor. Anybody who came up those stairs between the fourth hour and now has seen a piece of paper that was in that drawer, and about nine people have come up since the fourth hour, and I have counted them, because I count everything that goes up and down this counter and it is the only habit I have got left."

"Nobody knows whose it is."

"Nobody knows whose it is and that is exactly the trouble. A thing that is on a counter with no name on it is a thing that belongs to whoever picks it up next." She looked at the drawer and then at the paper. "**And a thing that has been out cannot be put back in.** I am not saying that to stop you. I am saying it because I have been at the other end of these boards for eleven years and I have watched two things go out of drawers on that counter and neither of them was ever the same afterwards, and both times it was nobody's fault and both times somebody paid for it in a way that never got written down anywhere."

---

Tamsin Rook put it back herself.

She did not use the drawer to reach for it, she turned it round with two fingers so that the date at the top went to the back, and she set it in at the front where the other three were, and she pushed it in with her thumb, and she shut the drawer with the flat of her hand and not with a noise.

"That is not what you should have done," Marn Ottery said.

"I know exactly what I have done and I have not said the date out loud and neither are you, and the undertaking on it is neither exercised nor retired, and it is still live and nobody has retired it, and it is the fourth hour and it is the fourth day of that week." She took her hands off the drawer. "We are the reason a piece of paper in a drawer is a piece of paper on a counter, and there is no form for that either, and I am not going to spend the rest of the week being the reason."

Marn Ottery said it from the other end. "**You are going to spend the rest of the week being the reason, and so am I, and the two of us can be the reason together instead of one at a time, which is a worse arrangement and the only one on offer.**"

---

It is the fourth day of the third week of the ninth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and the counter shuts at the sixth hour and nobody in that building was shut out by the weather this week, and the box of about four hundred bought blanks under the far end of that counter came out and went back in and was not opened.

Marn Ottery got a bundle of string out from behind the box and put the box back where it had been, and the drawer was shut by the fifth hour, and there are about a hundred feet of floor between that drawer and the stair and every foot of it belongs to people who came up in the fourth hour and read the top of a sheet of paper from the rail.
