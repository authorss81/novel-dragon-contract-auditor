# Chapter 0375: The Shed At The Slade End, A Lamp Not Lit, And A Paper At Its Own Terms

The lamp above the middle of that bench does not get lit until about the seventh hour and it was not lit at the fifth and it is not lit now. The mark in chalk on the end wall is at the height of the third link of a chain, and the chain is on a man, and neither one of them means anything to anybody in this building.

She is fifty-one and her name is Halla Wray. **Nine families met in a shed behind the second one of these and settled the foreman's job on that cut in an hour and a half, about eleven years ago. She was not in that shed and nobody went and got her.** Since the first week of the seventh month of the year after she has been in a box, put in by a class, and nobody has ever asked her about it.

At the back of that shed, on a bench of their own, the two of seventeen are sitting with a written engagement in front of them that came to the end of its own terms in the week. The paper is on the bench. The rate is set out at the foot of it in a hand that is not either of theirs.

They are there the way a bench is there. Nobody spoke to either of them before the fifth hour and nobody spoke to either of them after it, and neither of them has been thanked and neither of them has been sent for, and one of the four lines of that paper carries the name of a person who has never been in this lane.

**Neither of them is going to start.** There is no answer in that shed about why the paper ended and not one of the men in it has one, and there is no column anywhere in this empire that a person of seventeen could put a refusal in, because the paper was never asking.

---

The man of about twenty-eight was at the front bench and had been on that bank seven years. He put both hands flat on the wood.

"You have it in your face," he said. "You have had it in your face since you came in and you have not said one word about the paper at the back and you are going to, and I am going to stop you, and I want it understood that I am stopping you and not that I have a better idea."

"You have not got one."

"I have got a better idea than saying it out loud in front of nine men and a foreman, and that is a low bar and I am clearing it." He did not take his hands off the wood. "**A rate?**" He said it to the paper and not to anybody.

"That is what is at the foot of it."

Halla Wray put her hand flat on the end of the bench. "A foreman who explains out loud what a piece of paper has done to two people sitting behind her has put two people in a room with her. Neither of those two chose this room and one of them did not choose the year."

"I was going to say it was over and that neither of them had been spoken to."

"That is worse," said the man of about twenty-eight. "That is a foreman certifying the state of two children in a building where about nine men cannot make a paragraph out of anything, and the man who cannot make a paragraph out of anything is at the front of this bench and is going to hear it and is going to be one of four."

---

The man of about fifty-five with the chain said four sentences, and it took him as long as it takes to turn a hand over. He said them to the bench, on a thing that is not a figure and not a number, and nobody in that shed answered him, and he has never once wanted an answer and has said so out loud to nobody about once every few months.

There are four men in that shed who cannot make sense of a paragraph, and not one question about a number has ever been put to any of them, and the rate at the foot of the paper at the back is a number, and **it stayed where it was put.**

"He says it every few weeks," the man of about twenty-eight said, after a while. "Seven years I have been at the front of that bench, and there is a thing about the four of us I have never got to the bottom of, and it is not a thing anybody is going to get to the bottom of."

"I have worked it out," the man with the chain said. "That is why I say it to the bench."

---

It is the fourth day of the third week of the seventh month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and the shed at the top of that lane is about nine foot by eleven with the bench longer than that, and the gate at the bottom of the lane was shut all week and nobody in this building asked anybody anything.

The shed fills the way a shed fills, which is slowly and one man at a time, and it had nine men in it, and the two at the back of it had been in there since the first hour, and every one of the nine had come in, looked at the two of them, and gone to the front bench without saying one word to either of them, and that had been the arrangement since before either of them was put at the back of it. The lamp over the middle of the front bench is not due until about the seventh hour, and at the fourth hour in the middle of the week the whole of the shed was worked out by the sound of it, which is nine sets of boots on a floor and a chain its wearer keeps moving against himself and, at the back, two people not moving at all.

A man who keeps the books of that Company has a room four hundred yards up the same lane, and he has wanted the rest of a sentence of hers since the third month of the year after, and she gave him half of one in a doorway in a month that is gone. The half she did not give him did not arrive in this week. Nobody has sent for it, and there is no form for ending an engagement in writing between two people who were never spoken to about it.

Four lines and a rate at the foot of them, and the four lines are the four it was written with, and the two of them have read them more times than a person reads a thing they cannot act on. Nobody at the front bench had ever asked either of them to read any of it out, and nobody had ever said to either of them that it had ended, and the only two people in the building who knew it had ended were the two of them, and they had known it since they had both been there.

**There is no form for a rate set out at the foot of a paper**, and the two at the back of that shed did not write the one at the foot of theirs, and she has never once said that out loud in that building and she is not going to start this week.

The gate at the bottom of the lane was shut all week, and that is the only part of the week anybody at the Slade end would have called ordinary, and the chalk on the end wall was still at the height of the third link of what the man with the chain goes about carrying, and neither of those two things had moved in the whole of the week. The two of seventeen were still sitting where they had been sitting since the first hour, on their own bench at the back of it, and the lamp came on at about the seventh hour and the paper was still in front of them where it had been all day, and neither of them had said one word to anybody in the building.

The paper at the back of the shed came to the end of its own terms. The shed went on being a shed, the class she is in is still a class, and the chalk on the end wall is still at the height of the third link of what he is carrying. Nobody in that building was asked one thing about a number this week and nobody is going to be.
