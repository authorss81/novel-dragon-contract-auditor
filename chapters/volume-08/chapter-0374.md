# Chapter 0374: The Same Counter At The Top Of Four Flights, And A Woman Who Goes Down The Stair

**The counter shuts at the sixth hour in this weather and it is not on the board**, and the board is on the wall behind it with four things on it and not one of them a time.

She is twenty-four. Her name is Tamsin Rook, she is at that counter for the second year, and she is not a lawyer. **Nobody has asked her anything in about two years.** She has gone over that in her own head more than once, on slow afternoons, with the two of them at the same counter, and she has never been able to make it anything other than a choice that was made for her and not by her. **Wanting is not owing, and owing is not asking**, and there is no form in this empire for the wanting.

The drawer is under the board. **There are four things in it and none of them has been opened**, and nobody has ever offered her a key to that drawer and she has not asked for one.

---

"There is a woman and a boy at the bottom of your four flights," Marn Ottery said from the other end. "They have been there since the fourth hour. I have looked down twice and I cannot do a thing with it from up here and I have stopped looking. **There is no form for shutting a counter in weather**, and the board behind you has four things on it and not one of them a time, and those are the same fact, and I have not said so to anybody in eleven years."

"You could send for them."

"I could send a man down with a piece of paper and the piece of paper would concern the counter and not them, and they would be two people who had been written on for the length of a minute." Marn Ottery squared a sheet on the wood. "I have been at this end eleven years and I have watched about nine people a day come up those stairs in a fair week and about four in a week like this one, and the ones who do not come up are the ones who have furthest to come, and I have never once said one word about that to anybody and I am not starting with you this week."

"Then somebody had better go down and say the counter is shut."

"I said I could not do it from up here. I did not say you could not do it, and there is a difference and I put it in the room on purpose." She did not look up from the sheet. "You have been at that counter two years and you have not gone down those stairs once. I have watched you not go down them. I have watched you stand at the rail and look at the landing window with the rain running along it and then go back to the rail."

---

She went down.

Four flights with a stair that has no door at the foot of it, and the cold coming up off the flags at the bottom of them, and a woman of about sixty standing with her back to the wall and a boy of about eleven beside her holding a paper in both hands the way a person holds a thing they have been told not to drop.

"The counter is shut," said Tamsin Rook. "It shuts at the sixth hour when the weather is like this. It was not shut at the fifth hour."

"That is the first thing anybody has said to me here in an hour and a half," said the woman of about sixty. "I did not think anybody was going to."

"There is nothing else I can tell you."

"There is nothing else anybody can tell me, and I have been standing at the bottom of this stair since the fourth hour working out which of the four things it could be, and I got as far as a hand." She looked at the boy. "Put it by. We are going."

The boy did not put it by. He held it, and he looked up at Tamsin Rook with the face of a person who has been told something and does not yet know what it was about, and she did not ask him, and she has not thought about his face since except that she has thought about it.

---

She came back up the four flights and put her hands on the counter and said nothing at all.

"That was a service," Marn Ottery said, "and you did not have to do it, and I am not going to say a word about whether you should have, because I have been at this counter eleven years and I did not go down those stairs until about two years ago and I went down them once and the woman I met was gone before I got to the bottom."

"You went down once."

"**I went down once** and I have not told anybody that and I have not decided to tell you. You went down and there is a woman who knows that you went down, and that is a different arrangement from mine and I am not going to take it off you because I would have liked to have had it and I have not got it."

Then Marn Ottery put her chin on her hand and looked at the drawer under the board for a while, and neither of them said a word about the four things in it.

"That drawer is not mine," Marn Ottery said. "It is not locked. It has not been locked in eleven years. There are four things in it and none of them has been opened. The fourth of the four is the one with a date at the top of it and it has been yours since before you came, and the other three belong to people who left. I have watched you not open it for two years and I have said nothing about it for two years, and I am saying something about it now because you have just gone down four flights in the cold for a woman you are not going to see again."

"You could have said that at any hour in two years."

"I could have, and I did not, and I have my own reasons and one of them is that a person who has been asked nothing for two years does not need one of her colleagues to point out that a thing in a drawer is hers." She went back to her end. "**There is no form for a service a person does not ask for, and there is not one for going down a stair either, and I have had two years of getting used to being the one who stays at the top of it.**"

---

The date is the second day of the second week of the seventh month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the sixth hour now, and the counter is shut, and the undertaking of the fourth of the first month is live and unretired and unamended and has not been exercised and was not exercised today, and nobody has retired it.

The cold at the foot of the stair did not go away when she did. It stayed where she had left it and came up the four flights behind her, and by the time she was back at the counter with her hands on the boards she had brought a good deal of it up with her and had nowhere to put it down. Coming back up a stair you have just come down takes everything a person has, and both women at the counter watched her come up the last flight without either of them saying one word about how she looked when she got there.

The two of them came off the four flights together, **which is not a thing either of them decided**, and Marn Ottery said at the second landing that it was not a favour and it was not an arrangement, and Tamsin Rook said that she knew it was not, and neither of them thanked the other for walking down half a stair.

They went down at the pace of two people who had agreed without agreeing, and the stair took them the way it takes everybody, one at a time in the narrow of it and both of them with a hand on the rail. Nobody standing at the bottom of them is anybody's business, and the landing on the flags is where a person coming up has to go round whoever is on it, and there was nobody on it at all, and the woman of about sixty and the boy of about eleven were not there either, and four flights had been climbed for that and nothing else.

**None of the four things in the drawer has been opened, and the fourth of the four is the one with the date at the top of it, and nobody at that counter has said that date out loud in two years, and neither has she.**
