# Chapter 0377: A Door Nine Miles Off, A Fire Out At The First Hour, And Two Men Not Going

The fire had been out since before he got up and there was no coal in the grate and there is a place four miles nearer the work where a man can buy a half-hundredweight on a morning, and he has never once done it and he is not going to start.

He is about sixty-one and the second hand on a sheet has been his work since the year he was forty-two. **A paragraph is a shape to him. The figure on it comes through, and the name at the top of it comes through, and most of the little words holding those two to the paper come through, and everything after that is a weight he can feel in his hands and not read.** His hand goes at the foot of about nine hundred of them a year.

The door is on the latch. It has been on the latch every night for two years and it is on the latch this morning, and there is a man of about thirty-one standing on the other side of the step doing the same thing with his coat on, and the two of them have been standing on the same step at the same hour on and off for a year.

---

"You are not going either," said the man of about thirty-one.

"I have not said a word yet."

"You had your coat on and your back to the wall and you looked at the road, and I have done that more times than I have bothered to count." He turned his collar up. "I go to the yards, not to the room. Same road, same nine miles, and I have been on it six years and you have been on it nineteen, and neither of us has ever once been met at the other end of it."

"The cold is the reason."

"The cold is a reason. The road is the reason. A man who has walked nine miles on a morning like this one, once, and has come to a building where nobody knew he was on the road, does not get up at the first hour and do it again for nothing." He put his hands deep in his coat. "**I have been in six buildings in six years and not one of them has ever sent for me, and there is no form for that, and I have not asked anybody for one because asking is being served.**"

---

"There is a third reason," the man of about sixty-one said, "and I am only telling you because you said yours out loud on my step."

"Go on."

"A man of thirty-eight came that whole distance. On his own two feet. In a month that is gone now." He looked at the road and not at the man on the step. "He came to the room at the end of the passage and there was nobody at the far end of the flags to meet him, and I was not told he was on the road, and I would not have been able to do anything about it if I had been told, and that is the part I have been turning over since the spring."

"He never said he was coming."

"No. He did not say it to me and he did not say it to the woman who keeps the minutes, and she did not enter any of it, and there is no column in any of the eleven books for a man coming up a road." The man of about thirty-one did not turn round. "That is the cost of it and I am the one paying half of it, and I have never met him and I have been told about him by a boy who does not know what he saw."

---

The man of about thirty-one went off down his own lane at a quarter past the first hour. The man of about sixty-one went in and took the door off the latch and shut it and stood in the cold room with his coat on for a while before he took it off.

He has wanted about four minutes for about nineteen years. Four minutes is what it would take to go through the ones he can remember and say no to about half of them and yes to the rest, and there is nobody in that building who can be given four minutes, and there is nobody on that road who can be given them either, and this week he is not walking nine miles to a room where nothing would be different at the far end of it.

The three of them are not the same, and he has never let them be the same. Four men in a shed cannot make a paragraph out of anything and he is not one of them and has said so out loud to nobody about once every few months. A woman of twenty in a counting room four hundred and thirty miles up this river is owed nineteen days of a crew's money, and the man holding it is four miles off in a coat, and she is not going to ask him what it is held against. A foreman of fifty-one is in a box entered by a class. **A man of thirty-eight walked this road once, on his own two feet, in a month that is gone, and the road has not stopped being his since.**

---

It is the second day of the first week of the eighth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next. The door went on the latch at the seventh hour that night. There was a sheet in his hand at the fourth hour of the second day of the week before that, and it was not what the man above him had written, and he put his name at the foot of it, and nobody at that desk has ever been in a position to put him one question about it. **There is no form for a sheet that is not what the man above wrote**, and there is none for a man who signs it anyway, and the second of those two is the ordinary condition of a man who is on no list.

The door went on the latch at the seventh hour and the grate was as cold as it had been at the first, and there was no form for a man walked nine miles and not met, and nine miles is a long road to walk in this to arrive at a desk that is going to hand you the sheet it handed you last week.
