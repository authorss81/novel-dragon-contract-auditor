# Chapter 0382: A Room At The End Of A Passage, Four Days Empty, And A Clerk Not Going

She has been in this room nineteen years and there is a window about two feet wide in it with no pane in it, and the cold comes in at the one corner of that window and stands there, and there is a shelf of eleven books under the window and a table and one chair and nothing else in the place worth the name of furniture.

**She is about fifty-five, and what she does in this room is minutes, and she advises nobody and asks nothing, and about four thousand entries have gone into those eleven books and not one of them carries a name.** The four that come up the flags in a morning go in before the fourth hour, because the light is going, and they are entered in a hand that has not varied in nineteen years.

**There is no form for a room at the end of a passage.** There has never been one in this building or in any building that this one answers to, and a room at the end of a passage is a room that a person goes to or does not go to, and there is nothing in the whole of this matter that says which of the two is a duty.

Her own finding is on that shelf with a day and an hour on it and no full stop at the end of it, and her own name is at the foot of it, and it is the only thing on the whole of that shelf that anybody's name is at the bottom of. Nobody has asked her about it. She is not going to be asked about it a second time, and she has made her arrangements accordingly, and the arrangements are that it stays a note.

At the far end of the room there is a woman of about nineteen who came into the job in the spring and who keeps her coat on all day.

---

"You have gone to the back door twice this morning," the woman of nineteen said, "and you have not gone past it either time, and I am not asking you where you would be going if you went past it."

"I was getting air."

"There is no air at that door. There is about four hundred yards of stone flags and a cold off them that comes up the way water comes up a drain, and a person gets worse standing at that door and not one inch better." She had a sheet squared in front of her and she was not writing on it. "**No form for a clerk going and looking**, and there has not been one in this building since before either of us, and the not going is not going to be written down anywhere either, and I want you to hear both halves of that sentence at the same time."

"I heard them."

"I know you heard them, because you have done the same thing at the front of this room for about a month and I have watched you do it there as well." The young woman put her pen down. "**A room that has had nothing at the end of a passage for four days is a room somebody has stopped coming to.** That is not a question. That is what four days is. And a clerk who goes down those flags to find out has made a record of it by being seen going, and there is nowhere in this building for that record to go, so it stays in the heads of the two of us, and you have nineteen years of not giving anybody a head to put that in and I am not going to be the one who starts it."

---

She went on with the column she was in and did not look up, and after a while she said that she was not going to go.

"I know you are not."

"I am not going to go, and I want it entered that I decided it and that nobody in this building asked me for a decision." She carried the line up to where the line was. "That is not modesty. If I go, then the four days was a thing somebody found out about, and if I do not go, then the four days is a thing that happened at the far end of my own passage and nobody at this end of it knows anything, and I have been the one who knows things at this end for nineteen years and I am extremely good at it, and I am not giving it up over a room I have never once been in."

Then she said the other part, unasked, which is how she has given away the only two things she has ever given away in nineteen years of that room.

"The key to the back door of my own building is on the ring by my elbow and I have never once been down that passage. I know what is at the end of it the way I know a figure in a book, off other people's hands."

---

"A man of thirty-eight came the whole of that distance on his own two feet," she said, "in a month that is gone, and there was nobody at the far end of those flags to meet him, and the person who should have been told he was on the road is the man whose hand goes at the foot of the sheets." She said it in the voice she uses for minutes, which is the only voice she has got for anything. "**The room at the end of those flags is the room he came for, and he has not been in it in the whole time since.** I have entered none of it and told nobody of it. There is no column in any of these eleven books for a man coming up a road."

"You will have wanted to go and look at that room."

"I have wanted to go and look at that room every working day since the spring, and I have not gone, and the not going is not about him." She turned her head towards the back of the room. "It is the four days. **A woman of twenty-six stands at that end of those flags and has never once been past the lock, and if I walk down there this week she will see me come, and she will be told by my boots that somebody at this end of the passage has finally gone and looked.**"

The woman of nineteen said nothing to that. She picked her pen up and set it in her hand and did not write with it.

---

It is the second day of the second week of the ninth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the second hour, and the cold at the one corner of the window had not moved an inch in a month.

**Four hundred yards of flags run out of the back of that building and she has not walked ten yards of them this week, and she has stood in that doorway twice and turned round both times**, and there is a woman of about nineteen at the far end of the room who saw her do it both times and has written none of it down and has thrown nothing out.

She went out of the room at half past three again, which is the fourth time in two days, and the cold in that corner had not moved in a month. The four sheets went into the eleven books before the fourth hour and the note on the shelf is still a note with no full stop on the end of it, and her feet were colder when she came back in than they were when she went out.
