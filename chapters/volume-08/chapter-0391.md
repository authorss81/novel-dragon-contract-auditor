# Chapter 0391: Four Inches Along A Counter, Six Years Of Not Opening A Box, And A Young Man From The Shipping Floor

The counter at the top of those four flights has the board on the wall behind it that it has had for as long as anybody can remember and the drawer under that board with the door of it shut, and about a hundred feet of floor run between the near end of those boards and the stair. The box stands under the far end of that counter where it has stood for six years, and it holds about four hundred bought and unfilled blanks, and it is not locked, and the key that has never been wanted is in the same hand as the counter was cut in.

**Marn Ottery is her name and she is thirty-four**, and one end of those boards has been hers for eleven years, and she is not a lawyer. She has said the shape of a thing out loud in that room twice and been asked one thing back twice, and a fortnight ago she said a third thing out loud to the woman at the other end of the same counter and was asked nothing, and she has said once since that she is not going to say a second one. Nobody has ever asked her one question about those blanks, and no form in this empire has ever been drawn to account for them. **She has had cause to look at them twice in eleven years and on both of those times she was after something else.**

It is the fourth day of the third week of the eleventh month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the sixth hour, and the box has not been opened in six years.

---

She wanted a length of twine. The twine was behind the box and the box was on the boards at the far end with nothing at all on the lid of it, so a hand on the lid and a pull was all the thing needed.

The sixth hour was still a quarter off and the floor had been going down the stair for a while, and she had been standing where the boards run out at the far end, with nothing in her hands, which is how a person stands when the last thing is done and the next one has not come. She had been in this room eleven years and there was not one hour of them in which a person could reach into the space at the far end of the boards without moving something first, and everybody who worked the floor had known it for years, and nobody had ever said so, and she had never brought it up either.

She did it in one movement without deciding to, and she was aware of the whole of it while she was doing it, the way a person is aware of a thing they have done in a room where somebody else can see it.

The woman at the other end had her back to her and was going along the last of a set of figures, and there was a whole floor between the two of them, and a floor is a distance, and a distance hides a movement absolutely on the day it happens and hides nothing whatever on the day after, and she knew which of those two days she was standing in, and she decided it in about the time it takes to decide it.

**Four inches.** The box is deeper than it is long and it went forward and slightly out, and the boards under it are the same boards they have been, and the four inches are a distance no person on that floor could measure with anything except their own two hands.

The young man from the shipping floor was on the stairs at the time of it with a sheet he had come up to leave, and he came the rest of the way up.

He came up the stair the way he always comes up it, and then he came further up than he had any need to, and no man does it by accident, and she heard the sheet go against his coat on the way and understood that he had heard her, and understood at the same moment that she had no way of telling him which of the two she had heard.

"I will set this down here," he said. "It goes down with the rest at the six."

"That is all right with it."

"It is not all right with it and I am not saying it to you, I am saying it to the box." He put the sheet down on the boards and then put both hands flat on them, which is what a man does when he has decided to stand still for a while. "**That box has not been opened in six years and it has not been moved in six years either, and you have just moved it four inches, and a box somebody has decided about is a box that has been moved on purpose, and a clerk who moves a box is a clerk who has made a statement about it.**"

"There is no form for moving a box."

She went along the end of the boards with her head down while he was saying it, and the length of twine she had got went slack in her hand and had to be gathered again, and the gathering took about as long as the rest of what he had to say, and she was aware while she was doing it that she was gathering twine very carefully in front of a man who had just told her she had made a statement, and there is nothing at all a person can do with being seen to do it.

"That is the half of it I have come up here to say and I will now say the other half." He did not look at the box and he looked at her. "**Nobody can be told what the statement was.** Not me and not her at the other end of these boards and not whoever comes up these stairs tomorrow morning. You have made a statement on that floor and there is nowhere in this building that a statement can go, so it stays up here on the boards with the box on it, and I am the only person who has seen you do it, and I have now said that out loud in a room, which makes me a party to it, and I have not got a room to be served in."

---

She put her hand flat on the lid of the box where her hand had been and felt the dust that had come off it onto her palm, which was the first thing about that box she has touched in six years.

There was a print in it where her hand had been, and she turned the palm over and looked at the mark on it for a moment, and then she wiped the palm down the front of her skirt, which took the dust off her hand and put the print on the boards instead, where it would still be in the morning for anybody coming up the stair to stand over and decide for himself what it was. She had known there would be a print. She had not known, until her hand was on her skirt, that she would rather have it on the boards than on her own skin, and she stood there a moment while the reason for it went by and did not find a place to put it.

"I am not going to say a second thing about this," she said. "I said on this floor a fortnight ago that I was not going to, and a man on the shipping floor is not the person that was said to, and I would rather you took that as a statement about the other end of these boards than as an opening."

"I have not come up here to be spoken to twice," he said. "I have come up here to leave a sheet and I have now said a thing, and there is no form for a statement made on a counter and a sheet left on it, and I will take the two of them down the stairs together at the six and I will not say one word on the way."

"You will say one word at the bottom of them."

"I will say one word at the bottom of them and it will be the word for the sheet, and there is a difference and you know there is a difference."

He did not go. He had come up those four flights with a sheet to leave and he had said a second thing he had not come up for, and he stood on the boards with his hands in his coat pockets and looked at the place where the box was not sitting, and the looking went on long enough for the light on the stair to change a very little, and she understood that he was not waiting for an answer and was in fact waiting for her to be the one to say the next thing first.

---

She moved the box back an inch.

She thought about the first thing while her hand was on the lid, which was to set it back the four inches and have the whole of the day be a woman at the far end of some boards who wanted a length of twine. Saying no to a thing took no longer than saying no takes, and the deciding was not about the box and had never been about the box.

Not to where it had been. She set it down an inch short of where it had been, so that the two distances were on that counter together and anybody coming up those stairs could see both of them and neither of them would have to believe her.

"That is not the four inches back," he said.

She had not expected the two of them to be talking about inches. He had come up four flights with a sheet to leave and he had stood in front of a box and made an accusation about a distance a person could put a foot either side of, and she found that she could not go back to the boards and start on the day again while he was standing there doing it.

"It is not, and I am not going to do the other one either, and I would rather it went in as a thing I settled on my own account than as a thing anybody in this building put to me." She untied the twine where it had been sitting behind the box and put a foot and a bit of it on the boards. "**A thing that has been moved four inches and put back where it was is a thing that was moved four inches, and a thing that has been moved four inches and then moved one of them back is a thing that is three inches nearer where it was, and both of those are on the same counter at the same time, and I would rather have the two of them than the one of them.**"

"You have made a second statement."

"I have made a second statement in front of a man who has told me out loud that he is a party to the first one, and who is paid by a shipping floor and not by this counter." She put the twine over her shoulder. "Go down with your sheet at the six. I will not say one word to you about this on the stairs and you will not say one word to me at the bottom of them, and neither of us is going to find out this week whether three inches holds."

Behind her the woman at the other end put a sheet down on the pile and squared the pile, which is how she ends a morning. Marn Ottery did not turn round. It had been a fortnight since she had said the third thing out loud in the room and got nothing back, and she had understood at the time that the nothing back was the good half of the arrangement. She understood it now, with a man on a stair and a box standing where she had put it. The good half of an arrangement is the half which leaves a person with nothing to be grateful for and nowhere to put the gratitude, and it meant she would go on working the floor with the woman behind her for the rest of her life without once being able to say so.

---

He went down with the sheet under his arm and the twine is on the boards at the near end of that counter and the box is three inches forward of where six years of nobody has put it, and at the sixth hour that counter shut with both of those on it and the drawer shut and the four things still on the board behind her, and neither woman at that counter said one word to the other about any part of it.
