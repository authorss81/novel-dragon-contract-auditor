# Chapter 0372: The Same Counting Room With The Stove Out, And A Rule Not Told A Third Time

Somebody lit that stove before the light came and went down the lane again, and somebody is not in this lane today. The stove has been out since before the first of us came and the broken light in the window has been broken since long before either of us.

She is twenty. **Her name is Nell Kest, and sixteen books stand in this room, and fifteen of them are on loan from elsewhere and the sixteenth carries her name in capitals at the head of it and has never once had a question set against it anywhere.**

The second of the four was on the step at the turn of the hour and came in with her shawl pulled over her head, and she set the rack of the day's numbers down in the place it is always set and did not take her coat off.

"You have not lit it," the second woman said.

"Whoever lit it went down the lane again. I have not lit it and I am not going to."

"I am not asking you to. I am telling you what the room is doing to my hands." She put her hands flat on the bench and then took them off. "It is the cold, Nell. It is not a thing anybody has done to me."

---

"I want you to say the rule again," said the second woman, "and I have gone at how to want that for four days and I have not found a way of wanting it that is not a question, so I am going to say it as a statement and you can do what you like with it."

"Then I will tell you now that I am not going to, and I would rather you had that first than at the fourth hour."

"I know you are not. I have come anyway. That is the whole of what I came for and I am not dressing it up as something else, because you were decent the second time and I am not going to insult you by pretending this is about the eleven houses." She had a folded sheet in the inside of her shawl and she took it out and put it on the bench and did not open it. "**I said it to a woman in the fourth house off this lane on the second of the two days, and I said all of it, and she has not spoken to me since, and she sends her numbers in on time every morning like a person who has never once heard a rule out loud.**"

Nell Kest did not touch the sheet. "You said it to her."

"I said it to her because I had it and she was standing in her own doorway and I could not carry it any further on my own and I am not sorry I said it and I am not going to say it was wrong."

"Then you will not be saying it again here, and I am not saying it a third time, and those are two different things and I am putting them in that order on purpose."

---

The eleven houses off that lane send their figures in on the first of the two days and get eleven sets back on the second. The rack held them. Nell Kest set them out and wrote the column in a hand that has not varied in three years, and there is a figure in the fourth column that she cannot account for and has never touched, and she did not touch it this week either, on the grounds that the day a counting room begins to correct figures is the day it has begun to have views.

There is no form for a stove going out, and there is no form in this lane or in this building or in this room for one person telling another a true thing twice, and the second of those two holes is the one that has been in it longest.

"I am going to say you one thing and then I am going to take my numbers and go," said the second woman. She did not open the sheet. "When you said it the second time I did not understand a word of it. I had understood it the first time. **I have been carrying it for two months on a thing I could not follow, and I would not go back to the version I could follow, and I do not know what to do with that and there is nobody to take it to.**"

"Then you have it," said Nell Kest. "And that is the part I am sorry for. The second telling is what did it. A rule that travels once belongs to whoever said it, and a rule that travels twice belongs to whoever hears it, and I have just told you out loud that you are somebody a rule could be fetched out of, and I cannot take the telling back."

"You could have not said that last part."

"I know. I said it because you had your hands on my bench and because I have been twenty for a year now and this is the first useful thing I have done with it."

---

This is the fourth day of the fourth week of the sixth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and the second woman went down the lane at the turn of the hour with a paper in her shawl that she had not opened, and the stove was out by the fourth hour and the room had the temperature of the yard outside it the whole of the time.

The lane had been busy since the turn of the hour and went quiet by degrees, and what the room had then was the quiet of a place where a fire had not been in it for weeks and a window in it had not been whole for longer than that, which is a quiet with two causes in it and neither of them a person. Nell Kest stood at the far end of the bench with the day's figures squared in front of her and her hands at her sides, and the cold had got into the paper and into the gap at the back of her hand where it does not matter, and she had known all of it before she was in the room and had come in anyway, as she came in every morning, because the alternative was not a thing available to a woman whose pay had stopped arriving.

Before she went she put a twist of kindling from her own bag into the firebox and lit it, and it burned for half an hour and then it was out again, and she said nothing about the kindling and Nell Kest said nothing about the kindling.

The twist of kindling was the only thing burnt in the room for weeks and it showed, and the smell of it got into two coats and stayed there after the fire had gone, and it went up the wall behind the end of the bench and into the backs of the books and sat there for the rest of the day, and by the time the stove was out again the room had the cold in it and the smell in it together and neither of them was going to be first past it. The door at the end of the room stood open on the step for a while after the lane had gone quiet and came to by itself in a draught, and Nell Kest did not get up and shut it, because a woman who shuts a door on another woman in the same building is doing something, and there is nowhere in the building for what she would have been doing to go.

Nell Kest is not going to say the rule to that woman again, and neither of the other two of the four is in this lane or coming to it, and there is no third telling in this room in any month at all. Her pay stopped arriving in the second week of the third month of the year after and has not come since, and it is nineteen days of a crew's money, and it is sitting in a coat four miles off, and she is not going to ask the man what it is sitting against.

The coat was four miles off and she had not been near it since the pay stopped arriving, and nineteen days of a crew's money is a shape a person can go on carrying without it changing in any way an outsider would be able to see, and it had not changed this month either. She had got so used to it sitting where it sat that she had stopped doing arithmetic about it, and she noticed she had stopped, and she did not start again. A woman who stops doing arithmetic about a figure has not forgiven anybody for it and has not forgiven herself either. She had worked out where the figure was kept, and it was kept in a coat in a building she had no reason to be in, and there is no form anywhere for a woman with no reason to be in a building, and that is the whole of the difference between owing a thing and being able to do one single thing about it.

**She is not going up this river this season, and that is not never, and she has not used the word and is not going to.**

The sentence went to a wall of books with nobody in the room but herself, and a thing said to books does not come back to a person, which is the whole of what she has ever been sure about in the room. It was not never, and she has never once put the other word anywhere in the lane or out of it, and the difference between the two is a difference she can keep for as long as it is useful, and there is nobody in the lane who is in a position to take it off her. She had said one true thing out loud to a person in the building this week and had refused to say it a third time, and both of those were the same act, and the refusal is what it cost her.

The eleventh set of figures went back down the lane on the second of the two days, in a hand that does not vary. By the fourth hour the stove was out again and the room was the temperature of the yard, and the four of them on the bench had gone, and the rule had stayed where it was the first time it was put down.
