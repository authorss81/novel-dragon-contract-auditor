# Chapter 0387: A Notice With A Sum On It, A Space On The Back, And A Woman In A Counting Room

The counting room is at the back of a dye end, about four hundred and thirty miles up this river, and there is a stove behind the bench and a broken light in the window that has been broken since long before either of the women who work in it. Eleven houses off the lane send their figures in on the first of the two days and get eleven sets back on the second.

She is twenty. **Her name is Nell Kest, and sixteen books stand in this room, and fifteen of them are on loan from elsewhere, and the sixteenth carries her name in capitals at the head of it and has never once had a question set against it anywhere.** Her pay stopped arriving in the second week of the third month of the year after and has not come since. It is nineteen days of a crew's money and it is sitting in a coat four miles off in a room she has not been inside since she was seventeen.

---

The boy from the front of the dye end came in at the first hour with a notice folded once and put it on the bench face down and went out again without waiting, and he has made that run about twice a week for about a year and has never once stayed longer than the putting down of it.

She turned it over. There is a sum at the top of it in a fair hand, and a line of type under the sum, and a line of type at the foot, and between the two lines of type there is nothing at all.

It is the first notice in about two years that has come to that bench. She read the sum twice and did not say the figure out loud to an empty room and did not write it anywhere, and there is a reason for not writing a figure down in a room where a person works alone, and the reason is not modesty.

On the back of the notice there is a ruled space.

**No form for a space on the back of a notice**, and the difference between a form and a notice is the whole of it. A form is a thing that asks a person to put something in it. A notice is a thing that is told to a person, and somebody has printed a ruled space on the back of a notice about two inches deep, with a heading over the top of it in small type, and the heading is the one that takes the reason.

The heading was printed before anybody knew what was going to be left in it.

---

The second of the four women went past the door at about the first hour and did not come in, and that is the fourth day running of her not coming in, and Nell Kest went out into the lane after her with the notice in her hand and caught her at about twenty feet.

"You have not been in," Nell Kest said.

"I have not been in."

"I have something to tell you and I am going to tell you standing out here, and I would rather say it at once than have it standing in that room on the second day."

The second woman stopped. "**No form for a reason a person is owed**, and I have been used once already, and what used me was carrying a rule out of that room to a woman in a house off that lane, and I have not been able to put it down since." She did not look at the notice. "That is a ruled space on the back of a piece of paper and a heading printed over it, and a heading over a space is a place where a person puts the reason that they are owed something, and the reason is the thing that can be held against you afterwards. I have watched a rule go out of a room and come back in a different shape and I am not going to watch a person put a reason in a printed space for a man who is owed money he has not been given."

"I have not filled it in."

"I know you have not. You have had it in your hand for a quarter of an hour." The second woman took her shawl up over her head. "**A reason a person is owed is a reason a person can be held against, and once it is in there it belongs to whoever printed the heading.** Say that back to yourself once on the way in and then do not say it to me."

**She went down the lane and did not look back, and that is the second time a woman has carried something out of that room and not come back with it, and the first time cost the fourth house off that lane.**

---

It is the fourth day of the third week of the tenth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the first hour, and the stove had been out since before the lane woke.

The boy came back up the lane at about the fourth hour and Nell Kest stood in the lane until he did, with the notice folded in half, and she gave it to him with the ruled space on the back of it as clean as the day end had put it.

He went down the lane with it. Nell Kest went in and shut the door of that room behind her and stood at the bench a while before she lit the stove, and the stove took about half an hour to come and was out again by the seventh hour, and she did not ask the man four miles off what any of it is sitting against, and she is not going up this river this season, and that is not never.

By the end of the week that notice was in a coat four miles off with the other nineteen days and a printed heading on the back of it, and the heading is the only part of the whole of it that will still be legible in a year.
