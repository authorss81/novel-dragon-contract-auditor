# Chapter 0395: The Shed At The Slade End, A Form That Has Been On The Board A Month, And A Third Reason

The form has been on that board since the tenth month. It came round the front of that shed on a paper about the size of a man's palm and it was pinned where a person coming in would have had to read it to get past, and it carries two ruled spaces with a heading printed over each of them and a date set in the middle, and there is nowhere on that paper for a person of seventeen to put no in. **Four second days have gone by with it on that board and nobody has found it void, and there is nobody in that lane with the standing to find anything void.**

She is fifty-one and her name is Halla Wray, and she came by the foreman's job of that shed in a room behind the second one of these about eleven years ago. Nine families settled it there in something over an hour, and she was elsewhere, and nobody went out of that building to fetch her.

Since the first week of the seventh month of the year after, that job has been held in a box that a class put her in, and nobody in that shed or in the lane behind it has asked her a single thing about it in all the time since.

The two of seventeen are on the bench at the back. **Their engagement was in writing,** and the writing went up to somebody and came back signed, and it came and went between the second of the two days four weeks ago and the second of the two days three weeks ago, and not one person at the front bench has said a syllable about any part of it to either of them, and the girl was not given the chance to say no to a person in that room.

**There is no form for how long a paper has been on a board.** There is one for the paper itself and there is one for each of the two ruled spaces on it, and the length of time a thing has hung on a board is not a thing anybody in that lane has ever been asked to account for, and a foreman who accounts for it is a foreman who has taken that paper off the board in her own head and read it.

---

The lamp over the middle of that bench does not come on until about the seventh hour and **had not been touched at the fifth,** and the man of about twenty-eight was standing at the front of it with his back to the door when she came in off the yard.

"It has been on that board a month," Halla Wray said, to the board, and she had got as far as that.

"No." He did not turn round. "And I am going to stop you, and it is not the reason from a month ago and it is not the one before that, and I would take it as a kindness if you noticed and as a fault if you did not."

"You have told me twice that you would think about it on the way in before you gave me one."

"I have, and I have, and this one took me the whole length of the walk and I am not going to pretend it cost me anything to arrive at." He turned and put his hand flat on the front bench, which is where he puts it. "**There is no form for saying a month.** That is the whole of it, and it is the plainest of the three, and I have kept it back until now on purpose. You stand in the middle of that shed and you say the word month out loud, and there are about nine men in here who have been in this lane longer than I have, and in saying it to that board you have told nine men that there are two people at the back of this shed, and you have said it on a day when I could not have stopped nine men hearing it."

---

"They are not children. They are on that bench doing the second of the two days of an engagement, and the engagement is over, and nobody has told either of them anything."

"They have not been told anything and neither has anybody else in here, and that is the arrangement, and you have just been about to say out loud in front of nine men that it is an arrangement." He took his hand off the wood. "The time before last I told you that a form nobody has found void is one that is still on that board next week, and the last time I told you that a foreman who explains a form is a foreman somebody has acted on. Both of those were true and neither of them was the reason, and I have spent a month finding out what the reason was, and there it is, and it is not an argument."

She said nothing. **She put her hand flat on the end of that bench the way she does and left it there,** and the sentence about the month stopped where it had got to and is still stopped there this week, and the half she gave away in a doorway in a month that is gone has not arrived either, and nobody in that lane has sent anybody for it.

---

The man of about fifty-five with the chain got down off his own end of that bench and said his four sentences to the middle of that shed without putting his hand on anything at all. There is a thing on that paper that is neither a figure nor a name, and he has never made out what it is for, and neither could the four of them behind him, and that is not the part of it he is saying. What he is saying is the weight of a folded sheet and of a chain he has worn since he was a young man. **And that a man can keep a hand on a thing like that from the first hour to the seventh hour and nobody in that building would know that he had.** Nobody answered him and he has never once wanted an answer, and nothing in that shed was read out loud this week, because nothing in that shed is being done with.

There are about four men in there who cannot make a paragraph out of anything, and no question about a figure has ever been put to one of them by anybody, and the chalk mark on that end wall is still level with the third link of that chain and is still only a mark.

---

It is the fourth day of the third week of the twelfth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fifth hour, and nothing on that board has been touched today.

The form went back up with the rest of the morning's papers and the gate at the bottom of that lane stayed shut all week, and at the seventh hour the lamp came on over the middle of the bench. The two at the back were still on their own bench with that paper in plain sight nine foot in front of them. **It will be up there again on the second of the two days next week, and nobody has said one word to either of them and nobody is going to.**
