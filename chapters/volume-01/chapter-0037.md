# Chapter 37: Two Named Duties

The Mosswake Roll Court moved its fixed benches out onto the terrace for the sitting, because the river courtroom has one window and the window faces the channel, and at the eleventh hour on the third of the fourth month the light off the water came straight in and sat on the front bench, and the clerk had decided in advance that the light was a feature of the room and would be complained about afterwards.

Sivra Oris came in at the hour and did not sit on the front bench. She sat on the second one, side-on, with her forelegs folded under her and her wings against her back, and she is smaller than she is supposed to be and that is the first thing about her. A dragon's drop-off at a Reed Cut gate is two hundred and eleven pounds of iron, and a water keeper stands beside one for the better part of a working day, and she weighs about what a draught horse weighs, and a man who has read about her in a file for five months is not ready for that.

The gate was nine feet behind the clerk's shoulder through the open embankment door, and it had been back inside a quarter of an inch since the water office crew had reset it in the eleventh month under their own posted authority, which is the fifth term, and which is the only part of the whole of this that anyone in the room got the way they wanted it.

Marek Kest sat on the front bench with Tamsin Rook beside him, because Rell had made a direction about that which nobody argued with, which was that an applicant in a limited sitting must have a person with him who is not his office and who is named in the minute book, and Tamsin Rook's name went in the minute book under the words *attending for the applicant, having no office*.

Hallis Dren came up from Reed Cut on the morning boat and was in the room in his own coat with the water office's entry in front of him, and Mara Venn and Tavi Venn came down from the lane, and there were eleven other people, and six of them were in the lane in the cold waiting to be told.

Rell read the nine lines of the direction aloud at the start, because a court that has narrowed a thing must say the narrowing out loud, and then she said the other thing, which is the thing a court has to say before it lets anybody in the room understand what they are being offered.

"Before we begin. The person at the second bench is not a witness and is not being examined. She is a counterparty to an instrument of the nineteenth of the second month of the year four years back, and she has been notified of this proceeding on the notice of this court, and she is not under this court's authority, and this court cannot require her to do anything, including to be here. She may leave at any point. She may refuse every question. What she says will be sworn and entered if she asks for it to be sworn, and it will not be sworn and will not be entered if she does not. There is no consequence to her in this room of any kind, and I want that said in the room."

"I have asked for it to be sworn," said Sivra Oris.

"And I am going to say what swearing it is, in the room, because you are entitled to know it and you are entitled to be asked before you do it and not after." Rell turned the form round on the desk. "A statement in scale form, sworn, is an admission against interest. You are the party. Anything you say about your own duty under that instrument is a thing a district or a person may thereafter hold you to, in a court that is not this one, for as long as the duty or the instrument lasts. That is what swearing costs. You may refuse, and a refusal is entered and nobody may say afterwards that you were asked and refused, because I will say so in the minute book. And you may say it and not have it sworn, and then it is a conversation and goes in nothing."

"Then it is not a conversation."

"No. It is not."

Tavi Venn stood up on the third bench, which she had not done, and the room let her.

"Ma'am."

"Yes."

"Does this change anything for me."

Rell did not look at the papers.

"No. Not one thing. Your mother's house, the four quarters of the upper holding, the two rooms over a shop, and a gate are exactly what they were this morning, and they are exactly what they were on the second of the fourth month when that term ran out, and what was sworn in this room is a statement by a dragon about her own duty and not a decision about yours, and I have told you in advance that this court will not turn a document into a thing you can win with, and I am not going to do it now because you are seventeen and in the room."

"I did not think it would." Tavi Venn sat back down. "I wanted to ask in front of them. I wanted it said out loud in a room that it was asked and that the answer was no, because in March there is a man going to come and say the channel needs an undertaking and there is nobody in this lane who can say that the last person who signed one for this ground was caught by a word in a box."

"That is a different matter and it will be a hearing and not a sitting, and you will be in it, and nobody will be able to say you were not heard."

"Then that is the whole of what I came for," said Tavi Venn, "and I have got it, and I am sorry it took a dragon to say it."

"You did not need a dragon to say it," said Sivra Oris, without moving. "You needed a room and a witness. There is a trestle table at the mouth of your lane and four questions on it and eleven people in your lane whose names are in chalk. You have been using them for six months. You do not need me and I have not come here to be useful to you."

That is the whole exchange, and it is entered in the minute book because everything said in that room that day was, and Tavi Venn read it out afterwards in the lane to about thirty people, and the sentence that went round the lane was not the keeper's and was not the magistrate's, and was: *She said you do not need her.*

---

The form was drawn by the court and it was four leaves and it had a number, because it was an entry in the minute book and not a form, and there were four copies of it and every copy of it would go out of that room with a number on it and a court seal on the corner.

It had two boxes.

The first box was headed *the party swearing* and the second was headed *the other party to the instrument*, and there was nothing printed under the second one but a rule, and a note in the court's hand at the foot of the first page, which was:

*This statement is made once. A party who has made a statement in scale form in a matter cannot be asked to make another in the same matter, and cannot be asked to fill the second box. The second box is filled, if it is ever filled, by a person who was a party to the instrument at the time of the signing.*

Nobody in the room understood that sentence at the moment it was read, and about nine people in the room understood it completely by the time the sitting was over.

"The estate of Aldren Venn was notified with you," said Rell. "Nobody has come. There is nobody to come, and I am going to say the reason in the room, because otherwise the second box looks like a thing somebody forgot. Aldren Venn signed on the nineteenth of the second month of the year four years back and died in the fourth month after that. The instrument was between two people. One of them is sitting on the second bench. The other one is in a ground on the upper lane with a stone on him and no capacity to swear to anything ever again, and there is no proceeding under which that capacity returns."

"Then the box stays empty," said Sivra Oris.

"The box stays empty, and the statement is complete, and it is complete for ever, and it is the only complete document about that instrument in the empire and it is complete with a hole in it."

Sivra Oris looked at the form for a while.

"Enter the hole," she said. "Do not rule it off. Do not write *no answer* in it. Leave it blank and enter that it is blank, with the reason, and let the reason be the estate of a man who is dead and a court that cannot reach him. In four years a clerk will open this and see a box with a line through it and a clerk's tidy word in it, and he will think somebody refused. There was no refusal. There was a man in a ground."

"That will be entered," said Rell, "in those words."

---

She swore it at the twelfth hour, and it took about nine minutes, and Marek Kest found afterwards that he could not remember any of the nine minutes, only the shape of the room and the fact that somebody read a document out loud in a language he could follow perfectly in a voice he had never heard use a sentence that way.

> *I am Sivra Oris, of the Reed Cut water office of this province, keeper of the Reed Cut gate and the navigation above it, and I am a party to an instrument made on the nineteenth of the second month of the year four years back between myself and Aldren Venn, a human, of the lower holding at the Reed Cut, who is dead.*
>
> *I speak to my own duty under that instrument. I make no statement as to the duty of any other person, and no statement as to any office, and no statement as to what any person has done since, and I have not seen the correction and I will not describe it.*
>
> *The instrument created two duties and it named the people who held them. Aldren Venn held the field-side upkeep and the winter seed, and they were his, and they were his work, and they were his name on the top of the sheet, and he was a man and not a place.*
>
> *I held the gate and the navigation, and they were mine, and they were my work, and they were my name on the top of the sheet, and I am a dragon and I am still a person doing a piece of work, and a duty of mine is discharged by me and by nobody else, and a gate is not held by whoever is nearest the water.*
>
> *Neither of those duties is an office. Neither of them can be filled by anybody else without the person who takes it on signing for it, and the instrument says so, in the words it had, at the end, where it says that whoever thereafter assumes a continuing field-side duty at that place shall sign a new obligation for it.*
>
> *The alteration of that instrument did not alter either duty. I know that because neither duty has moved. My gate has been shut and opened through six seasons and the water office crew has paid the cost of it and the district has paid the cost of it and the household has not, since the ninth month of the ninth year, and that is because of an order this court made, and not because anybody argued me out of it.*
>
> *The alteration changed who was held by the duties. That is all it did. It did not make a new duty. It did not make an office. It took two people who cannot be occupied and put a word in their place that can be, and the word is a word any person may stand in.*
>
> *I have watched that word used in two district courts in seven years, in two other matters, under my own seal's authority, without being asked, and I have no office to complain to about it that is not the office that did it, and I have never in seven years asked this court for anything, and I am not asking it for anything today.*

Rell read it back. Sivra Oris said it was hers. Rell read it back a second time, slowly, and then it was sworn, and it was entered, and the second box stayed blank, and the clerk wrote in the margin at the foot of it, in Ivet Sarn's own hand and at the magistrate's direction and in the words the keeper had used, that the box is blank and is complete so and is not to be treated as a refusal, and the reason is an estate and a grave.

---

Four people wanted something from her, and she gave it to none of them, and Marek Kest has been able to account for all four ever since.

The first was Rell, who wanted her to say whether the holder of the field at the date of the correction was in occupation or in possession, and who had to have it, because Rell had a date to strike a rate by on the sixth, and a sentence in nine hundred and forty sheets and a channel to keep.

"That is a question about the other party," said Sivra Oris. "I did not see the field. I see water."

The second was Marek Kest, and what he wanted was a date, and he wanted it so badly that he had written it in the nine things he was not asking for as a way of not asking for it, and he asked it anyway, at the fifth hour, in one sentence, and she answered the whole of it in half a sentence.

"You want to know whether the seed was ever repaid. I do not know. I know what I was paid and I know when, and it was the winter after he died, and the water office has a receipt for it, and the receipt is for water, and a receipt for water is not a statement that a man's debt is paid. Ask the water office. Not me."

The third was a man from the Notaries' Table who had come up on the morning boat and whom Marek had not known was coming, and who wanted to know which of two hands in a filed correction of the ninth of the eighth month was the ordinary hand of the district and which was not, because if the ordinary hand was the broker's then the Table had certified the work of a man with a bench in a lane off the Kiln road and the Table's own position was different from what the gallery believed.

"I have not seen the correction," said Sivra Oris, "and I will not describe a hand, and there is nothing about a hand in this form, and if your office wants to know whose hand it is then your office has a question about your office."

The fourth was Tamsin Rook, and the only thing Tamsin Rook wanted was for the keeper to say the sentence about the word one more time, slowly, so that a copyist could get it exactly, and Sivra Oris looked at her for slightly too long and then said, "You want the sentence. You have the sentence. I gave it the way I gave it, and it is written down, and the writing down is the whole of what a statement is for. Nobody has ever yet asked me for a thing twice and been given the same thing at the same speed twice, and I am not going to start in a room this size."

---

She left before the hour was out, which nobody tried to prevent, and at the door she stopped and looked back at the room for about as long as it takes to decide something, and Marek Kest thought for the rest of his life that she had been looking at the person in the room who most needed the thing she had not said, and that she had decided, on the balance of seven years, not to give it to him.

Then Rell made the order, and it is the only thing in the ten days that the batch produced that is going to survive longer than the people in the room, and it is one sentence, and it has her name at the foot of it.

> *This court cannot order the disclosure of any document. It cannot strike, void, amend, or suspend the correction filed on the ninth of the eighth month. It cannot touch the series MC/FS, or any standing instruction of any district, or any return made under any instrument sealed with the fire-and-water device. And it cannot say, and does not know, and after four months of asking four offices and one district water office has not been able to find out, who authorised the instruction sealed with the device which stands upon the Venn file. Nothing said in this room alters any of those things. The statement entered above is not a decision, is not a precedent, and may not be cited in any other matter, and this sitting is not an audit and may not be described as one.*
>
> *A party to this matter may apply, on this record, in the fifth month, to the court named in the clause for appeal from a scale instrument of this district, which is the Cinder Court at Auremar, and this court has no power to make that application and does not make it.*
>
> *Leda Rell, magistrate, Mosswake Roll Court. Entered at the first hour of the afternoon of the third of the fourth month.*

And on the terrace, afterwards, in the cold, at about the second hour, with the channel going by under a sky the colour of a knife, Marek Kest said to Tamsin Rook, in about eleven words, the thing he had not been able to say for six months.

"She will not say who did it."

"No," said Tamsin Rook. "And she told you why, and the why is true, and it is a worse answer than a name would have been, and she gave it to you anyway, in a form that cannot be reissued, in a room, with her name on it, and you are going to want to argue with her about it for a year."

"I am going to want to argue with her about it for a year."

"Then argue with her about it in a room, at the gate, where she works, twice a year, like a person, and not about this, because this is done."
