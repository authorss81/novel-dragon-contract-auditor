# Chapter 40: A Gate Is Not a Field

The rate was struck at the water office on the sixth of the fourth month at about the third hour of the afternoon, and it took a clerk of thirty about ninety seconds, and four hundred and sixty relief rolls, parcels and households all came out whole, which they did because the process had been run in the twelfth and the first with the window shut, on purpose.

The schedule for the fifth term was still nailed to the rail of the Reed Cut gate, on the water office's own paper and under the water office's own authority, saying that the bank works and the gate reset were to be done by the district and not under the instrument, and that the household was not required to perform the field-side upkeep for the purposes of the order. Nobody in nine provinces had ever come to that gate to read it and it was going to be there all summer.

Marek Kest came down the lane at the fifth hour on the sixth with a letter in his coat that he had been owing since the tenth month, and Wendt Sayer was on the channel bank with a crew of four, because the crew does what the crew does in the fourth month whether or not anybody is watching a gate.

"There is a thing I have to pay for," said Sayer, before he had got his rope down. "Two shillings and sixpence. The guild charges two and six for a canal examination, and the girl has been examined for reading and writing and arithmetic, and the rest of it is the trade, and the trade costs two and six whether you are seventeen or forty."

"Who is paying it."

"I am. The works fund, and I have put it in as what it is, which is a recruitment cost, because in about three years the crew on this reach wants a second pair of hands and there is nobody in this district under forty who can cut a channel, and I would rather we had her in three years than a girl who is good with a bar in the fifth month." Sayer put the rope down. "It is in the book under the head *labour, examinations, engaged*, with the day on it, and in a year a man who is not me will read that head and ask why a district works paid a guild's fee for a person who was not under a district contract, and I shall have to write him four lines saying that a reach of channel costs the same whoever digs it. I am not being kind. Kindness is what I tell the crew at the end of the day. This is a cost."

Marek Kest stood on the bank and did not put his hand in his pocket once, and Sayer watched him not do it and said nothing about it, and that was the entire negotiation.

---

Mara Venn was in the kitchen with the door open and a bar of soap on the table, and she had been seven weeks without a field, and she did not ask him in and did not ask him to sit down, which are the two things that told him the whole shape of the conversation before it started.

"I have a letter for you," he said, "and I have owed it since the tenth month, and it is not good news and I have not found a way to make it good news, so I am going to read it out and you are going to say things at me."

"Then read it out. I am forty-three and I have a bar of soap."

He read it out.

> *Madame,*
>
> *There were two ways to enter your husband's file as complete, and I have to tell you that neither of them has happened, and that one of them is not going to happen to anybody.*
>
> *The first was ordinary. A clerk takes a filed correction, compares it with a summary, and enters the summary's version in the file. That is done forty times a year in every district in this empire and it is not done in this file, because I saw the difference between the summary and the leaf on the seventh of the ninth month and I would not have it entered. A summary is what a later file copies from. If a summary is allowed to overwrite a leaf, then what happened to your husband happens to the next one, and the next one is a person you will never meet.*
>
> *The second was the only one that was ever good. Your husband's file cannot be closed as paid or as unpaid without somebody's word about the winter seed of the second month, four years back. There is exactly one person who can give it. She held the Reed Cut gate. She swore a statement in this court on the third of the fourth month in the only form a scale statement can take, which is a form made once, and it has one box in it for the other party, and the other party is Aldren Venn, and he is in a ground on the upper lane, and that box will be empty for ever.*
>
> *So his file cannot be made complete. It is not a punishment and it is not a decision against you. It is a document that will say what it says: that a man signed a seven-year term on the nineteenth of the second month of the year four years back, that the term ended on the nineteenth of the second month of this year, and that no court has since decided what was owed at the end of it. That is what it will say in a hundred years. It is a better thing to have than a false one, and I know that it does not pay a bill.*
>
> *You have a house, four quarters of the upper holding, two rooms over a shop, and a gate. The fifth term's works are done under the water office's own authority, the schedule is nailed to the rail, and nobody has taken it down. I am not going to stand on your step and tell you that a gate is a field.*

Mara Venn listened to the whole of it with her hands flat on the table on either side of the bar of soap.

"Two things. I have known about both those ways since the September, because he kept his papers and was careful about the season he signed in, and the second one was never going to come, and I made my peace with that in about eleven days. And the first I heard about at the reading table in the ninth month, from a woman who was not going to tell anybody what to do, and she told me." Mara Venn took her hands off the table. "I want to know one thing and it is not about the letter. Does the keeper's statement say the field-side work was his or mine."

"His."

"Good." She said it the way you put a thing down. "Then I want to know what I am."

"You are a house and a gate and four quarters of the upper holding and two rooms over a shop, and you signed nothing after the nineteenth of the second month of the year four years back, and there is no duty standing against you or your daughter in any file I have ever seen. And the fourth term means that if anybody comes and asks either of you to take a continuing field-side duty at that place, one of you has to sign for it, and if you are asked to do it and you say no, that is a complete answer and you do not have to give a reason for it."

"Six weeks," said Mara Venn. "Six weeks I have been going up and down this lane being asked what I am going to do, by people who are being kind. You are the first one who has answered me in a noun. I have not been asked a question in six weeks that I could put on a slate."

Then Tavi Venn came in from the yard at about the seventh hour with her sleeves rolled to the elbow and stood in the doorway and did not come further in, and said, "Is that the canal examination fixed."

"Wendt Sayer has paid two shillings and sixpence out of the works fund as a recruitment cost and put it in the book under his own head," said Marek. "It is not paid by you, it is not paid by me, and it is dated the sixth of the fourth month, and it is in a district works book where a foreman can see it and cannot quietly take it out."

"Can I come."

"You can be examined. The guild witness is Odile Fenn, and the date is not the fourth month, because Odile Fenn's docket in the fourth month is the Dunn review, and a canal examination is not a thing she will do at a pinch." Tavi Venn put her hand flat on the frame of the door. "So it is the fifth month."

"It is the fifth month."

"Then that is a day," said Tavi Venn. "Neither of us has had a day since the nineteenth of the second month."

---

He read the letter on the Venn step, which took about six minutes, and then he sat on the step for a while, which he had not planned to do, and then he did the thing he had come down the lane to do, and which he had permission to do, and which is a thing he is not supposed to be able to do.

The limited audit of the Venn instrument was issued on the twelfth of the ninth month in open court and has never been discharged, because closing it would have required somebody to write a conclusion and the conclusion was never the point. The reference is on the fifth term's schedule, which is nailed to the rail eleven feet from where he was sitting, and the term it refers to ended seven weeks ago.

Then the seam came up the way it does, which is not like seeing and is not unlike hearing a note in the next room, and he had it in about four seconds, and he did nothing with it, because there was nothing in the room to do it with and nobody to see.

> **Named:** the present holder of the Venn field.
> **Promised:** the present holder may be joined as guarantor until full review.
> **Carried after death:** nothing. The term is at an end and the record names no successor, and there is nobody in it to carry anything.

He read it three times and it did not change and it did not improve, and it was the first time since the eighth of the ninth month that a reading had come back and told him nothing he could not have read off a deed.

The instrument named a place and not a person, the way a field is in a deed, and the whole of the disaster of the last seven months was that a word had been put in a box on a form in a corridor in Auremar to make a place answer to a person. The word was *the present holder*. The present holder of a field whose term ran out on the nineteenth of the second month is nobody at all, and a record that carries nothing is the most accurate record in this case.

It decided nothing. It was three lines, it was accurate about a file, and it had no opinion about a woman with a gate. What it told a twenty-nine-year-old clerk was that he had spent seven months on a file which now says nothing of its own accord, and that a file which says nothing cannot be made to say something by a clerk, and that the only person who could ever have spoken to the winter seed was standing nine feet away with a bar and was seventeen.

The cost came at the fourth minute and it came the way it had come in September, which was taste first — ash, and iron, and something underneath it that he had never been able to name — and then the headache, behind the left eye, worse in the second minute than the first, and then the nose, which is the part he has never written down in a book and has never told anybody about, and which bled about a teaspoon onto the rail of the gate in the rain and stopped when it stopped.

He sat on the step for half an hour afterwards while the lane went about its business, and then he could not look at print for two days, and on the seventh of the fourth month he wrote the whole of it in his own book with a hand that was not his own and had to stop twice. He did not go to Doctor Perrin Ashe at the landing. There is nothing a penny buys, and there is no medicine for this, and he stopped looking for one in the eleventh month of the ninth year.

---

Magistrate Rell wanted to know what it cost and she asked him at the fourth hour of the seventh, in her rooms, with the court clerk there because a thing said in a room to a magistrate gets written down or it does not get said.

"You ran it outside the window."

"I did. The clerk wrote the reason on the wall in the first person on the twenty-sixth of the third month with the day on it, and it says that she gave me the answer and that I asked her the question so that it would be in her hand and not mine, and that is all true and I would like it to stay in that order."

"I read the wall."

"Then you know it better than I do." Marek Kest put his hands flat on the table, which is what he does when he is about to say a number he does not enjoy. "Thirty-four hearings. Two of them at the ninth hour at night, because the woman who asked for them works the channel. Four people said the words *I do not understand this*, and they are in the book as answers and not as refusals, and eleven of the other thirty signed something else inside a month, and both facts are in the same box."

"I read both out loud. On the fourth. Twice."

"Then you know it compels nothing and creates no precedent, and this district may or may not do it again next season and will decide that itself."

"It does not," said Leda Rell. "The review is over. That wall does nothing now, except that nine people in this district know where a hearing can be asked for and every one of them can prove it happened. That is not a rule and it is not a precedent, and it is the only thing I have got out of two months of my own time."

"And the price."

"Two hundred and eighty-four relief rolls, unencumbered, and struck whole on the sixth with every one of them certified, because a clerk had the sense to run the thing in a month where it cost nothing." She turned a page. "Say it, because it is going to be in a book somewhere and I would rather it was in yours first. The thing you were offered and did not take would have cost this district two hundred and eighty-four relief rolls it has not yet lost. It was a good offer and it would have worked. It is a price and it is not a bill, and I will not have anybody in this building say in five years that a district ate late because of a refusal."

"I would refuse it again."

"I know you would. That is not what I asked." Rell put the pen down. "What I asked is whether you can say it in the past tense, on the record, about work you did and about a decision that is finished, without talking about it as though it were still happening to you."

Marek Kest looked at the wall, which was not in that room.

"It was made on the ninth of the eleventh month and I declined it on the twentieth, in a room, with a witness, and the record is signed on the back of the offer. It would have been applied by forty-six people each doing their own honest best, and I have never been able to find the sentence in which that is a good outcome, and I would refuse it again tomorrow, and I am going to stop thinking about it, which is the fourth and last thing I have to say about it."

"Entered," said Ivet Sarn, "at the seventh of the fourth month, at the fourth hour, in the magistrate's rooms, in the words of the applicant."

---

The second thing in the room was the Dunn household, and it was the reason the magistrate had said *now* and not *in May*.

Orra Dunn's request had been on the hearing slate since the second of the first month in Sarn's hand with *asked, not granted* under it, on the stated ground that a creditor holds a paper and has said he will serve it on a seed-share in the second month. The paper was served on the twenty-ninth of the first month, a month early, and the forty quarters of rye went thirty-one miles up a canal to a mill at Coldsfoot and were sold there at an unpriced price, and the sale is in a book, and the book is right.

"Mr Dunn's household asked for this review to be called in the second month," said Rell, "and I am going to answer it in a room and I am going to answer it now, in April, in front of a clerk, and I am not going to have it sit on a slate for a third month with somebody's name on it."

"You could not grant it in February," said Marek. "You had a docket."

"I could not grant it in February because the docket was the rate and I was striking it, and I said so at the time." Rell did not look up. "The request is refused and the refusal is dated today. The review was held on the fourth and it was not a hearing of any application. I told your clerk in November that if she built a thing needing a magistrate in it, it dies in the fourth month. It did not die. That is the only reason I can refuse a request from a room instead of from a wall."

"And the second hearing."

"Called for the twenty-first of the fifth month, at the second hour, in this room, and the household is to be heard and the girl is to be heard, and I am going to say the rest of it in the room in May and not in a corridor in April." She wrote a date on a slip and turned it round. "And I am going to tell you one thing about a creditor, since you are the person in this building who has been treating him as a question, and he is not. Anselm Grice promised that household, in his own door, in the ninth month, that he would hold to the first week of the fourth month and not one day past, and he put it in nothing. That promise has now expired. It expired on the seventh day of this month, three days ago, and a man's private promise to a woman is the only thing he had, and it has gone, and I have no power over him, and there is nothing in this room that stops him and there will not be until the twenty-first of the fifth month."

"Then what stops him."

"Her, and a date, and Ilsabet Kell, who is sixty-one and who is coming in the fifth month with her own note in her own hand, and whose son knows about the note and has not been asked to stop her." Rell put the slip down. "Do not go and warn them. I mean that. If you go up that lane tonight and tell them a creditor's promise has expired, then in three weeks he will say a lawyer advised her, and in six weeks it will be a hearing about a lawyer instead of a hearing about a girl of fifteen who signed nothing."

---

He filed the closing record in the third gallery on the evening of the seventh of the fourth month, with a hand that was not his own, and it is four lines, and it is the only thing he has produced in four months.

> *Filed in this matter, in the order in which it was made: a sworn statement in scale form, entered at the Mosswake Roll Court on the third of the fourth month, in which the keeper of the Reed Cut gate swears that the instrument of the nineteenth of the second month of the year four years back created two named personal duties and no office — the field-side work and the winter seed being Aldren Venn's, the gate and the navigation being the keeper's — and in which the other party's box is blank, and is entered as complete so, and the reason is entered, and the box is blank for ever.*
>
> *A review, held at the same court on the fourth of the fourth month, of a six-week process tried between the first of the twelfth month and the twelfth of the first month, which created no precedent, is not binding on any district, and compelled nothing, and whose whole accounting is on a public wall in the court's own hand with the day on it.*
>
> *A sealed decision on the scope of the request of the eighteenth of the ninth month, received in this building on the ninth of the fourth month, which is true, is signed by nobody, and refers three of five questions to a document held in another province; and a further request filed on the ninth of the fourth month over the signature of a roll-keeper of this gallery, in her own name, naming the district of Glasswater, and unanswered.*
>
> *The request of the eighteenth of the ninth month and its supplement of the eighth of the eleventh month are not withdrawn, are not answered, and remain in Examinations custody.*

Then he took the copy list out of the chest under the winter coat and looked at the bottom of the fourth column. Forty-one names, thirty-eight addresses, thirty-two answers, and nine who had not answered: one in Reed Cut, one man who had put a copy in a drawer and not confirmed it, and four of them one house of dyers in a furnace town four hundred and forty miles away, in a lane with no numbers. One of those four was a person called Osa Ferren, who had written to a trestle table on the sixteenth of the fourth month and had asked, in four lines, in a schoolroom hand, what a person does who is owed an answer.

He wrote the name in the fourth column and put the book back in the chest, and the chest did not lock, because it had never locked, because it is a chest of clothes.

And then Marek Kest sat on the end of a bench in the copying alcove of the third gallery of the Roll Hall at about the eleventh hour on the seventh of the fourth month, with a headache that had been going for two days, and found out what he had.

The hearing slate was a hundred and forty miles down the river with thirty-four names on it, and a wall beside it with nine feet of writing on it and a magistrate's name at the foot of the third paragraph. The four questions were on a board at the mouth of a lane with four house names in chalk under them and a trade's private arithmetic in a smaller hand, and they were being asked of assessors and foremen and women who could not read. The witness order was on a wall in Mosswake, and a signed copy of it was four hundred and forty miles away in a kitchen in a furnace town, in a woman's own hand, under a name.

Every one of those things was a person. A copyist in a river courthouse was writing out the four-box form by hand two hundred and forty times and had asked nothing of anybody. A woman in a cooper's shop three hundred miles down the water had written in a book that says on its face it is worth nothing that no form in this case was broken. A foreman had entered two shillings and sixpence in a works book under the head *labour, examinations, engaged*, and a man will read that head in a year and ask why. And a woman of sixty-one is going to a room in the fifth month with a note in her own hand, because a widow wrote the sentence out for her in the old woman's own words and would not let her thank her.

All of it was in other people's hands and all of it was working, and Marek Kest was the only one of them with nothing to hand anybody. He had built it exactly the way he had said in the eleventh month that he would, which is to say in a way that could be read by somebody he had never met and would never know his name.

He had got what he came to Auremar for. It had taken him twenty-nine years and four months and a missing mother's name and a man's signature on a piece of paper and a woman on a channel bank, and it was not in a room, and it was not in his hand, and there was no next thing on any list in this building with his name at the top of it.

Which was, sitting there at the eleventh hour with a hand that would not do what it was told, the thing he had wanted since the fifth of the ninth month. The reason he could not sleep was that he had got it, and that the next morning he would have to be a person with nothing to hand anybody, on a Monday, in a corridor, for a year.
