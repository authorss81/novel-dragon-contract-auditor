# Chapter 46: A List of Offices

The Cinder Court sits in seven numbered rooms off the great stair of the Crown Kiln, and a court with numbered rooms is a court that has heard so many foundational and scale disputes that it has had to stop pretending to have a name for each one.

Room the fourth, on the morning of the seventeenth of the sixth month, at the second hour, with the doors open and a clerk at the back of the room with a fire-door key on a chain, which is a thing the court does in the fourth room because a clear hearing may not have a door shut in it.

The registrar read the caption twice.

He was about fifty and he had a heavy file under his arm that had been heavy for years, and he read the first line, and he came back to it, and he read it a second time, and then he put his thumb on the second line.

"Upon the record of the Mosswake Roll Court as stated at the third of the fourth month, and upon its decision of the twenty-first of the fifth month, the Mosswake Roll Court refers to the Cinder Court at Auremar. Marek Kest, clerk of the Inheritance Desk, is a party and not a claimant."

"Good," said Marek.

"I have not said whether it is good." The registrar put the file down. "I have never had a document from a provincial court. I have had four hundred from a Chancery, every one of them an application, every one with a man's name at the top of it, and every one asking for something." He looked up. "You are asking for nothing. Where is your asking."

"There is no asking," said Marek Kest. "There is a record. It was put together over eight months by people who are not in this room, and the person who put it together is the applicant, and he is not a claimant in it, and the only thing in it that is in his own hand is eleven lines saying what it does not ask for."

"Then the first question." The registrar turned a page. "The referring court's three questions, quickly. Are the nine paragraphs of the eighteenth of the ninth month before this court."

"They are not."

"Are the four lines a roll-keeper filed on the ninth of the fourth month, naming a district, before this court."

"They are not."

"Is the series before this court. The circular, the base issue of the second month, run five hundred and sixty, the amendment sheet of the twenty-second of the ninth month, run nine hundred and forty, the distribution line, the standing authority, the box in a third bay of a shelf."

"No." Marek heard himself say it and heard how it sounded. "None of it. The referral names the amendment sheet and the fact that it carries the sentence, and nothing else. This court will be told the words out of a record of a decision, not out of a document, because the document is not before it."

The registrar wrote for about eleven seconds.

"That is a very small case," he said.

"It is the smallest case in this building."

"See room two last month." The registrar squared the file. "Now. The respondent."

---

A seat is not a party. That is the whole difficulty and the registrar had clearly been expecting somebody to raise it since the eighth hour and nobody had, and he raised it himself, in the flat voice of an official stating a procedural fact that has a name.

"The thing that would be answering this referral is the instruction sealed with the device which stands upon the Venn file. The face of that instrument carries a number in a ring and a device and nothing else. There is no office at the head of it. The provincial hold's reference, which is a different document and is not before this court, carries no office name either, and the fire-and-water device was first named aloud in an open court on the twelfth of the ninth month and has not been given a name on the face of anything since, which is either scruple or design and this court is not going to decide which."

He turned round a sheet and put it on the desk.

"Four hundred years of this court have been spent on the assumption that a document has a person at the top of it. Where it does not, this court enters the device. There is a form for it. It is called an entry by device, it was drawn in the reign of the third of the Ashfall line for a case nobody can now find, it is used about twice in a decade, it is in the book behind the bench, and if I read the whole of it out it will take twenty minutes and it will be the most quoted thing said in this room in nine years."

"Read the whole of it," said Marek Kest.

So the registrar read the whole of it. It took seventeen minutes and it was in the flat form used at the Crown Kiln, and the operative sentence was that where a document is produced whose face bears the seal of a seat and no name, the seat is entered as a respondent by its device, and no person of or belonging to that seat is a party, cited, or examinable, and no inference of any kind may be drawn in that proceeding from the fact that a person has not been named, and the entry is made in the open minute book with the device struck in wax in the presence of the clerk at the back of the room.

And when it was finished the registrar looked up.

"That line has been read aloud in a hearing four times in nine years, and three of those were read by a person who was relieved." He looked down the room. "It does not say that a person was not named. It says that this court will not draw a conclusion about a person out of the shape of a document, and that is the only rule in the compact's own procedure that does any work in this room, and it works on a record that is nothing but names. Nineteen of them. Six are in this room. None of them is at the top of anything."

---

"Marek Kest. You have eleven lines of things you are not asking for. I am going to give you four questions and you may put them, and I will tell you now what I am going to do with three of them, which is refuse them, so that you do not spend an hour on a thing I have already decided."

"Go on."

"One. Whether this court will order disclosure of any document from any office, including the Standing Office of Provincial Continuities and including any district. Two. Whether this court will strike, void, amend or suspend the correction filed on the ninth of the eighth month, which is four hundred and thirty miles away and in another building. Three. Whether this court will say who set the sheet of the twenty-second of the ninth month in type." The registrar put his pen down. "Those three I will refuse, and I will refuse them in a form with a name at the foot of it, because that is the only shape of refusal anybody in this matter has ever got a use out of, and I have read the four months of this file on the way up here, and a refusal with a name at the foot of it was worth more to you than a decision without one."

Marek Kest had not expected that and had about four seconds to decide what to do with it, and what he did was stop.

"Then ask me the fourth."

"Fourth." The registrar wrote it down before he said it, which he had not done for the first three, and it went on the record as having been put by the referring court and not by the applicant. "Whether the sentence stated in open court by the Mosswake Roll Court on the twelfth of the ninth month, as printed in the amendment sheet of the twenty-second of the same month issued as approved model wording, may be the recorded reason for a decision in any district without a hearing of the person it names."

"Yes," said Marek. "That is the fourth."

"I will take it," said the registrar, "and I am going to take it late, after the document, because you have been talking about the case for an hour and this is not a case, and the first thing I am going to do is put a document into this court, and the document is going to be one that says on its face that it may not be cited in any other matter."

---

The statement in scale form came out of the sleeve in front of the court at about the fourth hour. It is four leaves and it has a number on it because it is an entry in a minute book and not a form.

"May I ask the referring court, through the applicant, what that line means on the first page, in the hand of the magistrate who drew the form."

"It means that a party who has made a statement in scale form in a matter cannot be asked to make another in the same matter, and cannot be asked to fill the second box, and that the second box is filled, if it is ever filled, by a person who was a party to the instrument at the time of the signing." Marek said it in the words. "The second box is blank. It is entered as complete so, with the reason entered, and the reason is that the other party is dead and no proceeding returns that capacity. The box is empty for ever."

"Then it is a document with a hole in it that is entered as complete, made once, not again, and it may not be cited." The registrar put his hand flat on the first leaf. "I am not citing it."

"You are reading it into this court."

"I am having it read into this court as a document. The rule the referring court wrote is against being put to the same question twice in another matter and being compelled to answer it a second time. This is not another matter. This is the same matter, brought up here, and the party is not before this court, has not been cited, is not examinable, and will not be." He looked up. "What goes in the record is its existence, its date, its court, its form, its two boxes, the fact that one is blank and entered as complete so, and the fact that the person who swore it spoke only to her own duty and named nobody and was not examinable by four people who wanted something. That is a fact about the record and not about the oath, and if the referring court thinks I have taken a statement out of a box I will hear that argument at length."

The document was read. It took nine minutes, in a language the room followed perfectly, in a voice that had never said a sentence that way before and did not say it that way again, and at the end of it nobody asked the dragon anything, because she was not there and had not been cited and could not be, and the only sentence said in that room about her was the one about a box.

---

And then the registrar asked for the file, and read the rest of it himself, and nobody argued with him while he did it.

He had the minute of the third of the fourth month, and the review of the fourth and the wall, and the decision of the ninth of the fourth, one page, sealed, true, with no name at the foot of it, whose second paragraph he read twice and about which he said nothing, because nobody in the room had ever told him and a judge-magistrate of the Cinder Court does not go into a Chancery gallery in the middle of a hearing.

He had the ruling of the twelfth of the ninth with its five terms, numbered; the ledger, or the copy, ninety-one leaves, six columns, three hundred and six lines, sixty-one certificates and ninety-eight sequences at four shillings; the two provincial exhibits, admitted on a district clerk's own authority, and the endorsement on a dyers' house instrument entered itemised with the word *Itemised* underlined for the next reviewer by a clerk being more careful than his form required; a line in a register of her own, in a copyist's hand, in a book that says on its first page that nothing in it is evidence; and the four months of the record, in the order it happened, on nine leaves, because the referring court's clerk had put it in that order and had said in a covering line that he did it on purpose.

The registrar read for about forty minutes. He read the way Tamsin Rook reads, which is that he read the whole thing and stopped once.

He stopped on the eighth leaf, in the middle, and read a line again, and then said, "Registrar's note, for the minute. This is not a document and it is not evidence and I am putting it in the record as a note because if I do not put it in now there is no version of the next ten years in which anybody finds it."

> *Registrar's note. The referring record contains the following, each of them in the name of a person or a body that did exactly what it was required to do:*
>
> *First: a district water office which issued, on the eighth of the eighth month, a general memorandum authorising bank works, naming no parcel, and received on the next day a particular correction filed under it, and did not read it because corrections under flood authority are entered and not argued.*
>
> *Second: a provincial court which received that correction, entered it, and did not strike it, because a district court's business is to enter what is filed.*
>
> *Third: a Chancery standing office which issued, in a numbered series, a sheet on the twenty-second of the ninth month, ten days after a sentence was stated in a court in another province, carrying that sentence as approved model wording, under a note which permits a court to adopt it or any other wording, to forty-six addresses.*
>
> *Fourth: a landing office three hundred miles down the water which stamped a creditor's filled-in copy of the printed model into the routine column in about four seconds, in the ordinary way a landing office stamps what is put in front of it, and which cannot now produce a document, and which told the truth about the stamp when she was asked in front of two witnesses.*
>
> *Fifth: a dyers' combination in a furnace town, with a rule thirty-one years old at the second leaf of its own book, by which a house in the trade may not write to a court, a guild, a factor's office, a district office, or a stranger about an instrument without the assessor's leave in writing first, and which has had no dispute in forty years because of it, and by which four women have sat on four copies of a court ruling since the twentieth of the ninth month with nowhere to put four questions.*
>
> *No two of these five are within four hundred miles of each other. Four of them can no longer do harm. The third can do it again tomorrow, and there is nobody in it, and it is not a person.*

The registrar put the leaf down.

"The referring court has asked for a person four times in eight months and has been refused four times by three bodies and one drawer, and has been right four times." He looked at Marek Kest. "I do not know who authorised the instruction sealed with the device which stands upon the Venn file. The Mosswake Roll Court has recorded on the third of the fourth month, with a magistrate's name at the foot of it, that after four months of asking four offices and one district water office it does not know. I have had that record for six weeks. I have this court, a fire-door key on a chain, and a rule about beneficiaries, and I have no power that reaches a seal. I cannot compel it and I do not know it, and I am not going to say *unknown*, because that is a word an office can be asked about again, so I am saying it in this form, in a room, with a name at the foot of it."

And at the foot of the minute of the Cinder Court, room four, on the seventeenth of the sixth month, in the hand of Sela Vint, presiding, it says:

*This court does not know and cannot compel, and knows of no person at the head of the office whose standing authority sets the sheet of the twenty-second of the ninth month in type. — S. Vint, presiding, room four, Cinder Court.*

Then Sela Vint read out the orders, and there were four of them and the fourth was not the one anybody wanted.

---

"First. It is ordered that no guarantor duty shall attach to any person who has not been heard in a room and has not signed. This court states it in those words because a person is the only thing in this record that can be made to owe something, and the sentence referred to in this referral names a person, and no court in this empire may join a person as guarantor on the strength of a printed model of general wording without that person being heard and signing. It binds this court, and it binds any court that takes a recognition from this court, and it does not reach back to a decision already made in a district that has not been heard by this court, and the referring court made one on the twenty-first of the fifth month, and that one stands, and that one was made in a room."

"Second. It is ordered that the sentence stated by the Mosswake Roll Court on the twelfth of the ninth month, as printed in the amendment sheet of the twenty-second of the same month, is recorded by this court as model wording of general application and is not a decision of any court. It may not be the recorded reason for a decision in any district without a hearing of the person it names. The record of the sentence is a record of a document. It is not a record of a law, it is not a precedent, no district is bound by it, and no district is permitted to use it as a reason."

"Third. It is ordered that the seal certificates of Corvin Dray be suspended. It is made on the registrar's own authority and no argument was heard, because there is no argument: a certificate is a mark pressed cold with a screw press and a true seal is laid and takes about eleven seconds in the presence of the holder, and the registrar has had that in front of him twice and did not need it here. The count of the certificates is sixty-one. The ledger records the sale of a third witness mark in ninety-eight instances, of which twenty-two are in the Reed Cut district, six are in the Venn file, and one is in the Dunn file. It records, in the fourth column against the carry column, a mark — a filled square with a bar through it and a notch cut out of the top left corner — beside the word *paid*, against thirty-eight lines in three districts. The name of the house that keeps that mark is not in the file and this court does not know it. The mark goes into the record as a mark and not as a name, and it is in a counting house on a row by the Kiln sheds, and putting it in a public minute book will not alter one thing in that building except the number of people who know it is there."

"Fourth," said Sela Vint, and Marek Kest put both hands flat on the rail of the witness box, because he could hear the shape of it coming and could not stop it, "the sealed instruction standing upon the Venn file is escalated by this court, on its own motion, to a full sitting with jurisdiction, to be heard when this court's list permits, and the parties are notified."

"No," said Marek Kest.

"The applicant may be heard."

"I am objecting, not to the escalation, but to the seat of it in this room, in this document, on this day." He had it out in about four seconds and it was the only thing he had said all day that he had not written down first. "Magistrate, the first three orders are good and I want them and I will not argue with any of them. And you have just taken the thing I asked you to look at and put it in your own list for a year, and I have spent eight months finding out that a thing on a list with a date and nobody's name on it is a date and not a review, and I have written that down in my own book in my own hand, and I am saying it in your room so that it is on your minute book and not only mine."

Sela Vint looked at him for about four seconds.

"That is the best thing anybody has said in this room in a year," she said, "and it is an objection, and I hear objections and enter them and decide, and you have just lost."

"Enter it."

"It is entered." She turned the minute book round. "The applicant objects that the escalation places the only question in this referral in a list for a year, giving it a date and no name, and states that the ruling of this court today is on the wording and not on the instruction, and that this is correct and he does not contest it and objects to the consequence."

"Then what is the consequence, in a sentence, since you are in a hurry."

"That in about a year this room will hear a full sitting about a document with no person on it, and the record of it will be very long, and whoever opens the file will read my first three orders out at the start of it, and about four hundred and forty people will be in four offices and one trade doing exactly what they did last year." Sela Vint put the pen down. "And I am escalating it anyway, because a thing that is not on a list is in a drawer, and a drawer in this building is where things go in the ninth month of a year and are not opened again. You have spent eight months learning that a review date with nobody under it is not a review. I have sat in this chair four years learning that a thing nobody has complained of is not held. They are the same fact and it is in the record and I will not pretend to have won anything."

---

They were out of the room at about the seventh hour, into a stair that smells of fired scale and hot stone and has done since anybody can remember, and Tamsin Rook was standing on it with a copy of the minute in her hand and the seal of the court on the corner and her name entered in the minute book as attending for the applicant, having no office, which is how a court in a river province had entered her in a terrace in the fourth month of this year and will enter her in nine years' time or will not.

She did not say *well done*. She said, "There is no name in it."

"No."

"Eight months, and there is no name in it, and a high court has just written in its own hand that it does not know and cannot compel, and it is signed, and that is the only way anybody in this empire has ever been made to answer for not knowing." Tamsin folded the copy once. "And you argued it badly."

"I argued it badly."

"You argued the case for four hours when there was no case. You put four questions and asked for a fifth and you did not once say *a person*." She looked at him. "I have watched you do that every month since the seventh of the ninth. And today the registrar did your work in a minute, out of a document, and it is better than anything you have put in a room in eight months, and I am not going to pretend to you that I liked watching it."

Marek Kest stood on the stair of the Crown Kiln for about as long as it takes to find a thing you have put down.

"I lost the escalation."

"You lost it, and the court told you why, in a room, with a name, which is the first time anybody in this matter has done that against your objection." Tamsin put the copy under her arm. "Five offices. Four hundred miles apart. All of them right. One of them able to do it again tomorrow, and nobody in it."

"That is not what I asked for."

"No. It is worse than what you asked for, and it is the only one there is, and it is a thing you can work on for nine years, and I have watched you be given a worse answer four times in eight months and do the same thing with it every time, and I am going to keep telling you about it, because I have run out of other ways of doing it."

The stair of the Crown Kiln goes down into Auremar and it is nine hundred and some steps and there is a door at the bottom of it onto a quay where the packet boats come in, and at about the sixth hour of the seventeenth of the sixth month a man in a good coat came up the quay past that door carrying a leather case and went into the Crown Kiln and asked, at the first counter inside, whether the minute of the Cinder Court for the seventeenth was on the ordinary distribution, and how many copies, and on what day, and was told the whole of it, in about nine minutes, by a clerk with a heavy file.

And on a wall at Mosswake, four hundred and thirty miles down the river, a clerk with a board and a nail was waiting for it, and she had a line in her own hand already ruled off for it, and she had ruled it off on the fourth of the sixth month before anybody had thought of asking her to.
