# Chapter 248: A Man Tells His Wife, Which Is The One Thing Nobody In This Matter Does

He is about thirty-four and he has a trade and a house with a door on it about four miles from a bank on a river, and his name is in a fair hand in a place he has never been, because his name went onto a list of works in the second month of next year in a hand that is not his and has been going onto a list four times a season ever since.

His wife is thirty-one and she keeps the house and has done since before the house had a door that shut, and there are three of them at the table and the eldest is nine.

**He came back from the bay on the second day of the first week of the ninth month of the year after next with a number written on the back of his own left hand in pencil, and it was four, and by the fourth day of the fourth week of the tenth month it was not there any more**, and there is no copy of it anywhere and there never was.

---

The house was four miles from a bank on a river and about four miles from a rented room in another city, and he had walked both of those distances in the six weeks since the first week of the ninth month of the year after next, and neither walk had been about work.

He had told nobody at the place he worked. He had not told the two men he had asked about the hold, and he had not told the man of about fifty-five with the chain, and he had not told the foreman, and there were about four people in this matter he had told nothing at all, and one of them was a woman of twenty-four at a counter about nine miles from where he lived, who had said the price out loud twice in one afternoon to a man who did not understand her the first time.

**And the price she said was the price of being a party, and he is not in this**, and she is four hundred and thirty miles from where he lives and is not going to be sent and nobody is going to ask her, and he has thought about that for about six weeks and he has not worked out whether it is a comfort or a theft.

---

The thing that happened in that kitchen was that he told her.

He had worked out for about six weeks that he was not going to. He had a reason and it was a good one and it is the reason a person gives in this empire every day of a working life, which is that a person who knows a thing can be served with a paper about it, and being served is what makes a person a party, and a party is the only person who can be told what a thing is going to be used for.

He had that ready on the fourth day of the fourth week of the tenth month of the year after next, at about the sixth hour, with the eldest asleep on the floor and the other two eating, and he had it ready in the sense that he had said it out loud to a door twice and to a wall once and had got further with the wall.

The argument against telling her had four parts and he had been through all four of them about a hundred times each. A person who knows a thing can be served with a paper about it. Being served is what makes a person a party. A party is the only person who can be told what a thing is going to be used for. And a person who can be told what a thing is going to be used for cannot take it back, and he had not been able to get past the fourth part, and he had never once been able to get past it, and the reason he could not get past it is that it applies to him as well and he has been living inside the fourth part for about a year without noticing.

"Take your plate through," she said.

"After."

---

"About six weeks ago you came in here and you did not eat for about a quarter of an hour and then you asked me whether I had ever had a person be angry with me for asking a question."

"You said no."

"I said no because nobody ever has." She put her hands flat on the table. "And you have been different since, and I have not asked you about it because I have never once in nine years asked you a question about your work and I did not think I was going to start, and so whatever this is, you are bringing it to me."

"Then I am bringing it to you."

---

He told her the four of them, and he told her where he had got them, which was a man of about fifty-five with a chain in a yard, and he told her that a man of about fifty-five with a chain had held four things in his head for six weeks and had not had any of them written down anywhere, on purpose, and had said out loud that he knew what he was doing.

"Four in about a hundred and forty years," he said. "**And I have had the number on my own hand for six weeks and it is gone** and nobody will ever know I had it, and that is not a sad thing, that is the way to keep one."

"Why are you telling me?"

"Because I am going to do the fifth one."

---

She did not say anything for a while, and the youngest one asked whether he could have the crust, and she gave him the crust, and then she said the thing.

"You have come about four miles twice and a third time to a bay to find out how to ask a question about a word on a form, and you are going to do it, and nobody sent you."

"Nobody sent me. I have worked out that I am the only one anybody could send, and that is exactly why nobody is going to, including me."

"Do you want to be sent?"

"I want to be asked."

---

There it was, and he had not expected it and he sat with it for a minute.

"Then say it to me properly," she said, "and not as a joke at a table, because I have known you nine years and you have never once asked me a question about anything that mattered and I have never once had anybody ask me a question about anything at all, and that is not a complaint about you, it is a complaint about about nine hundred and forty people, and I have never met one of them and I am one."

"You are not on a list."

"I know I am not on a list. I have been not on a list for about nine years and I have never known what that was worth and now I know and I have been told it by a man in a kitchen." She looked at him. "**You are going to go and ask a foreman a question about a word on a form, out loud, in a room**, and there is no post in this empire whose job is to ask, and nobody is going to send you, and if it goes wrong the person it goes wrong for is her and not you, and you have worked that out, and I have not."

---

"I have worked out the other half of it too and I have not said it and I am going to say it now, and it is the reason I am telling you and not doing it quietly."

"Then say it."

"**Telling you has just made a second person in this house findable by a thing I know, and I did it on purpose, and I could not have done it without asking you first, and I did not ask you first**, and I am not going to pretend to you that I did it kindly."

"You could have asked."

"I could have asked, and if I had asked you would have said no, or you would have said yes and been frightened, and both of those would have been me arranging the room." He put his hands flat on the table the way people in this matter do and neither of them noticed. "There is no form anywhere in this empire for asking a person whether they will mind being told something, and about nine people in this city have made a thing out of a habit in their time and every one of them ended up with a heading over it, and I have decided that I am not going to be a person who asks his wife for permission to be found. **So I have done the same thing to you that a foreman of fifty-one did in a bay in the ninth month of the year after next, on purpose, without asking, and it cost her nothing to tell nine men** and it is going to cost you whatever it costs."

---

"You are not going to tell me what it costs."

"No. I have worked out about as much of it as I can and the rest of it is a thing that happens to a person afterwards and there is nothing to do about it and I am not going to be a man who tells his wife what is going to happen to her so that she feels better about it happening."

"That is the first sensible thing you have said since the second day of the first week of the ninth month."

"It is also the cruellest, and I have thought about it for six weeks and I am going with it."

---

Then she asked him the question, and it was the first one in nine years and it was a plain question and not about a word on a form.

"Are you frightened?"

"Yes."

"Of what?"

"I do not know," he said. "And that is not a brave thing to say and I have been not saying it for about six weeks. I have been frightened since the second day of the first week of the ninth month of the year after next and I have got two names for it and neither of them is right. One of them is that I am going to stand in a bay in front of about nine men and a foreman and open my mouth and find that there is nothing in it, because there is nothing in it, because a question about a word on a form is a question about a piece of paper and the paper is not in the room."

"And the other one?"

"The other one is that it will work," he said. "**That somebody will answer me, and that I will feel the answer in the room, and that I will want it again**, and that is the exact shape of the thing the man of about fifty-five with a chain has been left alone to avoid for nine years, and I have met him once and he is right and I am going to do it anyway."

---

She was quiet for a long time and the eldest one came back in and asked whether there was any more of the bread, and there was not, and she said there was not in a voice that ended the evening for everybody in that room.

"You are not going to be thanked for it," she said, "and I want to say that now, while it is cheap, and not in four years when one of us is old enough to want to be thanked."

"I know. A person who is paid to read is a person who can be told to stop, and a person who is thanked is a person somebody has decided to be grateful to, and being grateful to somebody is a form in this empire, and there is not one." He got up and put the plates in. "**Nobody in this matter has ever been thanked and there is not going to be a form for it now.**"

---

And the eldest one, who is nine, was awake the whole of it and understood none of it and has since told a person at school that his father was going to ask somebody something, and was not corrected, and it is the only account of this evening that exists anywhere in the world.

And then, because a kitchen is a room and a room gets a day, and because he was standing up with plates in his hands and she was sitting down and neither of them had said the half that mattered, he put the plates in the bucket and came back and said the other half.

"I am not going to tell you which word it is, and that is not a withholding, and I have been not saying it since the second day of the second week of the ninth month and I have said it to a wall about nine miles from a forge twice."

"Then why will you not say it here?"

"Because in about four years somebody is going to read that a man of about thirty-four asked a question about a word on a form, and they are going to want to know which word, and if it is in my wife's mouth then it is in two mouths. **two mouths is findable in a way one mouth is not, and I have spent six weeks working out that the only person in this house who is not findable is the one who does not know**, and I am not going to be the person who takes that away from her on a table."

---

"You have thought about this a great deal."

"I have thought about nothing else since the first week of the ninth month and I have got four feet of a story out of it and none of it is the thing." He sat down again, which he had not meant to do. "And here is the whole of it, and then I am going to bed, and I am saying it because you are the only person in this empire I could say it to and because you have already been told and there is no going back to the other side of the table."

"Go on."

"**Nobody is going to know that I did it.** Not a clerk and not a foreman and not a book-keeper, and nobody is going to write it down, and in about four years it will not be in any document anywhere."

"The only record of it will be that a foreman of fifty-one in a bay said something out loud that she had said in a shed in the fifth month of the year after next. That is all I am buying, and it is not a small thing and it is not a large one, and I have got no way of working out which."

---

**And a woman of twenty is about four hundred and thirty miles up that river with sixteen books** and a wage that has been held since the second week of the third month of the year after.

A foreman of fifty-one has said out loud in a bay that she is in a box and nobody has said one word about it since, and a clerk of about fifty-five in an office about nine miles off a building has entered a question in a minute book with the day on it and her own name at the foot of it. A man of about thirty-four with a wife and a trade and a house with a door on it has told his wife, on purpose, without asking, that he is going to do the fifth one, and nobody sent him. Nobody is going to, and there is no post anywhere in this empire whose job is to ask, and a mark in chalk on a wall about four hundred yards from a person who can be served means nothing and is nobody's and is not going to be wiped.
