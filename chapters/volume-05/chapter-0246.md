# Chapter 246: A Sheet Of Clean Paper Nobody In This Empire Has Ever Issued Him, And A Man Who Tears It Up

The room is rented by the week and there is a table in it and a chair and a bed against the wall he does not use, and about four feet of that wall is bare, and there is a nail in it about four feet up that came with the room and has nothing hanging on it.

Marek Kest is thirty-eight. No office, no fund, no register, nothing in this empire he could certify anything with, and a bar of his own that came off him on the twelfth of the ninth month of last year on an application of one line which he wrote himself. And a line about two inches long on the inside of his left wrist that reads nothing and does not know where the ford is.

**The road is not being walked.** It has not been walked once in this matter and nothing in this week is going to walk it, and a sheet went down it on the last day of the fourth week of the eighth month of the year after next and he did not go with it, and he is not going to.

---

The thing he was doing on the second day of the second week of the tenth month of the year after next was nothing, and he had been doing it for about nine weeks, and it had got to the point where a woman who brings him bread at about the sixth hour on four days in a week had started to leave it on the step and go, which is a thing she had not done in about a year.

He had not opened the door on any of them since the second day of the fourth week of the eighth month of the year after next.

He got up on the second day of that week at about the fourth hour, and put his hand flat on the door, and then he did not open it for about four minutes, and then he opened it and picked the bread up and did not say anything to the step, and went back in and shut it, and that is the whole of what happened at the door.

Then he sat down and did the other thing, which he had been working out for about nine weeks and had got to on the first day of the ninth month of the year after next and had not done.

**There is a sheet of clean paper in the drawer of that table**, and there is one sheet, and nobody in this empire has ever issued him anything of any kind at any time in his life.

---

He had been keeping it the way a person keeps a thing they are going to need.

That was the whole of the reasoning and it took him about four seconds to say it out loud to the four feet of wall, and the four seconds were the entire distance between the two candidates and he had been living in them for about nine weeks.

A sheet of clean paper is a thing a person writes on. A person with a sheet of clean paper in a drawer in a rented room in a city where a woman has come about nine miles twice and stood on a step and a man of about thirty-four has knocked on this door and not been let in is a person who is waiting to be asked something, and a person who is waiting to be asked something is a person with a document, and a document is the only thing anybody in this empire can be held to.

**About four years is how long anything about him can be found for**, and a clean sheet in a drawer is the only thing about him in this city that is a piece of paper, and I have been sitting on it for nine weeks waiting for a person to come and give me something to write down, and the reason I have been waiting is that I have got nothing else and I do not want to be the man who is waiting.

---

He said the rest of it out loud as well, because a room is the only kind of place in this empire where a thing like that can be said.

"There is no form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to keep a clean sheet, and there is not going to be one, and the sheet is the only thing in this room that is not a thing somebody else gave me and then went away from. That is what it is for. **A thing that leaves no mark can be carried a long way, and a sheet of paper that nobody has asked for cannot be carried anywhere at all, because there is nothing on it.**"

Then he tore it up.

He tore it into pieces, and then he tore two of those, and then he swept the lot off the table with the side of his hand and it went on the floor and he looked at it on the floor for a while and then he left it there, because picking it up would have been a thing he was doing with it.

Then he sat down again and found that his hands were shaking, which he had not expected, and it took him about four minutes to stop, and he has thought about it since. A man who decides a thing in about four seconds and then shakes for about four minutes has not decided it in about four seconds, and there is nobody he could say that to, and he is not going to be able to say it to anybody about four years from now either.

---

And then he said, out loud, to the four feet of wall and the nail with nothing on it, what the tearing up had actually been, and it is not a thing anybody would have printed.

"It is not tidiness. It is that about four years is how long a piece of paper about me can be found for, and there is one piece of paper in this city that is about me and does not say so, and it has been in a drawer for about nine weeks. I have been the only person in this empire who knows that, and in about four years somebody with a copy of the right page will be able to stand in a room and say that a man who cannot be found was keeping paper."

"And what does that get them?"

"Nothing," said Marek Kest. "**That is the part I have been not looking at for nine weeks, and it is the whole of it.** It gets them nothing, and it gets me nothing, and it is a piece of paper in a drawer that could have been nothing, and I have thrown away a thing that could have been nothing, and that is the whole of the decision and I made it in about four seconds and I have not improved it since and I am not going to."

---

There was a knock at about the seventh hour and he did not open it, and it was a man of about thirty-four who had come about four miles once before and had been on that landing once and had not been let in.

"You have heard the answer."

"I have heard about the answer," said the man of about thirty-four, "from a woman of about twenty-four who said the price out loud in a counter and then said it out loud again because I did not understand the first one, and neither of us sent you anything, and I have not said any of it to anybody since and I want that said before I ask."

"Ask it."

"What did the choice cost?"

---

Marek Kest stood in about two inches of door and worked out, in about four seconds, that the answer to that was the one thing he had promised himself in the fourth week of the eighth month of the year after next he would not say out loud twice, and that the promise had been made to nobody.

Then he told it, and he told it in a flat voice, standing in a doorway, to a man on a landing who could not see him.

"A man who is a labour is a man somebody can pay for a day, and a man somebody can pay for a day is a man somebody can tell to stop, and I have not told you what I did about that and I am not going to now. **There is no fund and there is no form anywhere in this empire for a fund**, and I have got about forty-four pounds a year and a road of about four hundred and thirty miles and the day comes out of my own money and not out of anybody else's."

"That is the cost of the choice?"

"That is the price of the day," said Marek Kest. "The cost of the choice is a different sentence and I have said it once and I am not going to say it twice, and you did not hear it and I am not going to pretend to you that you did. **I have taken the shape of the thing that can be refused** and I have removed the only person in this empire who could have refused me, on purpose, and out of my own hand, and that is a worse position than the other one, and there is a woman of twenty who worked that out before I did and told me so in a room in the third week of the fourth month of the year after next."

---

The man of about thirty-four was quiet for a minute.

"Then what is the point of having chosen it?"

"There is no point. That is what a cost is." He heard the man shift on the landing. "A thing that costs nothing has not been chosen, it has been described. I have got nine weeks of daylight and about four hundred and thirty miles of river in front of me and a person at the far end of it who does not know what it is and has written four lines to me about it. I have posted him a day back and the day has gone by and he has not written again, and I am not going to be able to tell you whether he is angry or whether he is a person who was never angry, and there is no form in this empire for asking a man why he did not write again."

---

"Will you go?"

"No." He said it without any weight, the way a man says a thing about the weather, and it was the answer he had given himself on the last day of the fourth week of the eighth month of the year after next and had not gone back on since. "**And I am not going to say why in a doorway twice either, so that you do not go up that road and tell somebody I have got a reason for staying.** I do not have a reason for staying. I have got about four feet of wall and a nail with nothing on it and a decision I made in about four seconds, and a man who says a reason for staying out loud is a man who is about to be asked to justify it."

---

He shut the door and stood in the room with a floor and nine pieces of paper on it.

And then he said one more thing out loud, to the floor, and it is the reason he opened the door to the bread and it is about a person about four hundred and thirty miles down this river and it took him nine weeks to get to and it took him about four seconds to say.

"**A person who is paid to read is a person who can be told to stop.** I have known that since a man of thirty-eight in a rented room nine miles off a counter worked it out in about nine minutes and stood at a window and looked at a river, and I have never been able to get past it. A foreman of fifty-one on a bank has worked it out standing in that bay since she was forty-two and has never been asked about it, and the two of them have never met and there is no instrument in this empire that would put them in a room, and a man who is a labour is a man somebody can pay for a day, and I have chosen to be that."

"And what do you do with knowing it?"

"Nothing." He said it to the floor and it was not a shrug. "**There is nothing to do with it.** That is what it is for. A man who is not paid and cannot be instructed and cannot be stopped and cannot be thanked is the most dangerous person in this matter, and every person who has met me has worked that out in about four minutes, and the man of about thirty-four has just worked it out on a landing in about four minutes. I have got a floor with paper on it and no fund and a bare wall, and I am not going to be able to help anybody and I have known that since the third week of the fourth month of the year after next."

---

And then, because the lamp was not lit and the room was very quiet and because a man who has torn up a sheet of paper about nine minutes earlier is in a condition in which he says true things, he said one that he has not said to anybody.

"There is a woman about four hundred and thirty miles down this river who has decided to go and tell four women that she is not paid, and she started on the fourth week of the ninth month of the year after next, and there is a foreman in a furnace town four hundred and thirty miles the other way who has said one true thing about himself out loud five times and is holding a piece of paper in a coat. There is a clerk of thirty-four at a counter nine miles from where I am sitting who has about four hundred bought and unfilled blanks in a box under her counter and has said out loud that a blank is the only piece of paper in this empire that cannot be held against anybody."

"I have said four sentences out loud in nine weeks and three of them have been about other people."

"Yes," said Marek Kest, "and that is the only arrangement in this matter that anybody has ever got right, and I have got nine weeks of nothing and a nail with nothing on it, and I am not going to be able to improve on it. The bread woman is going to be back at about the sixth hour tomorrow and I am going to open that door, and it is not going to be a decision about anything and it is going to be the only thing I do this week."

---

**And about four feet of bare wall is not a plan, and there is a nail in it about four feet up that came with the room and has nothing hanging on it**, and he has looked at that nail every morning for about a year and has not worked out what it is for. He is not going to work it out this month, and the lamp was not lit.

A sheet of paper with a day in it and no name at either end of it is on a bench about four hundred and thirty miles away, with two other pieces of paper that have been on that bench longer. Nobody has answered it, and nobody was sent, and the road is not being walked.

Four askings in about a hundred and forty years, and a fifth one is going to be asked in about five weeks by a man who has come about four miles twice and has told his wife in a kitchen.
