# Chapter 210: A Bay, And No Post Whose Job It Is

The second season came into its last weeks on the third day of the last week of the eleventh month of the year after, and the water was at the third-of-the-way mark, and about four hundred and forty foot of bank stood behind it with about nine men on it who had come because a word had been put in the yard and not because anybody had asked them.

Halla Wray is fifty-one. She is the foreman of the Slade Cut, which is an office with no office in it. She has been on that bank since she was nine. She can read and nobody at the district works has ever once in nine years asked her to read anything, and in the fourth week of the ninth month of this year she read six numbered lines out loud to nine men in that shed because she had decided to and nobody asked her, and it is the first time in her life that anybody in that shed has heard her read anything.

There is one sheet of her own hand in a book of her own, with the day against it, and it is the words of a document and not the document. It has no name in it, she put it there on purpose, she has read it since and has not added her name and is not going to. The notice it was copied from is on the bench, and has been since the seventh month, and the two of them are two sheets and not one.

---

She did the ordinary things, because a foreman does the ordinary things and a foreman who stops doing them is a foreman who has begun to make a speech of her own job.

The count of the works, which takes about four minutes. The water. The hold, which is against a company whose whole money is four shillings a year and all of it the rent of a building, and which has not moved since the fourth week of the fourth month and is not going to move because a foreman is fifty-one and has a boy at home. The factor's letter on a bench up the road, four lines, wanting a person who may be served and that person's consent, and it is not going to be answered, and every day it is not answered is a day nobody has been entered.

Then she read out the nineteen reasons, in a bay, in about four minutes, and not the names, and the second reading was slower.

"They said it in the seventh month and they have said nothing since, and that is correct, and nobody is going to ask them again until the season comes round. **A person is entitled to make a refusal once and have it kept and not be troubled about it again, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that entitles anybody to be troubled twice.**"

---

Then she came to the four, and she came to them in the open, standing, with the men looking at her, none of them understanding that this is the fourth time in a year she has said it and the first time she has said it in a room with nobody to answer her.

"About four people on this cut cannot read a paragraph. They have never been asked once. Not in the first season, not on the fourth day of the seventh month, not in the second week of the tenth month, and not today, and there is no column for them and there is not going to be one, and the reason there is not going to be one is that a column for them would be a column somebody would have to fill.

"A book-keeper on this bank worked out the one way of reaching them in the second week of the tenth month and wrote down that he was not going to do it, with the day on it, and read it back twice, and he did it because a person who is paid to read is a person who can be told to stop. **That is correct and it is the whole of the argument and I am not going to improve on it and I am not going to say it as though I thought of it.**

"And I am going to say the other half of it, and the other half is mine, and I have been carrying it since the third week of the seventh month and I use it about four times a season.

"**I have been the reason I could not be found for forty-two years. Forty-two years on this bank and nine years holding the only job on it that is written down nowhere, and a child of nine did not choose any of it, and a foreman of fifty-one is in a box because of it.** And I would like to be found. I said that in this shed in the fourth week of the ninth month and some of you heard it and not one of you wrote it down, and that was correct, and there was no paper and nobody thought to fetch any.

"So we have a woman on this bank who wants to be found, and four people on this bank who have never been asked anything, and one of those four is about the same age as the years I have been unfindable, and that is not a coincidence and I have not got a speech for it and I am not going to make one."

---

Then she asked the question, and it is the question this whole of it turns on, and she asked it out loud, in a room, standing up, in front of them, and she said in advance that nobody was going to answer it and that she was asking anyway.

"Who asks.

"That is the whole of the question and it has been in me since the ninth month and I have not said it in a shed because a shed is not a room that wants a record and I have spent nine years in rooms that do not want anything.

"So here it is in a room. A woman of twenty in a furnace town got put in a box in the fifth month and I got put in a box in the seventh month, and neither of us was asked, and there is no form in this empire for asking either of us, and the only instrument anybody has ever found for saying no to a box is a refusal to be served, and it did not work on the woman of twenty and it cannot work on me at all, because I was not served anything, I was entered by a class, and a class is not served on anybody, and a person who has not been served cannot refuse to be served. That is the fifth time the protection and the catch have been the same act and I am not going to list them and I am not going to count them again, and nobody chose it, and the reason nobody chose it is that the fifth time it happened it happened in this shed to me, and the four people it is about are not in this shed and have never been in a room together either.

"Now. There is no post in this empire whose job is to ask. I have been told by two people in the last six weeks that it has now been done three times about forms, and that two of the three were accidents and nobody chose either of them. One was a room that wanted a record. One was a man who was frightened about his own household. And one was a woman of twenty who walked about four miles and stood at a desk and asked how the box got filled, and that one was on purpose, and nobody sent her, and I have not been told who she asked.

"So three, and no post, and no plan, and I have asked nine men in a shed in the last two minutes and not one of them has said a word, and I did not expect one and I want it entered that I said in advance that I did not expect one, because a woman who asks a question in a room and then says afterwards that she expected nothing is a woman who has been in a room before.

"And here is what I have got, and it is not a speech and it is not in a book. Somebody asks, or nobody asks. And I do not know which, and neither do the three of them, and the only thing I have found out this year is that it happens to people who were not going to do it. It happened to a room that wanted a record. It happened to a man who was afraid about his own household. It happened to a woman who was twenty and had come four miles.

"**There is no post. And that is the whole of the answer, and I have been waiting since the first week of the seventh month for somebody to stand in this shed and tell me what happens instead, and nobody is going to, and I am not going to ask them twice.**"

---

The shed stayed quiet for about a minute and a half and then a man of about thirty from the second gang said, "So you want one of us to do it."

"No," said Halla Wray. "I do not want one of you to do it and if one of you offers I am going to refuse you, and I am telling you that now, in a room, in advance, so that none of you is a hero about it in about four years.

"Here is my reason and it is the reason I have had since the fourth week of the ninth month and it is not a good reason and I know it is not a good reason and I am saying it anyway.

"The moment one of you asks those four people, they are found. Not by the district and not by a seat. Found. By whoever comes asking next, and there is always somebody who comes asking next, and I have watched a woman in a furnace town get found by a box and a day and a hand in about nine seconds by a person who was not a villain.

"And I have been the one that could not be found, for forty-two years, and I have said in this shed that I would like to be found, and I have meant it, and I am telling you both of those things in the same breath and the second one is the one that decides it.

"So I am not going to be the reason four other people are found. **If the four stay unasked this season then that is a decision and it is mine and I have made it and I am not going to be talked out of it by a man in a shed who thinks he has found something to do.**"

She put the book with her sheet in it on the bench beside the notice and left it there, and it stayed there all night, and in the morning the shed was open and about nine men came in and looked at it and none of them asked about it and none of them will.

---

He came in at about the sixth hour on the second day, in the middle of it, the way a person comes in who has a reason to come in and is not going to be put off by nine of them.

He is about fifty-five and he has been on that cut for about thirty-three years, which is nine years less than she has, and he came the year after her and has never once said so, and he is one of the four.

Halla Wray knew which one of the four he was before he was through the door, and she knew it in the ordinary way a foreman knows the face of a man on her own bank, and there is no form anywhere in this empire in which a foreman's memory of a face is a record, and there never has been, and she has relied on that for nine years.

"Can you look at my drum," he said. "The chain has gone stiff and it wants oiling and I have brought it up here because the smith is at Lowcross and Lowcross is a bridge and the bridge is shut and I am not going over it for a chain."

"The chain wants the third link out and a drop of something in the bush," said Halla Wray. "That is the whole of it and you could do it yourself with a nail."

"I have not got a nail."

---

That was the whole of it. It took about two minutes and he stood at the bench with the drum on it and she told him about the third link and about the oil, and every man in that shed watched a foreman do a perfectly ordinary thing to a man who cannot read a paragraph.

And she did not ask him.

Not about the hold. Not about the season. Not about the nineteen reasons or the two sheets of paper with a seal and nothing under the seal lying on the bench where anybody who came in could read them. She had asked the room in the last two minutes whether they knew who fills in a box, and she had said in advance that nobody was going to answer, and she had meant it as a kind of honesty, and a man of fifty-five walked in about two minutes later who is one of the four people it is about and she did not ask him one word.

She thought about it for the rest of that week and she has not got a better account of it than that and she has not told it to anybody.

He would have said yes. That is the part she cannot get round. He would have said yes, in a shed, out loud, in about two seconds, and then he would have been a man who had been asked, and a man who has been asked can be asked again by whoever comes next, and she would have started it. And that is not a reason, and she knows it is not a reason, and she gave it out loud in a shed in the second week of the eleventh month to a room as though it were.

"It is a bad reason," she said. "**I have said in this shed that I would rather be asked than not, and the district has put the bank to me five times and I have said no five times, and about the four in that bay I have not been asked once, and the reason is that nobody has thought of it, and I have thought of it, and I have not asked, and that is the whole of what I have done in a year of standing in rooms. I would like somebody to put that on a slate and read it back to me, and there is nobody here who is going to, and I am not going to ask one of the four men in this shed to do it either, for the reason I have just given, and I know exactly how that sounds.**"

---

Then the water went down about a foot before the end of it and the second season came to its end with about nineteen people still behind that bank, and about four of them in that bay never having been asked anything, and a guarantee under the fire-and-water device standing offered and unanswered with about sixty children under sixteen inside it, and a bill at Lowcross unpaid and nobody liable and nobody paying it in a shed either, and a factor's letter on a bench wanting a person who may be served.

Nobody sent anybody anywhere. The lamp went on at about the seventh hour, which is later than it ought to be, and the door of the shed was open, and **there is no post in this empire whose job is to ask, and three accidents are not a plan, and the woman who has the most reason in the world to be asked about a thing has still not been asked, and a foreman of fifty-one has decided, out loud, in a room, in front of about nine men, that she is not going to be the reason four other people are found, and that is the end of the season and it is not the end of anything else.**
