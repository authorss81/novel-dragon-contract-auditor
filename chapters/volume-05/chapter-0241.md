# Chapter 241: Four Of Them, And The Only Place In This Empire They Can Be Held

He is about fifty-five and he has been on the Slade Cut nine years and there is a chain on him that came off a gate, and nobody on that bank has ever said his name out loud in a room except a clerk of fifty-five in an office about nine miles off a building. He cannot read a paragraph. He has never pretended otherwise and nobody has ever asked him to.

He had been asked one question in about four months and it had been a fair one, and it had been asked by a book-keeper who came out of a store about four hundred yards up the road and read a piece of his own paper to him in a yard, and the man with the chain had put a thumb through the third link of it afterwards and thought about it for most of a day and told nobody what he thought.

Then on the second day of the first week of the ninth month of the year after next a man of about thirty-four came about four miles and stood at the second end of the bay and asked him whether anybody had ever asked a person out loud what a word on a form meant.

---

"It is a plain question and it is not about a word on a form," the man said, before he had said the rest of it, "and I have been practising that sentence for about four miles because I have never said it before and I did not want to get it wrong."

"Then ask it."

"I have asked it." He was holding his cap in both hands the way a man holds a thing he has not decided to put down. "And I have been told four, and I want to hear them, and not from a foreman and not from a book, because a foreman will tell them the way a foreman tells a bay and a book will tell them the way a book tells a clerk."

"Why not a book?"

"Because a book is a thing that can be found," said the man of about thirty-four, "and I am a person who can be found, and I have got a wife and a trade and a house with a door on it, and I am not going to be a man who has read four things out of a book and cannot say where he read them."

---

The man with the chain put the thing he had been mending down on the bench and did not pick it up again.

"Four," he said. "And I am the fourth person you are talking to and I am about the fifth one that has said that number out loud this year, and I want to say the other half of it before I say any of them, and it is not a warning."

"Say it."

**"You cannot write them down and you cannot have them written down, and if anybody writes them down then the first thing that goes on the paper is a heading**, and a heading over four things turns four things into a practice." He took his thumb out of the third link. "A practice is the one thing in this empire that grows a column over it on its own without anybody deciding anything, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that says a person may not make one. I have watched about nine people do it and every one of them ended up with a heading and then a person to put under it."

"So I hold them."

"You hold them. That is what I am telling you and it is why I am the one telling you and it is not a favour." He looked at the man of about thirty-four properly for the first time. "**I am the only person on this bank that findability does not work on**, and I have known that for about four years, and I have never once used it, and this is the first time and I want it said out loud that I know what I am doing."

---

Then he said them, and he said them the way a man says a thing he has heard rather than a thing he has read, which is to say that he got the order wrong on purpose and said so.

"I am going to start at the end and work back, because I do not know the order and I am not going to pretend I do. You will have the order from somebody else and you can put it right and that is a different job from this one."

---

"The fourth," said the man with the chain, "was a woman of twenty, and she came about nine miles in one day, and she asked a man with no office what the word *engaged* means, in a room he had hired by the week, in the first week of the first month of the year after next. She was answered with a price and a refusal. That is on about nine people's mouths and it is in nine people's hands and I have never met her and I am not going to."

---

"The third, and this is the one that matters to you and it is the reason you have come four miles, and it is in the second week of the eleventh month, and it was a desk and a woman of twenty again, and she came about four miles and nobody sent her."

"How do you know nobody sent her?"

"Because a foreman of fifty-one has said out loud in that bay that nobody sent her, twice, and a foreman does not say a thing twice about a thing that is not true." He shifted the chain. "And it was the first one anybody did on purpose. The first two were accidents, and one of them was a room that wanted a record, and the other one was a man who was frightened about his own household. **The third was a person deciding to, and there is nobody in this empire whose job it is to decide to**, and that is the whole of what has ever happened about this."

---

"The second was a clerk's window in the first week of the tenth month of the year after, and it was a man, and he was frightened about his own household. That is the one I would think about if I were you, and I am not going to tell you why because I do not know why."

"You do not know why?"

"I know that he was frightened. I do not know what frightened him and I have never met him and he is not on this bank." The man with the chain shrugged. "A person who asks a question about a word on a form is frightened of something, and it is almost never the form. **Whoever is on that list of four next is going to be frightened of something too**, and you have not worked out what you are frightened of yet, and that is the work and it has not been done."

---

"And the first is a long way back."

"The first is a court room in the fifth month of the year after and it wanted a record, and I have never been told it by anybody who was in it and I am not going to invent it." He stopped. "That is a thing I have got wrong before about other things and I have got no age for it and neither has anybody else, and a man who puts an age on a thing he does not know the age of is making a figure."

---

The man of about thirty-four stood at the second end of the bay with his cap in his hands and did not say anything for about a minute, and the bay went on being a bay, and about nine men were in it and not one of them had heard a word of any of it.

"Is that four?" he said.

"That is four and I have not added anything to them and I have not left anything out, and I am not going to say it a second time because I do not know how many of them I would get right the second time and a man who gets a thing right once and then gets it wrong is two things in nine mouths." The man with the chain picked his thing up off the bench. "You will want to know the number, and I will give you the number, because you are going to be asked it. **Four in about a hundred and forty years, and there is no post anywhere in this empire whose job is to ask**, and the third of the four was the first one anybody did on purpose."

---

"Can I write that down?"

"You can write that down and you should, because it is a number and numbers want paper, and that is exactly the right place for it." He waited for the man to get a pencil out and then he said the other half, and he said it slowly, because it is the part of it he has thought about longest. "**What you cannot write down is the four, and I am not being careful, I am being exact**, and the reason is that a list of four things that have happened in a hundred and forty years is a list, and a list has a heading, and the heading is either *things that have happened* or *things somebody should have done*, and about nine people in this city could write either one and both of them would be true and neither of them would be any use to you."

"Write the number. Keep the four in your head. And in about four years, when somebody with a copy of the right page stands in a room and asks you where you got four, you will have to stand there and say that a man of fifty-five with a chain told you in a yard, and that will be worth nothing to you, and that is what it is worth to everybody who has ever been told anything in a yard, and it is the only kind of being told there is that does not cost the teller."

---

The man of about thirty-four wrote the number on the back of his own hand with a pencil that was nearly blunt, and then he stopped and looked at what he had done.

"That is on me," he said. "That is going to be on me for about a week and then it will be gone and there is nobody who can look at me and say what it says."

"Yes."

"That is the reason I came."

"That is the reason anybody comes," said the man with the chain. "**A mark on your own hand cannot be carried off you by anybody, and it cannot be read by anybody, and it cannot be produced, and in about four years it will be the only thing about this that is still anywhere.**"

---

Then a man of about thirty-four asked him whether it costs anything, which is the third time in about four months that anybody has asked a man who cannot read a paragraph whether it costs anything, and the man with the chain gave the same answer he gave the last time, in the same number of words, because he had it ready and because he was not going to improve it.

"It costs about four seconds," he said. "Somebody has to hold forty-one names while a foreman reads them out, and this time somebody had to hold four other things while I said them, and he was me, and four seconds of a week is a thousandth of a day's pay and I have not been paid for one of them and I am not going to be."

---

The man of about thirty-four wrote the four on the inside of his own hat, and that is the only place any of them has ever been.

He wrote them in the order the man with the chain gave them, which was the wrong order and which he was warned about and which he did it anyway, and when he had finished he read them back once and did not read them back a second time. The man with the chain asked him why not, and he said because a man who has only had it in his head for twenty minutes is not going to get it right a second time either and there is no point in finding out twice.

"That is the first thing anybody in this matter has ever got wrong on purpose," said the man with the chain, "and it is wrong on purpose, and I am not going to correct you."

---

"You have not asked me the second question yet."

"I have not asked you any question, you have asked me three."

"You asked me whether it costs anything, which was the second question, and you did it in a bay, and a question about money asked in a bay is a question about money." He turned the link over once. "The third one is whether I would do it again, and the answer is yes, and I am not going to ask it because the answer is mine and it is not for you, and I have had about four years to work out that those are two different things and a man who cannot read a paragraph is the only one in this empire who gets them for free."

---

"You have not been asked anything in nine years and I have just asked you three things."

"You have asked me one thing and I have asked you two, and we are both of us standing in a bay, and if anybody asks me at the end of this week I will not remember this." He said it without any weight at all, the way a man says a thing about the weather. "**And I am telling you that the reason nobody asks me anything is the best thing that has ever happened to me, and I said that to a book-keeper in a yard in the third week of the sixth month of the year after next**, and it is not about a form and it is not about a girl of seventeen and I am not going to say it twice in a year."

---

The bay went on. A man of about twenty-eight wanted to know whether a person could be on two lists at once, and a man of about fifty wanted to know whether the hold would be on the list before the season shut, and a foreman of fifty-one told both of them what she tells everybody, in about four seconds, out loud, without turning round, from behind the bench, and about nine men watched a foreman do a perfectly ordinary thing and not one of them noticed anything at all.

And at the back of that bay there is a girl of seventeen on a written engagement at the rate the list is set at, and she was in it, and nobody spoke to her about the season, and she was not thanked and she is not going to start, and nobody sent anybody to her and nobody is going to.

**And a man of about thirty-four went back down about four hundred yards and up a road he has walked twice, with a number on the back of his own hand and four things in his head that are in no document in this empire**, and he is the fifth person anybody has told and he is going to be a person who has asked. There is no form anywhere in this empire that will pay him for the standing of it, and nobody is going to thank him for asking, and the four who cannot read a paragraph in that bay have still never been asked one question.
