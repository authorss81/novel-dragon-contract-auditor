# Chapter 236: The Rule Used Once, And An Answer Given In Words

The counter is the fourth at the Notaries' Table in Auremar and it is a rented room with a counter in it and four bays, and there are four bays because a paper says there are four bays, and the paper is not the reason and she has said so out loud in about nine minutes and is not going to say it again this year.

Tamsin Rook is twenty-four. She has been a clerk for two years. She keeps the copies and advises nobody and asks nothing, and she has been findable on purpose for about two years, and she is a person who is safe because nobody can instruct her and useful because somebody needs her standing in a room, and she has two boxes filled in on a page ruled by hand in a book of her own, and both of the boxes have the same name in them.

He was in the room at about the fifth hour of the afternoon on the fourth day of the second week of the seventh month of the year after next and he had a sheet of paper in his hand and he had it folded in three, which is a thing a man does when he has written something and has not decided to have written it.

---

"Put it on the counter," she said, "and then go and stand by the window, and do not read it while I am reading it, because you will look at my face and I have spent two years being worth looking at in a room and I am not going to spend it on a piece of paper."

He put it on the counter and went and stood by the window.

She read it, and then she read it again, and the second reading was slower, and it is his rule and it is hers and neither of them ever decided it.

> *Fourth day of the fourth week of the eighth month of the year after next. On that day I am going to fill the twelfth line in my own book and I am going to read it back twice.
> A day after that I am going to answer a letter of four lines that came to my room on the fourth day of the first week of the sixth month of the year after next, and I am going to do both of those things without being paid for either of them, and there is nobody in this matter who has to agree to it and I have not asked anybody.
> I have not got a fund. I have got about forty-four pounds a year and a road of about four hundred and thirty miles and there is no form anywhere in this empire for a fund.
> The rule of the twelfth month of last year, used once, and this is the use.*

---

She put it down and she did not pick it up again for about a minute and a half, and then she said the whole of her answer, standing, which is how she gives things, and it was about twenty words and then about nine minutes.

"**The second half, no, and not one word of it in writing at all.**" She waited about four seconds. "The first half, yes, on the day you have written."

---

"That is an answer and it is not an answer."

"It is two answers and you have written one question and I have answered half of it, and that is the entire point of the rule and you made the rule and I have watched every person in this matter who has made one do it badly and you are the only one of them who made one that could do that."

"Go on."

"A fact and a decision are not the same thing and you have put them in the same letter." She put her hand flat on the sheet. "The twelfth line is a fact. It says what you are and once it is in that book it can only ever be repeated, and you have known that since the third week of the third month of the year after next, and you wrote it down then. Answering a letter is a decision. A decision is a thing about what you are going to do and it can be taken back and a fact cannot. If you do both of them in about two days then in about four years a person with a copy of the right page can put the two lines together and say that a man who wrote a fact about himself on one day is a man who decided on another. That is not a true thing and I am not going to let it be in a room I have been standing in."

---

"So what am I to do with the second half?"

"Leave it. Write nothing. It is in your own hand and there is no copy of it anywhere and I have not taken a copy and I am not going to." She picked the sheet up and gave it back to him and did not keep her hand on it. "You answer the letter on a day that is not a day you have written about yourself on. And I am not going to tell you which day, and that is not a withholding. **A person who is told which day is a person who has had the day taken off him, and I have watched that happen to a foreman of fifty-one about four hundred and thirty miles down this river and she is the only person in this matter who has had a day taken off her and she did it to herself and she has said out loud that it was the wrong thing to do."**

"Her name is not in this matter and she is not a clerk and you have never met her."

"I have not met her and I know a clerk of thirty-eight four hundred and thirty miles down this river who says the same sentence about a man of about thirty-four, and that is twice, and I am not making it three." She put the pen down. "That is the price of it. I have said it out loud and there is nobody in this room who is going to thank me for it. I would like it written down that I did not expect to be thanked once. I have now said the price of this out loud in about four rooms this year and a handful of people have heard it, and not one of them has told anybody else. The reason that is a good number is that it is a number of people and not a number of rooms."

---

Then the man of about thirty came to the counter, **and he had four words in his head and fourpence in his hand**, and she knew the fourpence because it was a coin she had watched about four people not spend for about a year, and he put it on the counter and did not ask his question.

"Buy a copy and ask me a question about it and it is fourpence," she said, "and a question of mine on my own is a shilling and I do not do those either, and I have not done a question of my own in this matter in about a year."

"I am not going to spend it."

"Then what are you here for?"

"I want to know one thing and it is not about a word on a form." He did not put the coin down. "**Does a thing have to happen in a building to count?"**

"No."

"That is what I thought," said the man of about thirty, "and I have had four words in my head for most of a week and one of them is your name and I have not said it and I am not going to say it, and I have come about four bays to be told no by a person and I have been told no and I am going." He picked the coin up. "My name is not going to be in anything and I have thought about that for nine days and I have got a wife."

"Then it is done correctly and it is the ordinary way this works and I am not going to improve it," said Tamsin Rook, "and I am not going to charge you for the no."

---

When he had gone she said the other thing, and she said it to the four bays and not to the man at the table.

"You have not asked me once this year whether I trust your judgment and you are not going to, and I want to say why before one of us does it by accident. It moved three times between the third week of the third month of the year after next and the fourth week of the fourth month of it, and it has not been made and I am not going to be the one who makes it. Two pieces of evidence is not a decision and three is not either, and I have said that in a doorway in a rented room in the third week of the third month of the year after next and I have said it in every other room I have been in since, in other words, and I am not going to say it in this one because I have said it enough."

"You could have stopped me putting that sheet on the counter by not picking it up."

"**A person who is not asked has not been given a duty, and I have known that since about the seventh month of next year.** The reason I picked it up is that I have two boxes on a page of my own and both of them have your name in them, and neither of the boxes is the reason I am in this room. I have never once merged them and I am not going to start in a bay with about nine people a day up those stairs."

---

The counter is not a room that waits. It is a counter, and it is open from the second hour to the sixth, and there is a fee board on the wall behind it with four things written on it and none of the four is time, and she has looked at that board every working day for about two years and has never once been able to make it say anything else.

A copy is fourpence. A question of hers, at her own table, is a shilling and she has not done one of her own in about a year. A certificate is four shillings, and she has never issued one, and there is no form in this empire that requires her to issue one, and there has not been one since she came. The fourth thing on the board is a thing she has never worked out what it is for and she has asked two clerks about it and they have both said they do not know either.

A man of about forty came for a copy at about the fifth hour and paid fourpence and it took her about eleven minutes and she read it back to him twice and he read it back once and said thank you and she said that is the ordinary way and it is not a favour.

Then a woman of about thirty came and asked whether the copy would stand if the paper it came from was lost, and Tamsin Rook said no, and did not soften it, and did not charge her, and the woman went away with the answer and without a copy, and that is the fourth person in about a year to leave that counter with less than they came for.

There are about nine people a day up those stairs and there has been one of them coming for about a year who has never asked her anything, and she has worked out that he is not shy, because a shy man does not come nine times. That he is working something out at the same rate she is, and that he has got to the same place she has, and that neither of them is going to say so out loud, and that this is the only arrangement in this counter that neither of them put there.

"**I have thought about asking him one question four times since the fourth month of the year after next, and I have not asked it and I am not going to, and the reason is not care.** The reason is that a clerk of about fifty-five in an office about nine miles off has said out loud that she has never once been asked a question by anybody, and has been telling people about it for about a year. I have been on the other side of that counter and I have wanted to be asked every working day since, and there is not one form in this empire for a person to be asked."

---

"Two boxes," she said, after he had sat down again, "on a page ruled by hand in a book of my own, and both of them have your name in them, and I have not ruled a third and I am not going to, and I want you to notice that I have just said the number out loud in a room to a man and I have not said how long it is or what the two boxes are for, and that is on purpose."

"What is the number?"

"That is the question you are not going to get," said Tamsin Rook. "A person who is safe because nobody can instruct her and useful because somebody needs her standing in a room is a person with two boxes filled in on the same form. I said that out loud in the third week of the seventh month of the year after and about nine people have written it down since, and not one of them has asked me whether there is a third. You are the second, and you are asking me for a different reason, and I am not answering it either."

---

**The watch is not in force and it is not spent.** It requires only that a person who is not from the office that cannot be named be present, and about six hundred people in this city could satisfy it, and nobody has ever been paid for satisfying it and nobody has ever been thanked. And the paper that puts her in his rooms is a different instrument and it is the older of the two, and neither of them has ever been merged, and she said so out loud in about four minutes and would not say which of the two she wished was the only one.

"And there is a rule in a drawer of mine with the day on it that has been used six times and once not used, and I am not going to tell you how many times it has been used, and that is not a withholding either. It is a count, and a count is a thing that gets a heading put over it. **About nine people in this city have made a thing out of a habit in my time in these bays and every single one of them ended up with a heading over it**, and I have also watched a man of about thirty carry a sentence out of a room into a street in the second day of the first week of the first month of the year after next, and there is no form in this empire that puts a street in a book."


And a man of thirty-eight with no office and no fund has a sheet of paper in his own hand that is now in his own hand and not in a book of hers and not in a drawer and not in a room, and it is two things on a day and only one of them has a day on it. **The counter shut at the sixth hour and I put the counter down and I did not go up a stair and I did not send anything.** I have never once been one of the four. I have said the price of that out loud four times this year and been thanked once by a man of about thirty who is not in the matter and did not know what he was thanking me for.
