# Chapter 242: A Foreman Says Out Loud That She Is In A Box, And Nine Men Do Nothing With It

The water was down about where it had been a month before, which is a foot and a bit off the mark it had in the third month, and the hold against a company whose whole money is four shillings a year and all of it the rent of a building has not moved since the fourth week of the fourth month of the year after.

Halla Wray is fifty-one. She is the foreman of the Slade Cut, which is an office with no office in it, and she has been on that bank since she was nine, and she has been in a box since the first week of the seventh month of the year after, entered by a class, and she was not asked, and no form in this empire has ever been found for asking her.

Nobody on that bank knows that. There are about nine men in that bay between them and every one of them has been told something about her in nine years and none of it was that.

---

A man of about thirty-four came about four miles on the second day of the second week of the ninth month of the year after next, and he had been in the bay twice by the fourth hour of the afternoon, and he came a third time and stood at the end of the bench and did not sit down. She knew what he wanted because he had the look of a man who has been holding something in his mouth for about four miles and has decided to let it out in front of a person instead of in front of his wife.

"Ask it," said Halla Wray, without turning round.

"You are not going to like it."

"You are not going to get it out if I am busy, and I have four bays and a day." She put the pencil down. "**I have been doing this since I was forty-two and I have never once been asked what I wanted, so if you have got a question, say it and let me tell you what I think of it, and if you have not got a question then stand there and be quiet, and both of those are ordinary and neither of them is a favour.**"

---

He asked her what an entry in a box does to a life.

She did not answer for about four seconds, and she turned round, which she does perhaps four times in a week, and about nine men in that bay noticed the turn and none of them could have said afterwards why it was different.

"That is a question about a word on a form and it is the first one anybody has asked me out loud in this bay, and I have been in one since the first week of the seventh month of the year after, and I am going to answer it, and I am going to answer it in front of these nine men on purpose and not in a doorway, and I want the reason for that said first."

---

"The reason is that I have four reasons why nobody finds out that I am in one, and I gave all four of them out loud in that shed in the second week of the sixth month of the year after next, and three of them were about other people and one of them was about me. The one about me was that I am the only person on this bank who can say what an entry in a box does to a life and that being the only one is a door." She put both hands flat on the bench. "**I have been standing in front of that door since the seventh month of the year after and I did not know that was what I was doing until this month**, and I have told one person in a doorway and I have not told a bay in nine years, and I am telling a bay now because a man of about thirty-four walked about four miles to ask me and because I am tired of being the only one who knows."

---

Nobody said anything, because about nine men in a bay do not say anything when a foreman says a thing like that, and that is not rudeness, it is what a bay is.

"Some of you have worked on this cut for nine years," said Halla Wray. "None of you is going to be asked anything about that box this week or any week, and I am not asking you, and I am not telling you what it is for, and I worked out what it is for in about a minute and a half in the second week of the third month of the year after next and I have not told one living soul and I am not going to today."

"Why not?" said a man of about thirty.

"Because a person who is entered in a box and then asked how it feels has been asked twice, and I am not going to do it to about nine hundred people in this empire who have been entered in one since the seventh month of the year after by a class." She picked the pencil up again. "That is roughly the right number because that is what a class does. I do not know what it is like for any of them and I have not asked and I am not going to."

---

Then she said the other thing, and it was shorter, and it was the reason she had said the first one out loud in a bay and not in a doorway.

"I am also going to say that there is one person in this bay who could read a hold out loud in about four minutes, and she is at the back, and she is seventeen, and she is on a written engagement at the rate the list is set at, and nobody has thanked her and nobody is going to." She did not turn round. "I have given three reasons in a shed for not asking her and every one of them was a bad one, and I am not giving a fourth today, and I am not going to be told one either by anybody in this room who is not one of the four of them. **A foreman who admits in a bay that she has three bad reasons and no good one is a foreman who has begun to make a speech of her own job**, and I have nine men in here and a season that nobody agreed to."

---

The man of about thirty-four said the thing that made the week, and he said it badly, and it was still the right thing.

"Then how does anybody ever find out?" he said. "In a hundred and forty years. About how many of them."

"Four," said Halla Wray. "**Four in about a hundred and forty years, and there is no post anywhere in this empire whose job is to ask**, and the third of the four was the first one anybody did on purpose, and the count has not moved since the second day of the first week of the first month of the year after next. You are going to write that number on something and I am not going to stop you and I am not going to ask you what."

---

"He wrote it on the back of his hand about a week ago in a yard."

"Then somebody who knows what a hand does will be able to read it before the ink is off, and I am not going to tell him, and if he asks me I will not know what you are talking about." She went back to the bay. "**And if you are about to do something about that number, do it on a day that is not a day you have written about yourself on, and do not do it in a doorway.**"

"Why?"

"Because a doorway is where a thing gets said so that a person can hear it and not be in it, and you are not in a doorway at the moment, you are in a bay, and about nine men are in it with you, and if you say something in here then it is in nine mouths and there is no instrument in this empire that can take it back out of nine mouths." She did not look up. "I know that because I did it myself on the fourth day of the fourth week of the seventh month of the year after next, and I would do it again, and I am telling you about it and not about my box."

---

The rest of that afternoon was ordinary, and a foreman who stops doing the ordinary things is a foreman who has begun to make a speech of her own job.

A man of about twenty-eight wanted to know whether a person could be on two lists at once. She told him to take the paper with him and put it back the way he found it, because a person who takes a thing out of a list and puts it back wrong has done the one thing on that bank that cannot be undone by anybody. He said that was fair, and she said it was the ordinary way a list works and she was not going to improve it.

A man of about forty came in and asked her how long the poles had been stood against that wall instead of in the vat, and she told him the whole of it in about two minutes, out loud, without turning round, from behind the bench, and the men in that bay watched a foreman do a perfectly ordinary thing to a man who cannot read a paragraph and not one of them noticed anything at all.

A man of about twenty came in at about the seventh hour with a list of works from a different cut in his hand and asked whether the men on it were on this one as well, and she told him that two cuts do not share a list and that a man can be on four of them and not one of the four knows about the other three. He said that was not fair on the four, and she said it was not fair on anybody and she had never once been asked to make it fair and she was not going to start on a week when nobody agreed to a season.

"You are not going to make it fair?"

"I am going to do the ordinary things, and one of the ordinary things is a foreman telling the truth in about four seconds and then getting on with the list, and if the truth is not fair then the truth is not fair and it is not my job to make it so, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that says a foreman makes it so." She went on with the list. "I have been on this bank since I was nine and I have never once been asked to make anything fair and I am not going to start in the ninth month of the year after next."

---

Then she did the other thing, which is the only piece of administration in a week of work.

"You have a piece of paper stood up on that bank and nobody has answered it since the year before last," said the man of about thirty-four. "That is a plain question and I am not asking it as a complaint, I am asking it because I have been in four rooms about a thing that has not moved and I want to know whether this one has."

"It has not moved and it is not going to, and it is not an instrument, and I am not going to read you the terms of it in a bay, and I have not got the terms of it in my head and I am not going to go and get them." She said it in a foreman's voice and it took her about four seconds. "**There are about four hundred and forty foot of that bank** and there are about sixty children under sixteen inside it and about nineteen of us are not inside it, and the paper is about all of us, and it does not care which of us is which, and there is nobody in this empire I can ring up about it."

"Have you read it?"

"I have read it and I could not tell you one thing in it that I could repeat out loud, and I have been asked to describe it four times since the ninth month by people who came four miles and I have said no four times." She put the pencil down. "And a person who is paid to read is a person who can be told to stop, and there is a girl of seventeen at the back of this bay who is paid to read and is not going to start, and I said a sentence in this bay this morning that is not mine and I am not going to say it again today, and that is twice in one day I have put a thing into a room that I cannot take out of it."

---

The bill at Lowcross is nineteen pounds three and fourpence and is not being paid and is not going to be paid, and there has not been a line for a bridge in that fund in nineteen years, and she is not going to fund it, and she is not going to stand in this bay and ask any of them for a penny. She said it to the bench. "Two men on this cut worked a wall that is on a district's list at a shilling a day when the rate on a wall that is not on a list is three, and one of them argued it four times in a shed and lost four times, and neither of them has been made whole and neither of them is going to be and nobody on this bank has asked either of them a question about it and nobody is going to."

---

"And about nineteen of you said no again in the last week of the seventh month of the year after, with the same nineteen reasons, and the reasons are in the third column of this bay's own ledger with a day against every one of them and no names in that column at all, and you are still behind the bank and you are not going to be asked again."

Nobody in the bay said anything to that either, and about four of them had heard their own reason come out of a foreman's mouth in the seventh month of the year after next and had not enjoyed it, and one of them had stood up afterwards, and she had not stopped him, and she is not going to.

---

**So a woman of twenty is four hundred and thirty miles up this river in a room two streets off a street that goes down to the river road with sixteen books on a table** and a wage that has been held since the second week of the third month of the year after. She is in a box too, and she has been in one since the fourth day of the third week of the fifth month of the year after, and she said no out loud in a room with the day on it and the no did not work, and a piece of paper with a seal on it told her that no further consent of that person is required.

**And the foreman of this bay is in a box as well, and has been since the seventh month of the year after, and has just said so out loud in front of about nine men, and neither of the two has been asked anything**. There is no form anywhere in this empire that will un-fill a box, and the person who would administer one if there were one is a clerk of about fifty-five in an office about nine miles off a building who has never been asked anything either and would not be a villain about it.
