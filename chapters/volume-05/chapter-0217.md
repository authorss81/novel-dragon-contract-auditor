# Chapter 217: Nine Years Of It And Nobody Wrote It Down

The list of works for the coming season was made on the second day of the third week of the first month of the year after next, in a bay, out loud, and it took about four minutes, and the four were on it the way they are on every list, which is as four men whose work is on the list and who are not in the bay while it is read.

Halla Wray is fifty-one. She is the foreman of the Slade Cut, which is an office with no office in it, and she has been on that bank since she was nine. She can read and nobody at the district works has ever once in nine years asked her to read anything.

She had known since the fourth day of the fourth week of the twelfth month that she had said a whole sentence out loud in a room to a man with a satchel, and she had had about five weeks to do something about it and she had not done anything about it, and on the morning of the second day she worked out why, and the reason was that there was nothing in the world she could have done about it.

---

Then a man of about forty from the third gang asked a man of about fifty-five a question about the water, standing where he was, in the ordinary way, and the man of about fifty-five looked at the bench for about two seconds.

And Halla Wray answered it from behind him without turning round and in about four seconds flat, in a foreman's voice, in the plainest words there are.

She has been doing that for nine years. She did it that week in the fourth week of the twelfth month, and she has done it since she was forty-two, and nobody has ever asked her to, and about nine men have seen it, and nobody has ever written it down, and she is the only person in this empire who knows it and she worked that out on the second day of the third week of the first month of the year after next at about the ninth hour standing in a bay with a list of works in front of her."

"There," she said, to nobody. "**There is the answer to the question. It has been here the whole time and it is not a form and it is not a post and it is not a person asking anybody anything, and it costs nothing and nobody can be told off for it, and it has been running for nine years without anybody deciding to.**"

---

A book-keeper of thirty-eight had seen the whole of it in the fourth week of the twelfth month and had not understood it for about two hours, and she had watched him not understanding it, and she had said nothing to him then and she has thought about that since and it is the same decision twice.

She is not going to ask him whether he is going to keep doing it. She worked that out on the second day of the third week and the reason is hers and she gave it out loud in the shed in the second week of the eleventh month of the year after and it is the bad one, and it is still the bad one, and she is not going to improve it.

Asking is the only instrument there is and I have watched it cost a man of about thirty-four an afternoon and a woman of twenty about nine miles of walking, and I am not going to spend either of those on a thing I could hear about in a bay in about four minutes."

She is not going to do that. And the thing she is not going to do is bigger than that.

---

There was a girl of seventeen at the back of that bay.

She is on a written engagement and there is a copy of it in a satchel with a date on it and nobody has ever thanked her and there is no form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be thanked. She had been there until about the eighth hour on the two days a week she comes, and she reads better than anybody in that bay and she is at the same rate as everybody else on the list, and Halla Wray has known her since she was about eleven and has never once asked her to read anything.

**And a book-keeper had worked out in the second week of the tenth month of the year after that she was the one way of reaching the four, and had said so out loud in that bay, and had not done it, and had written down that he was not going to and read it back twice, and the reason he gave was that a person who is paid to read is a person who can be told to stop.** Halla Wray credited that argument in the second week of the eleventh month without pointing at her, and has not improved it since, and is not going to."

She could have said, in about four seconds, to a girl of seventeen at the back of a bay: *you are the one person here who could read that column to four men.* It would have been true. It would have been the correct thing to do in the ordinary way, and a foreman is allowed to say a correct thing in the ordinary way, and nobody would have thought twice of it.

And she is not going to, and she has worked out the reason, and it is not the book-keeper's reason, and she gave it out loud to a bay that did not ask her for it, which is the only kind of giving there is.

"Because the moment I say it to her she is a person who has been asked about it, and she is paid, and that is the whole of the argument in one act. He took nine days and a written finding to get to it. I have just got it out of my mouth at about the ninth hour and it took me five weeks and a man with a satchel. And afterwards, in about four years, there is a man with a copy of the right page who can stand in a room and ask a foreman of fifty-one what she said to a girl of seventeen in a bay, and the foreman will have to say it, and the girl will have to hear that she was asked about it, and neither of them will be able to say that nobody put it in her.

"And I would rather be the woman in this bay that nobody ever says one word about than the woman who said one word to the only person in it who reads for money. **That is not brave and I know it is not brave. It is the same shape as the reason I gave about the four and it is the same bad reason, and I have now got two of them and they are the same one,** and a woman with two bad reasons is not a woman with one good one."

---

Nobody in that bay said anything to her about any of it, and the girl of seventeen read out a list of works at about the eighth hour in the ordinary way and the second reading was slower, and she was not thanked and did not ask and is not going to start.

Then the ordinary things, because a foreman who stops doing them is a foreman who has begun to make a speech of her own job.

The count of the works. The water, which was up about a foot since the twelfth month. The hold, which is against a company whose whole money is four shillings a year and all of it is the rent of a building, and which has not moved since the fourth week of the fourth month and is not going to move because a foreman is fifty-one and has a boy at home. A factor's letter of four lines on a bench up the road, wanting a person who may be served and that person's consent, not going to be answered, and every day it is not answered is a day nobody has been entered. About nineteen reasons in a column in a book in a hired stone store off the Slade, with a day against every one of them, and the names in a different book and a name in a room is a thing that can be carried out of a room.

A bill at Lowcross at nineteen pounds three and fourpence, unpaid, nobody liable, and no line for a bridge in that fund in nineteen years. A guarantee standing offered and unanswered on about four hundred and forty foot of bank with about sixty children under sixteen inside it, and inside a guild's blank list, and inside a rotation they have not been asked about and have been told about none of it, and she has not got the terms of it in her head and is not going to read them out in a bay at about the seventh hour.

And the thirty-one of Mosswake, which is thirty-one, and which is another district's figure and which she said once and moved on from, and about nine men in that bay do not know whose it is and are not going to be told, because there is no form anywhere in this empire for a foreman to explain another district's number to a room full of men who have never crossed it.

---

He was in the bay for the reading and she had not expected him to be and she had about two seconds to decide what to do about it, and what she did was read the list in the ordinary way, and his work was in it, and she read his work the way she reads everything, and she did not read it any differently, and about nine men did not notice anything at all.

Afterwards, at the bench, he asked her a question about his own work. It was an ordinary question, it was about a length of bank and a number of days, and it took him about nine seconds to ask and about forty to explain that he did not know the number, and she gave him the number in about three seconds and told him where it was written, and then he asked her what the day under it meant, because there is a day against every line of a list of works and he has never once asked anybody what a day is for, and nobody has ever told him.

She told him. She told him in about four seconds, in a foreman's voice, in the plainest words there are, and he said that was not what he meant, and she said it was the only meaning a day has, and he said, "I meant whether it is the day it started or the day somebody decided," and she said it was the day it was written down and that those are the same day about nine times out of ten, and he said, "Then somebody decided," and she said, "Then somebody decided, and you have just worked that out in about nine seconds at about the ninth hour in a bay, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that requires a man to work that out and there never has been one, and I have never once been asked to help anybody work it out before and I have been doing it since I was forty-two."

He said thank you and she has not been thanked in nine years and she told him not to, in the ordinary way, and he said he had not meant it as anything, and she said she knew, and that was the end of it.

**And she had done it four times in about a quarter of an hour, on purpose, in a room, to a man who cannot read a paragraph, about a day on a list, and she has not written a word of it down, and she is not going to, and the four of them have still never been asked what the hold is.**

Then she said the other thing, and she said it standing up, and about nine men were in the bay and one of them is one of the four.

"There is a man in this bay who had a piece of paper read to him by a book-keeper in the first week of the twelfth month of the year after, and nobody wrote a word about it down, and the book-keeper has told me nothing about it and I have asked him nothing, and I know about it because a foreman on a bank knows everything a bay says by about the second hour, and that is the whole of how I know.

"He is one of the four. He is one of the four and he has had a piece of paper read to him and nothing was written down and he is not going to be asked about the hold this season and neither am I going to ask him about the paper. And I want it said in a bay that both of those are decisions and neither of them is mine.

"And I am not going to say his name in a room and there is no form anywhere in this empire that requires a foreman to say a man's name to nine men, and the reason I have just given about the girl of seventeen is the reason I am not going to say his name about the paper, and it is the same reason three times over, and a woman who has found the same reason three times has not been thinking, she has been refusing."

---

She was at the bench for about a quarter of an hour after the bay emptied and she did not light the lamp, which is later than it ought to be, and she had a book of her own in front of her with one sheet of her own hand in it, six numbered lines and a day against it and no name on it, and the notice was on the bench beside it with a seal and nothing under the seal, and the two of them are two sheets and only one of them is in her hand.

Four, she thought. **Four askings in about a hundred and forty years and no post anywhere in this empire whose job is to ask,** a court room in the fifth month that wanted a record, and a window in the first week of the tenth month, and a desk in the second week of the eleventh month that a woman of twenty walked four miles to, and a rented room in the first week of this month that the same woman walked nine miles to. **Two of the four were accidents. The third was on purpose. The fourth was on purpose and she did it on her own account and nobody sent her,** and I have not been told her name and I have not asked and I am not going to, and there is no post and there is not going to be one, and it will not happen again by itself.

And a book-keeper on her own bank had read a man's own paper to him in a yard and written nothing down, and she had been answering a man of about forty from behind a bench in about four seconds flat for nine years without being asked, and neither of those is one of the four, and neither of them is a form, and neither of them has anybody's name in it.

**Somebody asks, or nobody asks. That is still the whole of it. And the answer that has come out of this bank since the second week of the eleventh month of the year after is not a person. It is a habit that two people have and neither of them has appointed and neither of them has written down,** and I have not decided whether that is better and I am not going to decide it in a bay at about the seventh hour with the lamp off.
