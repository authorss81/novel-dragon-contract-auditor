# Chapter 201: The Form With Her Name At The Foot Of It

The bundle came up on a tray on the second day of the first week of the ninth month of the year after, and it came up because a thing that has been heard gets a file, and a file that has no heading over it cannot be found, and this office finds its own things twice a year and does not enjoy it either time.

Orla Denning is about fifty-five. She has kept the minute books of the office of a seat for nineteen years and she did the first four of them in a hand that is not the hand she is using now. The books are on a shelf in a room on the ground floor with a window about two feet wide and a pane out of it, and there are eleven of them, and a shelf is what a book that is looked at twice a year lives on. She is not a lawyer and she is not a party and she is not a judge, and she has never been asked what any of it meant.

The tray had a bundle on it, a sheet of good paper, and a slip of his own with a clerk's hand on it that said the file wanted a head.

---

The head of a file is a printed label about the length of a hand, and it has four things on it, and one of the four is a box.

> *Matter.*
> *Place.*
> *Day.*
> *The person, institution or thing engaged in the matter.*

The fourth of those is the one that takes the work. The other three she can do standing up. The fourth of those is the box, and the box is printed in about nine hundred buildings, and a person fills a box the way the box is printed, and nobody has ever been asked what the words mean and there is no post in this empire whose job is to ask.

She read the slip. She read the bundle's wrapper. She read the four lines again with her thumb against the edge of the tray, the way a person reads a thing four times when they are about to write one word.

Then she went and got the file the bundle was to go into, out of the press on the shelf, and the head of it was blank, and she had about four minutes before the tray went back down.

---

What the matter is, she can say without looking. It is a recorded claim of property and debt, entered and marked disputed, entered and marked disputed for longer than either of the two women who have to do with it has been in a room. It has a seat's device cut into the head of its notices and the device is not the fire-and-water mark. It has a hearing that has been and gone. It has a finding on a page with a day on it and a clerk's name at the foot of it.

**And it has no person in it.** That is the thing she has had for about nineteen years without a phrase for it, and she has a phrase for it now, and the phrase is that the matter is a claim about a house, and the house is a Kest house, and the house is about four miles off the old river road, and there is nobody in it and nobody has been in it for some years and there is nobody on any roll of households in this empire who is living in it.

She put the pen down and picked it up again.

"You are going to write the house," said nobody, because there was nobody there.

---

She did not write the house. She went and got a bundle of returns instead, from the second shelf, where they live because a return is a thing that comes back.

A return is one printed sheet with a column on it, and the column is headed for the person, institution or thing engaged in the matter, and the column is filled in at this end and sent out, and a keeper of a roll is to copy it down into her own book. That is the ordinary way. It has been the ordinary way for about a hundred and forty years and there is no form anywhere in this empire that requires a keeper to be asked whether the column is right before she copies it.

She had the bundle on the tray in about a minute and she went down it, and it took her about four minutes to get to the thing she was looking for, and she was not looking for it on purpose.

In about nine of the returns in that bundle, what was in the column was a household. In about four of them it was a company. In two of them it was a company of watermen and in one it was a chapel, and in one of them it was nothing at all and there was a line through the column where a name had been and somebody had struck it out with a pen and not a rule.

**Not one of them had a person in it, except one.**

---

The one was in the middle of the bundle and it was dated the fourth day of the third week of the fifth month of the year after, and it was for a district about four hundred and thirty miles up this river, and it went to the keeper of the roll of households of that district, and the column said a name.

She knew the name. She has not said it out loud in this room and she is not going to now, and there is a name in the room in the bundle and she read it and it went in her the way a name does.

It was a name of a person of twenty, and it was of a family that holds nothing enforceable anywhere in this empire, and the definition that caught her turns on a district's roll of households, and the roll does not list rights, holdings or claims, and this is on the face of the schedule and has been for longer than she has been in the chair.

**So the office asked a roll of households for a person, and a roll of households gave it one, and the office wrote the one down, and the office did everything correctly and the person said no and the no did not work.**

She sat down, which she does not do at a tray.

---

The return had her name at the foot of it.

Not a clerk's name in a column of clerks, and not a name in a book of names, and not an office. Her own name, in her own hand, at the foot of a sheet, on the day, where a name goes when a person is going to be answerable for a thing.

She had put it there in about nine seconds and she had not been asked and nobody had been asked, and she had done it correctly, and she has thought about those nine seconds every week since the fifth month and has told nobody that she has thought about them.

**A form which cannot be asked cannot be un-filled, and it cannot be un-filled by the person who filled it.** That is the whole of what she has got out of nineteen years and a tray, and it took a return and a bundle and a strike-out with a pen in it, and it is not in any of the eleven books on that shelf and she has looked.

She read her own handwriting from about a foot away, the way a person does.

---

The tray had to go back down. That is not a threat, it is a tray, and it goes down when it goes down, and if it does not go down it goes down later and with a question on a slip about why.

She had about four minutes and she had one word to write in a box.

She wrote *household* on the head of the file, because that is what the claim is about and a household is a thing and the box takes a thing. Then she wrote the day, and the place, and the matter, and then she came back to the fourth line and she did not leave it as she had written it.

Underneath *household* she wrote a second line, in the same hand, in about nine seconds:

> *I am asked to state the person, institution or thing engaged in the matter. There is no person engaged in it. There is a household of nine doors about four miles off the road, empty, and a claim, and a finding on a page. Nothing in this bundle is a person except one line, and the one line is a person of twenty about four hundred and thirty miles from this desk, and I did not choose her and I was not asked about her and I entered her in the fifth month, correctly, in the ordinary way, and my name is at the foot of that sheet and it is the second thing in this matter with my hand at the foot of it and the first one I did not choose. I am entered as the person who filled that box. — O. Denning, first day of the first week of the ninth month of the year after.*

It took longer than nine seconds. She read it back once and did not read it back twice, and the reason she did not read it back twice is that there is nobody in the room to hear either and she has been doing this alone since the first of them and she is not going to start now.

---

Then she did the thing that has no form behind it, which is that she looked at the head of the file with her two lines on it and worked out that a file with two lines under a box is a file that will be asked about, and that in about four years a person with a copy of the right page can put it side by side with the return and ask a clerk of this office to explain the difference between the two, and that she will have to explain it, and that she cannot.

"So write the one line," she said out loud, to a room on the ground floor with a pane out of the window. "**Write what the box says and put my name at the foot of it and go down the corridor with the tray and say nothing to anybody, and in four years there is a file with a household in it and a clerk in it and a day on it, and that is the whole of the protection and it is worth fourpence of ink.**"

The room did not disagree.

"And the other thing," she said, "is that a person who writes the second line is a person who cannot be asked to produce the first one, because I will produce it, and I will produce it gladly, and I will hand it to whoever asks, and I will say out loud in whatever room I am in that I filled that box on the fourth day of the third week of the fifth month and that nobody asked me and that I did it correctly, and I will say it in the same breath with the finding that the no did not work, and both of those are true and I have never in nineteen years put both of them in one room."

---

**A clerk who takes a file out of the ordinary course has not done anything a form forbids, because no form in this empire has a line about it, and there is no form anywhere that requires a clerk to put a sheet in a press, and that is why it is hers to decide and why it is hers to be shown.**

She put the tray back down on the counter by the door with the bundle on it and the slip of his own hand on top, and she wrote nothing on the slip, and she went back to the shelf.

---

The man who carries notices between the rooms of this building came for the tray at about the fourth minute and she had to hand it across, because that is the arrangement and there is no form anywhere that lets a clerk keep a tray.

He is not a clerk and he does not read a return and he has carried trays up and down that corridor for about nine years. He put his hand on it and stopped, because the head of the file was on the top of the bundle and there were two lines on the head of the file where there is one.

"That is a lot for a head," he said.

"It is two lines."

"It is a lot for a head," he said. He did not take his hand off the tray. "**Who fills that one in — the person, institution or thing? I have carried more of them than I can count and I have never once seen anybody stop to read that one.**"

And she answered him.

She answered him out loud, in a corridor, in a room with a rail nowhere in it, in about four minutes, standing at the end of a table with a tray between them, and she told him what the box is and where the words come from and that the box is printed in about nine hundred buildings and that the office is the only place in this empire that fills it in and does it right, and she told him that what goes in that column is a household or a company and that nobody has ever asked which of the two a thing is, and she told him the definition, and the roll, and the four miles of road, and the day in the fifth month, without once saying the name.

She did not tell him her own name. He knows it. It is on a door and it is on nineteen years of books and he carries trays for the room.

**He asked because he was going to say something about the trays, and she answered because he was standing there, and neither of them chose it, and she has thought about that since and it is the reason the answer was any good.** A room wants a record. A corridor wants a tray gone. Neither of those is a reason to ask a question and both of them are why one got asked.

"Right," he said. "That is a lot for a head."

"Yes."

"Do you want me to put it down as usual?"

"**Put it down as usual and read the head of the file to the man at the counter on the second floor, out loud, both lines, and I want him to hear the second one and not only the first, and I am not going to say why and you are not to ask me.**"

"That is not usual."

"No," said Orla Denning. "**And that is the second time a form in this empire has been asked what a word on it means, and the first time it was a court, and this time it was a man with a tray, and I would like it noticed that neither of those was anybody's plan.**"

He took the tray. He read the head of the file out loud on the stairs, both lines, which is not usual, and she heard him do it from the bottom of them with the books behind her and did not go up.

---

The window is about two feet wide and there is no pane in it and there has not been one since the spring. On a good day in the ninth month a person can see the end of a street and a bit of the low wall at the back of the building opposite. On a bad day there is a grey rectangle and nothing in it.

She looked at the grey rectangle for about a minute and a half.

"Two," she said.

She has kept the books for nineteen years and she has entered four thousand and some things into them and she knows roughly what is in all of them and she knows exactly what is in none of them. Nineteen years of writing down what a room decided, and not one line in any of the eleven books says what a room is, and this morning a man who carries trays asked her what a box is for and she told him and it is in neither.

**There is no post in this empire whose job is to stand in front of a heading and ask what it is for.** There is not one in this building and there is not one in this city and there is not one in any of the four hundred and thirty miles between this desk and a district where a woman of about sixty walks down a lane with a sheet on a board and a basket once a year, and she does not know what the fourth column of her own roll is for either, and she never will, and nobody is going to ask her, and I am not going either, and it is four hundred and thirty miles and there is no fund.

The file is on the table with two lines on the head of it. It is not in the press. It is going to stay not in the press for as long as she is the clerk who keeps the books, which is for as long as they let her, and there is nobody who can ask her about that either, and she is going to find out how that is possible by getting it wrong in public.
