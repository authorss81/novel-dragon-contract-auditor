# Chapter 232: The Fourth Reason, And The Sentence She Is Not Going To Give

The list of works for the coming season was read out in the bay on the second day of the second week of the sixth month of the year after next and it took about four minutes, and Halla Wray read it, and the four were on it the way the four are on every list, which is as four men whose work is on the list and who are not in that bay while it is read.

Halla Wray is fifty-one. She is the foreman of the Slade Cut, which is an office with no office in it, and she has been on that bank since she was nine, and she has been in a box since the first week of the seventh month of the year after, entered by a class, and she was not asked, and no form in this empire has ever been found for asking her.

The water was up a foot and a bit where it had been in the third month, and the hold against a company whose whole money is four shillings a year and all of it the rent of a building has not moved since the fourth week of the fourth month of the year after.

---

She had a fourth reason by the fourth hour of the afternoon and it was not about the four who cannot read a paragraph and it was about herself, and she gave it out loud in the shed where the other three were given, because that is where a foreman gives a thing and not in a room.

"I have got one more and it is worse than the last three, and I want it said that it is worse in a different way, and the way is that the other three were about other people and this one is about me," said Halla Wray.

Nobody said anything. The men in that shed filled it and the lamp was not lit.

"A guarantee is a piece of paper with a name at the foot of it. That was the third reason and it was the worst of the three and I gave it in the fourth week of the fifth month and I am not going to give it again. **This is the fourth reason and it is this: I am the only person on this bank who can say out loud what an entry in a box does to a life."**

---

"Why," said a man of about thirty from the second gang.

"Because I have been in one since the first week of the seventh month of the year after," said Halla Wray. "I have not been asked a single thing about it. I have not been told what it is for. I worked out in about a minute and a half in the second week of the third month of the year after next what it is for and I have not told anybody and I am not going to, and there is nobody on this bank who could tell you and there is nobody anywhere else who could tell you either."

"So you are the only one who knows."

"I am the only one who knows what it is like. That is not the same and I am not going to let a bay make it the same. About nine hundred people in this empire have been entered in a box this year by a class. I expect that is roughly right because that is what a class does. I do not know what it is like for any of the nine hundred and I have not asked and I am not going to, because a person who is entered in a box and then asked how it feels has been asked twice."

---

"And that is the door," said a man of about forty from the third gang.

"That is the door," said Halla Wray, "and I have been standing in front of it since the seventh month of the year after and I did not know that was what I was doing until this month. I told a book-keeper in a shed in the second week of the third month of the year after next that I would like to be found, and I meant it, and I am not going to unsay it. **Being the only person who can say it is worth more than being nobody, and it is worth a great deal more than being safe, and I have known that for about eleven weeks and I have not told one person, and that is the worst thing I have done in nine years."**

"You have told us."

"You are not anybody," said Halla Wray. "You are nine men in a shed and a foreman talking to a bay, and in about four years none of you will be able to repeat a word of it and neither will I, and I have worked that out and I did it in the second week of the fifth month of the year after next when a book-keeper said a question out loud in this bay and wrote it down nowhere."

---

Then she said the other half of it, and she said it more slowly, and it was about eleven weeks old and it had not gone anywhere.

"And I owe a second sentence and I have owed it since the third month of the year after," she said, "and I have not given it, and nobody has asked me for it, and a book-keeper of thirty-eight is four hundred yards up that road and has wanted it since the third month and has not asked and is not going to."

"Why not."

"Because of a half," said Halla Wray. "**In the last week of the eleventh month of the year after I said half a sentence to a man from the district works who asked me a question about the works, and he heard it and did not ask and did not write it down, and it cannot be got back, and the arrangement we made in the seventh month of the year after in that shed is that the day he asks me is the day I stop saying the half I have got."**

Nobody in that shed knew what the arrangement was. Nine men in a shed never do.

"He has not asked," she said, "and that is not him being careful, and I want it said that I know it is not, and he is a book-keeper and he has a wage and no form and no post, and I have decided that the half I have got is worth more to me than the whole of what I am owed, and I have decided it about four times."

---

Then the ordinary things, because a foreman who stops doing them is a foreman who has begun to make a speech of her own job, and she has been on this bank since she was nine and she is not going to start.

A man of about forty from the third gang asked her, standing where he was, whether the chain at the third link wanted oil in it or the week before, and she told him the week before, in about four seconds, out loud, without turning round, from behind the bench.

**And about nine men saw a foreman do a perfectly ordinary thing to a man who cannot read a paragraph, and not one of them noticed anything at all, and that is what a foreman is.**

She had done it four times in about half an hour that week. She has been doing it since she was forty-two, without being asked, and nobody at the district works has ever once in nine years asked her to read anything, and she is the only person in this empire who knows that she does it.

And she did not ask him. Not about the hold and not about the season and not about a piece of paper a book-keeper read to him in a yard in the first week of the twelfth month of the year after, which she knows about because a foreman on a bank knows everything a bay says by about the second hour, and which she has not said a word about to a living soul.

---

And then, on the second day of that week, she worked out something about herself that she had been working out for about nine years without arriving at.

She was standing behind the bench and a man of about thirty-four was at the other end of it and she answered him without turning round, and while she was answering him she noticed that she could not have told anybody which end of the bay he was standing at.

"**I have been reading this bay with my ears since I was forty-two,"** she said, to about nine men and to nobody. "I know how many are in it to about nine."

---

"Is that useful," said the man of about thirty-four.

"It is the only thing I have got that nobody can take off me," said Halla Wray. "It is not useful. It is nine years of a habit that started because I could read and about four men in this bay could not, and I have never told one of them and I am not going to, and I have got exactly one reason for that and I gave it in the second week of the ninth month of the year after and it was a bad one and I am not giving it a second time in a bay."

---

Then a man of about twenty-eight came in with a folded paper in his hand and asked whether a person could be on two lists at once, and it was a plain question and not a question about a word on a form.

"It depends which two," said Halla Wray.

"There is a list of works and there is a list of what the district is doing, and I am on the first and my brother is on the second and we have the same name."

"Then you are two people as far as either list is concerned and neither of them can tell that."

"My brother cannot read a paragraph either."

"Then the two of you have about four lists between you and not one of them knows about the other three," said Halla Wray. "And I am not going to find out for you, and I am not going to tell you why not, and if you go and find out then take the paper with you and put it back the way you found it, because a person who takes a thing out of a list and puts it back wrong has done the one thing on that bank that cannot be undone by anybody."

---

There is a sheet of paper on a bench in the hired stone store with a seal at the foot of it and nothing under the seal, and it has been on that bench since the seventh month of the year after, and it is not going to be answered.

There is a copy of six numbered lines in a book of her own with the day against it and no name on it, and the six lines ask that no inference of any kind be drawn from the absence of any name, and the sixth of them declines to name whoever holds the office at all. **Two sheets and only one of them is in her hand and neither of them has her name in it and she did not put it there.** She has read the second line nine times and the sixth line about four times and she said the second of those counts out loud in that bay in the fourth week of the fifth month of the year after next and she is not going to say it again.

A factor wrote four lines wanting the name of a person who may be served. It is on that bench and it is not going to be answered and every day it is not answered is a day nobody has been entered.

A bill at Lowcross is nineteen pounds three and fourpence and there has not been a line for a bridge in that fund in nineteen years, and she is not going to fund it and she is not going to stand in a bay and ask any of nine men for a penny.

About nineteen people said no again in the last week of the seventh month of the year after with the same nineteen reasons, and the reasons are in the third column of that bay's own ledger with a day against every one of them and no names in that column at all, and they are still behind that bank and they have not been asked again and they are not going to be.

And about four people in that bay cannot read a paragraph and have never been asked anything and are not going to be this season, and she has given one reason about them and two other reasons that were not about them, and all three were bad, and the last one was about a piece of paper with a name at the foot of it.

---

Then a man of about fifty came in and asked whether the hold would be on the list before the season shut, and she told him it would not and why, in a foreman's voice, in about four seconds, and he said that was not what he meant, and she said it was the only meaning it has, and he went away.

And a man of about twenty-eight came in and asked her about a folded paper and she told him the thing about putting it back the way she found it, and he said that was fair, and she said it was the ordinary way a list works and she was not going to improve it.

She had the whole of the afternoon and the whole of the next day after that, and about forty-one people on a list, and a bay of about nine foot by eleven with a bench along the north wall, and a lamp in it that was not lit until about the seventh hour.

And a man of about twenty-eight came back on the second day and said that he had found out for himself and that the district list said a household and not a person, and she said that was about four years late for him and about four years early for him, and he asked her which, and she said she had four bays and a day and she was not going to do it for free and she was not going to do it at all.


And about four hundred and thirty miles down this river there is a man of thirty-eight with no office and no fund, and a foreman of fifty-one in a bay has just said out loud that she is the only person who can say what an entry in a box does to a life, and neither of them knows the other's name, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that will put a bay and a rented room in the same place, and the lamp is not lit.

**A third season shut on that cut and nobody agreed it and nobody refused anything, and about nineteen people are still behind the bank, and about four people in that bay have still never been asked one question.** A foreman of fifty-one has given four reasons out loud in a shed and every one of them was bad, and the last one was about herself. The woman who did the right thing is four hundred and thirty miles up this river and has not been asked and is not going to be asked this season.

And the half of a sentence is in her still, and it came out of her mouth in the last week of the eleventh month of the year after to a man from the district works who asked her a question about the works. A book-keeper of thirty-eight heard it and did not ask and did not write it down, and it cannot be got back, and he has wanted the rest of it since the third month of this year and has not asked, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to ask a foreman for the rest of a sentence. **The lamp is not lit and the half is not asked for, and both of those are the way the week ended.**
