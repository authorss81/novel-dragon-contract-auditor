# Chapter 213: The Second Time She Said The Price

He came on the third day of the third week of the twelfth month of the year after, at about the ninth hour, and he had about four minutes' worth of patience left in him and he used all of them in the wrong place, and she has thought about that since.

Ivet Sarn is thirty-eight. She has been at the clerk's window of the Mosswake Roll Court for nine years. The window is on the ground floor with the embankment corridor behind it, and there are four presses in the corridor, and there is a back room off that corridor that she has walked past every working day for nine years and has never once been inside. The court keeps a roll of households of the district for the water. About nine people a month ask her for a book in the wrong form and she admits it and tells them what it will cost them.

He is about thirty-four. In the first week of the tenth month he came to this window and asked her what the word *resident* means on a return, because he had moved in the second week of the ninth month from a street with a shop on it to a set of rooms over a yard, four streets off, inside the same district, and he had a wife and a boy, and he wanted to know whether a word was going to be used about them.

**He had said afterwards that he would rather not have asked, and she had said that that was the correct answer, and that was the whole of it, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that requires anybody to be told anything after that.**

He did not ask her anything. That is on the page because it is the fact the chapter is built on.

---

He put a slip on the sill with a reference on the top of it, and he gave four words for it, and she looked at the reference and said fourpence and made a copy on a sheet of press in about a minute and a half, and she read the copy out, and he said it was right, and he paid fourpence and went.

That is the whole of the transaction. It is the ordinary trade of that window and about four hundred of those are bought and filled in in a year and not one of them is a question.

Then he did not go, which is the part she has thought about.

"I have got about four minutes," he said, "and I would like to use them, and you can go back to your books."

"No I would not," said Ivet Sarn. "**What did you come back for.** You had your fourpence worth and you said the copy was right and that is the end of it, and a person who has had his fourpence worth and stays at a window is a person who has come back for something, and I am going to guess what it is, and you are going to tell me if I am wrong."

"You did not answer my question. Not the word. The other thing you said first."

"I always say it first," said Ivet Sarn. "**A person who asks a question about a form in a room becomes a person somebody has answered, and there is no instrument anywhere in this empire that takes that back off him.**"

"You did say that. I did not hear it. I heard a clerk being careful."

"Then I am going to say it again," she said, "and this time I am going to slow down, and I am going to say it before anything else instead of after, and if that is not what you came back for then I have wasted about ninety seconds of both our mornings and you may go and I will not think about it again."

---

She said it again, and it took about a minute, and it was the same words, and he listened to it properly the second time, and the second time is the one that counts and there is no form anywhere in this empire that says a thing counts twice.

Nobody had told him and nobody was going to. **The only reason he knew was that he had asked her to say it again in a room, and that is a thing a person can do, and it is the only thing anybody can do about it,** and there is no form anywhere in this empire for asking a person to tell you what you have become.

Then he said, "It is worse than I thought."

"It is exactly what you thought it was. I told you that at the time."

"You told me a lot of things at the time," said the man, "and about four of them have kept me up since the first week of the tenth month, and this is the one that has kept me up most, and it is the one I did not hear."

"Then that is the correct answer and I am not going to take it back, and there is nothing whatever in this empire that would let me."

---

Then he asked whether anybody had found him. It was not a question about a word on a form. It was a plain question, in a room, out loud, and it is on the page because she has never had one of those at that window either, and the count of those is a different count and is not three and is not printed here.

"Yes," said Ivet Sarn. "**You have, and there is no undoing it and no form anywhere in this empire to undo it with.** And you are the second of the three. The first was a room that wanted a record, in the fifth month, in a court. The third is a woman of twenty who walked about four miles in the second week of the eleventh month and asked a clerk to explain how the box got filled, and she was the first one anybody did on purpose, and nobody sent her. Three, and none of the three of you is a plan."

"And I am going to tell you what you actually came to find out, because you have not asked it and I am going to ask it for you, and I am going to answer it, and you are not to thank me for it afterwards."

---

She took her own book out of the drawer of her own desk, which is not the court's book and has no office on it and has her hand in it in about nine places, and she did not open it, and she put it on the sill face down.

"You moved in the second week of the ninth month. Four streets off, inside the same district, and you did not tell anybody, and the reason you did not tell anybody is that there is nothing at this counter that asks you, and that is not a complaint, it is the shape of the building.

"So your household is at a door four streets off and it was at a door on a street with a shop on it, and both of those doors are inside this district, and the roll of households for this district has both of them in it, and the roll does not say which people live at either door. It says a household is at a door. That is all it has ever said and that is on the face of the form.

"**And what came back to the office about your street, in the fifth month of this year, was a household.** The box on that return wanted a person, and a household is what the roll had, and the office wrote the household down, and that is the whole of what has happened to you and your wife and your boy, and nothing has been done to you at all.

"And that is what *resident* means. It means the roll at a door. Not living in it, not paying anything, not anybody having agreed to anything. A man can be resident on four rolls in four districts in this empire and be on none of them in the sense he means, and you have been on one of them since the second week of the ninth month and you moved four streets off and the roll never noticed and the water did not care and nobody in this building ever knew there was anything to notice."

---

The man stood at the sill for about a minute and a half and did not say anything, and about nine people went past him into the corridor and he did not turn round.

"That is what I was afraid of," he said.

"That is the opposite of what you were afraid of, and I told you that in the first week of the tenth month in about four minutes and you went out of here and thought about it for a fortnight and came back and bought a copy, and I would like it noticed that the answer you got was an ordinary answer and it cost you the only thing you have."

"I have not got anything," said the man. "That is what I have been finding out since the first week of the tenth month."

---

Then he asked about the basket, and she had known he would before she answered the first thing, and that is on the page too.

"There is a basket under that sill," he said. "It has been there every time I have come. I have never once asked anybody about it and I have thought about it four times."

"There is a basket under that sill. It is about nine inches deep. It is a basket that comes off a form, and the form calls what goes in it rubbish, and the form has called it rubbish for about a hundred and forty years."

"What is in it."

Ivet Sarn did not answer him for about a minute, and the reason she did not answer him is the reason she gave to herself in the first week of the tenth month, and it was not caution and she has never said it was caution.

Then she said, "They are returns. There is one for a great many years in that basket and every one of them has a column headed for the person, institution or thing engaged in the matter, and every one of them has that column filled in at the other end, in ink, in a hand that is not mine, with a day on it, and the day is the day the column was filled and not the day it was sent, and there is no space on the sheet for the second day."

"And what is that."

"That is the only document in the world that says how a box got filled," said Ivet Sarn, "and I have never thrown one away, and I have never shown one to anybody, and I have never told anybody what is in it, and there are about nine sheets deep and every one of them is the only one of its kind. And I have just told you."

---

The man did not ask to see one and she was watching him not ask, and she had a reason ready for it and he never gave her the chance.

"You are not going to show me."

"No. **You are the second person in a hundred and forty years to have asked me what a word on a form means and you came back and asked me nothing, and a man who comes back and asks nothing is the only kind of person in this empire it is safe to tell anything to at all.**

"And I am not going to take one out, and the reason is that there is one in that basket about the street you used to live on, and if it goes into a man's hand then in about four years there is a person who can hold it up beside a sheet of mine and ask me to explain the difference between the two, and that person would be you, and you would be doing it about yourself and about your wife and your boy, and I have got no way of stopping you and I would not want to."

"And if I wanted one."

"You would have to come and ask me for it in a room with two people in it, and there is no form for that, and I would have to say yes in front of the other one, and I am not going to, and you did not ask, and I noticed that you did not ask and I want it entered that I noticed."

---

Then he said, "That door has been open the whole time you have been talking."

It was, a couple of inches, on the left of the corridor about halfway along, and it has been every working day for nine years, and she has been past it about two thousand times, and there is nothing in it that anybody has ever told her about.

**A person who opens a room nobody has asked her about is a person who has given herself a duty, and a person who has given herself a duty cannot stop.** She has got enough of those in nine years and one of them is the reason there is a paper about a winter in a drawer in her own room that she has never told the court about.

"It has," said Ivet Sarn.

"You have not looked at it."

"No."

He nodded at it and did not ask her what was in it, and that was the second time in about ten minutes that a person had stood in front of her and failed to ask her a question, and it is the whole of the reason she has said as much as she has said.

Then she said the thing that has cost her the most this year, and it is on the page because she said it out loud in a room with one other person in it, and a room is the only kind of place in this empire where a thing like that can be said.

"**You asked me in the first week of the tenth month and I answered you, and the answer was true, and the answer was that nothing has been done to you,** and you have been carrying the price of a true answer for about nine weeks, and I am not going to be the reason that is a strange thing to be carrying, so I am going to give you the other half of it and then I am going to shut the window on this.

"The other half is that I did not write it down. Not on the sheet, and there is no place on the sheet, and not in the court's book, because a return that cannot be copied is a return with nothing in it that this court is the person to copy. And I have not written it in my own book either, and I have written eleven things in my own book in nine years and I read every one of them back twice with the second reading slower, and **I did not write this one, because nothing was asked, and a rule that was made by a girl of seventeen about four hundred and thirty miles away in a bay does not cover the case where a man comes back and buys a copy.**

"So there is no document in this empire that says you asked me anything, and you asked me something, and I answered you, and you are the only person who knows it, and I am the only other one, and in about four years neither of us can prove a word of it, and I would like that written down somewhere and I am not going to write it anywhere, and that is the last thing I have got."

---

She shut the window at about the fourth minute past the hour and she did not mean to, and it was the ordinary time, and a man of about fifty came for a copy at about a minute past and had to wait, and she served him in about two minutes and read it out and he paid fourpence and went the way they all go.

**That is the whole of the trade at that window, and about four hundred of them are bought and filled in in a year, and not one of them is a question, and the reason is not that the people who come to a counter do not want to know things.** It is that a person who wants to know something at a counter does not come to a counter, they come to a counter to buy fourpence worth of paper, and they would be ashamed to stand there and use up a minute of a clerk's time for nothing.

She has got a register on the top shelf of the second press, and it is a book with a column in it and a day against every line of it, and its fourth column has had no heading over it for nine years and she has not filled it and nobody has ever asked her why, and that is a column in a book on a flood terrace and it is not a column in a book about four hundred and thirty miles up this river and she is not going to stand at a window and pretend the two of them are one thing.

And he had gone down the corridor by then and there was nobody at the sill.

She wrote in her own book with no office on it, in her own hand, with the day on it, and read it back twice, and the second reading was slower, and at the bottom she put the reason, which is the reason she has written eleven times in nine years and which has not got any better:

**A hand of mine is the only hand anybody is going to be able to put side by side in about four years, and I have now done this for the twelfth time and I have not stopped and I am not going to.**

And after that, in the same book, on the same day, a second line, which is the only second line she has ever written in it, and it is four words and it is not a rule:

**He came back and asked me nothing.**
