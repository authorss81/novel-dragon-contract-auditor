# Chapter 218: An Undertaking Agreed And No Form For A Fund

He came to the bays on the fourth day of the fourth week of the first month of the year after next, at about the ninth hour, and he had a sheet of paper in his hand and he wanted to put it on her desk, and she made him hold it.

"Read it before it goes," she told him. "Read it in front of me. And if you have a reason against it, say the reason in a room and out loud and not on a page."

Tamsin Rook is twenty-four. She is a party to an instrument about a dead woman's name. Two instruments require her in any room he is in and she has never once merged them and he has never once merged them, and a form gets merged by being inconvenient: the first is a paper made in a form in four days with a day on it, and the second is a watch that does not name him and does not name her and requires only that a person who is not from the office that cannot be named be present. About six hundred people in this city could satisfy the watch. She has satisfied it every time it has been called for and nobody has ever paid her for it.

Marek Kest is thirty-eight and has a rented room with a table in it and about four feet of a wall he does not use, and he had asked her to come to a counter four bays along and she had said she would not and had said why and he had come anyway.

---

"I have a reason," he told her.

"I know you have. Give it."

"I want to buy paper."

She put her pen down.

"You have a room with a table in it and about four feet of a wall you do not use, and I have a press in the next bay with about six hundred printed slips in it that say the fourth line is not to be completed by this office, and there are about four hundred blanks in a box under a counter four bays along. A copy is fourpence. A question is a shilling at a notary's table. **I have worked out that in about nine hundred buildings in this empire there is a printed form that nobody has ever shown to a person, and that the only instrument anybody has ever found for saying no to a box is somebody asking a question out loud,** and that the two have never been in the same room in about a hundred and forty years.

"And the reason it has never been done is not money. It is that nobody thinks of a form as a thing you can hold. A clerk of thirty-four stands behind a slab in this city four bays along with a fee board on it with four things written on it and none of them is time, and those four words are printed, and every person who reads them in a year reads a heading they have never been asked about, and there is no post in this empire whose business it is to ask what a word on a board means, and the reason there is not is that a board is not a form, and a person does not think of a board as a thing that can be asked a question."

---

She did not say no straight away, and she has thought about the four minutes it took since.

"You know I am not going to be able to do it," she said.

"I do not think you are going to be able to do it either. I have not got any money."

"Then that is the first thing and it is a fact and not advice. There is no fund, there has never been a fund, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for a fund, and I have looked for one in about nine hundred buildings and there is not one, and about four hundred and thirty miles of road is what it costs anybody who wants to come and see a page in a book, and I have said out loud that I am not going to add it up and I am not going to now."

"I am not asking you to add it up. I am telling you what I want and it is paper and I have not got the price of it, and a price is a small thing to say out loud in a room and it took me about a fortnight to be able to say it."

---

She read the sheet of paper he was holding, which was a copy of four definitions in a copyist's hand, in ink, on foolscap, and she read it twice and then a third time which he had not asked for.

"You are not going to buy anything with this."

"No. **I am going to hold it. That is the entire instrument and there is nothing behind it and there is not going to be anything behind it,** and I have got a sheet of eleven questions in a book with no office on it and a twelfth line that is empty, and what I am proposing is that a person should be able to hold a printed form in a hand and not have to ask anybody to read it to them, and that is a thing about paper and not about anybody's duty, and there is no post in it and there is no fund and there is no form."

---

"And you have brought it to a clerk of twenty-four," she said.

"I have brought it to the only person in this city I am not allowed to ask to do it," he said. "You are a party to an instrument about me and you cannot be instructed and you cannot be paid and you have said out loud that if anybody in this matter ever asks you to go and ask a question in a room on that bank you are going to say no, and you said it in a room and I was in it."

"**I did say that, and I said I would say no, and I am not going to unsay it, and nobody is going to ask me and I have made sure of that myself and I would like it written down that I did.** And you have not asked me. You have brought me a piece of paper and a reason and you have stood in a bay and waited for me to say something about it, and that is the correct use of a room and it is the whole of what there is.

"Agreed," said Tamsin Rook, and she wrote *agreed* and the day on it in her own hand under his reason, and the day is the fourth day of the fourth week of the first month of the year after next.

---

Then she did the other thing, and she did it because a thing which has never happened can be made to happen by a person and the reverse is also true: a thing which has happened once can be made not to happen again by nobody doing anything.

They counted it out on the counter between them, in about four minutes, because she is a person who counts and he is a person who counts, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for counting anything.

"One, a court room in the fifth month of the year after, and the room wanted a record. Two, a clerk's window in the first week of the tenth month, and a man was afraid about his own household. Three, a desk in the second week of the eleventh month, and a woman of twenty walked about four miles and nobody sent her. Four, a rented room in the first week of this month, and the same woman walked about nine miles and nobody sent her.

"**Four in about a hundred and forty years, and the first three are all months back, in the fifth month and in the first week of the tenth and in the second week of the eleventh, and the fourth is three weeks old, and two of the four were accidents and nobody chose either of them, and the other two were on purpose, and one of the two was done by the same person as the other.** And here is the count that is the one I have to say: in the three weeks since the fourth one, nobody has asked anybody anything. Not at this counter. Not at the one four bays along. Not in a bay, not at a window, not in a shed, not in a rented room. Four, and it did not spread.

"And that is not a finding about people," she said. "That is a finding about what happens when a thing happens once. It will not happen again by itself. **And the reason it did not spread is not that nobody wanted to know. It is that there is no way to want to know a thing you have not heard about, and there is no way to hear about it, and the only way anybody found out about the first three was to be in a room where somebody else was asking.**"

---

He nodded at that for about a minute and then said, "That is the whole of it and you have said it in about nine sentences."

"I have said it because it is about a person and not about a form, and that is the only reason I have ever been able to say anything at all in a room." She closed her book. "**And I am not going to be a room that knows a thing can be asked.** I told you in the third week of the tenth month of the year after that one question from you teaches a room a thing it does not know, and that a room that knows a thing can be asked stops being a room you can ask, and you wrote a thing down afterwards in order to be refused and you were refused and the reason was a fact and not advice, and I have not improved on either of those and I am not going to.

"And I have two boxes in this book that I ruled by hand and filled in with the same name, and they are not the paper and they are not the watch, and the paper and the watch are two instruments and I have never once merged them, and I have not ruled a third and I am not going to. A person who rules a box for every thing that happens to her in a year has turned a book into a form, and I would not be the first clerk in this city to do that and I would like to be the last."

---

A man came in at about the ninth hour for a copy of a form at fourpence and she had to serve him, and serving him took about two minutes, and he did not ask her anything, and she has never once in this matter had a person stand at a counter she was serving and ask her a question.

That is not the point of any of this and it is the reason for half of it. A room gets a day. A person can come up to a counter, be served in it, ask in it, and walk out of it, and there is nothing on the board under the rail and no fee and no form that says what may be asked at it and none that says what may not. **And in the whole of the time this counter has stood nobody has asked at it, and the reason nobody has asked is not that they do not want to know.**

It is that a person who wants to know something at a counter does not come to a counter. They come to a counter to buy fourpence worth of paper, and they would be ashamed to stand there and use up a minute of a clerk's time for nothing, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that a person can put in front of a clerk saying *I have a question and I have not got a shilling*, and there is no shilling's worth of nothing in the world.

"I have thought about that for about four days since the first week of this month," she said to the counter, after the man had gone, "and the honest shape of it is that the room is there and the asking is not, and a thing that has now happened four times in a hundred and forty years has still not happened once at this counter, and I stand behind a rail every working day and I have had about nine people a year want to ask me something and not one of them has opened their mouth.

"And the reason I have not said anything to any of them is that **a clerk of this table is paid, and a person who is paid can be told to stop, and a person who is paid can be shown the day she answered and the words she used and cannot say no to any of it.** And I have said that reason out loud on a bank about four hundred and thirty miles down this river and a book-keeper said it before me, and I am not going to say it as though I thought of it, and I am not going to improve it.

"And that is the answer to who asks, and it is the fourth time in the year after next that the answer has come out the same way, and I am the second person in this matter to have arrived at it and neither of us appointed anybody."

Then she told him the price, out loud, standing, because she has never once in this matter had a person stand at a counter she was serving and ask her a question, and this one had not asked her a question at all, and that had cost more.

"You have not asked me anything and I have said a great deal, and I am going to say the price of it now because that is the trade at this counter and it is the only trade there is.

"I have said the price of being a party out loud ten times and nobody has thanked me once, and I did not expect to be thanked once and I would like it written down that I did not. And I have not been thanked in this bay and I am not going to be thanked and there is no form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be thanked.

"And there is one thing left and I am going to say it and then I am going to serve the man at the counter who has been standing there for about four minutes, and he has a slip with a reference on the top of it and he is going to pay fourpence and he is not going to ask me anything, and about four hundred of those are bought and filled in in a year and not one of them is a question.

"You have never once asked me whether I have decided whether to trust your judgment, and I have never told you that I have not, and both of those are the same silence and it has been going on for a long time, and I am not going to decide it in a bay at about the ninth hour, and the decision is not going to be made in a room and it is not going to be written down. **You have now been refused by me in a room in writing twice and you have not argued once, and that is two pieces of evidence, and two is not a decision, and a woman who treats two as a decision is a woman who has been alone too long.**"

She served the man at the counter, and he paid fourpence, and she read his copy out, and he said it was right, and he went, and the door of the bays was open, and there is no post in this empire whose job is to ask, and the count is four, and the fourth is three weeks old, and a piece of paper was agreed in a bay and could not be bought, and the undertaking of the fourth of the first month had been done for the sixth time and the reason was the best one either of them has ever brought her, and it is a reason about paper.
