# Chapter 219: A Question Is Not On The Board

The counter is the fourth one and it is the one for scale instruments and it has been for about a hundred and forty years, and there are about four hundred blanks in a box under it that are bought and filled in and a book of forms on the shelf under that, and the fee board has four things on it and none of them is time.

Marn Ottery is thirty-four and has been at that counter eleven years and she came to it from the second counter, where estates are. She is not a lawyer and does not want to be one, and she is a party to an instrument about a dead woman's name, and there is no form in this empire for a document about a person who did not ask to be in one, and she knows that better than anybody in this city and has had a woman of twenty tell her so to her face in the ninth month of the year after.

She was on her own at the rail from the ninth hour until about the eleventh, and about four hundred people a year come up to that counter and buy fourpence worth of paper, and she made nine copies and sold four blanks, and not one of the thirteen asked her anything.

---

Then there was the part of the morning she had been thinking about since the ninth month of the year after, and she thought about it while she wiped the slab, which takes about a minute and is the only part of the hour in which she is not a clerk.

A woman of twenty had come up to this counter three times that month. The first two times she had stood at the rail and looked at the board and gone away, and a clerk notices that the way a clerk notices everything, which is to say she notices it and does nothing about it for about eleven days.

"That was not the same as not asking," said Marn Ottery, to the slab. "That was the board working exactly as it is printed, and the board is printed to be a list of what a thing costs, and a person can stand in front of a list of what a thing costs for about a minute and a half and work out in about nine seconds that nothing on it is free, and then there is no way at all to stand there, and so she goes away, and I have watched about nine people a year do it and not one of them has ever come back and told me why."

**And the reason I could not put a free question on that board, and the reason she had to come three times, is the same sentence, and it is the sentence about being identified,** and it took me the second week of the ninth month, the second week of the eleventh, a man of sixty and a knife in a drawer to get to it, and nobody in this matter has said it out loud yet, and I am not going to be the one who says it in a room where it goes in a book.

Then a woman of about thirty-five came up with a piece of paper in her hand and asked a plain question, which was how long a thing stayed on the counter once it was brought in, and Marn Ottery answered it in about four sentences in the plainest words she has, and the woman said thank you and Marn Ottery said there was nothing to thank her for and the woman said she was thanking her anyway and went.

Nobody has thanked her once in eleven years at that counter and a woman of thirty-five did it in about four seconds in the first week of this month, and Marn Ottery has not got a rule about that and did not have one about the other thing and is not going to get one now.

---

He had been coming for years. He is about sixty and he comes at about the tenth hour and he has a slip with a reference on it and he has had one every week for longer than she has been on that counter, and he pays fourpence and she makes the copy in about a minute and a half and reads it out and he says it is right and he goes.

She has never once known what is on the slip. He brings a reference and four words and she does not ask him what the matter is, because the counter is for scale instruments and not for anybody's business, and because about nine people a year find out what happens when a clerk asks.

That week she watched him do it, and she watched herself not ask him, and she worked out in about a minute and a half why.

**He is not ashamed of buying fourpence worth of paper. He is ashamed of asking a question that costs him a minute of a stranger's time and nothing else.** There is a difference, and the difference is the whole of this counter and the whole of this matter, and I have never in eleven years been able to put it into a sentence a customer could read.

---

The man of about sixty came at about the tenth hour on the fourth day of the first week of the second month of the year after next and put the slip down and gave four words for it, and she made the copy and read it out and he said it was right, and neither of them said anything else, and that is the ordinary trade of that counter and it is the whole of it.

Then he said, "Are you all right to be asked something."

She put the copy down on the slab face up.

"**I am going to answer that in about four minutes and out loud and for nothing, and I am going to say the price first, and I am not going to write any of it down.** Here is the price: a person who asks a question about a form in a room becomes a person somebody has answered, and there is no instrument anywhere in this empire that takes that back off him. I am telling you that now, at the start, and I have said it to two people in this city since the second day of the second week of the ninth month of the year after, and one of them said afterwards that he would rather not have asked, and that was the correct answer."

"Have you had this before."

"No. **Three times, in about a hundred and forty years, in this empire.** A court room in the fifth month of the year after that wanted a record, and a clerk's window in the first week of the tenth month, and a desk in the second week of the eleventh month that a woman of twenty walked about four miles to. Two of the three were accidents and nobody chose either of them and one was on purpose and nobody sent her, **and there is no post in this empire whose job is to ask, and it will not happen again by itself.**"

He said, "Then I had better not."

"That is a decision and it is yours and I am not going to argue with a man of sixty about it. I have said the price and you have heard it and you have said no, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that entitles anybody to be troubled a second time about it, and I am not going to trouble you about it again."

---

Then she did the thing she had worked out on the fourth day, and it is the whole of this chapter, and she did it standing up the way she gives things.

"You may ask me anything. **Not because of anything that has happened in another county, and not because a woman of twenty asked me a question in the ninth month of the year after and I answered it and could not write it down.** Ask me because I have been at this counter eleven years and I have said that sentence to two people since the ninth month, and there has been a hundred and forty years of a board over my head with four things on it and none of them is a question, and I have never once had the nerve to say out loud that a person may stand here and use up a minute of my time for nothing."

"Why," said the man of about sixty, "is it about you and not about me."

"Because a person who is paid to read is a person who can be told to stop, and a person who is paid to answer is the same thing with a different word on it. I have been paid to answer for eleven years and I have never been asked to, and I have said the price every time and I have answered anyway, and I would like it said somewhere that a clerk of this table is the cheapest person in this city to send and nobody has ever sent me."

She put her hand flat on the oak, which she does about twice a year.

"And I have said that I would not go and ask anybody anything, and I said it in a room to a woman of twenty-four in the third week of the tenth month of the year after, and I am not going to unsay it, and nobody is going to ask me, and I have made sure of that myself.

"So it is about me, and I am not going to be a post, and there is no post, and a person who appoints somebody to a post that does not exist is doing the thing the whole of this is about, which is filling in a box the way the box is printed."

---

The man of about sixty looked at the board over her head for about a minute.

"Is there anything on that board about a question."

"No."

"Then a man of sixty has to be told he may ask, out loud, by a clerk, once, before he dares, and he has to be told it by a person and not by a slip, and there is nothing at that counter to point at. That is the whole of the finding and I have arrived at it at about the fourth minute past ten of a day in the first week of this month —"

He stopped.

"You have not been here today," said Marn Ottery.

"No," said the man, and put his hat on, and did not say thank you, and was not thanked, and went.

**And he did not ask her a single question. She had told him in about nine sentences that he could, and he had used none of it,** and he had found the shape of the whole thing out loud in a room in about four minutes, and she has thought about that for a fortnight and she is not going to improve on it."

---

Then she said the other half of it out loud, standing at the rail, to a counter with nobody at it, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that requires a clerk to say a thing to an empty counter, and she says things to counters about four times a year and has never once got it right.

"**A board is not a form and that is the whole reason nobody asks at it.** A form is a thing that happens to a person and it is printed in about nine hundred buildings and it is filled in by somebody else and it has a box on it that a person can be put in. A board is a list of what a thing costs, and a list of what a thing costs is the friendliest piece of furniture in this empire, and it is the reason a person can stand at a rail for a minute and a half and not be ashamed, and if I put a question on it I turn the friendliest thing in this empire into the thing a woman of twenty stood in front of three times in the ninth month of the year after and could not use.

"And there is a line on the way to that which I have not seen anybody take, and I am not taking it now, and I want it said that I know what it is and that it is not a form and that it is not a post and that nobody sent me to think about it. A clerk of a notary's table can stand up in a public room and say, once, in front of about nine people, that a person may come and ask a question, and about nine hundred clerks can do that, and none of them has, and the reason none of them has is that saying it in a room is a thing a room writes down.

"So it goes on the counter and not in a room. That is the whole of where it goes."

Then she did the other thing, and it was the decision, and it is not a small one.

She took a blank out of the box and looked at the printed strip along the top where the four things are, and the strip is the same strip on every blank she has ever sold, and it takes about four seconds to cut a line into it with a knife, and she has the knife in the drawer because it is the same knife she uses for the box.

She did not cut it.

"**A question is not going on the board.** Not because I have not been asked one since the ninth month, and not because it is a good idea, and not because it would cost nothing, and not because there is no post, though there is no post.

"A person who is charged for asking will not ask. That is the whole of it and it took me about a fortnight. A shilling is on that board for a question at a notary's table and there are about nine hundred of those tables in this empire, and if the asking is on the board here then it is on the boards of all of them, and then a man with a wife and a boy stands at a rail and does the sum, and the sum is that he cannot afford to be identified. **A free answer is the only thing in this empire that leaves nobody findable.** The moment there is a price on it, the person who pays has a receipt, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for a receipt about a question."

She put the blank back in the box, and there are about four hundred of them in it and they are bought and not filled in, and the box is under the counter and the counter is the only place in this city where a person can come up, ask a question, and walk out with nothing.

"**And a question of mine that is free is the only thing I have that the office of a seat does not have, and I am not going to put a price on it and I am not going to write it down,** and I am not going to let a person in a room about four hundred and thirty miles from this counter decide that I should.

"There. That is the decision, and it is mine, and it is a decision not to do a thing that would help, and I would like somebody to put that on a slate and read it back to me, and there is nobody here who is going to, and there is no form."

---

A man of about fifty came in at about the eleventh hour and gave her four words for a copy and she made it in about a minute and a half and read it out and he said it was right and paid fourpence and went, and the board over her head had four things on it and none of them was time, and none of them was a question, and she was not going to make it one, and a man of sixty had been in a room and had been told in nine sentences that he might ask and had not asked, and about four hundred blanks sat under her hand in a box, and a woman of twenty in a city about four hundred and thirty miles up this river had a book of eleven houses in it that nobody paid her to keep, and there is no post in this empire whose job is to ask, and the count is four, and it will not happen again by itself.
