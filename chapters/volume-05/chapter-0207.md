# Chapter 207: The Fifth Time She Brought Him A Sheet

The sheet went out on the second day of the third week of the tenth month of the year after, and it went out because it had to, and it went out because she brought it to him first, and both of those are the undertaking of the fourth of the first month and neither of them is a form.

Tamsin Rook is twenty-four. She is a party to an instrument about a dead woman's name. Two instruments require her in any room he is in and she has never once merged them and he has never once merged them, and a form gets merged by being inconvenient: the first is a paper made in a form in four days with a day on it, and the second is a watch that does not name him and does not name her and requires only that a person who is not from the office that cannot be named be present. About six hundred people in this city could satisfy the watch. She has satisfied it every time it has been called for and nobody has ever paid her for it. Both instruments require her and neither of them says anything about her.

The bays have a press in them and a corridor, and the press has about six hundred printed slips in it that say the fourth line is not to be completed by this office, and there are about four hundred blanks in a box under the counter four bays along. She has a desk of her own and a hard-backed book with no office on it and her own hand in it in about nine places, and the book is not the court's and has never been in the court.

---

"Read that before it goes," she told him. "Read it in front of me. And if you have a reason against it, say the reason in a room and out loud and not on a page."

He read the four lines twice and then a third time, which she had not asked for, and then he read it a fourth time, standing up, and it took him about a minute and a half all told and he did not sit down.

It is a copy of four definitions. It is set out in ink on foolscap in a copyist's hand, which is hers, and it has a heading at the top of it that says what it is and where it came from, and it has no date on it, and it has about four lines under the heading arguing why there is no date, and it is going about six miles to a household at the end of a lane, in a hand and not in a wrapper, at fourpence a copy, and nobody is being paid to carry it because the person carrying it is walking it and has agreed to walk it.

"I have a reason," he told her.

"I know you have. Give it."

"I gave it in the second month of this year and you said no and it went anyway, and that was the arrangement, and the arrangement is mine and not yours, and I put it in a book on the fourth of the first month at about the seventh hour and told you it was worse than the rule we made." He put it down. "**The reason is that this is the first sheet in eleven weeks that says out loud what a household is inside a definition, and it is going to the one household in this matter that has a person in it who cannot read a paragraph, and the woman who keeps that house is hard to find and has been for nine years and has never once been asked anything and is never going to be, and a sheet is the cheapest way in the world to a house and there is no form that asks a house's leave.**"

"And I am going to tell you that you are right," Tamsin Rook told him. "That is the arrangement. Say the rest of it."

"The rest of it is that the woman who keeps that house is not my mother's sister and is not in this matter and has done nothing that anybody can be shown, and about four years from now a man with a copy of this page can put it on a table in front of her and say *this is about you*, and she will not be able to say one word about it, and there is no form anywhere in this empire in which she can be told first, and that is the whole of the cost and it is a small cost because she is a person who is hard to find and has been for nine years, and I am not going to pretend it is not a cost just because it is small."

"Agreed," said Tamsin Rook, and she wrote *agreed* and the day on it in her own hand under his reason, and the day is the second day of the third week of the tenth month of the year after.

"That is the fifth time the undertaking has been done," she answered, "and it is the second time you have given no reason against one word, and I am going to say the number out loud because a number a person says can be held to and I would like this one held to. **And the rule of the twelfth month of last year has not been used today and I am entering the not-using as a use, because a correct non-use is a use and it is the only kind either of us gets right.**"

---

A man came into the bays at about the ninth hour for a copy of a form at fourpence, and she had to serve him, and serving him took about two minutes, and about four hundred of those blanks are bought and filled in in a year, and the buying is done at the fourth counter four bays along, and nobody at this counter and nobody at that one has ever been asked a question by anybody.

He did not ask her anything. She has never once in this matter had a person stand at a counter she was serving and ask her a question, and they come in their hundreds in a year, and the number is not the point.

The point is that this counter is a room. A room gets a day. A person can come up to it, and be served in it, and ask in it, and walk out of it, and there is nothing on the board under the rail and no fee and no form that says what may be asked at it and none that says what may not. It is the only counter in this city where a person can come up, stand in a room with a clerk in it, and the clerk is a person and is not an office. And in the whole of the time this counter has stood nobody has asked at it, and the reason nobody has asked is not that they do not want to know. It is that there is no post, and a person who wants to know something at a counter does not come to a counter, they come to a counter to buy fourpence worth of paper, and they would be ashamed to stand there and use up a minute of a clerk's time for nothing.

"I have thought about that for about eleven days," she told the counter, after he had gone, "and the honest shape of it is that the room is there and the asking is not, and in the fifth month of this year, in a room with a rail in it, a clerk of thirty-four who works at the fourth counter four bays along was asked one question out loud, and there was that same box of blanks under that clerk's own counter at the time, and that is the only time in about a hundred and forty years the two of them were in the same place, and it was an accident, and it was because a room wanted a record."

Then she came to the other thing, and it was the reason she had actually come to the desk with.

"I have a fifth version of the sentence you did not finish. I have had it since the third week of the seventh month and I have not said it out loud and I have not written it down and I am not going to write it down, and I am telling you that it exists and that is all I am going to do about it.

"The reason I have not written it is not privacy and it is not the third line. It is that **a sentence a man has not finished, written down, is a sentence a clerk can read back to him in about four years and ask him whether he meant it, and a man asked whether he meant a thing has to say what he meant, and what he meant is a thing about two people, and there is no third person in this empire who could be made to hold a version of it that is wrong.** And so it goes in nothing, and it stays in the room, and I have said that out loud in a room to you and it is the fifth time and I am not going to improve on it and I am not going to be thanked for it, because I have asked you in advance, in the third week of the seventh month, not to thank me, and you said you were not going to, and there is no form anywhere that requires a person to be thanked and there never has been one in this matter or in any other."

"You have the two boxes in your own book," he put in. "Both of them filled. Both of them the same name."

"They are two boxes and one name in each and they are not the paper and they are not the watch and they are not this," she said. "I have not ruled a third and I am not going to, and a person who rules a box for every thing that happens to her in a year has turned a book into a form, and I would not be the first clerk in this city to do that and I would like to be the last."

---

Then she put her hand on the sheet, because it was the only object in the room that was going six miles to somebody's house, and she gave him the thing she had come to say.

"You have been asking me for about nine days who is going to ask the questions on your sheet, and I have been not answering you, and I am going to answer it now in a room and I am not going to write it down afterwards, and both of those are deliberate.

"It is not you. It is not me either, and that is the part that has taken me the whole of these nine days, because a woman of twenty-four in four bays with a book with no office on it is the cheapest person in this city to send, and I know that, and I have known it since the second month, and nobody has sent me and nobody is going to and I have made sure of that myself and I would like it written down that I made sure of it myself. You cannot be asked, because you have no office and no fund and a bar that came off on the twelfth of the ninth month of last year on a line you wrote yourself, and there is nothing anybody can instruct you about and nothing anybody can pay you for and nothing anybody can thank you for, and that is the reason you are the most dangerous person in this matter and it is also the reason nobody in it can protect you, and I have said both halves out loud in this room and I am not going to soften the second one.

"So it is going to be nobody, and here is the part that is worse than that, and it is a fact and not advice, and the third line of the office that does not exist forbids advice and the fourth says a fact is not advice, and I have said the words of both of those in this room tonight and I have never said them out loud in a room before:

"A person who asks a question in a room about a form becomes answerable for the answer. Not the person the question is about. The one who asked. A clerk of thirty-four in this city has said the number out loud twice, which is about four years, and there is a clerk of thirty-eight at a window four hundred and thirty miles down this river who wants a form for a thing she has found and cannot get one, and I have not met her and I am not going to, and wanting a form and not finding one is the worst morning there is at a window.

"**And I have said the price of being a party out loud ten times and nobody has thanked me once and I did not expect to be thanked once and I would like it written down that I did not.** And I know exactly what a room does to the person who says a thing in it. And I am four hundred and thirty miles from that bay, and I am not going to be sent, and nobody is going to ask me, and if anybody in this matter ever asks me to go and ask a question in a room on that bank, I am going to say no, and I am saying now that I will say no."

---

He did not argue with it, and she watched him not argue with it, and about nine days ago she would have found that flattering and about four hours ago she had worked out that it is the only evidence she has ever had of anybody's judgment and she has not decided what to do with it.

"Say one thing for me," she told him, "and say it in words, and I am not going to write it down and you are not to either, and if either of us writes it down then I will know that one of us is going back to being careful and I would rather have the sentence than the caution.

"**There is no post in this empire whose job is to ask. One person asking another what a word on a form means has now happened twice, and the first time was a room that wanted a record and the second time was a man who was afraid about his own household, and neither of those is anybody's plan, and two is not a plan either, and in about four years there is going to be somebody at a counter who is going to be asked what a word on a form means, and they are going to say the same thing you would say, and I want you to have heard me say it before that happens.**"

He said it. It took him about four seconds and he said it flatly, the way a man says a thing he has agreed to and is not going to improve, and then he asked her one question, and the question was not about the sheet.

"Are you going to be all right," said Marek Kest.

"No," said Tamsin Rook. "Nobody in this matter is going to be all right and I would like it noticed that I have never once been the person who said that, and I have said it now, and there are four of them in my rooms and one of them has been there since the first month of the year before last and it has not been sent and it has not been opened, and I have not thought about it and I am not going to start."

Then she carried the four lines down the corridor to the door of the bays herself, and put them in the hand of a woman who was going to walk them six miles for nothing, and did not watch her go, and shut the door, and it is not the door she does not shut on the third day of the third week of the seventh month of this year and the two are not the same door and she is not going to be told they are by anybody.
