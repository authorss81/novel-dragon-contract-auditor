# Chapter 249: A Foreman Says The One Question Nobody Has Answered, In His Own Mouth, In A Room Where Nobody Can Read It

The dye end is at the back of a house in a lane in this furnace town, and there are nine men in it on a vats day, and the end runs from about the second hour to about the seventh, and the water has been cold enough since the ninth month that the poles are stood against the wall instead of stood in, and there is no skin on it now and there was one on it in the fourth month.

He is about forty. His name is on nothing, and it has not been on anything since he was about nineteen, and he has never asked anybody why.

He is one of about four people in this matter who cannot be asked, and nobody has told him that he cannot be asked, except a woman of twenty in a room two streets off a street that goes down to the river road. She told him on a stair in about four seconds in the second day of the second week of the fourth month of the year after next, and he did not notice for most of a working life after that.

He is holding a wage-day notice with a day on it and no name on it, in the inside pocket of the coat on the hook by the door, and it has been there since the fourth day of the third week of the seventh month of the year after next, and it says a sum and it does not say against what.

**He is not going to tell anybody.** He has worked out why, and the reason is that about nine men have heard him say out loud that he cannot say what a wage is held against, and a document would let him say it without a word.

---

He said the question on the second day of the first week of the eleventh month of the year after next, at about the sixth hour, at the end of the dye end, with the third vat between him and the door, and nine men in the room.

He did not call them together. A foreman who stops the work to make a speech has begun to make a speech of his own job, and he has been the foreman off this end for about nine years and the poles are older than he is in it.

He said it to a man of about thirty first, and then to a man of about forty, and then to a man of about twenty-six who had come in, and then to a man of about fifty who was the fourth man in that end that day, and he said it four times over about half an hour in the ordinary way, one man at a time, in the middle of ordinary work, and a thing said to nine people is not a thing said to nine people. It is a thing said to the air.

And then he asked the fifth man, who was about forty-four and had been in that end for eleven years and had said four words in a week for as long as anybody could remember.

"I am not asking you a fifth time because I have said it four times and you have heard it four times and a fifth time would be me enjoying it."

"Then I will tell you what I thought of the first one." The man put his pole down. "I thought you had gone mad. And I have thought about it every day since, and I have not worked out a different answer, and I have worked out something else instead, and I am a man of forty-four and I have never once said a thing out loud in this end that was not asked for, and I have not slept properly since the first one and I have not told my wife why."

"Then you have paid for it and you did not know you were paying."

"I have paid for it and I did not know I was paying," said the man of about forty-four, "and I would like it noted that that is the whole of what a question costs in a place like this, and about four people in this town have found that out since the year turned and two of them have stopped coming to work."

---

And then he said the thing he had gone to the end of that room to say, and he said it last, and it is the reason he has not been thanked for any of the rest of it, and he is not going to be.

"A man of about thirty-four in a city about four hundred and thirty miles down this river is going to ask a foreman a question about a word on a form in a bay in about two weeks, out loud, in a room with about nine men in it, and he decided on his own and nobody sent him." He put the pole back against the wall. "I have had about six weeks to work out whether a man who cannot read a paragraph is allowed to want that, and I have worked out that he is, and that wanting it is the only part of it that is free, and that everything after the wanting costs."

"Does he know what it costs?"

"He knows it. He has worked it out in a kitchen with his wife and he has told her, on purpose, without asking her first, which is the same thing I did in this end in the fourth week of the fifth month and about nine people here have not done and about four of them have." He wiped his hands. "**And I am not going to be the man who goes up that road and stands in that bay**, and nobody is going to send me, and I have got a notice in a coat that says a sum and does not say against what, and if I stand in that bay on that day then whatever he has done stops being a thing a person did on purpose and becomes a thing two people did, and that is the one thing I am not going to do."

---

And the last thing in that end that week was a boy of about nine with a slate, who was in the doorway at about the seventh hour.

The boy had a slate and a bit of chalk and he was standing in it the way a child stands in a doorway when everybody is talking, and the foreman looked at him and did not tell him to go away, and the boy said he had written it down.

"You have written what down?"

"The office. The one that was meant to be temporary." The boy came about four steps into the room, which is further than a child of nine comes into a dye end. "**I have written down that somebody changed it, and I have not written down who**, and I have written down the day I was told it, and that is the day you said it and not the day it happened, and I have not put a name on it because you did not say a name."

Nobody in that end said anything at all.

"That is the first time in about four years that anybody has written any of it down anywhere," said the foreman, "and it is on a slate, and neither of us has a slate, and I am not going to take it off him, and there is not one form in this empire that will take it off him, and if there were one I would not fill it in, and that is the first thing I have said today that is about me."

**And nobody thanked the boy, and nobody has ever thanked the boy**, and he is not on a list of works and he is not going to be.

The slate is in his own pocket, and a slate is not a document and cannot be produced, and there is no clerk's name at the foot of it and no day in anybody's hand. It is nine foot from nine men in a room in a lane in a furnace town about four hundred and thirty miles up this river from Auremar, and that is the safest it has ever been.

---

**"If that office was meant to be temporary, then somebody changed it into an inheritable one, and I would like to know who**, and I have asked it in a shed in a different city in the fifth month of the year after next and nobody answered, and I am asking it again in my own mouth and nobody is going to answer it here either."

Nobody answered him.

A man of about thirty said that the office was a thing in a room and not a person and there was nobody to change. A man of about forty said he did not know what an office was meant to be in the first place, which was the best of the answers and it is a true one, and the foreman wrote it down in his own head and did not put it anywhere.

A man of about twenty-six said that the question was about four hundred and thirty miles away and not about a vat.

---

"That is the answer and I am not going to improve it, and I want it noticed that the four of you have now heard me say it and not one of you is going to remember it in about four years, and that is not a criticism, that is the arrangement." He put the pole back against the wall. "I have got a thing to say about that and it is not about the office, and it is the whole of why I have said this out loud four times in a room where nobody can read a paragraph."

---

"A foreman of fifty-one said this exact question out loud in a shed in the fifth month of the year after next, and every man in that shed heard it, and she was a person nobody in this matter can ask anything, and she has never been asked since and is never going to be." He wiped his hands on his coat, which is a thing you do in a dye end. "And for about four years that question has been in exactly one mouth. And a thing in one mouth is a thing one person can be asked about, and a person who can be asked about a question is a person who can be told what to do with the answer, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that stops that."

"So you have said it in nine."

"I have said it in five, and you have all of them, and a man of about thirty-four in a kitchen in a city about four hundred and thirty miles down this river has a number on the back of his hand that has gone. A foreman of fifty-one in a bay has a box she is in and about nine men who know about it and have said nothing, and now there are five mouths and none of them can be stopped and none of them can be written down." He said it without any weight at all. "**A reason that has been read out loud in a bay is a worse kind of findable than a name**, and a question that has been said out loud in five places is worse still, and I have just made this one worse on purpose, and I want it written down somewhere that I did it on purpose and I knew what it cost."

---

"A man who cannot read a paragraph is the only person in this empire you can say anything to and have it not come back," said a man of about twenty-six, who was the only one of the nine who had worked out who was in the room.

"Yes," said the foreman. "And you have just worked that out in about four seconds, and I have been in this end about nine years and I worked it out in about four years, and it is the same four seconds for everybody and the four years is the part nobody can shorten for anybody else."

---

Then he said the other thing, and it was about a book, and it is the part of him that nobody has ever thanked and that nobody in this empire will ever pay for.

"In the sixth year of a guild's blank list there was no list of works at all in this district, and the whole trade ran for a season on what people were told at the gate, and I was the only person at that end who wrote anything down, in a book of my own. I threw it out afterwards, and I have never regretted throwing it out and I have thought about it about four times since the second day of the second week of the fourth month of the year after next."

"Why is that worth saying to nine men who cannot read a paragraph?"

"Because of the next part. **Nobody in this empire pays a man to hold a list, and every single person in this empire turns out to need one, and those two facts have never once been in the same room**, and I have been in both of the rooms and I have been the one holding the list in each of them, and I am about forty and I have done it for nothing twice."

---

The man of about thirty asked him about the notice in the coat, because it is in a coat on a hook by a door and a man who says a true thing to nine people gets asked the next true thing by somebody.

"You are not going to tell me what is on it."

"No."

"Everybody else has said no to me once and every one of them gave me a reason."

"I am not giving you a reason, because a reason is a thing a man can be asked to give again, and I have given this one to nobody, and I would like it noticed that a woman of twenty told me in a rented room in the second week of the third month of the year after next to write it. Keep it and not send it, and I have not sent it, and she made that reason up in about four seconds and told me it was not a good one." He moved the coat on the hook about two inches. "**And the day I want it to exist, I will send it, and on that day it stops being a thing I have thought and starts being a thing that has happened, and I will not be able to take it back**, and that is the only reason anybody in this matter has ever had for writing one down."

---

He did not ask the other half of it, and that was on purpose, and about four people in this empire know there is an other half and not one of them is in this room.

He had worked out in about a minute and a half, standing at the third vat, that a man who has been asked nothing for forty years does not get to choose the shape of the one question he is going to spend, and that if he asked the whole of it he would be asking two things in a room where nine men cannot read either of them, and a question asked in halves by a man nobody can ask is a question with his fingerprints on it.

So he asked the half about the office, and he left the other one, and he did not say out loud that he was leaving it, and the only person who will ever know that he knew there was one is a man of about twenty-six who looked at him for about four seconds and then went back to his vat.

---

**The river is still running.** A second season was shut and a third one is open and nobody agreed to either. About nineteen people said no again in the last week of the seventh month of the year after with the same nineteen reasons, and they are behind a bank about four hundred and thirty miles down this river and they have not been asked again and they are not going to be. A guarantee stands offered and unanswered on about four hundred and forty foot of bank with about sixty children under sixteen inside it. A bill at Lowcross is nineteen pounds three and fourpence, unpaid, nobody liable, and there has not been a line for a bridge in that fund in nineteen years, and nobody in this room is going to pay it.

**And a woman of twenty in a room two streets off a street that goes down to the river road has sixteen books** and a wage that has been held since the second week of the third month of the year after, and she has decided to go and tell four women that she is not paid. She is not going up this season and has not said she is never going to, and there is a difference between not going and saying she is not going and she is the only person who knows there is a difference.

**And a foreman of about forty in this end has a notice in a coat and has said one true thing out loud four times over to nine men who cannot read a paragraph and is not thanked and is not going to be.**

A man of about thirty-four is going to ask a question about a word on a form in a bay in about two weeks, and nobody sent him, and the lamp was not lit in either building, and the question that is the largest thing anybody has said out loud in this matter is now in five mouths and in no book at all.
