# Chapter 211: A Man Reads It Aloud And Nothing Goes In A Book

The season was shut on the fourth day of the last week of the eleventh month and this was the second day of the first week of the twelfth, and there was not much to do, and that is when a man of about fifty-five came to the end of the bench with a printed slip in his hand and a question about it.

Rennick Adley is thirty-eight. He has been keeping the books of the Slade Cut Company in a hired stone store off the Slade since the second month of next year. The Company's whole money is four shillings a year and all of it is the rent of the building. He is not a clerk. He can count and he has a fair hand, and the book he keeps has no heading at the top of it and a name at the foot of every line of a list of works.

He did the ordinary things first, because they are the ordinary things and they are on a day, and because a book-keeper who stops doing them while a man is standing at the end of the bench is a book-keeper who has begun to make something of an ordinary morning.

The count of the works, which takes about four minutes. The water, which was down about a foot and going down. The hold, which is against a company whose whole money is four shillings a year and which had not moved since the fourth week of the fourth month. And the third column of the ledger, which has nineteen reasons in it with a day against every one of them, and which he read out in the bay in the ordinary way when the bay filled, and the second reading was slower, and it is his own rule and nobody decided it.

The factor's letter of four lines was on the bench where it has been since the second week of the third month. It wants the name of a person who may be served and that person's consent, and it says without both it cannot proceed and will not proceed, and the matter will stand against the works. **It is not going to be answered, and every day it is not answered is a day nobody has been entered, and he knows what a day of that is worth and he is not going to be the one who spends it.**

---

The slip was a district's, printed, and about half a sheet, and it had a heading across the top of it in a bigger hand than the rest, and a day under the heading, and underneath that four lines and a line for a name and a space for an officer's hand.

"It is off the water," said the man. "That is what the man at the door said. I want to know if it is my four."

He has been on that cut about thirty-three years. He came the year after a foreman of fifty-one and has never once said so, and he is one of the about four people in that bay who cannot read a paragraph, and Rennick Adley has known which four since before the man came in and has never said a word about it to anybody.

"You have got the whole of it in your hand."

"I have got the whole of it in my hand and I cannot tell whether the four is mine or the next one along, and I am not going to put my name under it until somebody has told me what is on it."

---

He could have said no.

He thought about it standing up, with the slip in his other hand, and the bay had about nine men in it and the lamp was not lit because it is not lit until about the seventh hour and it was the ninth, and most of those men had come in out of the wet and were standing about.

**A man who has been read to in a bay is a man who has been seen.** That was the whole of the thought and it took him about four seconds and it is the reason he got up off the bench, and he did not give the reason out loud, because the reason is that a foreman of fifty-one decided in the second week of the eleventh month, in a shed, in front of nine men, that she was not going to be the reason four other people were found, and a book-keeper who reads a slip out loud in a bay in front of nine men is doing to four men at once what she refused to do to one.

So he walked out of the bay into the yard with the man, and the man came after him, and two other men came out because you cannot stop a yard from having people in it.

"The heading," said Rennick Adley. "Read me the heading first and then I will read you the rest and you can stop me anywhere you like."

---

The heading was four words and he read it, and then he read the four lines under it, and it took him about a minute and a half, and the man stopped him twice, both times at a figure, both times to have the figure said again more slowly, and the second time he said it slower, and that is a rule he has and it is not the rule about the reasons and it came from the same place.

It is a notice that the water at four houses is to be shut from the first hour to the last hour of one day, and a night, for the cleaning of a main, and that any house without stored water will be classed as a complaint and that a complaint has to be made in writing by the person of the house, and that the district cannot do it for anybody.

**And there is no box on it anywhere, and the person of the house is a person, and not a household and not a trade, and somebody in a room in a building about four hundred and thirty miles from this yard has written that down on purpose, which is more than a form has ever done, and this man cannot read a word of it and did not know it was a favour.**

"That is mine," said the man. "The third and the fourth. Not the first two, they are on the main already."

"Then write your name on the line."

"I cannot."

He said it the way a man says a thing he has known since he was about nine, and Rennick Adley put the slip on the flat of the bench in the yard and put his own hand flat on it beside it and did not say anything for about a minute.

Then he said, "I can put it down and you can make a mark under it, and I will read the mark back to you so that you know which one it is, and I will not write anything else and there will be no book."

---

He did that, and it took about nine seconds, and the man made a mark with a bit of chalk off the yard wall, and Rennick Adley read the mark back to him out loud, twice, and the second time was slower.

Then the man said, "You are not going to write it down."

"No."

"I asked you to read it and I have made a mark on it and both of those have happened and neither of them is going anywhere."

"Neither of them is going anywhere and that is the correct arrangement and I would like it noticed that I worked it out in about four seconds and that the four seconds were about you and not about the works."

---

Then he said the thing he had been carrying about the yard since the man sat down, and he said it to the man and not to the two other men, and one of the two other men heard it and has not repeated it and is not going to be asked.

"**Nobody asked anybody what a word on a form means, and I want that said, and I want it said by me and not by a clerk, and I am the wrong man to say it and I am saying it anyway.**

"A man came to the end of my bench with a slip in his hand and asked me to read him his own paper. That is not a question about a word on a form. That is a man asking a man to read him a thing that is his. It is not the first one and it is not going to be the four hundred and fortieth and it is not one of the three and I am not going to let it be counted as one of the three, because a count of what has been asked about forms is a count of people and that is the only thing that count is, and a man asking a man to read him his own water is not a person who has been asked what a word means.

"**And it is the only one of the three that has ever been done, and it is the only one of the three that has cost nobody a thing.**

"For about four hundred and forty foot of this bank there is a hold on the wages of about forty-one people, and a guarantee standing offered and unanswered over it with about sixty children under sixteen inside it, and a bill at Lowcross at nineteen pounds three and fourpence that nobody is liable for and no line for a bridge in that fund in nineteen years. And none of that can be given to four men who cannot read a paragraph. I have worked that out and I wrote it down in the second week of the tenth month and read it back twice and the finding was that there is a girl of seventeen at the back of this bay on a written engagement and a person who is paid to read is a person who can be told to stop, and I am not going to say that again, and I am not going to improve it, and nobody in this yard is going to be thanked for anything tonight.

"**And there is this. A man asks a man to read him his own paper and the paper goes back in his own coat and no name goes in a book and there is no day on anything and nobody is answerable for it and it is the only thing I have ever found that gets to a person without a person being put anywhere.**"

---

Then the man said the thing that Rennick Adley is going to be thinking about for the rest of the season.

"You are not going to do that again, are you."

And the book-keeper of thirty-eight stood in a yard in the wet with a bit of chalk in his hand and worked out the answer in about nine seconds, and it is the decision he made that day and it is his and it was not anybody else's, and he read it back twice before he said it.

"I am going to do it again," he said. "**I am not going to write it down and I am not going to tell the foreman and I am going to do it again, and there are three things in that and I will take them one at a time.**

"I am not going to write it down because if I write it down then in about four years a man with a copy of the right page can come into this building and ask me which men I read to and on what days and in what words, and I will have to say it, and the man will have a day against his name on a page in a book that keeps the Company's works, and that is how a person who cannot read a paragraph gets found, and nobody has to mean it. **A thing that is written down does not need anybody to want you found. It only needs somebody to go looking.**

"And I am not going to tell the foreman, and that is the harder one and I have been about nine minutes on it. She decided in the shed in the second week of the eleventh month that she was not going to be the reason four other people are found, and she gave the reason out loud and then said the reason was a bad one, and she was right that it was a bad one. **A foreman who knew would be a foreman somebody could ask.** That is the whole of it. If she knows that I read papers to men in that bay then she is a person on this bank who knows a thing about four men in that bay, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that she could be asked under, and in about four years somebody would ask her, and she would not be able to say she did not know. And I am not going to be the reason there is a person on that bank who knows something and cannot say she does not.

"So she does not know and I am not going to tell her, and if she ever finds out that I have been doing this for a year I would like it entered that I did not tell her and did not write it down and that a book-keeper of this cut has said out loud in a yard that he was not going to tell her."

"And the third thing is the one I am worst at. **A thing that is written down is the only thing anybody can be held to and a thing that is not written down is the only thing that costs nobody anything, and the whole of this matter from the ninth month of this year has been a thing that was written down, and I have worked out this week that the instrument that gets to a person without costing anybody is the one that leaves no mark, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for a thing that leaves no mark, and I am not going to try to invent one, and I would like it noticed that I noticed.**"

---

The man put the slip back inside his coat and the chalk back against the wall and neither of them said thank you and neither of them was thanked, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be thanked, and Rennick Adley has not asked for it and is not going to.

He went back into the bay and did the count of the works again, because he had not finished it, and about nine men were in there and the lamp was not lit and the door of the store was open and the factor's letter was on the bench.

Nobody asked him what he had gone out for, and most of them would have asked him if he had been a different sort of man, and he is not that sort of man, and that is the whole of what he has got and it is not nothing.

---

Then the man came back into the bay, which Rennick Adley had not expected him to do, and stood at the end of the bench again with his coat done up, and said the thing he had been working at in the yard.

"Am I one of the nineteen."

It took about four minutes to find out and it took four minutes because the answer is not in the book he is sitting in front of.

The names are not in this book. The names are in a different book with about nine hundred and forty of them in nine hands over about sixty years and a day against those too, and it is on a shelf behind him and it is about four inches thick, and the third column of the ledger on the bench is nineteen reasons with a day against every one of them and no names in it at all, because a name in a room is a thing that can be carried out of a room.

He did not read the name out. He put his own thumb on the line and read it to himself and then said, "No, and you never have been."

"Never?"

"**You have never refused anything, because nobody in about thirty-three years has asked you whether you would, and a person who has not been asked has not said no and has not said yes and is not on either list.** The nineteen on that bench are nineteen people who were asked out loud, in this bay, on a day, one after another, and every one of them gave a reason and every one of the reasons went in that column with the day against it. The four of you have never been asked and so there is no reason of yours in that column and there never will be one, and a column for you would be a column somebody would have to fill, and nobody has appointed anybody, and there is no post in this empire whose job is to be appointed."

"So I am nothing," said the man. "I am not a no."

"You are not a no and you are not a yes," said Rennick Adley, "**and that is a third thing and it has no word and it is the only one of the three that nobody in this matter has ever been in.**

"And I am not writing that down either, and I want it noticed that I have just read a name out of a book to a man in order to tell him what he was not, and that is the one thing I have done in about four minutes today that I would not be able to do again, and I have not got a rule for it and I am not going to make one up at the end of a morning."
