# Chapter 216: The Thing He Wrote Down In Order To Be Refused

He could not sleep much on the night of the second day of the first week of the first month of the year after next and that was nothing unusual, and on the fourth day of the second week he wrote a thing down and put a day against it, and he did it in about nine seconds and he had been about four days deciding to, and the whole of the four days was the question of whether doing it was fair to the woman.

Marek Kest is thirty-eight. He has a rented room with a table in it and a book with no office on it and about four feet of a wall he does not use. He has no office and no fund and no register and nothing in this empire he could certify anything with, and a bar of his own that came off him on the twelfth of the ninth month of last year on an application of one line which he wrote himself. There is a line about two inches long on the inside of his left wrist and it reads nothing, and it decides nothing, and it does not know where the ford is.

Tamsin Rook is twenty-four and she was in the room because two instruments require it and they are not one instrument, and a form gets merged by being inconvenient.

---

"What I am going to ask you to do is a thing I have done before," he said, "and you are going to answer it in a room, in words, with a date, and it is the rule of the twelfth month of last year and it is not a form and it is not on your office and it is the only instrument either of us owns, and I am going to put it in writing with a day on it and I am not going to write the day against the line."

He wrote it out and turned the book round.

> *Marek Kest's choice, the fourth day of the second week of the first month of the year after next. Either I write down what I said to a woman in this room on the second day of the first week of this month, and stand in front of whoever comes and be asked what I meant by it, or I do not. If I write it down then it is a document and I am a person with a document, and in about four years a man with a copy of the right page can put it beside the return that made her and ask me why a man with no office answered a question about a box. — M.K.*

---

She read it twice and put her pen down on the table beside it, which is what she did the last time, and he has thought about that twice since.

"My answer is no, and the date is today, and I am going to give you the reason and then I am not going to discuss it."

"Give me the reason."

"It is a fact and it is not advice, and the third line of the office that does not exist forbids advice and the fourth says a fact is not advice."

"Go on."

"**A document with your hand at the foot of it does not stay about you. That is the whole of what I have been trying to tell you since the second month and you have not believed a word of it.** In about four years there are two of you in the same thing. A sheet of a man's hand saying what he said to a woman about a word on a box, and a return with a column filled in at the other end with a woman's name in it that a clerk filled in in about nine seconds in the fifth month of the year after. And a man with a copy of the right page does not have to know anything about either of them. He has a date on one and he has a name on the other and that is a matter, and you would be in it, and she would be in it, and she is twenty and she has been in a box since the fifth month of the year after and she does not know that she is in this.

"And you did not know either, and that is the part I have been about four days on, and it is why I think the answer is no and not because I want to be difficult. **If you write that down she is findable from a page of a man with no office, and you would be doing to her the identical thing that was done to her in the fifth month, in about nine seconds, by a person who was not a villain and who is sitting at this table.**"

---

He did not argue with it, and she watched him not argue with it, and about four hours after that she would work out for the second time in three days that it is the only evidence she has ever had of anybody's judgment and she had still not decided what to do with it.

"That is a good reason," he said.

"It is a fact. And you have asked me this once before and I refused you in the second week of the ninth month of the year after, and you wrote that one in order to be refused as well, and you told me so afterwards, and it is the only reason I have believed a document you wrote."

"So I have written two things down in a book with no office on it in order to be refused, and I have had two real answers, and there is no office to appeal to."

"There is no post in this empire whose business it is to ask whether I was right," said Tamsin Rook. "That is not a complaint about you."

---

Then he took his left hand off the table and looked at the inside of the wrist at a line about two inches long, in a room with about four feet of a wall he does not use.

"It has not told me anything," he said. "It told me nothing in the fourth week of the tenth month of the year after and it told me nothing in the fourth hour of the second of the fifth month of next year when it was up for about nine seconds, and it does not know what the word on that box means, and it is not going to, and I want that written down in a room and not on a page."

"**Then it goes in a room and not on a page, and I am not going to thank you for telling me that you have nothing, because there is no form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be thanked and I have asked you in advance not to and you are not going to.**"

---

Then he said the thing he had come to say, and it was the third thing, and it was the one he had been about four days on, and it was not about the paper.

"I am not going to go and tell her."

She waited. She is twenty-four and she does not fill a silence for anybody.

"**A woman of twenty walked about nine miles and asked me a question and I could not answer it and I have been answerable for nine days for a thing I do not know, and I have spent those nine days working out what I owe her, and the answer is nothing, because there is no fund and there is no form for a fund and I have got about a fortnight's money in a drawer and a book with no office on it.** I cannot pay her. I cannot buy her a copy of a form at fourpence and I cannot pay her a shilling for a question and I cannot send her four hundred and thirty miles with a carriage and there is no instrument anywhere in this empire for any of it, and there is nothing anybody can instruct me about and nothing anybody can pay me for, and I said that out loud in this room in the third week of the tenth month of the year after and I am not going to improve it by finding a way to be generous with a person who cannot be protected.

"And I am not going to go and tell her what she is answerable for, because **in the second week of the eleventh month she decided to be the person who knows how the box got filled and not to be a person who knows it alone, and the minute I walk about nine miles and tell her what she has become, she has a person to hand it to, and that is the end of the thing she decided and it would be me ending it for her.**"

"She has three other people."

"She has two other people and one of them is four hundred and thirty miles up this river with a board and a basket and no fund, and the other is four hundred and thirty miles down it at a window, and I have not met either of them and I am not going to, and about four years from now she is going to find out that she chose well or badly and I am not going to be what finds out."

---

"You could stand in the room where the boxes get filled," said Tamsin Rook, not as advice and not as a suggestion. "Nine miles. It has a door on it and a window about two feet wide and a clerk at a table every day of her life."

"No," said Marek Kest.

"That was fast."

"**I have been thinking about it for four days and I have got a reason and it is yours.** Being served is a party. A party is the only person who can be told what a thing is going to be used for, and I do not know what that thing is going to be used for, and a man with no office and no register who walks into a building off the old river road and asks a clerk what a box is for is asking to be served, and about four years later a man with a copy of the right page will be able to say that he came asking, and there is no way in this empire to come out of a party once you are in it.

"And I have said that the twelfth line of my sheet is going to stay empty because there is no post whose job is to ask, and I am not going to be the man who walks into a building to find out whether there is one."

---

"That is consistent," said Tamsin Rook. "It is also the first decision you have made in about two years that you did not make in order to be caught at it, and I have not got a rule about what to do with that, and I am not going to invent one at about the seventh hour on a day when I have been asked a question I cannot answer."

She picked up the sheet with the choice on it, read it once more, and then she did a thing she has done about nine times in two years and has not told anybody about except in a clause in a sentence that did not act on it.

She put it in the book with no office on it, at the back, after the four definitions, with the day on it, and shut the book.

"That is the rule of the twelfth month used once, in writing, with a day on it, and the answer was no, and the answer was given in about a minute by a person who had already thought about it, and you did not argue, and it is the second time."

"The second time I have written a thing down in order to be refused."

"And I have not said a word about the twelve questions on your sheet and I am not going to, and the twelfth line is still empty, and I would like it read back to me now, in this room, in words, whether you think that is cowardice."

"It is not," said Tamsin Rook, "and it is the second time I have said that to you and I am not going to be thanked for saying it, and the twelfth line is still empty, and I have not ruled a third box and I am not going to."

She was gone for about six minutes, and she was gone because of the paper and not because of the watch, and he said which one it was out loud to an empty room, because a form gets merged by being inconvenient and he had been letting two instruments get merged in his own head for about four days and it took her six minutes going out of the door to be honest about it.

A paper made in a form in four days with a day on it. The watch does not send anybody anywhere. He said that to the table and then he did not say anything else for six minutes, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that requires a man to account for six minutes to himself and he gave the account anyway.

Then he opened the book with no office on it.

There were eleven lines in it with a day against the sheet and not against the lines, and a twelfth with nothing on it, and he had not read the eleventh line out loud to anybody since the third week of the ninth month of the year after and the eleventh line is the one about what a person of twenty has been put into, and he wrote it in the third week of the ninth month of the year after, and she read it in that room and let him, and neither of them said anything about it for about a minute.

A person of twenty had been put into a box on the fourth day of the third week of the fifth month of the year after and had walked about four miles to a desk in the second week of the eleventh month and asked how it got filled, and had walked about nine miles to a room in the first week of this month and asked what the word on the box meant, and her wage was held and there was no fund and no form for a fund and she had four people to ask and two of them she had asked.

**The eleventh line had stopped being a question. It had become a fact, and a fact on a line in a book with no office on it is the worst of the two, because a question can be answered and a fact can only be repeated.**

He read it to himself once, out loud, in about four seconds, and then he shut the book with the twelfth line still empty, and when she came back in with the second order under her arm he said:

"I did not read you the eleventh line while you were out."

"I know you did not. I heard you not read it through the door, and I am not going to ask you why and I would like it entered that I did not ask."

"Because you would be asking me to say a thing about a woman of twenty out loud in a room, and the only person in the room who could contradict me is her, and she is not in the room, and I would like it entered that I noticed that too and that I still did not read it."

He looked at the two of them for a moment — the page with eleven lines and a day against the sheet, and the empty one, and the second choice at the back with her writing on it in ink because she has never been able to leave a thing unsigned in her own book.

"Four," he said.

"Four," said Tamsin Rook. "**Four in about a hundred and forty years and no post and no plan, and the first three are all months back — a court in the fifth month, a window in the first week of the tenth, a desk in the second week of the eleventh — and not one of them is a year old yet, and the fourth is nine days old, and the third was the first one anybody did on purpose.** I am not going to be one of the three of us who is standing in a room saying it will not happen again by itself while the one person it happened to has her wage held about four hundred and thirty miles from here."
