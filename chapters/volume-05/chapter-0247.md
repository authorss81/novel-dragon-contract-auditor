# Chapter 247: Four Hundred Blanks In A Box Under A Counter, And The Only Person Nobody Has Ever Asked Anything

Marn Ottery is thirty-four and she has been a clerk eleven years at the fourth counter, and she is not a lawyer and does not want to be one, and about nine people a year come up those stairs and find out that a clerk is a person who can be asked things.

She says so at the counter, before anybody asks her. **A foreman can be sent for and a clerk cannot, and the difference is about nine days of somebody else's wage** and a great deal worse than that, and she has said it at that counter about four hundred times and has never once been thanked for saying it.

---

There are about four hundred bought and unfilled blanks in a box under her counter, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that says she may keep them, and she keeps them, and nobody has ever asked her to account for them, and that is the one piece of administration in eleven years that has gone entirely unnoticed.

**A form bought and not filled in is fourpence of proof that the question was asked.** That is what the box is for, and it is the closest thing to a post in this empire and it is not a post. It is a box under a counter, and about four hundred people a year buy one and walk out with a piece of paper that says they asked something, and there is no heading over it and there is no register and there is no clerk's name at the foot of it.

---

Then a woman of about forty came up the stairs at about the fifth hour with a paper in her hand and wanted to know whether her father's name was on it, and Marn Ottery read the paper, which took about nine minutes, and told her that it was, and that it was in a fair hand and not the hand of the man whose name it was, and that there was no form anywhere in this empire that said who writes a name in a column or why.

"Can I have a copy?"

"Fourpence."

"I have got fourpence." She put it on the counter and then did not pick up the copy, and stood there. "Then can I ask you something about the name, and not about the paper?"

"You can ask."

"You are the first person in this building who has said that to me in about nine years and I want you to know it cost me two months to get here and a day of my father's wage to stand in this stair, and nobody sent me and nobody told me I could." She was not crying and she was steady, which is worse. "**He has been on a list of works for about four seasons and I have never been able to find out whether he is on it**, and I have asked a foreman once and a clerk once and a man in a lane, and all three of them were kind to me and not one of them told me, and I have worked out that kindness is not a reason and I would like to know the reason."

---

Marn Ottery did not answer that in about nine seconds, and she answered it properly, and it is the sentence she has said at that counter about four hundred times and has never once said to a person who was asking for herself.

"A name in a column is a thing a person can be served at," she said. "That is the reason, and it is the whole of the reason, and I am not going to soften it and I am not going to charge you for it, and I have said it to about four hundred people at this counter and not one of them has ever said thank you for it, because it is not a kindness and it does not read like one and I have stopped trying to make it read like one."

"So I will not be told."

"You will be told in about four years, or you will not, and neither of those is in my hands, and I have been at this counter eleven years and I have never been able to make it be in anybody's hands." She put the coin back on the counter rather than taking it, which is not a thing a clerk does, and the woman of about forty picked it up and went down the stair with a copy and an answer and no comfort.

---

She works out, about twice a year, what that actually is, and she has never said it to anybody in eleven years.

"It is a form that cannot be asked anything," she said, to the box, on the fourth day of the third week of the tenth month of the year after next. "**Every other piece of paper in this building is a document**, and a document is the only thing anybody in this empire can be held to, and a blank is not a document, because a blank has nothing on it and a thing with nothing on it cannot be produced against a person. A person can walk into a room with a blank in his hand and a person cannot walk into a room with a form filled in and say he did not fill it in, and that is the whole of what is in this box, and it is worth about nine hundred blanks' worth of nothing and I have never had it explained to me by anybody."

---

The counter is open from the second hour to the sixth and there is a fee board on the wall behind it with four things written on it and none of the four is time.

A copy is fourpence. A question of hers, at her own table, is a shilling, and she has not done one of her own in about a year. A certificate is four shillings and she has never issued one and there is no form in this empire that requires her to issue one and there has not been one since she came.

The fourth is a line of writing she has never worked out what it is for, and she has asked two clerks about it and both of them said they did not know either, and one of those two clerks is a woman of twenty-four who has been at this counter two years and who has never once been asked a question by anybody who was not a person who wanted something out of her.

---

That woman is Tamsin Rook, and she has two boxes on a page ruled by hand in a book of her own, and both of the boxes have the same name in them, and she has not ruled a third and is not going to.

There are about nine people a day up those stairs, and there has been one of them coming for about a year who asked her one thing in the first week he came and has not asked her anything since, and she has worked out that he is not shy, because a shy man does not come nine times.

"He came in the last week of the ninth month of the year after and he asked me a question about a word on a form, and I said the price at him twice because he had not caught the first one, and it is the only time in about a year that anybody has asked me anything, and it is a day I have written down and a day I am not going to talk about." She squared a stack of copies that did not need squaring. "I do not know whether it counts. I have got no book here that says, and there is nobody I could ask without asking a person to look themselves up. **And a clerk who cannot say a number is not going to say a number.** He is not in this matter so far as I have been able to find out. He is a person who came up a stair and I have thought about him about four times a week since and he has never come back and I am not going to make him."

---

And there was one on the fourth day of the third week of the tenth month of the year after next, and Marn Ottery asked her one question, and she said it out loud before she asked it, which is a thing she has done about nine hundred times and which is the only reason a clerk of eleven years is still standing at that counter.

"I am going to ask you a question and I want the shape of it said before I ask it, and then I am going to ask it."

"Go on."

"**It is not a question about a word on a form.** A board is not a form and a line of writing on a board is not a word on a form, and I have thought about that for about four days and I am going to ask it anyway. I want it noticed that I thought about it, because I have watched about nine people in this city make a thing out of a habit in my time at this counter and every one of them ended up with a heading over it."

---

Tamsin Rook put her pen down.

"Ask it."

"What is the fourth thing on that board for?"

---

She did not answer for about a minute and a half, and Marn Ottery did not help her, because a clerk who helps is a clerk who has already decided what the answer is.

"I do not know," said Tamsin Rook.

"That is what you told me before, in the second week of the seventh month of the year after, and you told me before you told the other one, and I have never asked either of you again because I have never asked anybody anything in eleven years and that is not a complaint, it is a fact about me and I have made my peace with it in about four minutes."

"Then that is the fourth time somebody in this building has been told, and there are about four people here, and I am one of them and I have said no." She looked at the board. "**And you have just asked me a question, and nobody has asked me one since the last week of the ninth month of the year after**, and you did it in eleven words and you did not charge me a shilling for it, and I have been waiting for somebody to do that every working day since about the seventh month of next year and there is not one form in this empire for a person to be asked."

---

"That is not a reason and you know it is not a reason."

"It is the reason I have not been able to work out what the fourth line is for for about a year, and I have been at that board every working day of that year, and I have asked two people and both of them have told me the truth, and about nine hundred blanks have gone through that box and not one person has ever come up that stair and asked me what the fourth line is for." She put her hands flat on the counter. "**A person who is not asked has not been given a duty, and I have known that since about the seventh month of next year, and it has never once applied to me, and I did not know that until about four minutes ago.**"

---

"Then I am going to say the other half of it and I have not said it to anybody in eleven years."

"Say it."

"I have kept about four hundred blanks in that box for eleven years, and the reason a Table can do that and nothing else in this empire can do it is that **a Table is the only place where a person can come up, ask a question, and walk away with a piece of paper that says they asked it.** No post in this empire has that as its job. There is no appointment and there is no clerk's name and there is no register and there is no heading over it and there is not one form that requires a person to do it, and about four hundred times a year somebody does it and nobody decided anything and nobody can be told to stop." She moved the box four inches to the left and back again. "That is the whole of what there is in this empire and it is a box under a counter and it is not a post and it will not become one, and if it became one it would stop on the day the first person with a form wanted it to."

---

"You have just said the thing this whole matter is standing on and you said it to a clerk of two years at about the sixth hour on a wet day."

"I have said it to the box about four times and to you once, and you are the only person I have said it to, and I would like it written down that I did not expect to be thanked, because in about four years somebody is going to read that a clerk of eleven years said a true thing out loud and assume somebody heard it." Nobody heard it. "**And I have been on the wrong side of that counter for eleven years** and I have watched about nine people in this city be right, and I have never once been asked, and I have not said one word about that to anybody until this week."

---

Then a man of about forty came for a copy and paid fourpence and it took her about eleven minutes and she read it back to him twice and he read it back once and said thank you, and she said that that was the ordinary way and not a favour.

And a woman of about thirty came and asked whether a copy would stand if the paper it came from was lost, and Tamsin Rook said no, and did not soften it, and did not charge her, and the woman went away with the answer and without a copy.

And a woman of about sixty-three came up the stairs, went past the counter without stopping, and went down again, and the younger of the two clerks said afterwards that she had done that on four separate days and that neither of them had ever gone after her, and that a person who walks past a counter is a person who has not come to a counter, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that says a clerk goes after anybody.

And a boy of about fifteen came up and asked whether he could buy a blank and leave it, and Marn Ottery said he could buy as many as he liked and there was nothing at all to stop him, and he said his master had told him he had to hand it in filled, and she said then your master is wrong. There is not one form anywhere in this empire that says a filled blank has to be handed in, and the boy stood in the stair for about a minute and then bought four of them and left, and she has not worked out since whether she did him a favour, and has decided that is a question for somebody who is not going to answer it.

The counter shut at the sixth hour and Marn Ottery put the box back under the counter and did not go up a stair and did not send anything.

**And there are four people in this empire who have asked a person out loud what a word on a form means**, and the third of the four was the first one anybody did on purpose, and about nine hundred and forty people have been made answerable by a court that sat in nineteen years. There is no post anywhere in this empire whose job is to ask, and a box under a counter with about four hundred blanks in it is the nearest thing to one that anybody has ever built, and nobody appointed it and nobody is going to.
