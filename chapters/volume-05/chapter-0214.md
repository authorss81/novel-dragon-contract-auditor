# Chapter 214: The Reason She Gave To A Man With A Satchel

He went up the road on the fourth day of the fourth week of the twelfth month of the year after, with the ledger under his arm, because the count of the works was due and the water was due and there was nothing else in a closed season, and it took him about half an hour at the pace he walks.

Rennick Adley is thirty-eight and he is not a clerk. He has been keeping the books of the Slade Cut Company in a hired stone store off the Slade since the second month of next year, and the Company's whole money is four shillings a year and all of it is the rent of the building, and there are about nineteen reasons in the third column of the ledger with a day against every one of them and no names in that column at all.

The shed is about nine foot by eleven, up the road and not in the store, and there is a bench along the north wall that four men can sit on, and a lamp in it that is not lit until about the seventh hour, and it was the third hour of the afternoon.

---

She was doing the ordinary things and he let her do them, because he has worked out since the ninth month that a foreman who is interrupted in the first two minutes of a visit does not get to the fourth minute, and the fourth minute is where anything happens.

The count of the works, out loud, in the shed, to the men in it, which takes about four minutes. The water, which was down and holding. The hold, which is against a company whose whole money is four shillings a year. The factor's letter, four lines, on a bench up the road, wanting a person who may be served and that person's consent, **not going to be answered, and every day it is not answered is a day nobody has been entered.**

He read the nineteen reasons out, in the ordinary way, in about four minutes, and not the names, and the second reading was slower.

"They said it in the seventh month and they have said nothing since, and that is correct, and nobody is going to be asked again until the season comes round. A person is entitled to make a refusal once and have it kept and not be troubled about it again, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that entitles anybody to be troubled twice."

Before the satchel came in, a man of about fifty-five was at the bench with a drum between his knees taking a third link out of a chain with a nail, because the smith is at Lowcross and Lowcross is a bridge and the bridge is shut, and about nine men in that shed watched him do it and none of them helped him and none of them thought to.

A man of about forty from the third gang asked him a question about the water, an ordinary question, standing where he was, and the man of fifty-five looked at the bench for about two seconds, and Halla Wray answered it from behind him without turning round and in about four seconds flat, in a foreman's voice, in the plainest words there are, and the man of about forty accepted it and went back to what he was doing, and nobody in that shed noticed anything at all, and that is what a foreman is.

Rennick Adley noticed all of it. He stood against the north wall with the ledger under his arm and he did not understand what he had seen for about two hours afterwards and he has not entirely understood it since, and the reason he has never got the rest of it is that he wrote nothing down about it then and wrote nothing down about it afterwards, and a thing nobody writes down is the only kind there is that he has ever been able to keep.

He had been in the shed about a quarter of an hour when the satchel came in.

---

He is about thirty and he is from a district works and he had come about the list for the coming season, and he had a satchel with papers in it and a way of standing in a shed that said he had been in a great many of them that month.

"What I have to know," he said, "is who I enter."

"That depends what you want to enter him for."

"That is the whole of what I have come for. I have a column and a day and a column is a column, and last season the column came back with a household in it and I could not put a household on a list of works, and I want to know what a foreman puts in a column."

"I put what the man does," said Halla Wray. "That is the answer and you can write it down."

"And there are four of yours that do not come to the shed and that I cannot ask, and I have been told that in about four minutes by a man in a store and not by a person, and I am asking the person."

---

The shed went quiet for about a minute and a half, and **Rennick Adley stood against the north wall with the ledger under his arm and did not move,** and there are about nine men in that shed and not one of them has ever heard a foreman of fifty-one be asked a question about the four by anybody at all, ever, including the four.

And she answered it, in the ordinary way, standing up, in a room, with the shed behind her, and it took her about a minute and a half, and it is the third time, and the two men in it were the first two in this matter to hear it and neither of them was the one it was for.

"Because if I ask one of them I have asked one of them," said Halla Wray, "**and then there are three, and the three will be asked by whoever comes next, because a man who has been asked can be asked again, and that is the only door anybody has ever opened on a person who cannot read a paragraph. And the fourth will be found out by arithmetic.**"

Nobody said anything. The man with the satchel wrote it down, and then he asked a second question, and the second question was not about the four.

"Before I put my column in," said the man with the satchel. "Is there anything on that bank I am going to be told about that I have not been told."

"No," said Halla Wray, "and I will tell you the two things I know of, and I am telling you them because you have a column and not because I have decided to."

"A guarantee stands offered on this bank and unanswered, on about four hundred and forty foot of it, and there are about sixty children under sixteen inside it, and no such person has ever come forward to take it up, and I have not got the terms of it in my head and I am not going to read them out in a shed at the third hour.

"And there is a bill at Lowcross at nineteen pounds three and fourpence that is unpaid and nobody is liable for it, and there has not been a line for a bridge in that fund in nineteen years, and if you have come to tell anybody that they are owed it you have come to the wrong bank and I am not going to ask you for a penny and I would ask any man in this shed not to."

"That is not my district," said the man with the satchel.

"No," said Halla Wray. "It is nobody's. That is what a bill with no line against it is."

"That is a bad reason."

"It is the reason and it is mine and I have had it since the third month," said Halla Wray. "**I have not said it to a book-keeper since the second week of the eleventh month of the year after and I have just said it to a man with a satchel who had not been in the room when I decided not to say it, and I would like it noticed that the only way a sentence of mine comes out of my mouth is when somebody asks me a question about the works.**"

---

Rennick Adley heard it.

He has wanted that sentence since the third month of this year. He has wanted it the way a man wants a thing he has no standing to ask for, and he had told a foreman of fifty-one in the seventh month out loud in front of about nine men that the day he asked was the day she stopped saying the half she had got, and she had heard him, and he had not asked, and he was not going to.

And **it came out in a shed on the fourth day of the fourth week of the twelfth month, out loud, in a room, aimed at a man with a satchel who would not remember it in a week,** and it went to a stranger.

He stood there for about a minute with the ledger under his arm and worked out what he had, and then he worked out what he could not do with it.

---

He could not write it down. He had a book with a name at the foot of every line of a list of works and a day against every line of it, and he could have put a line in the back of it, and he did not, and the reason is that a line in the back of that book is a findable thing and what is in it is a foreman's own reason for a decision she made about four men on that bank, and a man with a copy of the right page would put it beside a note of a seat and ask a person who was not in the room why the district's man was told it in a shed and not told it to the book-keeper who had wanted it since the third month.

He could not ask her. He had said the reason out loud in the seventh month and she had heard it, and he had been right about it then and he was right about it now, and the sentence had come out of her mouth on its own and he was not going to walk up to a woman of fifty-one and say *I heard that* and make it a thing that had been given to him.

And he could not repeat it. That was the one he had not thought of until the yard.

**The half of a sentence she has got goes to strangers. It has always gone to strangers.** It is a foreman's answer to a question about the works, and a foreman's answer to a question about the works is said to whoever is asking about the works, and a man from a district works is a stranger, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that requires a foreman to give the same answer twice to the same person in the same room, so in a week or so she will be able to say a different thing and both of them will have been true.

**So a man who is owed a sentence and is not going to be given it is a man who has it, and cannot use it, and cannot put it anywhere, and has to carry it.**

---

She found out afterwards. He saw her find out.

The man with the satchel went at about the fourth hour and thanked her and she said the correct amount of nothing, and then the shed emptied the way sheds empty, and she stood at the bench with her book in it — the one with her own sheet in it, the words of a document and not the document, no name on it, and the notice on the bench with the seal and nothing under the seal — and she had both hands on the ledger in front of her and she was not doing anything with it.

"Mr Adley."

"Ma'am."

"That was a whole sentence and I did not mean to say it in a room."

"I know you did not."

"I have had it since the third month," said Halla Wray, "and I have given it to a district in about a minute and a half, and I have not given it to you since the second week of the eleventh month, and you know I have not, and I would like it entered that I know you know."

"There is nothing to enter it in," said Rennick Adley, "and that is not a complaint."

---

He got as far as the gate and she said it to his back, which she has not done in about nine years and which most of the shed heard.

"Mr Adley. I have thought about it for about two minutes and I am not going to say anything to you about it and I am not going to stop saying it either, and both of those are the same decision and it took me two minutes and I would like it noticed that a foreman who has given a reason away by accident is in a worse position than one who has not, because now there is a copy of it in a satchel going four hundred and thirty miles up this river with nobody's name on it, and I cannot get it back and I would not want to."

"I am not going to write it down," said Rennick Adley.

"I know you are not going to write it down, and I know exactly why, and I have known since the seventh month, and I would have given it to you in the seventh month if you had asked and you did not ask and that was correct and it is still correct and I am not going to say anything else about it."

---

He walked the half hour back down the road with the ledger under his arm and the water on his left and about four hundred yards of it in view the whole way, and he did not write it down, and he did not go into the store and tell anybody, and he did not sleep much and he did not decide anything that week.

The factor's letter was on the bench when he got in and it is on the bench now. The lamp went on in the shed up the road at about the seventh hour and not before, and the door of the store was open, and about four hundred and thirty miles of road is what it costs anybody who wants to come and see a page in a ledger, and there is no fund, and no form for a fund.

And on the fourth day of the last week of the twelfth month of the year after the year turned, and nobody in that bay noticed the day it turned on, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to notice a year, and it is not a form and it is not a day and it is in nobody's book, and the season was shut and the water was down and about nineteen reasons were in a column in a book with a day against every one of them and about four men in that bay still had never been asked anything, and a foreman of fifty-one had said a whole sentence out loud in a room to a stranger, and a book-keeper of thirty-eight had heard it and could not do one single thing with it.
