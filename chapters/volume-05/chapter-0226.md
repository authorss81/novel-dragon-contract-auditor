# Chapter 226: One More Book, And Nobody Asked Her To

The room is rented by the week and it is two streets off a street that goes down to the river road, and there are fifteen books in it on a table and a board, and every one of the fifteen is somebody else's and none of the fifteen has an office on it.

Nell Kest is twenty. She is a dye-house worker, which is what she is paid for, and she keeps the books of eleven houses on a lane in a furnace town about four hundred and thirty miles up this river and of four other houses in three other districts, and she keeps every one of them from this city and has not been up that river since she came down it, which is what she is not paid for. Her wage stopped in the second week of the third month of the year after and it is stopped now and the word for it is *held*.

She was in the room at about the ninth hour on the second day of the second week of the fourth month of the year after next with the fifteen books in a heap, which is how they are when she am doing them properly, and a woman of about thirty came up at about the tenth hour and stood in the doorway for about four minutes before she said anything.

---

"You are the one who keeps the books," said the woman of about thirty.

"I am."

"Not for them. For me."

"No," said Nell Kest. "I have never kept a book for anybody who has come to a room, and I am not going to say that as though it is a complaint, because it is not one, and I have been keeping other people's numbers since I was seventeen and none of the fifteen has ever known there was a person doing it."

---

The woman of about thirty came in and shut the door, which nobody does in that room, and stood with her back against it.

"Two streets off," she said. "There are four of us and there is a door and the water came to it in the first week of the second month of this year and went off again and nobody came. Not one person came. Not the man from the water and not a woman from anywhere and not a boy. Four of us and a door and nobody came, and I have had that since the first week of the second month and I have not said it out loud to anybody, and I am saying it to you because you are the only person I have heard of who does anything at all for nothing."

"Then sit down," said Nell Kest, "and I am going to say the price of it out loud before I say anything else, and then you are going to tell me the numbers, and I am not going to be thanked for either half."

---

"Go on."

"**Nobody is going to be told that I have done this.** Not the man from the water and not the man at the other end of the lane, and you are not going to be thanked for asking me and I am not going to be thanked for doing it, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for a person to be thanked for keeping a book and there is no form anywhere in this empire for a person to keep one at all, and I have looked, and I looked with a clerk of thirty-four in the ninth month of the year after because I cannot get through a shelf of forms quickly enough on my own."

"And here is the other half and it is worse than that. There is no fee. There is no wage and no day rate and nothing at the end of it, and if you come to tell me a number in a year I will be here because I live here, and if I am not here then the sixteen go to a woman of about thirty with a key and she will not know what to do with them and neither will anybody after her."

---

"How much is it," said the woman of about thirty.

"It is nothing, and I want that said first, because you have come in here with a face that has done a sum."

"I have not got anything."

"**Then it is nothing and it is going to stay nothing, and the day you can pay me for it is the day I stop, and I am telling you that now so that you cannot come back in a year and say that I did not tell you.**"

Then she pushed the heap of fifteen across the table and made a space at the near end of it, and took a book out of a satchel the woman of about thirty had brought with her and had been holding against her chest since she came in, and opened it, and it was about two-thirds full.

"Right," said Nell Kest. "Door first. Then how many. Then the water, and I do not care what the water says about anybody, I want what it said and the day it said it."

---
---

There was a knock at about the half hour and it was the foreman off the dye end, who is about forty, whose name is on nothing, and who has come up two stairs twice, and the second time was the second week of the third month of the year after, when he came and told her that her wage was held, and she has not been able to pay for a thing since.

He came in and shut the door and did not sit down.

"I have been told to tell you it is still held."

"All right."

"That is all I have got and I have had it since the first week of the fourth month of last year and I did not come up, and I want to say why I did not come up, and the reason is that I could not think of one thing to tell you that would not be a reason to keep holding it."

---

"And you have come up to say that you cannot think of a reason."

"I have come up to say it," said the foreman, "and I have asked twice. Once in the third week of the third month of the year after and once in the fourth week of the ninth, and both times I was told to keep asking, and both times a person said the same thing, which is that it is held and it will be looked at. **Nobody has ever told me what it is held against.** There is no form. I have been in a room with about nine hundred forms in it and I have never seen one that has a person's wage on it and nowhere on it to put the thing it is held for."

"And I am the only person in this city who is holding your money and I cannot tell you what it is held for, and that is not a way a person is usually described, and I noticed it on the second day of the first week of the third month of the year after next, standing in this room, and I have not been able to put it down since."

---

"A person who is paid can be told to stop," said Nell Kest. "You have just said that to me without saying it."

"I have not."

"You have," said Nell Kest. "**You are the only man in this city who can pick up or put down the whole of what I have, and you cannot do it for a reason nobody will give you, and that is being told to stop with a different word on it, and I have heard that sentence out loud from a man with no office in a rented room in the first week of the first month of the year after next and from a book-keeper on a bank four hundred and thirty miles down this river before that.**"

The foreman did not say anything for about four seconds.

"Then I am one of about four people in this matter who cannot be asked," he said, "and I have never once been told that, and I have been forty for about nine years and I have not noticed until this minute."

"Nobody is going to tell you," said Nell Kest. "**There is no post in this empire whose job is to tell, and the only way anybody finds out that they cannot be asked is that they work out that they have asked and been let off.** And you have let yourself off twice and come up two stairs for nothing, and I am not going to thank you for it and I am going to do the other thing, which is to tell you that there is a woman of about sixty-four somewhere in this matter who has said the same sentence out loud in a room with about nine men in it, and you are the first person in nine years who has arrived at it on your own and I have got nothing to add to it."

---

"Your wage is still held," said the foreman, at the door.

"Yes."

"Is there anything you want me to say to whoever is holding it."

"No," said Nell Kest, "**because there is nobody to say it to, and a thing said to a room in a building about nine miles off that is a document, and I have just spent an hour and a half refusing to put a wrong street name in a book and I am not going to undo that in a doorway.** Write to me. A line, with the day on it, saying it is held. That is all. Do not ask them anything and do not tell them anything and do not put my name at the foot of a thing."

"I have not got a book."

"You have got a hand," said Nell Kest, "and there is nobody in this empire more findable than a hand."

He went down the two stairs and she shut the door and stood in the room with a book open on a table and a wage held since the second week of the third month of the year after, and she did not write the street name again, and she did not write anything about the money, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for a woman to be told what her own money is held for, and there is not going to be one, and there is not one of them with a space in it for a person who is twenty and whose job was never written down.

---

It took about an hour and a half, and a third of that was the woman of about thirty going back to the door to check a thing, and Nell Kest did not go with her and did not offer, and when she came back she said, "I have got it wrong," and they went through it again.

And in the middle of it the woman of about thirty said the name of the street the way she says it, and Nell Kest wrote down the name the way the water office spells it, and the two are not the same, and Nell Kest did not correct her and said why.

"Because if this book is a place then it is a door with a name on it," she said, "**and a door with a name on it is a thing that can be carried out of a room, and there is about nine hundred and forty names in a book on a shelf about nine miles off that road that came off a building off it, and I am not going to be the second one.** A household book is not a place. It is some numbers that somebody agreed to write down. And I am going to write down the numbers and the day, and the street exactly as your water office spells it, because that is what the water will send a man to, and I am not going to be the reason a letter about four people goes to a street that does not have them in it."

"You would have found that out for me in a year."

"I would have found it out in about four years, when somebody came asking, and by then there would be a wrong name in a book that nobody has ever opened, and that is what I am frightened of."

---

The woman of about thirty asked her name in about the fortieth minute, which nobody has ever done in that room, and Nell Kest gave it, and then said the thing that had been sitting in her since the fortieth minute.

"**Nobody asks me anything.** I have had four people to ask since the fifth month of the year after and not one of them has asked me a single question, and I asked two of them, and one of the two is a woman of about fifty-five in a room nine miles off who has said the price out loud to about nine people since the second week of the ninth month of the year after, and the other one is a man with no office who has been answerable for something since the first week of the first month of the year after next and cannot be found and does not know it, and I asked him a question in a rented room about nine miles from where I live and I walked there and back in one day, and there is not one form in this empire for what I did."

"Did anybody send you."

"Nobody sent me. There is no form anywhere in this empire for a person who comes on her own account, and I have looked, and a woman of sixty-three in this city told me that in a room in the second week of the twelfth month of the year after, and she was not sent either, and she is the only other person I have met who did a thing for nothing and got nothing for it."

---

Then the woman of about thirty went, and did not shut the door behind her, which she had done on the way in, and Nell Kest sat with a new book on the table with a street two streets off written at the top of it in her own hand and a day against it and no name on it.

She had put a day against it. She had not put a name. She looked at that for about a minute and a half and then she put a name on it, and it is her own, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for a book-keeper to put her own name at the head of another person's numbers, and she is the only person who will ever read it, and in about four years there is a person with a copy of the right page who could put that name beside a door.

She did it on purpose, in about four seconds, and read it back once and not twice, and she has read the other four lines at the back of a book of eleven houses once each and not twice either, and that is the only difference between them and it is the whole of what she has got.

---

Then she counted, because she is a person who counts and there is no form anywhere in this empire for counting anything, and there are four people she has to ask and she has asked two and she is not going to ask the other two in the fourth month of the year after next.

**Four in about a hundred and forty years.** A court room in the fifth month of the year after that wanted a record. A clerk's window in the first week of the tenth, by a man afraid about his own household. A desk in the second week of the eleventh, by a woman of twenty who walked about four miles and nobody sent her. A rented room in the first week of the first month of the year after next, by the same woman, who walked nine miles in a day and asked a man with no office what a word on a form means, and was answered with a price and a refusal, and is answerable and cannot be un-answered.

And one of the four people she has not asked is a woman of about sixty with a board and a basket, four hundred and thirty miles up this river, who is the only person alive who can say how a box got filled and who does not know that anybody knows she is the only one. And the other one is at a window four hundred and thirty miles down it, on a flood terrace, and she has not got a fund and there is no form in this empire for a fund, and four hundred and thirty miles of road is what it costs anybody who wants to go and be asked a question, and she is not going this month, and she has not said she is not going at all, and there is a difference between those two things and it is the only thing in this room she is going to say out loud.

And the count has been four since the second day of the first week of the first month of the year after next, and it was four before that, and nobody has asked anybody anything since, and every year people stand at counters in this city wanting to ask a clerk something and not one of them opens their mouth, and it will not happen again by itself.

She put the coat on the back of the chair and did not go out in it, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that says a woman of twenty has to decide a thing on the day she decides it, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that says she has to decide it at all, and there are now sixteen books on a table in a room two streets off a street that goes down to the river road, and not one of the sixteen has an office on it, and nobody has been told about any of the sixteen, and that is the whole of the second day of the second week of the fourth month of the year after next.
