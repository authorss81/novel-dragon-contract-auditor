# Chapter 240: The Twelfth Line, And Then A Day That Is Not That Day

He is in the same room he has been in since the sixth month, rented by the week, and the table is in it and the chair is against the wall with the bed and about four feet of that wall is bare, and it was about the fifth hour of the morning on the fourth day of the fourth week of the eighth month of the year after next, and the lamp was not lit and he did not light it.

Marek Kest is thirty-eight and he has said those four things out loud in about four rooms since the third week of the third month of the year after next, and he said them again to himself at about the fifth hour of the morning because the twelfth line was going to be filled in about four seconds and a man who fills in a twelfth line ought to know what he has got. No office. No fund. No register. A bar of his own that came off him on the twelfth of the ninth month of last year on an application of one line which he wrote himself. And a line about two inches long on the inside of his left wrist that reads nothing, and decides nothing, and does not know where the ford is.

The book with no office on it was on the table with the eleven lines in it and the twelfth empty, and it has been empty since the third week of the ninth month of the year after, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that requires a man to fill a twelfth line and there never was one that required him to leave it empty, and that is the whole difference between the two things and nobody in this matter can pay for it.

He had not eaten and he had not been out of the room since the second day of the fourth week and there was a woman who brings him bread on about the sixth hour on about four days in a week and he had not opened the door on any of them that week. About four feet of bare wall is not a plan and it has never once been a plan, and there is a nail in it about four feet up that came with the room and has nothing hanging on it, and he has looked at that nail every morning for about a year and has not worked out what it is for either, and he is not going to work it out this morning because this morning is the morning of a line.

---

He read the eleven going back, and he read the fourth, and he read the ninth, and then he read the eleventh twice.

The eleventh is the one about the four definitions, and it says that not one of them has a word in it for a person who holds an office, and it has a day against it from the fourth week of the ninth month of the year after, and he put that in himself in one line and read it back.

The other ten are shorter and most of them are about a document, and two of them are about a woman of twenty and neither of the two says anything about her that she would want read out.

Then he put the pencil on the table and it was about nine minutes before he wrote anything, and he wants that said, because a man who says he sat for nine minutes before writing a line is a man who is telling you what the nine minutes were for, and they were for the four feet of wall.

---

"**A fact on a line in a book with no office on it is the worst of the two, because a question can be answered and a fact can only be repeated."**

He had written that in the third week of the third month of the year after next and it is the reason for everything he was about to do and also the reason he had not done it in about nineteen weeks.

He wrote the twelfth line, and it took him about four seconds, and then he put the pencil down and read it back once and read it back again, and the second reading was slower. That is his rule and it came from a girl of seventeen who is paid to read and is not paid to think, and he has never told anybody that and a foreman of fifty-one worked it out standing in a bay about four hundred and thirty miles down this river in about nine seconds and told nobody either.

---

Then he said it out loud, because a room is the only kind of place in this empire where a thing like that can be said.

"A fact and a decision are not the same thing, and a woman of twenty-four told me that in a rented room in the third week of the fourth month of the year after next and gave me an answer in a room in words with a date on it. The answer was that I was to do the fact on one day and the decision on another and not in one letter. I did it in one letter, she refused half of it, and I have not improved on the refusal."

He shut the book.

---

Then he said the other half, and it is the whole of the morning and he said it standing up at about the fifth hour of the morning with the lamp not lit.

"There are two things I could be and I have had both of them on a table since the third week of the third month of the year after next and I have refused the choice out loud in a room in the same week, and the reason I refused it was given to me by a woman of twenty-four and the reason is that choosing is a decision about what you are and I could not make one while that line was empty."

He put his hand on the shut book.

"It is not empty. It is the only honest thing in there and it is the eleventh line and I wrote it in about four seconds in the fourth week of the ninth month of the year after and it is true, and it does not tell anybody what I am, it tells them what I am not, and that took nineteen weeks and this morning and about four seconds of pencil."

---

There was a knock at about the seventh hour and he did not open it, and it was a man of about thirty-four who had come about four miles and wanted to know whether a thing said in a room could be held to by the person it was said in front of.

"You have heard the answer."

"I have heard about the answer," said the man of about thirty-four, "from a woman of about twenty-four who said the price out loud in a counter and then said it out loud again because I did not understand the first one, and neither of us sent you anything, and I want that said before I ask."

"It can be held to and it cannot be asked about," said Marek Kest, to about two inches of door. "Those are two instruments and they are not the same and this empire has spent two hundred years being careful about the difference and has got very good at it. A person cannot be sent a paper about a thing said in a room. A person can be sent a paper about a thing they wrote down. Everything in this matter that anybody can be held by, he wrote down himself, and one of those things is from the second week of the third month of the year after next and one of them is older, and neither of them has a name at the foot of it."

"Who is the woman of about twenty-four?"

"Not somebody who is going to be told by me," he said, "and I am aware that is a sentence that only a man who is not findable can say with any comfort, and I have said it to a door twice now and I am going to stop."

---

"So that is the labour," he said, to a room with a bare wall in it, at about the eighth hour. "**The other one is the shape of four years, and it costs nothing, and nobody has to pay anything, and there is no form in this empire for it.** This one is the shape of a road, and the day is mine, and I am not going down it at about the sixth hour in the morning. I am going to put one sheet of paper on it on the last day of this week, and the sheet will go down it and I will not, and that is the whole of the difference between the two of them."

And then the cost of it, out loud, because he has never in this matter made a decision and not said the price of it.

"A man who is a labour is a man somebody can pay for a day, and a man somebody can pay for a day is a man somebody can tell to stop, and I have not told you what I did about that and I am not going to now. There is no fund and there is no form anywhere in this empire for a fund, and I have got about forty-four pounds a year and a road of about four hundred and thirty miles each way and about six days, and the day comes out of my own money and not out of anybody else's."

"So I have taken the shape of the thing that can be refused and I have removed the only person in this empire who could have refused me, on purpose, and out of my own hand. That is a worse position than the other one. I have known it since the third week of the fourth month of the year after next, when a woman of twenty asked me whether anybody could protect me, and I said no and I meant it. I did not tell her that the reason nobody can protect me is that I have just arranged for there to be nobody able to stop me."

---

He was at the counter of a place that sells nothing on the last day of the fourth week of the eighth month of the year after next, at about the tenth hour, and the road is walked zero times in this and it is not going to be walked in this.

There is a sheet of paper in his hand. There is no name at the top of it and there is no name at the foot of it and there is a day in it, and a day with nobody's name on it is not a summons to anybody, and he has got about nine weeks of daylight and about four hundred and thirty miles and a man with a signature he has never heard, who asked him to come and look at a thing and to be asked nothing and to sign nothing.

"**He wrote it in about eleven minutes and he did not put the sender's name down and he did not put his own, and the reason is that a hand is the only thing anybody in this matter has ever been able to use, and a man with a hand is a man who can be found, and he is the one person in this matter that nobody can find.**"

And there is one person in this empire he could have told about it at about the eighth hour that morning, and he did not tell her, and the reason is not protectiveness.

"There is a woman of about thirty-five who keeps a day-book in a lodging house," he said. "She came about nine miles on the fourth day of the third week of the sixth month of the year after next and stood on a step and told me not to put anything at the head of anything about me, and she was right and I have not got an answer to it. She is findable. I am not. **A person who is findable holding a fact about a man who is not is a person who can be produced with the fact, and that is the whole of the asymmetry that a woman of twenty worked out standing in a rented room in the third week of the fourth month of the year after next, and she is right and I have had since the third week of the fourth month of the year after next to find a way round it, and there is not one."**

---

And then the last of it, and it is short, and he has not improved it and does not intend to.

**A letter is the only instrument in this matter that a man nobody can find can put into a room,** he said. "A form goes to a counter. A roll goes to a building. A list of works goes into a company book four hundred and thirty miles down a river in nine hands. Nobody has ever had to think about a thing that could be put in a room by a man with nothing, because there has not been one, and there is one now and it is in about nine days on about four hundred and thirty miles of road."

"And it costs a day of a wage of seventeen shillings and a penny the week, and there is no fund, and there is nobody who can stop me, and I am not going to be able to say afterwards that anybody made me do it, and that is what I have bought and it is the only thing I have bought in about four years and I did not pay for it in money."

---

He put the sheet in the post and walked back, and about nine miles off that road there is a woman of twenty-four in a room with a counter in it who has not been told anything and is not going to be. About four hundred and thirty miles down the river there is a bank about four hundred and forty foot long and a foreman of fifty-one who has said four reasons out loud in a shed and every one of them was bad, and about four people in a bay who cannot read a paragraph and have never been asked anything. A girl of seventeen at the back of that bay who is not thanked and is not going to start, and a book-keeper of thirty-eight whose wage is held against a roof he put there himself, and a man of about thirty from the second gang whose own reason is in nine mouths and who has decided not to have it taken back, and a foreman of about forty in a furnace town who has said one true thing about himself five times and is on a slate.

"**And a man of thirty-eight in a rented room has a twelfth line in a book with no office on it and a day against it, and a line in a letter with a day in it and no name at the top of it and no name at the foot of it, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for either, and about four years is how long either of them can be found for.**"

Four askings in about a hundred and forty years. Two of them were accidents. The third was the first one anybody did on purpose, and the count has not moved since the second day of the first week of the first month of the year after next, and there is no post anywhere in this empire whose job is to ask. The count is four, and it is the last day of the fourth week of the eighth month of the year after next, and a decision is not a question and a question is not a decision and he has got one of them and has not got the other.
