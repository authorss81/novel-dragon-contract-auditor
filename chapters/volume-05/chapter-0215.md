# Chapter 215: The Word Engaged, And The Fourth Time

She walked about nine miles on the second day of the first week of the first month of the year after next, and she did it in one day and in about eleven hours with two stops, and she had the address in her head and not on a paper, because there is a form in this empire for the address of a place a person may be served at and there is not one for the address of a person who is not in a matter.

Nell Kest is twenty. She is a dye-house worker, which is what she is paid for, and she keeps the books of eleven houses on a lane in a furnace town about four hundred and thirty miles up this river and of four other houses in three other districts, which is what she is not paid for. Her wage stopped in the second week of the third month of the year after and it is stopped now and the word for it is *held*.

She decided this in the second week of the eleventh month of the year after, in a room about nine miles from a counter, standing up, in front of a woman of about fifty-five, and what she decided was: **I cannot get out of the box, and I am going to be the person who knows how it was filled, and I am not going to be a person who knows it alone.** There were four people. She has asked one.

---

The building is a room over a shop with a door on it and a stair that is not a good stair, and a man of thirty-eight came to the door while she was on the third step, and a woman of twenty-four came out behind him, and the woman of twenty-four was not invited and was not a guest and had to be there.

Marek Kest has a rented room with a table in it, a book with no office on it, and about four feet of a wall he does not use. He has no office and no fund and no register and nothing in this empire he could certify anything with, and a bar of his own that came off him on the twelfth of the ninth month of last year on an application of one line which he wrote himself.

Tamsin Rook is twenty-four. Two instruments require her in any room he is in and they are not one instrument and he does not merge them and she does not let him: a paper made in a form in four days with a day on it, and a watch that does not name him and does not name her and requires only that a person who is not from the office that cannot be named be present.

"You did not send for me," she said, coming out.

"I did not send for anybody," said Nell Kest. "**There is no form in this empire for a person who comes on her own account and I have looked and you are the second one I have done this to.**"

---

He had a book open on the table and eleven lines in it with a day against the sheet and not against the lines, and a twelfth line with nothing on it, and she looked at the two of them and did not ask about either, which he noticed, and which was the reason he sat down.

"What have you come for."

"**What does the word *engaged* mean.**"

He put his pen down. It is a word on a printed form in about nine hundred buildings and it is the fourth of those words and it is in a box on a head of a file with two lines under it in a clerk's own hand in a room about nine miles from a counter.

"Ask it again," he said.

"I have just asked it. I came nine miles to ask it. I have never asked anybody anything in my life that took me nine miles and I am not going to stand on a stair and ask it twice."

"Then ask it in here, because I want it out loud in a room and not on a step, and I have not got anybody to hear it on a step."

---

She asked it in the room, standing, with her hat in her hand, and it was the fourth time in about a hundred and forty years that one person in this empire had asked another person what a word on a form meant, and the first three were a court room in the fifth month of the year after that wanted a record, a clerk's window in the first week of the tenth month, and a desk in the second week of the eleventh month, and two of the three were accidents and nobody chose either of them, and this one was on purpose and nobody sent her.

He said the count out loud, because a number a person says can be held to, and he said it standing up, and then he answered her in about four minutes.

"I can tell you what it has been used to do. **I cannot tell you what it means,** and the difference between those two things is the whole of your question and the whole of this matter, and there is nobody in this empire who can close it, and I have looked for about two years.

"A roll of households does not list rights, holdings or claims. That is on the face of the third line and it is older than I am. So a district can say where a household is and can say nothing else about it, and the office asks for a person, and the only question there is an answer to is *who is on the roll*, and the answer that comes back is a household. And the box takes a household because the box takes a thing. It says *the person, institution or thing engaged in the matter* and it does not say which of those three is wanted and there is no box anywhere in about nine hundred buildings that will only take one of them.

"**And nobody has ever needed the word defined, because a box that will take a thing can always be filled in, and a box that will only take a person cannot.** That is why it is not defined. Not because anybody is keeping it from you. Because a thing fits in a box and a person does not, and a form is a thing, and a form cannot be asked what any of its words mean, and a form is the only thing in this empire that has never once been asked and the only thing in this empire that has never once said no."

---

She sat down without being asked, which she had not expected to do.

"Then I am in it because *engaged* is a word for a person who has been got at and is doing nothing about it, is that it. It does not mean anybody is employed. It means I am on a roll and the roll has been read."

"It means you are on a roll at a door," he said, "and the difference between a roll at a door and you is a thing about four hundred and thirty miles away in a lane, and she walks it once a year with a sheet on a board and a basket, and she does not know what the fourth column of her own roll is for, and there is no fund and no form for a fund, and nobody has ever been to ask her anything, and that is the whole end of it."

"I have been on her list," said Nell Kest. "She is the one who told me."

"Then she is the only person alive who can say how your box got filled, and she cannot be asked, and that is not anybody's plot. That is a woman of about sixty with a basket and a board and about four hundred and thirty miles of river between her and the only person in this empire who could tell you."

---

Tamsin Rook said nothing for the whole of that and then she said the thing she had come out of the room to say, and she said it standing by the table with her arms down at her sides.

"Stop. Both of you. **I am going to say the price out loud and I have said it before and I am saying it again and I am not going to be thanked for it, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be thanked.**

"A person who asks a question about a form in a room becomes a person somebody has answered. Not the person the question is about. The one who asked. I have said that in this city and four hundred and thirty miles from it, and there is a clerk of thirty-four at a counter who has had it in her hand since the second day of the second week of the ninth month of the year after and a clerk of thirty-eight at a window who has had it for longer, and I have said the price of being a party out loud ten times and nobody has thanked me once and I did not expect to be thanked once and I would like it written down that I did not.

"And nobody has ever said the other half of it, and I have only just worked it out at about the fourth minute of this, and I am going to say it now and I am going to be about two seconds late saying it, and I would like that noticed.

"**Every person who has ever answered anybody about a form in this empire has been a clerk.** A clerk is findable. A clerk has a window, or a counter, or a room, or eleven books on a shelf, and in about four years a man with a copy of the right page can walk in and put a line under that clerk's name and be answered.

"And he is not a clerk. He has no office and no register and nothing in this empire he could be looked up in. **He is the first person in this matter who is answerable and cannot be found,** and the reason he cannot be found is that nobody was ever going to have to look him up, and that is the same sentence as the reason the office cannot tell you how many of you there are."

---

Nobody said anything for about a minute.

"Say that again slowly," said Nell Kest.

"No," said Tamsin Rook. "A number a person says can be held to and I have just said the whole of it, and if I say it again then there are two of them and they will not be identical, and I am not going to be a person who improves a thing she has already said in a room."

"Then say the part that is about him."

"That is not a number and I will say it," said Tamsin Rook. "**There is nothing anybody can instruct him about, and nothing anybody can pay him for, and nothing anybody can thank him for, and that is the reason he is the most dangerous person in this matter and it is also the reason nobody in it can protect him.** I said both halves of that in this city in the third week of the tenth month of the year after and I am not going to soften the second one now, and today he has come back into it from the other end, and the other end is you, and nobody sent you."

---

Then Marek Kest said the thing that was actually the answer to the question she had walked nine miles to ask, and he said it standing up in a room with a table in it and about four feet of a wall he does not use.

"You asked me what a word means. **I am a man with a bar that came off him on a one-line application and I have read every form in this business for about four years, and there is no definition of that word in any of them,** and there is not going to be one, because the moment there is one then a keeper of a roll has to decide whether a household is engaged in a matter, and a keeper cannot be asked what a word on a form means, and a keeper has no instrument and no protection and a basket once a year. So the word is not undefined by accident. The word is undefined on purpose, and not by anybody. It is undefined the way a gap in a hedge is a shape rather than a hole.

"And the honest answer to your question is the one I have got: **you are engaged in the matter in the only sense the form can hold, which is that you are the nearest thing to a person in it.** And I would like it entered that a woman of twenty walked about nine miles in one day in the first week of the first month of the year after next to ask a man with no office a question that nobody on this earth can answer, and that he gave her the truth and the truth was no."

---

Then she asked him whether she could write it down, and he said no, and she said why not, and he told her, and she said the thing back to him before he had finished.

"Because a hand of mine is now the only hand anybody is going to be able to put side by side in about four years, and you have no hand to put beside mine because you have no office, and I have a book of eleven houses that nobody pays me to keep, and there is nothing anywhere in this empire that would hold this except that book."

He looked at her for about a second and a half.

"I did not know that," he said.

"You have been in rooms with about four thousand entries in eleven minute books and you did not know that," said Nell Kest. "And I have known it since the second week of the ninth month, standing at a counter, where a clerk of thirty-four told me what a representative is and would not write it down, and she was right, and I have been carrying it since, and this morning I have found somebody else who is carrying the same thing for the same reason."

---

She wrote it at the table, on a page at the back of a book with about eleven houses in it that nobody pays her to keep, in her own hand, with the day on it, and she read it back once and did not read it twice, and Marek Kest watched her do it and said:

"You have not read it back twice."

"I have not read it back twice because the second reading is a rule about something a room decided," said Nell Kest, "and this is not a room and it is not a decision and there are only two of us in it and one of us cannot be found, and a second reading would be me reading a thing I have already agreed with."

Then she wrote four lines at the foot of the page, which were the reason, and she turned the book round and pushed it across the table so that he could read them, which nobody has ever asked her to do.

> *Asked in a rented room on the second day of the first week of the first month of the year after next, out loud, in a room, what the word engaged means on the box headed for the person, institution or thing engaged in the matter. Nobody sent me. I decided. I walked about nine miles. He has no office, no register, no fund, a bar discharged on the twelfth of the ninth month of last year on a line he wrote himself, and there is nothing anybody can instruct him about and nothing anybody can pay him for.*
>
> *He cannot answer it. He told me he cannot answer it, in a room, and he told me the shape of why, and he is right. This is the fourth asking and two of the first three were accidents.*
>
> *I am writing it here and not giving it to anybody. Three years of refusing to make records of people and I am starting with myself, and the reason is that a question with a day on it and no name under it is findable in about four years and there is nothing else in this empire that will hold it.*
>
> *A person who is paid to read is a person who can be told to stop. There is no post whose job is to ask. — N. Kest, second day of the first week of the first month of the year after next.*

---

She shut the book and put it under her arm, and the year after next had been four days old, and nobody had thanked her and she had not asked and there is no form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be thanked, and she was answerable, and he was answerable, and there were four askings in a hundred and forty years and no post and no plan, and it would not happen again by itself.
