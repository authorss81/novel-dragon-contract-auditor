# Chapter 220: The Woman Who Decides To Be Harder To Ask

The third week of the second month of the year after next came and went and the fourth week of it came in, and the coming season's list was read in a bay in about four minutes, and the water was up about two feet off the mark where it had been in the twelfth month, and there was no line for a bridge in the Lowcross fund and there had not been one in nineteen years.

Halla Wray is fifty-one. She is the foreman of the Slade Cut, which is an office with no office in it, and she has been on that bank since she was nine. She can read and nobody at the district works has ever once in nine years asked her to read anything, and in the third week of the first month of the year after next she worked out that she had been answering questions for four men in that bay since she was forty-two, without being asked, and that nobody had ever noticed, including her.

---

She did the ordinary things, and then she did the thing she had come to do, and the two are not in that order in her own head and she has given up trying to put them in that order.

The count of the works, in a bay, in about four minutes, with the men in it. The nineteen reasons in the third column of a book in a hired stone store off the Slade, with a day against every one of them and no names in that column at all, and the names in a different book with about nine hundred and forty of them in it in nine hands over about sixty years, and a name in a room is a thing that can be carried out of a room.

A factor's letter of four lines on a bench, wanting a name that may be served and that name's consent, and it says without both it cannot proceed and will not proceed, **and it is not going to be answered, and every day it is not answered is a day nobody has been entered**, and a carter asked in the second week of the tenth month of the year after to have his own line written in the place where the no is, and went up the road for the winter, and is not coming back for the asking.

A guarantee standing offered and unanswered on about four hundred and forty foot of bank with about sixty children under sixteen inside it, and no such person has ever come forward, and she has not got the terms of it in her head and is not going to read them out in a bay at about the seventh hour.

A bill at Lowcross at nineteen pounds three and fourpence, unpaid, nobody liable, and nobody in that bay is paying it and nobody is going to be asked for a penny.

And about four people in that bay who cannot read a paragraph, who are on the list of works, whose work is on the list, and who have still never been asked anything, and there is no column for them and there is not going to be one.

---

A man of about fifty-five was in that bay for the reading and his work was in it and she read his work the way she reads everything and did not read it any differently, and afterwards he asked her what the day under it meant, because there is a day against every line of a list of works and he has never once asked anybody what a day is for, and nobody has ever told him.

She told him in about four seconds, in a foreman's voice, and he said that was not what he meant, and she said it was the only meaning a day has, and he said, "I meant whether it is the day it started or the day somebody decided," and she said it was the day it was written down and that those are the same day about nine times out of ten, and he said, "Then somebody decided," and she said, "Then somebody decided," and neither of them made anything else of it and about nine men in that bay did not hear it at all.

**That is the fifth time in nine years.** She has done it since she was forty-two and nobody asked her to and nobody has ever written it down, and she is the only person in this empire who knows it, and a book-keeper of thirty-eight saw her do it in the fourth week of the twelfth month of the year after and did not understand it for about two hours, and has never been told that she knows.

---

Then she said the part she had come to say, and the bay was full and one of the about four was in it and had asked her a question that morning and had been answered in about three seconds.

"I told a book-keeper's face in a shed in the last week of the eleventh month of the year after that nobody had ever asked me what the words on a form mean, and it was true, and it was the only true thing I had said in a year.

"**And a woman of sixty-three stood in a room in this city in the second week of the twelfth month of the year after and gave a clerk her own name out loud, and nobody sent her, and a clerk of about fifty-five said no when she was asked to go and look for her, and did not look, and was right, and has not said so to anybody.** And a man of about thirty-four stood at a window in the third week of the twelfth month and had the price said to him a second time and heard it the second time. And a woman of twenty walked about nine miles in the first week of the first month of the year after next and asked a man with no office a question out loud in a room because she had decided to and nobody sent her.

"Four. A court room in the fifth month of the year after, a window in the first week of the tenth, a desk in the second week of the eleventh, and a rented room in the first week of the first month. **Four in about a hundred and forty years and there is no post anywhere in this empire whose job is to ask,** and two of the four were accidents and nobody chose either of them, and the third was the first one anybody did on purpose, and the fourth is about seven weeks old, and nothing has happened since.

"So the answer to who asks is that it is nobody's business, and there are four of them, and they were all found by accident or by a woman who decided on her own account, and not one of them was sent, and it will not happen again by itself."

---

Then she came to it, and it is the reason for everything else she has said that morning, and she said it out loud in a room with men in it and did not list the five and did not improve it.

"And now I have to say the other thing and it is about me, and I have had it since the third week of the first month of the year after next and I have not said it in a bay before and I am not going to have said it in one now.

"I am the person in this whole matter who has the most reason in the world to be asked about a thing. I have been in a box since the first week of the seventh month of the year after. I was not asked. **And the only reason nobody has ever come and asked me is that there is no form in this empire with a space in it for a woman of fifty-one on a bank, and there is no post, and no way to serve me.**

"And I have been glad of that. I have been glad of it for about five weeks and I have not admitted it to anybody, and I am admitting it now because a man of about sixty in another city has just worked out that a person has to be told out loud, by a clerk, once, that they may ask, and he worked it out in about four minutes standing at a counter, and nobody told him, and he is not a lawyer and he is not a party and he is sixty.

"**And I have decided that I am not going to make myself easier to ask.** That is the decision, and it is mine, and I made it in about four seconds, and I have not told anybody until now, and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to be argued with, and I would like it read back to me by somebody in a room and there is nobody who is going to."

---

"Why not," said a man of about thirty from the second gang.

"Because of the same reason as the four, and worse, because the four cannot read a paragraph and I can, and if a person stands in front of me and asks me what the third line means then I am a woman who has been asked, and then I am a woman who has been answered, and there is no instrument anywhere in this empire that takes that back off me either. **I would be found. That is the whole of it. I have spent forty-two years being unfindable and I said in this shed in the fourth week of the ninth month of the year after that I would like to be found, and I meant it,** and I am going to hold both of those at once for the rest of my life and it is not going to be comfortable and it is not going to be a rule."

"And a foreman who has been found is a foreman somebody can be asked about the four, and I have already given one reason for not asking them and it was a bad one, and I gave a second one about a girl of seventeen in the third week of the first month and it was the same bad reason twice, and I am not going to hand a third one to a person in a room who writes it down."

---

"And it is the wrong decision," she said, before anybody else could.

"**It is the wrong decision and I know exactly what the right one looked like, and it was a woman of twenty walking about four miles to a desk in the second week of the eleventh month of the year after and asking how the box got filled.** Nobody sent her and nobody told her to and there is no form for it and she did it on her own account, and she is the only reason I know what I am.

"And I have not asked her. She has not come to this bay. About four hundred and thirty miles of road is what it costs anybody who wants to come and see a page in a ledger, and there is no fund and no form for a fund, and I have said out loud that I am not going to be the reason four other people are found, and that still stands and nobody has talked me out of it.

"And I have now also decided not to be found myself. A woman of fifty-one in a shed in the fourth week of the second month of the year after next has arrived at two bad reasons and one wrong decision, and she is going to have to live in the room where all three of them are."

---

Then the ordinary thing again, because the bay empties and the lamp goes on about a quarter of an hour later than it ought to and the door of the shed is open.

A man of about fifty-five came in with a chain and asked about the third link, and she told him about the third link and about the oil, and it took about two minutes, and every man in that shed watched a foreman do a perfectly ordinary thing to a man who cannot read a paragraph.

And she did not ask him. Not about the hold, not about the season, not about the two sheets on the bench with a seal and nothing under the seal. And a book-keeper had read a piece of that man's own paper to him in a yard in the first week of the twelfth month of the year after and had written nothing down and had told nobody, and she knew, and she was not going to ask him about it, and the reason she was not going to is the same reason she was not going to ask the book-keeper in the third week of the first month of the year after next: **asking is the only instrument there is, and I am not going to spend it on a thing I could hear about in a bay in about four minutes, and I have not spent it once in about nine years and neither has anybody else on that bank.**

A man of about fifty from the fourth gang put his head in at about the seventh hour and said he wanted to get across to Lowcross in the morning and could he use the bridge, and she said the bridge is shut and has been shut since before the water came in, and he said he had a reason and it was that he had not been over it in about three weeks and there was a thing at the far end of it he wanted, and she said that is not a reason the bridge knows about.

"I know," said the man.

"Then it is not a reason and I am not going to ask you for another one, and there is no form in this empire that requires a man to give a better reason than *I want to get over there*, and I have read every form in that store and there is not one page in any of them that says a person has to give you a better reason than that."

"That is the reason I am still here," said the man. "You have said that in this shed as many times this year as there are weeks in the season and I have counted them and I have never been asked to count them out loud and I am not going to be."

"Nobody is going to ask you to count them out loud. If they do, there is no form for it and you do not have to help them."

He went and she stood at the bench for about a minute and a half and the third column of a book in a hired stone store off the Slade has nineteen reasons in it with a day against every one of them, and one of the nineteen is a man who wanted to get over a bridge, and the reason is in the book, and it is the same reason he gave in the seventh month, and nobody has asked him again and nobody is going to.

A bill at nineteen pounds three and fourpence with no line against it in nineteen years and a foreman who will not ask a man for a better reason than he has got, and those two facts have been standing on that bank since before the woman of twenty was born and neither of them is going to be paid and neither of them is going to be forgiven, and most of the men in that bay know both of them, and a woman of twenty with a wage held and a question of her own has still never been asked about any of it.

And a girl of seventeen was at the back of that bay on her fourth day, on a written engagement, at the same rate as everybody else on the list, and she was not thanked and did not ask and is not going to start, and nobody spoke to her about the season, and the foreman of fifty-one looked at the back of her bay twice in about four minutes and did not go over.

And the whole of the second season and everything that came after it is on a bank about four hundred and thirty miles down this river, and about four people in a bay cannot read a paragraph, and a reader of seventeen is the one person on that bank who is the wrong person to ask and the only one who is free to answer, and nobody sent anybody to her and nobody is going to, and there is no post in this empire whose job is to ask, and the count is four, and a foreman of fifty-one has just said out loud in a room that she is going to make herself harder to find, and the lamp is not lit yet, and that is the end of the movement and it is not the end of anything.
