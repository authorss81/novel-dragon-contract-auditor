# Chapter 203: A Class Nobody Can Count

The thing he could not do on the second day of the third week of the ninth month of the year after was count, and he had spent nineteen days failing to do it in about nine different ways, and on the third day he stopped, and the stopping was the finding.

Marek Kest is thirty-eight. He has a rented room with a table in it and a book with no office on it and about four feet of a wall he does not use. He has no office and no fund and no register and nothing in this empire he could certify anything with, and a bar of his own that came off him on the twelfth of the ninth month of last year on an application of one line which he wrote himself. There is a line about two inches long on the inside of his left wrist. It reads nothing. It decides nothing. It does not know where the ford is.

Tamsin Rook is twenty-four. She is in the room because two instruments require it and the two instruments are not one instrument, and he does not merge them and she does not let him: a paper made in a form in four days with a day on it, and a watch that requires only that a person who is not from the office that cannot be named be in any room he is in. She came at about the seventh hour with the second order in a bundle under her arm, and she put it on the table and did not explain it.

---

The office of a seat keeps eleven minute books. That is what it is for. A minute book takes down what a room did, on a day, with a clerk's name at the foot, and eleven of them on a shelf in a room on the ground floor is a complete record of a thing nobody looks at twice a year.

An entry is not a minute. That is the whole of the difficulty and it took him nineteen days because he kept trying to make the books into a register, and they are not a register, and they never were, and a book that takes down what a room decided cannot be made into a book of what a room did to a person, because the second thing does not happen in a room. It happens in an office about nine miles from a counter, in about nine seconds, in a hand, on a sheet, and the sheet goes out.

So the office of a seat has a shelf of what was said and no shelf at all of what was done.

"You could ask her for it," said Tamsin Rook.

"**I have got to the end of what I can do with a pen, and what I can do with a pen is describe, and a description of a thing that was done to a person is a shape with a hole in it where the person is.** I have written out the four definitions twice in this book in ink with the day on it and they are true and they are the page's, and the fourth one turns on a district's roll of households, and it says in its own words that the roll does not list rights, holdings or claims."

"Then how does anybody know a person is of age and resident."

"**A roll of households does not say a person is of age and resident. It says a household is at a door.** The office asks a district for the person of a family who is of age and resident, and a district has got nothing in the world that answers that, and so the office asks the next question, which is the only question there is an answer to, which is who is on the roll."

---

He said it out loud, and then he said it again in the other order, which is what he does.

"A roll of households does not list rights, holdings or claims. That is on the face of the third line and it has been there longer than I have been alive. So a district can tell the office where a person is, and can tell the office nothing else about them whatever — not what they hold, not what they are owed, not whether they are of age, and not which of the people at that door the office is asking for. And the office is asking for a person. And the office is putting the answer in a box that says the person, institution or thing engaged in the matter. And a roll of households has a household in it."

"That is the answer to something," said Tamsin Rook.

"That is the answer to a question nobody asked. That is how a company of nine families got into a box with a heading over it, and how a foreman of fifty-one got into one, and how a chapel and a poor-box and a company of watermen got into about four hundred returns in a bundle in about nine hundred buildings."

"And how your sister got into one."

"Yes," he said. "And how I did not. And I have had nineteen days with the four definitions in front of me and I want to say the thing I have got, and it is one sentence, and I have not said it out loud in about two years and I am going to say it in this room.

"The third line catches anybody of age and resident of a family that holds nothing enforceable, and it catches them by finding them on a roll of households. My mother made sure there was no Kest on any roll in this empire, and that was the right thing to do and it was done in a room in about nine minutes by a person who was not asked, and it is the reason there is no Kest on the roll of that district four hundred and thirty miles up this river, and the roll is the only thing in this business that could have found my sister, and she is on it, and she is found, and the finding is what got her in.

"The same hand is not a figure of speech and I have said so before in another room and I am not going to improve it. One family, one act, and it made two of its children unfindable and findable in the same year."

Nobody said anything for about a minute and a half.

---

Then he counted what he could count, and what he could count was this.

Two are known. He wrote the two in his own book on a clean sheet, in his own hand, and then he looked at them for about a minute, and then he took the pen and put a line through both of them, and wrote *two* against them instead.

"You have destroyed it," said Tamsin Rook.

"I have taken two names out of a book with no office on it, in a rented room, with nobody in it, and there is no instrument anywhere that stops me, and I did it before the ink was dry on the line I had struck through, and that is not a record of anything. That is a man tidying."

"What is the sheet for, then."

"It is for the questions." He turned it over. "**One line per question in this matter that nobody has asked. I have got eleven and most of them would take a person about four minutes to ask and about four years to answer,** and I have written them down in the order they ought to be asked and there is no order and I have put a day against the sheet and not against the lines."

She read it standing up, the way she reads everything, and she did not sit down and she did not touch it.

"There is a twelfth," she said.

"There is a twelfth line and it is empty and it is going to stay empty."

"Then write on it what it is."

"**If I write on the twelfth line who is going to ask the questions, then I have appointed somebody, and there is no post in this empire whose job is to ask, and a person who appoints somebody to a post that does not exist is doing the thing the whole of this is about, which is filling in a box the way the box is printed.** I am not going to do that and I would like it noticed that it took me nineteen days not to do it and that I was certain about it by the time I had the pen down."

---

He read the eleven out, because she asked him to and because a list nobody has heard read is a list of about nine lines of handwriting.

The first was what a second person is on a witness form. The second was whether a roll of households can say a person is of age. The third was what a company of nine families is, inside a definition that says a family. The fourth was what a room wants when it asks a question. The fifth was whether a minute book with a clerk's name at the foot of an entry protects the person the entry is about.

"That one has an answer," said Tamsin Rook. "And the answer is no, and it was given by the person who wrote it, out loud, in that room, and it protects the clerk and nothing else, and I would have struck it off the sheet."

"I have not struck it off."

"**Do not strike off the things that have been answered. A list of questions with the answered ones still on it is the only honest list there is, and the moment it is tidy somebody will believe there is less to do.**"

The sixth was whether a person can be un-entered. The seventh was what a hold on a household of four does. The eighth was who wrote a line in a margin in a hand that was not his. The ninth was whether a company that cannot be served can be said to have refused anything. The tenth was whether a person who is in a house that is shut has an address.

"That one is a finding," she said. "That one is in a minute book with a day on it and my name at the foot of it, and you did not ask it, I answered it in the first week of the fifth month, in a room, and you were in the room, and it is on your list as though it were still open."

"Then I will put a day against that line as well."

"Put the day against it," said Tamsin Rook, "**and not my name, because if you put my name on eleven lines then in about four years that sheet is a list of things a clerk of twenty-four has been asked, and I am a person who cannot be instructed and I am not going to be a document about a woman of twenty-four who was in a room.**"

He wrote the day against the tenth line and left the name off it, and read the sheet back once and did not read it twice, and the eleventh line was the one neither of them said out loud, which is the one about what a person of twenty has been put into, and she read it, and he let her, and neither of them said anything about it for about a minute.

Then he used the rule, and he used it once, and he wrote it out in a hand and not a form.

> *Marek Kest's choice, the third day of the third week of the ninth month of the year after. Either I go to the office of the seat and ask a question out loud in a room, or I do not. If I go, I am a person who has asked, and a person who has asked is answerable for what he is told. I am not asking for an answer. I am asking to be a person who has asked. — M.K.*

"You are putting a choice," said Tamsin Rook. "In writing. With a day on it. And I have to answer you in a room, in words, with a date, and it is the rule and it is the rule of the twelfth month of last year and it is not a form and it is not on your office and it is the only instrument either of us owns."

"Yes."

"Then I am going to answer it in words and with a date, and the date is today, and my answer is no."

---

She said the reason in the flat way she says things, and she said it as a fact and not as advice, and she has a third line that forbids advice and a fourth line that says a fact is not advice, and she has said the words of both of those in this room before.

"**The office of a seat puts more people through that room in one year than there are lines in any of the eleven books on that shelf. It does not remember them.** It has no book of them, because a book of them would be a register and that office does not keep a register, and that is the entire structural reason the third line works and the entire structural reason nothing can be done about it in a room. And a clerk of about fifty-five in that office is a person who has written about four thousand entries in nineteen years and has never once been asked to remember one of them, and that is a good life and it is not anybody's fault."

"And if you walk in there and ask what the fourth line of a form means, then you are a person that office remembers, because you are the first one, and you will not be remembered as a man who asked a question. You will be remembered as a man who came in about the fourth week of the ninth month of the year after and wanted to know what the words on the boxes were for. And in about four years somebody will come back to that room to look at a return, and the clerk will not remember him, and the room will, and one question from you teaches that room a thing it does not know, which is that the question can be asked, and a room that knows a thing can be asked stops being a room you can ask."

He read the sheet he had written. He did not fold it.

"That is a good reason."

"It is a fact. It is about what rooms are for. I have not advised you and if you take it as advice you are a man who has been advised in a rented room by a woman who is a party and cannot leave it, and I would rather you did not."

---

"You know what I did with it," he said.

"You wrote it in order to be refused."

"Yes."

"And you got refused, and the refusal was a real answer, and it was given in about forty seconds by a person who had already thought about it, and you did not argue." She put her pen down on the table beside the second order. "That is the first time I have believed a document you wrote. Every one of the others was true and every one of them was a man getting a thing ready so that somebody would catch him at it, and I have caught you more times than I can count and I have never once wanted to catch you again, and this is the first one where I did not want to."

He put the sheet in the book with no office on it, at the back, after the four definitions, and shut it.

"That is the rule used once, in writing, with a day on it, and it is the only instrument either of us owns, and it is the first one where the answer is a thing I did not know in the morning."

---

Then he took his left hand off the table and looked at the inside of the wrist, at a line about two inches long, in a room with about four feet of a wall he does not use.

It did not tell him anything. It has not told him anything since the fourth hour of the second of the fifth month of next year, when it was up for about nine seconds, and it does not know where the ford is and it does not know that a roll of households four hundred and thirty miles up this river has a woman of twenty in its fourth column.

"**There is no office in this empire whose business it is to ask me what that is for,**" he said, "and I have got about four feet of wall and a table and a rule I used once and a list of eleven questions with a day on the sheet and an empty twelfth line, and I am not going to write anybody's name on the twelfth line, and I would like it read back to me now, in this room, in words, whether you think that is cowardice."

"It is not," said Tamsin Rook. "It is the first thing you have done since the sixth day of the third month that is not a way of making yourself expensive to look at, and I am not going to soften it and I am not going to be thanked for saying it, and nobody is going to thank me, and there is no form anywhere that requires a person to be thanked."
