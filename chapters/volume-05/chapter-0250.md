# Chapter 250: A Bay, A Lamp Not Lit, And The Fifth Time Anybody Has Asked A Person Out Loud What A Word On A Form Means

The bay is about nine foot by eleven with a bench along the north wall and a lamp in it that is not lit until about the seventh hour, and the water is down about where it has been for a month, and the hold against a company whose whole money is four shillings a year and all of it the rent of a building has not moved since the fourth week of the fourth month of the year after.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the eleventh month of the year after next. There are nine men in that bay, and a foreman of fifty-one behind the bench, and a girl of seventeen at the back on a written engagement at the rate the list is set at, and a man of about thirty from the second gang, a man of about forty from the third gang, a man of about fifty-five with a chain, a man of about twenty-eight, and a man of about thirty-four who has come about four miles.

And a book-keeper of thirty-eight is standing in the doorway of it, which is a thing he has done once in two years and which no foreman on that bank has ever done.

---

Halla Wray said the count out loud before anything else happened, because she is a foreman and a foreman reads.

"Four in about a hundred and forty years," she said. "A court room in the fifth month of the year after that wanted a record. A clerk's window in the first week of the tenth, by a man who was afraid about his own household. A desk in the second week of the eleventh, by a woman of twenty who walked about four miles and nobody sent her. A rented room in the first week of the first month of the year after next, by the same woman, who walked nine miles in a day and asked a man with no office what a word meant, and was answered with a price and a refusal."

**Two of the four were accidents and the third was the first one anybody did on purpose**, and there is no post anywhere in this empire whose job is to ask, and the count has not moved since the second day of the first week of the first month of the year after next.

"Has anybody in here ever done one?"

"Nobody in this bay has ever done one," said Halla Wray, "and nobody sent anybody, and I want the man who has come about four miles to understand that in front of nine men before he does anything at all, because a person who does a thing in here and can be told afterwards that it was arranged is going to wish he had not done it."

---

The man of about thirty-four came the whole way into the bay, which nobody does.

"I understand it," he said. "And I am not going to be able to say afterwards that anybody made me do it, and I have thought about that for about six weeks in a kitchen and about four in a yard and I have not found a better way of doing it and I am not going to ask anybody to give me one."

"Then say what you came to say, and say it quickly, because there are nine men in here and the lamp is not lit."

---

He turned round, once, and looked at the back of the bay, and then he said the thing he had come to say first, and it was not the question.

"That is the one person in this bay who could read a hold out loud in about four minutes," he said, to a foreman and to the bay and to the back of it, "and I have worked out a reason tonight and last week why I am not going to ask her. I have not got it from a foreman and I have not got it from a book-keeper and I have not got it from the man with the chain, and I would like it noticed that all three of them have given a reason and none of them has been this one."

---

"Go on."

"She would say yes. That is the whole of it and it took me ten days. She is seventeen and she is on a written engagement at the rate the list is set at and she has not been thanked, and if a foreman asks her a question like that in front of nine men then she cannot say no, not because anybody has stopped her, but because there is nothing in this empire she can do about being asked in a room. And in about four years there will be a person who can say that a girl of seventeen stood in a bay on this cut and read a hold out loud, and there is no form anywhere that requires a person to say it and no form anywhere that says she may refuse to have said it."

---

"A man of about fifty-five with a chain gave a better reason than that in a yard in the third week of the sixth month of the year after next and he gave it two months before you worked yours out, and it was better than mine, and I have said so in a bay and I am saying so again." She had not moved. "**And it is not better, and I have had about two months to be sure of that, and I do not know what better is and I am not going to have it.**"

"Three reasons have been given to me and I have not acted on one of them and I am not going to act on a fourth, and you have not given me a reason. You have given me a fact about a person of seventeen. I have had a fact about a person of seventeen since the fourth day of the fourth week of the seventh month of the year after next and it is in nine mouths and it is in no book and it is not going anywhere, and I cannot do one single thing with it."

---

"I am not asking her," said the man of about thirty-four. "I have not asked her and I am not going to and nobody is going to send anybody to her and there is not a form in this empire for sending anybody to her."

"I know you are not. That is what makes it a fact and not a reason." She put her hands flat on the bench. "Say your question."

---

He had it ready for six weeks, and he had said it once in a kitchen to a woman with a plate in her hand and twice to a wall nine miles from a forge, and it took him about four seconds to say it in a bay.

"**Foreman.** What does the word *representative* mean?"

---

Nobody in that bay made a sound, and every man in it looked at a foreman who has been behind that bench since before the eldest of them could walk.

Then Halla Wray answered it, and she answered it in a foreman's voice, and it took her about four sentences and then about four minutes, and nobody in that bay had ever heard the second four minutes of anything she said.

"I have been in a box since the first week of the seventh month of the year after and I have never been asked a single thing about it, and I worked out about a minute and a half ago what it is for in the second week of the third month of the year after next. I have not told one person, and you are the first, and you came about four miles, and I am going to say it out loud in a bay because I said in the second week of the ninth month of the year after next that I would, and I have not improved on that."

---

"**The form was not asking me whether I would represent anybody.** It was asking who I represent, and there is a column for the answer, and there is no column for a refusal. That is the whole of it. A person writes a thing in a column and the column says *and if not*, and there is no column that says *no*, and there is not one in this empire in any form I have ever seen, and that is why my no did not work when I said it out loud in a room with the day on it. Not because a clerk did anything wrong. Because there was nowhere to put it."

"About nine hundred of us," said a man of about thirty from the second gang.

"About nine hundred of you this year, by a class, and that is roughly right because that is what a class does." She had not turned round. "And a class is a heading. And a heading is how a person becomes a company, and a company is a thing this empire can hold wages against and write letters to and take to law, and a man who is a person is a thing it can only send papers to. That is the finding. That took a clerk of about fifty-five about two days in eleven books in an office nine miles off a building to work out, and she has entered a question about it in a minute book with the day on it and her own name at the foot of it, on her own motion, because nobody asked her to, and it is a question and not an answer and there is not one other thing in this empire anybody has done about it."

---

Then she said the other thing, and she said it to the bay, and it is the reason the day happened.

"**It has not got me out and it is not going to.** There is not one form anywhere in this empire that will un-fill a box. Not in this room and not in the office nine miles off and not in any building either of us has ever been inside. The person who would administer one if there were one is a clerk of about fifty-five who has nineteen years of minutes about a thing she has never been in a room with, and she is not a villain, and she has done about nine hundred of them correctly, and she has never once been asked whether a box ought to have been filled."

"And the other one is still in one," said a man of about forty from the third gang.

"**The other one is still in one too.** She is about four hundred and thirty miles up this river in a room two streets off a street that goes down to the river road, with sixteen books on a table and a wage that has been held since the second week of the third month of the year after. She said no out loud on the fourth day of the third week of the fifth month of the year after and the no did not work, and one of her books has her own name at the head of it, and she has decided to go and tell four women that she is not paid. Nobody in this bay has got her out and nobody in this bay is going to get her out. I am in a box on this bank and I am going to be in a box when this season shuts and I have known that since the seventh month of the year after."

---

And then a man of about thirty-four said the other thing, out loud, to that bay, and it is the cost and he gave it away like a man giving a tool back.

"You are all going to want to know what this has made me," he said, "and I would like to say it before anybody asks, because a person who has asked a question in a room is a party from that day on, and a party is the only person who can be told what a thing is going to be used for. **I have a trade and a house with a door on it and a wife and three of them at the table, and I told her on purpose in a kitchen without asking her first**, and there is no form in this empire for asking a person whether they will mind being told something, and from today I am a person who can be served with a paper about what a foreman said in this bay. And there is no fund behind any of it, and I am not going to be able to say afterwards that anybody made me do it, and that is what I bought, and I bought it with about six weeks of a man's life and a number on the back of my own hand that is gone."

---

Nobody thanked him.

He had not expected it, and not one man in that bay thanked him, and a foreman of fifty-one did not thank him and he did not say he wanted to be. There is not one form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be thanked for asking a question, and there is never going to be one, and a reader of seventeen at the back of that bay has not been thanked for anything in her life and was not thanked now and is not going to start.

**And nobody sent anybody to her, and nobody is going to, and she was not asked, and she is not going to be asked.**

---

Then the foreman said the last of it, in the ordinary voice, standing behind a bench.

"About four people in this bay cannot read a paragraph and have never been asked what the hold is, and I am not asking them, and nobody is going to ask them. There is a finding in a ledger about four hundred yards up that road in a book-keeper's own hand with the day on it that says the four are not to be told this season. It is not mine to improve and I am not going to improve it, and what you have just done in this bay has not changed one word of it and was never going to."

"I said before I asked that it had not."

"You did, and I noticed, and I am not going to thank you for noticing either." She picked up the pencil. "**And that is the price of the fifth, and you paid it on purpose, and I made it, because I said in a bay in the second week of the ninth month of the year after next that I am in a box**, and a man walked about four miles because of it."

---

Then the ordinary things, and there were nine of them before the lamp.

A man of about twenty-eight asked whether a person could be on two lists at once and she told him to take the paper with him and put it back the way he found it. A man of about twenty asked whether the season had been agreed to and she told him it had not been agreed to and had not been refused either, and that a third thing had happened and that a third thing is what a season is. A man of about fifty came in and asked her about the third link of a chain, and she told him about the third link and the week before it wants oil, in about two minutes, out loud, and without turning round from behind the bench. The men in that bay watched a foreman do a perfectly ordinary thing to a man who cannot read a paragraph, and not one of them noticed anything at all.

---

The man of about thirty-four said his decision in the doorway, on his way out, to a book-keeper of thirty-eight who had been standing in it for the whole of it and had not said one word, and it is the last thing anybody said on that bank that year.

"I am not going to do that again," he said. "Not because it is wrong and not because of the four in here. Because a person who does a thing twice has made a practice, and a practice grows a heading over it on its own without anybody deciding anything, and a heading is how a person becomes a company, and a foreman told me ten minutes ago that a company is a thing this empire can take to law. I have had one question in my life that I asked on purpose and I am going to go on being a man on a list of works four times a season with a fair hand on somebody else's page, and that is the whole of what I have bought and I am not going to buy any more of it."

---

**And a man of about thirty-eight stood in a doorway and did not say anything, and did not write it down**, and a foreman of fifty-one went back to a list of works with nine men in it, and a man of about thirty-four went down about four hundred yards with a day he is not going to forget and a paper about him that does not exist.

**Four askings in about a hundred and forty years, and a fifth one in a bay on the fourth day of the fourth week of the eleventh month of the year after next**, and the count is five, and the third of the first four was the first one anybody did on purpose and the fifth was the second, and there is still no post anywhere in this empire whose job is to ask, and nobody is appointed, and nothing has been written down. The four who cannot read a paragraph have still never been asked one question, and the two who are in their boxes are still in their boxes, and the guarantee is still standing offered and unanswered on about four hundred and forty foot of that bank with about sixty children under sixteen inside it. The bill at Lowcross is still nineteen pounds three and fourpence and unpaid, and about nineteen people are still behind the bank, and a reader of seventeen was not thanked and is not going to start.

**And the largest thing anybody has said out loud this year is in five mouths and in no book at all**, and it is not going to be answered by anybody who is standing on that bank, and the river is still running, and the lamp is not lit.
