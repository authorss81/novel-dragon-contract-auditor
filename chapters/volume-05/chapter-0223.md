# Chapter 223: Four Definitions And Not One Of Them Says Office

The room is rented by the week and it has a table in it and a chair and a bed that is against the wall he does not use, and about four feet of that wall is bare, and he has never once worked out what it is for and he has never once covered it.

Marek Kest is thirty-eight. He has no office and no fund and no register and nothing in this empire he could certify anything with, and a bar of his own that came off him on the twelfth of the ninth month of last year on an application of one line which he wrote himself. There is a line about two inches long on the inside of his left wrist and it reads nothing, and it decides nothing, and it does not know where the ford is.

He had not read the four definitions out loud since the second month of the year after, and he read them out loud on the second day of the third week of the third month of the year after next, standing, in a rented room, to nobody.

---

The sheet is one side of a sheet of good paper in a fair hand, in ink, on foolscap, and it came into his hands in the second month of the year after, and it is the only document in this matter he has never wanted to give back.

Four definitions and a provision. The third of the four is the one everybody has argued about for about a year and it turns on a roll of households, and a roll of households is a list of households and not a list of what anybody owns, and it is a book a district keeps because a district has to know who is in it, and nobody has ever been asked whether the family on that book holds anything at all.

He read all four, twice, the second reading slower, which is a habit he got from somebody else and has never told anybody about.

Then he sat down and did the thing he had been putting off since the seventh month of the year after, which is to read the four of them looking for one word.

---

There is not one.

Not *holder*. Not *office*. Not *person*. Not *seat*. There is a word for a family and a word for a claim and a word for a dispute and a word for a thing that is recorded, and there is a person of that family who is of age and resident, and there is a person who may be entered, and there is a person who is not to be entered, and not one of the four definitions has a word in it for a person who holds an office and does a thing in the name of it.

**He wrote that down, in the fourth week of the ninth month of the year after, in a book with no office on it, in one line, and he read it back, and it is the eleventh line, and it has not been a question since the fourth week of the first month of the year after next.**

"A document is the only thing anybody can be held to." He said it out loud to the table. "A document is the only thing anybody can be held to, and the absence is in a document, and a thing that is not in a document cannot be held to anybody at all. **So the person who holds the office cannot be held to anything, and the office cannot be held to anything either, because an office is not a person, and the four definitions are the document and the office is not in them, and that is not an oversight. That is a hole somebody cut, and I have known that since the seventh month and I have not been able to find the hand.**"

He put the sheet down on the table and looked at the four feet of wall.

---

Then he opened the book with no office on it and looked at the twelve lines in it, and there is a day against the sheet and not against the lines.

Eleven lines with something on them and a twelfth with nothing at all, and he has not filled the twelfth and is not going to, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that requires a man to fill a twelfth line, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that requires him to leave it empty, and that is the whole of the difference between the two things and nobody in this matter can pay for it.

**A fact on a line in a book with no office on it is the worst of the two, because a question can be answered and a fact can only be repeated.**

He said that one out loud too, and then he shut the book, and the twelfth line was still empty, and he had a fortnight to decide anything about it and had decided nothing on about four days of that.

---

Then he took out a pencil, which he has not done in this room since the second month of the year after, and put the point on a clean sheet of paper after the twelve lines, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for a man to be issued with a clean sheet of paper, and he has had this one in a drawer since the fourth week of the first month of the year after next, and it is the only paper in this room that is his.

He has a word in his head that a clerk of about fifty-five said once, in a room with a rail in it, in the first week of the fifth month of the year after, and then stopped, and did not say anything else about it, and would not describe the hand and would not say which of two papers of the same office it was. The name is written in about nine hundred documents in this empire. It is not on any of the boards in this city. A court asked about it nine years ago in a room with a number on the door and it has not been answered by the court that asked it.

He put the point on the clean sheet and he did not write it, and then he put the clean sheet in the stove and it went up in about four seconds, and the twelve lines are the only lines in this matter with anything on them.

"**No,**" he said.

---

He said the rest of it out loud because a room is the only kind of place in this empire where a thing like that can be said and he has never once said it to a person and he has said it to this table about four times.

"Nine hundred documents carry that word and not one of them is mine and every one of them is a sheet of paper in a building with a door on it, and a man with a copy of the right page can have that word out of any of them in about a minute, and if it is also in my hand in a book with no office on it then there is a thing in this empire that has my name near it, and in about four years there is a person who can put the two side by side and ask me what I meant by it."

"**A man who cannot be found must not try to be found by writing down the name of a thing**, and that is the reason nobody in this matter is going to be, and he has known it since the seventh month of the year after."

He put the pencil back in the drawer with the two dead ones.

---

There was a knock on the door at about the half hour and it was Tamsin Rook, and she did not open it.

"You are in," she said through about two inches of door.

"I am in."

"I know you are in, I can hear a man in a room. I am not going to come in and I am going to tell you why and then I am going to go, and I have not got anything to carry and I have not come about a form."

"About what, then."

"About whether you are all right," said Tamsin Rook, "and I am not going to be told, because you will say yes."

---

"You have been sitting in there for about an hour doing something with a pencil and then not doing it," she said, "and I have been at the counter and I have been thinking about a thing that a man of about thirty who carries trays up a corridor told a clerk of thirty-four in a street on the second day of the first week of this month."

"He told her that a clerk of about fifty-five said out loud that a question written down is not an answer. In a room with a tray in it. **And she has not written one word of it down, and I have not written one word of it down, and I have not asked her for one word of it, and a man who carries trays has told a clerk of thirty-four in a street, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that puts a street in a book.**"

"How many times has a thing got out of a room for nothing this year."

"That is the second one I have heard about and I have been in this matter for about a year and I am not going to count them in a doorway," said Tamsin Rook. "And I am not going to be the one who starts counting. **The first person who counts is the first person who has made it a thing that can be asked about**, and I have watched about nine people make a thing out of a habit in my time in these bays and every single one of them ended up with a heading over it."

---

"Why are you not coming in," he said.

"Because a room you are in and a person who is in it is a party, and I have said in a bay in the fourth week of the first month of the year after next that I would say no to anybody who asked me to go and ask a question in a room on a bank four hundred and thirty miles down this river. I said it in a room and you were in it, and nobody is going to ask me and I have made sure of that myself."

"And this is not that, and I want that said. **This is a woman standing in a doorway in a city nine miles from an office of a seat, and if I come through this door then the two of us are in a room, and the room is the instrument, and there is no form that says what a room is for.**"

"That is the third time you have not let me argue with you."

"That is the third time and I have counted them, and the counting is mine and not yours, and I am not going to be thanked for it."

---

"Then tell me one thing through the door," he said, "and it is a plain question and not a question about a word on a form, and if it is about a word on a form I will shut up."

"Go on."

"You told a clerk of thirty-four in a street in the ninth month of the year after that a document with your hand at the foot of it does not stay about you. And I have had a pencil in my hand for about an hour and I have not written down the name of a thing that is in about nine hundred documents, and I want to know whether that is your sentence or a different one."

"It is your sentence and you are using it correctly," said Tamsin Rook, "**and I am not going to improve it, and if you use it on everything then in about four years you will have a book that is a form and no office on it, and that is the worst thing that can happen to a man with nothing, because a form is a machine and a machine needs somebody to be told they may use it.**"

---

"You have never once asked me whether I have decided," she said, from the stair.

"No."

"And you are not going to now, and I am not going to tell you, and that is correct, and it is the third time we have both not done it and I have worked out what that is and I am not going to say it in a doorway." She stopped on the stair for about four seconds. "Two pieces of evidence is not a decision. Three is not either, and there is a clerk in this city who has been told something true once and has been carrying the price of it since the first week of the tenth month, and being told a third true thing would not make her any safer, and I have watched that arithmetic get done twice in this matter by a person who was pleased with it and I am not going to do it a third time."

The door stayed as it was.

---

He stood in the room with the four definitions on the table and the book with no office on it shut on his knee, and he had about nine minutes before he had to be somewhere he did not want to be, and he used about four of them going through the count, because he is a person who counts and there is no form anywhere in this empire for counting anything.

**Four in about a hundred and forty years, and here they are with the day against each, because I am a person who counts and there is no form anywhere in this empire for counting anything.** A court room in the fifth month of the year after that wanted a record. A clerk's window in the first week of the tenth, by a man afraid about his own household. A desk in the second week of the eleventh, by a woman of twenty who walked about four miles. A rented room in the first week of the first month of the year after next, by the same woman, who walked about nine miles in one day. Two of the four were accidents and nobody chose either of them. The third was the first one anybody did on purpose. The fourth was the same person on purpose again. And in the ten weeks since, nobody has asked anybody anything, and there is no post in this empire whose job is to ask, and a clerk of about fifty-five has put a name on the question itself in about nine seconds and a man with a tray has carried it into a street for nothing, and neither of those is an asking, and the count is still four.

He shut the book with the twelfth line empty and put the four definitions back in the drawer with the two dead pencils, and there is no form in this empire that requires a man to fill a line and no form in this empire that requires him to leave it empty, and he has left it empty, and a woman in a bay four bays and a corridor away did not come up a stair, and a man of about thirty who carries trays said a thing in a street that a clerk of thirty-four wrote nothing down about, and that is the whole of the third week of the third month of the year after next in a room with a table in it and about four feet of a wall he does not use.
