# Chapter 227: Nine Miles A Second Time, And The Word He Would Not Put Down

She walked about nine miles on the fourth day of the third week of the fourth month of the year after next and she did it in one day and in about eleven hours with two stops, and she had the address in her head and not on a paper, and the reason she had the address in her head is that a clerk of thirty-four said it out loud at a counter in the second week of the ninth month of the year after and then said it again in the fourth week of the first month of the year after next, in about nine seconds, for nothing, and would not say why.

Nell Kest is twenty. She is a dye-house worker and her wage is held and there are now sixteen books in a room two streets off a street that goes down to the river road and not one of them has an office on it. She was in the room in a rented room about nine miles from a building off that road.

**She was let in because a person who is not from the office that cannot be named has to be present, and that person was standing at the table with a sheet of paper in her hand when she came, and nobody sent her, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for a person who comes on her own account.**

---

"You have not sent for me," said Nell Kest, from the stair, because she had a hand on the rail and had not been let in yet.

"Nobody sent for you," said Tamsin Rook. "A clerk of thirty-four told you where this room was at a counter in a few seconds and would not write it down, and you walked nine miles, and I was not told and I would not have been able to stop you."

"Then why are you standing at the table."

"Because of the other instrument," said Tamsin Rook, "and I am not going to say its name out loud to you because it is not mine, and it is the reason I am here and it is the reason you are being let up a stair, and neither of us has ever merged the two and I am not going to start in a doorway."

---

Marek Kest did not get up. He has a rented room with a table in it and a book with no office on it and about four feet of a wall he does not use, and he has no office and no fund and no register and nothing in this empire he could certify anything with, and a bar of his own that came off him on the twelfth of the ninth month of last year on an application of one line which he wrote himself.

"There is a line about two inches long on the inside of my left wrist," he said, "and it reads nothing, and I am going to say that now and then I am not going to mention it again this afternoon."

"Go on," said Nell Kest.

"It does not decide anything and it does not know where the ford is and it told nobody anything in the fourth week of the tenth month of the year after, and I have no way of asking anybody to be told anything about it, and that is the whole of what I have got and I have said it in a room twice."

---

"Then what are you answerable for," said Nell Kest.

He put his hand down off the table.

"**For an answer I gave you in the first week of the first month, in a rented room, in this city, when you had walked nine miles, and I could not give it to you and I gave you a price instead.** And there is nobody in this empire who can look me up. I want that said in that order and not the other way round. The answer came first and the unfindability is not the reason for it, it is the reason nobody ever found out about it."

"And I am answerable for asking," said Nell Kest.

"You are answerable for asking, and you are in a box at the other end of a column that a clerk filled in in about nine seconds in the fifth month of the year after, and if anybody comes with a copy of the right page in about four years then you are the one they will come to and I am the one they will not."

---

Tamsin Rook said nothing for about four minutes and then she said the thing she had been working on since the fourth day of the first week of the third month, in a bay, and she said it standing, which is how she gives things.

"That is the finding," she said, "and it has taken me since the fourth day of the first week of the third month, and about nine people have said parts of it, and it is this. **The only person in this matter who can be found is the one who is answerable for asking, and the only person who is answerable and cannot be found is the one who answered.** Every answerable person in this empire so far has been a clerk, and a clerk has a window or a counter or a table, and a clerk is the cheapest person in a city to send anywhere, and I have been one for two years."

"You have never been sent," said Marek Kest.

"I have never been sent," said Tamsin Rook, "and that is the good news and I have had two years of it and it is not as good as it sounds, because the reason nobody sent me is that nobody wanted anything sent."

---

Then Nell Kest asked the question she had walked nine miles to ask, and it was not about a word on a form, and both of the other people in the room said so out loud, in their own mouths, and said why.

"**Are you the most dangerous person in this matter,** and if you are, is it the same dangerous as the one a clerk of about fifty-five has a record of, or is it a different dangerous, and is there anybody in this empire who can protect you, and if there is not, is that because you would not let them."

Marek Kest did not answer it for about a minute.

"Yes," he said. "There is nobody who can protect me and that is not a misfortune. **There is nothing anybody can instruct me about, nothing anybody can pay me for, and nothing anybody can thank me for.** That is why he is the most dangerous person in this matter and it is also why nobody in it can stand in front of him. A person nobody can pay cannot be bought and cannot be threatened and cannot be shown a bill. A person nobody can thank cannot be repaid. And it is the same sentence as the one about a person with a discharged bar, and it is the same sentence as the one about a foreman nobody can serve, and I have heard all three of those said out loud in three rooms about four hundred and thirty miles apart and I am not going to pretend I did not hear them."

---

"And what is it worth you," said Nell Kest, "being that, with a woman of twenty standing in this room who can be found and you who cannot."

"About four years," he said, "and about fourpence a copy and about a shilling a question, and I have not got any of it. And I could be handed a hundred pounds tomorrow by a person who wanted me to stop and I would have to be able to refuse it, and there is no form in this empire that makes a man refuse a hundred pounds. **The only thing standing between me and being paid is that nobody in this empire has anything to pay me for, and that is not a defence and it is not a wall and it is a thing that is true this afternoon and not necessarily on a day in the next week.**"

---

Tamsin Rook put her pen down, which she does about twice a year.

"Then we are both of them," she said.

"We are not," said Nell Kest. "That is the part I have come nine miles for and it is the part I am going to be unhappy about for about a year. **He cannot be found and I can be found, and that is the whole asymmetry, and it means that the one person in this matter who could answer for him is the one person who could not be left to do it twice.** And I have got four people I was going to ask and I have asked two of them, and one of the two is him, and I have just found out what asking him costs and I am going to pay it, and I am not going to ask the other two this month."

"You knew that in the first week of the first month," said Marek Kest.

"I knew that in the second week of the eleventh month of the year after and I have been arguing with myself about it ever since and I have lost," said Nell Kest.

---

Then Marek Kest said the thing he had come to say and had said to a table on the second day of the third week of the third month of the year after next and had not said to a person until then.

"There is a word in this matter that a clerk of about fifty-five said once, in a room with a rail in it, in the first week of the fifth month of the year after, and then stopped and would not say anything else about it, and would not describe the hand and would not say which of two papers of the same office it came from. It is written in about nine hundred documents in this empire and it is not on any of the boards in this city and a court asked about it nine years ago and has not been answered."

"You have got it in your head," said Nell Kest.

"I have got it in my head and I have not written it down and I am not going to, and I have not written it down since the seventh month of the year after, and I have not said it to a person until this afternoon."

"Then write it down," said Nell Kest. "You have a book with no office on it and you have a hand. Write it down and then there is a record of it and if anybody comes in about four years there is a thing they can put a name against."

---

"That is exactly why I am not going to," said Marek Kest, "and it has taken me the whole of the third month to be able to say it to a person, and the reason is the one you have just used against me without knowing you were using it. **Nine hundred documents in this empire have that word in them, and I would not be the first person to have it in a hand, and I would be the first person to have it in a hand and nothing else behind it.** Every other person in this empire who has that word in a hand is a clerk, or a factor, or a man in a building with about nine hundred and forty names on a shelf, and every one of them has a trade, and the trade is the wall. I have got no trade. I have got a bar that came off on one line and about four feet of a wall I do not use. A word in my hand is the only thing anybody could put a name to, and there is nothing about me for the name to stick to, and that is the whole of why I am the most dangerous person in this matter, said by a person of twenty who has walked nine miles and is standing in the room."

---

Tamsin Rook had not moved and had not written anything down and she had said the price out loud at about the fifth minute and nobody had thanked her, because nobody was going to.

"Say the rest of it," she said to him.

"The rest of it is that there are two things I could be and I have not chosen between them and I am not going to choose in a room at about the third hour of the afternoon, and I will lay them out and leave them. A liability I cannot refuse. Or a labour I can be refused. The first is the shape of four years and it costs nothing and nobody has to pay anything and there is no form for it. The second is the shape of a road, about four hundred and thirty miles each way, about six days, and a man who can be told to stop because somebody is paying for the day. Both of them are true of me. I have not got a form that says which one I am, and there is no form in this empire for that, and I am not going to invent one at about the fourth hour with two people in the room."

"You have not chosen because choosing is a decision about what you are," said Tamsin Rook, "and you have not got one, and the twelfth line of your sheet is empty and it has been empty since the third week of the ninth month of the year after and it is the only honest thing in that book."

"That is the second time somebody has said that to me about the twelfth line."

"It is the first time I have said it and I have wanted to say it since the first week of the tenth month, and I am saying it now because she asked a question in a room and got an answer, and a person who gets an answer is a person somebody has answered, and there are two of us in this empire and one of us is twenty and one of us has nothing at all, and I have been the price of a party out loud ten times and nobody has thanked me once and I did not expect to be thanked once and I would like it written down that I did not."

---

Then she went out of the door and down the stair and did not shut it, and Marek Kest said which instrument it was after she had gone, out loud, to a room with a table in it, because a form gets merged by being inconvenient.

A watch does not send anybody anywhere. It is not in force now and it is not spent. It requires only that a person who is not from the office that cannot be named be present, and about six hundred people in this city could satisfy it, and nobody has ever been paid for satisfying it, and nobody has ever been thanked.

Nell Kest stood in the room for another about nine minutes and then said, "I am not going to write this down either."

"Then there is no document in this empire that says you were ever in this room," said Marek Kest.

"No," said Nell Kest. "**And I want it said that I worked that out in a few seconds and that I worked it out because of a woman of twenty-four who did exactly the same thing in the third week of the twelfth month of the year after, and told a man of about thirty-four at a counter about fourpence that she was not going to write his answer down and then told him what the price of it was a second time.** And that is the good news and it is the bad news, and I am going to walk nine miles back and I am not going to have anybody pay for the day."
