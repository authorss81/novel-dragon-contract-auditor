# Chapter 0612: The Room Taken By The Week, And A Jug Neither Of Them Has Emptied

She carried that jug out to the pail at the end of that landing and filled it and brought it back in five weeks ago, and this morning she came up at the first hour and did not come up at the fourth, and neither of them has emptied it since.

It is the second day of the second week of the seventh month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the third hour.

---

The chair is where it has been since a spring that is gone, with its back to that window and its face to four feet of plaster, and the stool is back against the wall under that window with the catch on it still turned, and the glass with the top left corner gone off it is face up near the edge of that table. One coin lies about four inches in from that edge, put there by a hand at the first hour about two years ago and still lying where it was put. The coat is on the nail. The door stands open about a hand's width.

The jug is about a foot back from that edge with about two inches of water in the bottom of it.

---

"That water has been standing five weeks."

"It has."

"You have not used it."

"No."

"You have not let it go off either. You did that once and I have watched you do it, and this is not that."

"I know it is not that."

---

She came the length of that floor and stopped at the near side of that table and did not look at the middle of it, and he stood by the wall with his hands behind his back like a man waiting for something he has arranged.

"You want me to ask you what that is."

"No."

"You do not want me to ask you what that is."

"No."

"You have been in this room with a thing on that table for four weeks and you have not said one word about it, and I have not asked, and that has been the arrangement of this room for as long as there has been an arrangement in it."

"There has not been an arrangement in it for five weeks. There has been a jug."

"There has been a jug and a sheet of paper and a door that is open, and I would like one of those three explained, and I am not going to ask, and you can decide for yourself what to do with that."

---

**What I want is a question. I have wanted one out of this room for about four weeks and it is the first thing I have wanted in about ten years and I am not going to be able to have it, and the reason is that there is one way and one way only to get a thing said out loud in this city, and it is somebody asking in a room.

Asking is a party. A party is a person, and a person is a thing that can be shown having asked, and there is nobody in nine hundred buildings that can be shown not having asked.**

She has come up that stair for about twenty-five weeks and has wanted one single thing out of me and it has been nothing every time, and nothing is the whole of what she wants and it is also the whole of what she is owed, and neither of us is going to be thanked for it and neither of us is going to be asked about it.

And a jug of clean water that she carried in with her own two hands is the first thing anybody has done for me in this city since before a fire, and I have not been able to pay for it and I am not going to say one word about it.

There are about two inches of it standing on my table going off at the rate water goes off, and that is the most expensive thing in this house and I put it there by not touching it.

And the sheet is in the middle of that table where I squared it against the wood that morning with the printing at the head of it turned to the wall, and a woman put her hand flat on it four weeks ago and said out loud that there was nothing written on it, and it is still there, and it is not going to be picked up, and I did not put it there to be picked up.

---

She took the glass off that table and carried it out along that landing to the pail and washed it there and brought it back and set it down near the edge again with the broken corner the same way round, and she did not touch the jug.

"I am not going to empty that for you. You carried it out or you did not carry it out, and both of those are yours, and if you want water you can go to that pail at the end of that landing in the morning like anybody in this house."

"That is fair."

"It is not fair. It is a jug. I have been up and down that pail twice a day for twenty-five weeks and I have not once asked you for anything, and I am telling you where the water is, and you can do what you like with it."

And she went out through that door and went down past the second tread, and there was nothing on that stair after that, and she has never knocked.

---

There is about two inches of water in that jug and it has been five weeks, and it will be six weeks tomorrow at the first hour, and both of them will be in that room looking at it.
