# Chapter 0583: A Room Taken By The Week, And A Man Carrying A Glass To That Table Four Times In A Fortnight

He had carried a glass to that table and put it back four times in a fortnight, and this morning he put it face up on the table about a foot back from the edge and did not pick it up again for the rest of that hour. It went down flat with the frame on the wood and no hand left on it afterwards, and the light off that window lies down by the stool and does not reach a table standing outside it.

It is the second day of the first week of the twelfth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the first hour and about the fourth.

---

The glass came with the room the way the lamp did, and it is about four inches by five in a frame of wood with the top left corner gone off it, and there is a chip out of the middle of one edge where somebody put a thumb on it a long time before he came. It is a small thing and it can be carried in one hand, and a man who does not want to be holding it does not hold it.

It has been on the table at the end of that bed for about two years and he has never once looked into it, and there is nothing else in that room that would give him anything back. A jug stands in the corner by the door with about two inches of water in it, and there is a coin on the table, and the glass is the only thing in the room that has anybody in it.

The rest of that floor has not moved in about two years. The bed is against the wall and the stool is under the window, and the chair is where he dragged it a spring that is gone so that its back is to the window and its face is to four feet of plaster, and the coat is on a nail and there is nothing else on that nail or under it. He is thirty-eight. He stands in no register in this city and has not gone into one. The nail went in crooked and it is up at the level a man's own hand comes to, and it sits in the middle of those four feet of plaster the chair is looking at.

---

The glass is the only thing in that room he has never used, and he has never used the lamp either, and both of them came from whoever had that room before him and neither of them was bought for anybody in particular. He has a table with a coin on it and a bed he does not make up and a floor he sweeps about four mornings a week, and that is the whole of the housekeeping of a man who takes a room by the week and does not intend to be in it for a week longer than the week costs. The lamp stands on the floor at the end of the bed and the wood about a hand's width across underneath it is burnt in one spot where somebody got a fire off the iron and then let it die, and the lamp has not been lit in about two years, and it is the only thing in that room that is worse than useless.

**I have worked out in about a fortnight what a person stands in a place for, and it is not for the thing that is said there. It is that a person who stands in a place cannot afterwards be shown having been anywhere else, and that is the whole of what I have had for about ten years, and I have had it by never once standing still.**

Every room I have ever been in I have been in the whole time and I have not stopped in any of them, and that is not a discipline. There was nothing to be gained by stopping and nothing to be lost by it, and so I did not do it. Now there is a person in a corridor somewhere in this city who has stopped four times in a fortnight at the end of a sill and worked out the whole of it in about a week, and I have been the other sort for ten years and I am not going to be able to be hers this month. She has had a fortnight and I have had ten years, and I have not got as far as she got on her first morning, and the whole of the difference is that she started at the end of a stone and I have started at nothing.

There is no way of standing still in a place and being unfindable at the same time. One of the two goes, and I have not worked out which, and I am not going to be the one who works it out in a room with a second person in it. I have a room of about eleven foot by fourteen and every one of those fourteen foot knows what I did in it this morning, and that is not a complaint, it is an inventory.

---

A boot came on the second tread at about the first hour and stopped there, and it was not the first morning it had stopped there, and she had not said anything about that either. She stood on it with her hand in the front of her apron and did not come the rest of the way up, and the stair behind her made no sound at all while she was standing there.

"You have had a fortnight of not saying anything."

"I have not said anything for about two years."

"That is not the same and you know that it is not."

"It is not the same."

The coin went under that door and came to rest about four inches in, and it was going to keep going to the far wall if that wall had been nearer. The corner of the mat went down under her boot and came back up when she lifted it. The door did not open, and nothing came through the gap under it, and the coin stayed where it stopped.

Then her boot went on down past the second tread and the stair went quiet, and neither of them said one word about a fortnight, and neither of them is going to be asked about one.

---

He put the lamp down on the floor at the end of that bed where it lives and he did the first hour at the table, and at about the second hour he picked the glass up off the wood and turned it round so that he could see himself in it, and there was a man of thirty-eight in it. He had to carry it over to the light by the stool to get anything at all out of it, because that patch is the only light there is in the room, and he held it a good deal longer than the looking took.

He is not a bad-looking man. That is the whole of what came of it. He had not wanted to know it and he got it, and there was nothing else in that glass at all, no coat and no room and no sill and no armful and no second person. A looking-glass is the only thing in this empire that will put a person back into a place they have left, and it takes a person out of the one they are standing in while it is doing it. For about a minute he was a man of thirty-eight holding a small broken thing in a room with a shut door in it, and then he was a man of thirty-eight in the same room with nothing in his hands.

He put it down face up on the table about a foot back from the edge and left it there for the rest of that day. It lay there face up on the wood for the rest of that day, and the coin was on the same table and neither of them was moved.

---

A coin went under that door every working morning of about two years. There is one of them on that table and nothing in that room would take it, and he is not going to put it back under the door and he is not going to spend it. A man of thirty-eight can now be shown to have been in that room on a morning at about the first hour, because his own face was in that glass at the second hour. Nobody in this city is ever going to be shown the glass and nobody is going to be thanked for the face, and he is not going to put the glass face down. The door is still shut and there is no way to write a man down in any of that and there is no way to write him out of it either.
