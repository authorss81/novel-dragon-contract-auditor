# Chapter 0559: A Second Bundle Left At The End Of A Bench By A Foreman Who Has Worked Out That More Of It Is Not The Same As The Chance To Say No

She had begun leaving two bundles at the end of that bench instead of one, and the girl had taken both of them, and nothing had been said in that bay for nine mornings, and this morning she took one of them back.

It is the second day of the first week of the sixth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour.

---

She is fifty-one. Eleven years of that bay and every one of them at the back. There is a paper on the board by that door that nobody has ever filled in. A class does not need to do anything to you. It only has to leave you where it found you, and where it found her is where she still is.

Twice a month for two months she came the whole way down that floor and laid her hand on the wood at the front of that bench and kept it there until the sixth hour, and the chain went along about the width of two fingers from it the whole time. The shed's own looking did not get done for an hour on two of those mornings and the day's count came out one short and nobody in that shed was told why. She has not come down the length of her own floor since. That is a fortnight old and there is nobody to notice a foreman standing at the back of a bench.

---

The first bundle was left about nine mornings a month and the girl put it back three feet along the bench twice in a fortnight without saying a word, and the foreman put it at the end again and the girl took it.

The foreman worked out over about a week what she actually wanted, which was for a thing to be put in that girl's reach and to mean that she could turn it down. What she got instead was a girl who had decided twice and could not be shown having decided either time.

That is the whole of the difficulty and it took nine mornings a month to get to it.

Then she did the obvious thing, and the obvious thing is more of it.

---

She began leaving a second bundle at that end.

The reason she gave herself, standing at the back of that bench, is that a foreman who puts work at the end of a bench has offered a person the chance to decline it, and the trouble was that the chance had only ever been about one bundle, and one bundle is a thing that can be done without saying anything, and two bundles is not a thing that can be done without saying something.

That is what she thought on the first morning of it. It took her until the fourth to find out that a girl of seventeen does not work that way.

The girl took both of them. She lifted them onto her own in the way she has always lifted that work, without her face changing, and she took the first one and then the second one. She put them down where she puts things down, and there was no word in that bay at the sixth hour about how a bundle gets to the end of a bench.

---

A girl who pushes a bundle back has refused something. A girl who takes two bundles has agreed to something.

And a foreman of fifty-one who wanted a person of seventeen to have the chance to say no has found out, in about a fortnight, that the only instrument she has got is a bundle, and that a bundle works, and that it works in the wrong direction, and that a bundle of work is the commonest form of not asking anybody anything there is.

**I have given a girl of seventeen more to do instead of a choice, and she has taken it, and neither of those is the thing I said out loud in this bay about a year ago. There is not one column in nine hundred buildings that a bundle at the end of a bench could be entered in, and I am not going to be thanked for a fortnight of mornings and I am not going to be asked what it was for. There is a girl at the end of that bench who is now doing more work than she was doing a month ago because of something I could not do.**

---

She went along that bench at about the half hour on the fourth morning of that run and took the second bundle off the end and put it in the middle, where a bundle goes, and squared it along the edge with the heel of her hand.

The girl did not put it back.

She worked the rest of that hour at the end of that bench with one bundle and she did not look along it and she did not say one word, and at the sixth hour the bundle that was in the middle of that bench went out with the armful in the ordinary way.

---

Nobody in that shed is going to be told what a foreman of fifty-one did or undid at the end of her own bench over about a fortnight. The girl at the end of it is not going to be thanked for taking two bundles and not for putting one back. She has not been asked one thing in about a year and she is not going to be asked one this month, and she has still not been given the chance to say no about anything. There is nothing in this empire with a line on it for finding out whether a person of seventeen would rather not.

The reader at that end of the bench turned a page and had read the top of every sheet that came down that lane for a fortnight and stopped, and neither of them is one of the four who cannot make sense of a paragraph.

The bracket was not on and would not be on until about the seventh hour, and a mark in chalk on that end wall stands at the height of the third link of a chain. Nobody in that lane has ever said what it is a mark of. It is going to be there in the winter.
