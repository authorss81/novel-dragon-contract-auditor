# Chapter 0554: One Plate On A Table For About Two Years And Then Two, And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Who Has Nowhere In This Empire To Put The Second

He had been putting the second plate on the other side of that table for about a fortnight, and this morning he washed it, and putting it out washed is a different thing from putting it out.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the fourth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the third hour.

---

There has been one plate in that room for about two years. There is a press beside the door with the crockery in it and there has been a second plate in that press for the whole of that time and it has the dust on the face of it that the other things in there have, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that takes a man for taking a plate out of a press.

He does not eat at that table. He stood at it and he has stood at it for about two years and the plate goes on the boards and the cup goes on the boards and he takes them both back to the sink with the same arm, and the other side of that table is a side.

The chair he dragged into the middle of that floor two months ago is still in the middle of it, facing about four feet of plaster with its back to the glass. The stool is against the wall where the stool is. Beside the window there is bare wall with a nail in it driven in crooked about the height of his hand, and his coat has been hanging on that nail for about two years. Two things are in the inside pocket of it, on opposite sides of a seam, and nothing has come out of them in this building or in any other, and no hand in this city has ever been in that coat.

---

The catch on that window is turned and the window is shut, and it has been shut for about two years.

At about the first hour of every working morning there is somebody in the back of the house behind, sweeping a floor, and the sweep stops at the end of that hour, and the two of them are about as close as two people in this city who are not in the same room can be.

He took the whole of that in about two minutes, standing up, in the last week of the last month. Whatever he says in this room goes out of the window whether the window is open or shut. He has been saying things out loud to this room for about ten years, and he is the only person in this matter who has worked out that he has spent two years shutting the cheapest second hearer in this empire out of a room nobody else holds a key to.

He is not going to open it. Nobody in that house is ever going to be told why.

---

**What he wanted was a room with a second person in it who had not come for him. The cheapest one going in this city has been a turned catch and a gap about as deep as a well, at one hour of every working morning, for the whole of the two years he has lived here. He has been keeping it shut against himself, and there is nobody to thank for that either, because there is nobody else in it.**

So he has got a plate.

That is the whole of what he has done about it in ten weeks. It is the first thing in about ten years he has wanted set down anywhere, and a plate is a thing with a use in it. A thing with a use can be produced by anybody who cares to come in off a lane, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that would take him for putting one on the other side of a table.

---

He washed it at about the second hour, which is earlier than he has ever washed anything in that room, and he dried it on the cloth and he put it on the other side with the cup beside it, and he stood at the table looking at the two of them for about as long as it takes a sheet to be squared.

Then he did not sit down, because there is nothing to sit down on that faces that table, and he was not going to turn that chair round for it, and he was not going to know why he was not going to for the rest of the week.

---

It was clean at the third hour and it was not clean at the fifth.

That is what a plate does in a room where nothing is made heat and where nobody comes. A plate that has been washed and put down and not used is not the same object on the fourth morning as it was on the first, and a man who washes a thing every morning on purpose acquires a second morning and a fifth and a ninth. By the fourth week there are more mornings than there are reasons.

He has been doing it about four days out of seven. On the other three it is on the table with whatever is on it and he has not worked out the three.

---

At about the first hour a coin came under that door from the woman two floors below, and she puts it in a tin she keeps at the top of the turn, and neither of them has ever said a word to the other about that either.

The stair goes at the sixth hour and comes back at the seventh, and it does not touch his floor, and he hears every foot of it go, the way he hears everything in this building go.

Nothing in this room is ever warm and nothing is ever going to be, and the man who owns it has not come through that door in about two years.

---

He did not put the plate back in the press.

He worked out standing up that a thing with a use in it can be produced by anybody who comes in off that lane, and that a second plate is worth exactly that and nothing else on the other side of it. He could put it in the press on any working morning of the rest of his life and it would make no difference to any person in any building in this empire. A plate does not go in a column and cannot be shown to have been part of anything.

He washed it on the fourth morning and the fifth and the sixth and put it on the other side with the cup beside it.

A man of thirty-eight has put a second plate on that table and is not going to take it off. There is no form in nine hundred buildings that can be filled in by anybody about a plate on a table. Nobody in that house has ever come up that stair for a reason, and nobody is ever going to be told there is a plate.
