# Chapter 0620: An Ordinary Room, A Child With A Line, And Two Copies Of The Same Thing In Its Hands

She had made a second of a line that was entered in that room a year and more before this month began, and this morning she carried it up a stair on the far side of that city that she has never been up, and she would rather it had not come to that room at all.

It is the second day of the first week of the eighth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour.

---

There is a table across the room and a bench along the wall under the window and two chairs on the far side of the table, and the window has a catch on it that has not held since before a person living was born. There is a lamp on the table with nothing in it. There is a floor of grey stone and nothing on the walls. It is a room people are brought into and it has been a room people are brought into for a good many years and it is a mile from any of the six rooms this matter has used, and nothing in it is on any roll.

A book is kept there. It is kept a year and then a year, and every line that goes into it carries a sum and a date and a name at the foot of it, and the lines are the ordinary debts of ordinary people and about one in ten of them is a year and more old and has not been had anybody.

---

The child came in with its mother and stood at the near end of that table and did not sit down, and the woman of twenty-four went round the table and put the second of those two sheets down in front of it and squared it against the wood.

The man of thirty-eight came in behind them and stayed at the end by the door, and the woman of twenty-four did not look at him and he did not go any further into the room.

"Two of those," she said. "I am not going to put this one down until it has been said out loud in this room that there are two of them and that I made the second one, and I am not going to put it down because anybody asked me to."

Nobody asked her.

"There are two of them. I made the second one at the top of four flights and I carried it up here on the ordinary morning. There is no name at the top of either of them and there is not going to be one, and the first of them is as old as the line is, and there is no way anybody could tell which of the two of them was made first, and that is not a fault in either of them."

---

The child put its hand flat on the sheet and read the line out loud to the end of it, which is the ordinary thing a person does in a room, and then it took its hand off and said, in the ordinary way, that the name at the foot of that line was its mother's, and that its mother was not in that room and had not been for about a year, and that it was not going to pay it.

"There is a rule about that," the woman of twenty-four said. "There is no rule about that. That is the thing I have come up this stair for."

"There is a rule and you do not know it, and I am going to tell you what it is, and I am not going to make you say one word back to me about it." The man of thirty-eight came away from the door and stood at the end of that table. "A line like that is a thing somebody wrote down. If nobody has ever been brought in front of it, then it is not a thing anybody has to answer for, and it cannot be put on to you, and it does not follow you out of that door and into a room where somebody wants paying. If you have been brought in front of it, then it can be heard, and a thing that has been heard can be limited down to what it actually was, and it can be refused, out loud, in a room where somebody is standing. And that is all of it. There is no other part of it and there is not going to be one this month."

---

The woman of twenty-four put the second of those sheets down in front of that child and squared it, and the two of them lay side by side on that table and said the same thing.

"There is another one of those two in this city," the man of thirty-eight said, "and I did not make it and I have not got it. I put it out into the world seven weeks ago and it went down four flights in somebody's apron and I have not seen it since and I am not going to see it, and there is no form anywhere in this city that will show anybody where that one is, and that is not a fault in it. That is what a copy is. It costs fourpence and anybody may have one and there is nobody standing in it after it has been made once, and that is the whole reason nobody in this city has ever been thanked for anything, and the whole reason this child can be handed two of them and told the truth about both of them."

"Then two of them is a floor and not a ceiling," said the woman of twenty-four, "and I would like that entered."

"There is nothing to enter it in."

"I know there is nothing to enter it in. I said I would like it entered and I know there is not, and that is the whole of what I have been able to say in this room in about ten minutes, and I am not going to be thanked for it, and I would have come up that stair about a hundred times if it had not come to this room at all."

---

The child put one sheet in each hand and looked at the two of them for about as long as it takes to fold a cloth once, and then it said that the two of them said the same thing and that it was going to keep them, and it went out into the lane with them, and the door shut behind it about as long ago as that happens.

The lamp on that table has nothing in it and the catch on that window has not held for longer than anybody can remember, and the book is on a shelf at the end of that room and it is kept a year and then a year.

---

The child goes out into that lane with one of them in each hand, and a copy costs fourpence, and about four streets off a woman at a case is making another of the same line out of a book that is kept a year, for somebody she has never met and has not written one word to, and the line is a year old and can be heard again, and the lane goes on past the corner the way it does.
