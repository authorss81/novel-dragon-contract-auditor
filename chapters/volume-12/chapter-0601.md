# Chapter 0601: A Woman Who Came Up At The First Hour And Not At The Fourth, And A Man At His Own Door

He had worked out in the last fortnight that there are two things in the pocket of the coat on that nail, and that the one that means anything is a leaf out of a bundle folded twice, with a line struck through whatever had stood at the foot of that line, and this morning he had got as far as his own door and had not taken the latch off it.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the fourth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the first hour and about the fourth.

---

The coat has been on that nail about two years and the nail went in crooked about the height of a hand. Its inside pocket carries two things either side of a seam, and that seam has not been opened in about two years either. About four feet of plaster is on that wall and the nail is in the middle of it. There is a jug on that table with a ring inside it where there was water, and a glass face up beside the jug with the top left corner off it, and a lamp on the floor at the end of that bed that has never once been lit.

He is thirty-eight. Nothing stands over his name in this city: no office, no fund, no list, no leave, no certification, no warrant, no commission, no post, and a bar that had been his own came off him on a form of a single line that he wrote out himself. There is three inches of writing on the inside of his left wrist which says nothing, settles nothing, and does not tell him where the ford is.

---

She came up that stair at the first hour and stood on the mat at the end of that landing and did not knock, and he did not go to the door, and the gap under that door has not changed in about two years. She has been coming up at the first hour and about the fourth hour for about sixteen weeks and she has wanted one single thing out of him for all of it, which is nothing, and the pail at the end of that landing is the only water in this house.

"The pail is fuller than it was on the second day."

"It is."

"You have not carried that jug out."

"No."

"You carried it out four times in a fortnight and you have not carried it out since."

"That is how it is."

---

**I have decided what I am going to do and it has taken me a fortnight, and the deciding was not the hard part. The hard part is that it cannot be done alone and there is one person in this city who comes to this door without being asked, and she is the one person I cannot ask.**

The two things are on opposite sides of a seam and the leaf is on the one that would mean something. If I take that one out and put it on that table in front of a person, then I am a man with a grievance, in a room, at an hour, and a form in nine hundred buildings would take a man for it, and there are two or three men in this city who would have the measure of what it was about inside a week. That is the thing anybody would expect a man to put on a table. That is the whole of what a person has been doing to me since before a fire and I am not going to hand it to a person who came up a stair to stand on a mat.

The other one is a sheet with a printed strip along the head of it carrying four short things across the top, and not one of the four is a name, and it cost fourpence out of a case four flights up on a wet morning about two years ago. There is nothing written on it. There is nothing on that sheet at all except four printed things that are not a name, and it has been in the pocket of that coat on the other side of a seam for about two years.

If that is the one that goes on that table then I am a man who went and bought a sheet of paper, and that is the whole of what anybody can show, and I cannot afterwards be shown to have been nowhere near any of it.

That is what I have worked out. It has taken a fortnight and it is the only thing I have worked out this month.

---

She stayed at that end of that landing about as long as it takes to put a coin down and take a hand away from it, and then she went down off that landing and the stair behind her emptied out and stayed empty, and the light went off that landing about a quarter of an hour afterwards and the room behind that door went dark with it.

She did not come up at the fourth hour. She has come up at the fourth hour every working day for about sixteen weeks and she did not come up this afternoon, and the stair did not make any noise at all at about the fourth hour, and there is nothing in nine hundred buildings that takes a woman for a stair that stayed quiet.

So the whole of what I have is a decision and no second person, and a door on a latch that I have not taken off in about two years, and one sheet of paper on the other side of a seam with nothing written on it, and I am not going to do this on my own and I am not going to ask her to come up.
