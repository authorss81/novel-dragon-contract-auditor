# Chapter 0601: A Woman Who Came Up At The First Hour And Not At The Fourth, And A Man At His Own Door

He had worked out in the last fortnight that there are two things in the pocket of the coat on that nail, and that the one that means anything is a leaf out of a bundle folded twice, with a line struck through whatever had stood at the foot of that line, and this morning he had got as far as his own door and had not taken the latch off it.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the fourth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the first hour and about the fourth.

---

The coat has been on that nail about two years and the nail went in crooked about the height of a hand. Its inside pocket carries two things either side of a seam, and that seam has not been opened in about two years either. About four feet of plaster is on that wall and the nail is in the middle of it. There is a jug on a table with a ring inside it where there was water, and a glass face up beside the jug with the top left corner off it, and a lamp on the floor at the end of the bed that has never once been lit.

The plaster is a different colour in the patch about the size of a hand where the nail went in, and that difference will not come out of it in two years and is not going to come out of it in twenty. The coat is a working coat and it is worn through at both elbows and it hangs heavier than a coat that is only hung up. The inside pocket falls away from the seam and swings a little when the door is shut, and that is the only thing in this room that moves.

The table is deal and eleven foot by six and stands with its far edge into the wall so that a hand will not go behind it. The jug is about a foot back from the near edge. Inside it there is a ring, pale, about a finger wide, and dust in the bottom of the jug as well. The glass lies face up beside it with the corner gone, and there is a thread of grey down the side of the glass that has not moved in as long as the jug has stood there.

The bed is against the wall and has not been made up since the spring that is gone, and the blanket is pushed up at the head end the way a blanket goes when somebody has sat down on the edge of it and then thought better of it. The lamp is on the floor where the bed ends, with a wick in it, and there is a dark place on the boards under it where somebody struck a match off the iron once and gave up.

The window has its catch turned and has had it turned about two years. What light comes in at the first hour comes in as one shape and lays itself down by the stool and does not travel much in a morning, and by the fourth hour it has gone round on to the wall and off it again. There is a worn place on the boards between the bed and the door, about a pace long, and nothing in nine hundred buildings is ever going to ask a man how many times he has gone over it.

He is thirty-eight. Nothing stands over his name in this city: no office, no fund, no list, no leave, no certification, no warrant, no commission, no post, and a bar that had been his own came off him on a form of a single line that he wrote out himself. There is three inches of writing on the inside of his left wrist which says nothing, settles nothing, and does not tell him where the ford is.

He has been in this room since before it was light. He has not made the bed and he has not lit the lamp. He has put his hand into the inside pocket of that coat twice, which is twice more than he has done in about two years, and both times he went in with the same finger and stopped at the seam and did not cross it.

He knows which of the two things is on which side of it and has known it for a fortnight. Finding that out was the fortnight. Knowing a thing and being able to do anything with it are two pieces of work, and he has finished the first one and has not begun the second, and he has not told one person in this city that there are two of them.

---

She came up that stair at the first hour and stood on the mat at the end of the landing and did not knock, and he did not go to the door, and the gap under that door has not changed in about two years. She has been coming up at the first hour and about the fourth hour for about fifteen weeks and she has wanted one single thing out of him for all of it, which is nothing, and the pail at the end of the landing is the only water in this house.

He heard the whole of her coming up. There is one place in the stair where the boards give, and she puts her weight over it without stopping, and this morning he heard her do it. After that there were the landing boards and then nothing at all, and then the pail took its own weight once and stopped.

The mat on that landing belongs to the landing and not to him, and the near corner of it stands up where the boards have lifted at a joint, and she stands clear of that corner every morning without appearing to think about it, which is a thing a person does four times a week for fifteen weeks and never notices doing. Her hands are in the front of the apron. Behind her on the boards the pail stands with its handle out and the water higher in it than it stood on the second day, and there is no other water anywhere in this house, and that is a fact about the house and not about either of them.

She has never once put her hand on the door and he has never once gone to it. Fifteen weeks of the two of them in one building with a shut door between them and nothing whatever happening on either side of it, and no form anywhere in this city takes two people for a shut door, which is either the arrangement working or the arrangement not there.

"The pail is fuller than it was on the second day."

"It is."

"You have not carried that jug out."

"No."

"You carried it out four times in a fortnight and you have not carried it out since."

"That is how it is."

---

**I have decided what I am going to do and it has taken me a fortnight, and the deciding was not the hard part. The hard part is that it cannot be done alone and there is one person in this city who comes to this door without being asked, and she is the one person I cannot ask.**

The two things are on opposite sides of a seam and the leaf is on the one that would mean something. If I take that one out and put it on the table in front of a person, then I am a man with a grievance, in a room, at an hour, and a form in nine hundred buildings would take a man for it, and there are two or three men in this city who would have the measure of what it was about inside a week. That is the thing anybody would expect a man to put on a table. That is the whole of what a person has been doing to me since before a fire and I am not going to hand it to a person who came up a stair to stand on a mat.

She wants nothing. I have had that for fifteen weeks without asking her for it, and getting to the bottom of it without asking her was most of the work, and it is the one thing about her that I would put into a form if there was one. She comes up the stair twice a day and has never once asked me for water or for a light or for one word said out loud, and she fills the pail because that is where the water is and not for me.

The other one is a sheet with a printed strip along the head of it carrying four short things across the top, and not one of the four is a name, and it cost fourpence out of a case four flights up on a wet morning about two years ago. There is nothing written on it. There is nothing on the sheet at all except four printed things that are not a name, and it has been in the pocket of the coat on the other side of a seam for about two years.

If that is the one that goes on the table then I am a man who went and bought a sheet of paper, and that is the whole of what anybody can show, and I cannot afterwards be shown to have been nowhere near any of it.

That is what I have worked out. It has taken a fortnight and it is the only thing I have worked out this month.

---

She stayed at that end of the landing about as long as it takes to put a coin down and take a hand away from it, and then she went down off it and the stair behind her emptied out and stayed empty, and the light went off the landing about a quarter of an hour afterwards and the room behind the door went dark with it.

He had not moved off the boards since before she came up. The pail is where she left it and the mat has taken the shape of a foot on the middle of it and the shape will be there tomorrow, and the water in the pail is the same water it was at the first hour, and none of that is going to be entered anywhere.

She did not come up at the fourth hour. She has come up at the fourth hour every working day for about fifteen weeks and she did not come up this afternoon, and the stair did not make any noise at all at about the fourth hour, and there is nothing in nine hundred buildings that takes a woman for a stair that stayed quiet.

So the whole of what I have is a decision and no second person, and a door on a latch that I have not taken off in about two years, and one sheet of paper on the other side of a seam with nothing written on it, and I am not going to do this on my own and I am not going to ask her to come up.
