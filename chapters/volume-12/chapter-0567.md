# Chapter 0567: The Front Of A Bench She Had Stopped Standing At, And A Girl Of Seventeen Working At The Wrong End Of It

She had not come down the length of her own floor for about a fortnight, and on this morning she came down it, because the girl was working at the wrong end of that bench.

It is the second day of the first week of the eighth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour.

---

She is fifty-one. Eleven years of that bay and every one of them at the back.

A class does not have to do a single thing to a person. It only has to leave her where it found her, and it found her at the back of that bay, and eleven years on that is still where she is.

She has been leaving the ordinary work of that shed at the end of that bench about nine mornings a month, and for about a week she has left nothing at all, and the end of that bench has been empty, and she worked out on about the third morning that an empty end of a bench is not a foreman's decision. It is a foreman's absence.

Then on the sixth morning the girl got her arms under an armful at the far end of that bench, where the count is made, and carried it to the end by the door and set it down there and worked at it.

---

She went down the length of that floor.

It is about ninety foot from the back of that bay to the front of that bench and she did it at the rate she walks, which is not fast, and the chain went along under a hand that has been on it eleven years and did not stop once for her.

She put the flat of her hand on the wood at the front of that bench and kept it there.

She had not wanted to do it. She has worked out in about as long as it takes a brush on boards that the only reason she went down that floor was to be able to say that she had gone down it, and there is nothing at the front of that bench to say to.

There is a paper on a board by that door in that shed and nobody has ever filled it in, and it has been on that board longer than she has been at that bench, and she has never once gone near it with a pen.

---

The girl worked at the end by the door with her back to about forty foot of that bay.

She did not stop and she did not turn round and there was no more of her at that end of that bench than there had been for about a week.

That is the whole of what a foreman of fifty-one gets to see when she has come ninety foot down her own floor for nothing, and she stood there with her hand on the wood for about as long as a sheet takes to square, and then she took her hand off and went back up the length of the floor and did not stop at the front of anything on the way.

---

On the eighth morning she left a bundle at the end of that bench.

She had told herself on the way down the lane that a thing at the end of a bench is the ordinaryest invitation in the world, and that a bundle of ordinary work can be turned down without saying anything at all.

A fortnight of standing at the back of that bay had got her down to the whole of it, and the whole of it was neither the work nor the girl. It was a thing put where a person could get at it and turn it over.

She put it there and she went and stood at the back of that bay.

The girl came down that bench at the ordinary rate, and she did not stop at the end of it, and she did not look down at it, and she carried it on past to the end by the door and set it down on top of what she was already working and squared it with the heel of her hand.

---

**I have put a bundle at the end of that bench and she has carried it to the other end of it, and she has been at that other end for a week. A girl who pushes a thing back has refused something. A girl who carries a thing on to where she works has made no decision that anybody could enter, and there is no column in this city that takes a girl of seventeen either way, and I have gone ninety foot down my own floor to be in a room where nothing was going to be said to me.**

She is not going to ask. There is nothing to ask with. A foreman of a shed is nobody's agent and has no heading and is not going to get one, and nothing in this empire has ever put the question to a person of seventeen, in writing or out loud.

Nobody has put one question to that girl in about a year and nobody is going to put one to her in this month either, and she is not going to be thanked for carrying a bundle past where it was put, and nobody is going to be sent for.

The reader at that end of that bench went on with what he was on and read nothing at all, and a reader of seventeen who can take the sense out of a page is not among the four in this matter who cannot, and nobody in that shed has ever had cause to find out.
