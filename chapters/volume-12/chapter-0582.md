# Chapter 0582: The Room At The Back Of That Floor, And A Woman Who Is Not Going To Stand At The End Of That Sill Again

On four mornings in about a year she came out of that room and carried a true sentence as far as the end of that sill, and nobody in this building answered any part of it, and last week she came out of that door and stopped four foot short of the sill, and she has not opened it since. Somebody has propped the door off its frame for the whole of a week, the same as it has been propped for nineteen years, and a person coming along that corridor can see the whole of the table in there from the far end of the stone.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the eleventh month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the second hour.

---

Nineteen years of minutes. The book is in her own hand and the day goes at the head of a line and nothing else does, and there is nobody in this building she has advised about anything in about four years. Nobody reads it and it has to go on being written, and there is not one page in the whole nineteen years of it that anybody has ever asked her about, and the last page has gone soft at the corner her thumb keeps going to.

On a shelf under a window about two foot wide, with the pane out of it and the weather straight onto the paper, there is a question and it does not finish, and her own name is written underneath it. It has been on that shelf about eleven years and it was put there on purpose so that she would be a door somebody could walk through. The stone under the window takes it straight on as well, and the paper has lain there all that time and gone the same colour as the sill.

Nobody has gone to her. Nothing has ever been set down after it. It is not going to be picked up this month and she is not going to ask anybody about it, and it is not going to be printed out in any building in this empire. Eleven years of weather on one sheet of paper and there is not one line after the question, and the shelf it lies on is the width of the sheet and no more.

---

She does the second copy herself now, on her own hand, once a week, in that room, with the door standing open. She writes it out from the other one and reads it back against that, and her pen follows the day at the head of each line and comes off the paper at the end of the seventh day and not before.

It is a copy and a copy can be made twice and still be honest, and there has been one every week for nineteen years, and the girl of nineteen has put about half of them in over four years, and nobody has ever said one word to that girl about where any of them went, and she has never asked, and she is not going to be thanked for it now that she has stopped. One of the two of them knows where those copies go and one of them does not, and neither of them is going to find out this month.

She put the day down. She did not say one word about the other six days. That is the way it is done and it is the only way anybody has ever done it. There are six finished days in that book in front of her and not one of them has anything against it but the day itself, and she has never once been asked to fill any of them in and she has never once offered to.

---

The girl came to the door of that room at about the third hour with the week's sheets on her hip and stopped about four foot inside the threshold, the way she has stopped about four foot off that table since the second week of the fourth month. The floor of it is bare boards and the light off the lane comes in over the sill and lies down by the table and stops there, and a person four foot inside that threshold is standing in it.

"I stopped at it four times this fortnight."

"I know you did."

"You did not come the last four foot."

"No."

"That is the whole of what I came to say."

"Then you have said it."

---

**There was a person at the end of that sill this morning and there was a person four foot off me and the one who said a thing in that room is going to be the one who does not stand anywhere. I have been answered not once in four times, and I have worked out in about four years that the sill is the worst place in this building to be, and it is the only place in this building where anything is ever said. And in about a week I have worked out one other thing, which is that a person four foot off a thing in the open is not a person who has gone anywhere. She is on my floor in my light with a week's sheets on her hip and she can be produced for this room and this hour and for no part of any other, and I can be produced for this room and this hour with the door standing open, and there is no column in nine hundred buildings that takes either of us for one half of that and not the other.**

And the worst of it is that it has got a person at it now, a girl of nineteen with a thing on her hip who has not asked me for one thing in four years, and she stopped there four times in a fortnight, and if I had gone the last four foot this is where I would have started. That is the whole of the trade, and I have had nineteen years of the other end of it, and there is no way of having both.

So I am not going to stand at that end again. It is a thing I have decided and nobody in this building has been asked about it and nobody is going to be thanked for it, and there is nowhere in nine hundred buildings to enter a woman as the one who was not standing there. And I want it said that nothing was asked of me to decide it, and that I decided it on an ordinary morning with a pen in my hand and nobody else in the room.

---

"That is not mine to say."

"No. It is mine and I have said it and you have not asked me anything, and that is the arrangement of this room and it has not changed since the spring that is gone."

"I have not asked you anything."

"You have not asked me anything in about five weeks and you came to my door this morning, and those are the same thing and neither of them is a question."

She turned a page of that book over and squared it against the edge of the table, and the girl of nineteen stood about four foot inside that threshold with the week's sheets on her hip and did not come any further in. The armful went off her hip onto her arm and back onto her hip again while the older woman squared the page, and it made no sound on the boards.

"You will do the copy once a week," the woman of fifty-three said, "and so will I, and neither of us is going to say one word to the other about it, and the shelves of this building are not going to have anything on them about it either."

"That is the way it is done."

"It is not the way it is done. It is the way it is going to be done."

---

The girl went back out of that room and along the corridor with the armful and did not stop at the end of that sill, and the door of that room stood open about as long as it takes to square a sheet, and then it shut. That door has made that noise for nineteen years, and nobody came through it in the whole of the hour after.

A question has been on that shelf for about eleven years and the paper on it has gone the colour of the stone and the weather is on it every day. Nothing is going to be set down after it this month and nobody is going to be asked about it, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for a woman who has decided not to be standing in the one place in her own building where anything is ever said. The stone under that shelf is the same stone as the sill at the other end of the corridor, and it is about as cold on one as on the other.
