# Chapter 0598: A Woman At A Case Twenty Feet Off A Man With A Bag On His Shoulder, And One Thing She Will Not Say

She had come in twenty minutes early every working morning for two years and had never wanted one single thing out of anybody on that floor, and in the last fortnight she has stayed after that bag went down four flights, which is a thing she has not done since before the spring that is gone.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the third month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the first hour and about the third.

---

She is twenty-four and she has an undertaking and it is live and it has never been exercised and its date is not said out loud in that room by anybody and it is not going to be said out loud this morning. She has a case behind her and about four hundred blanks a year come out of it, and there is a box shut under the boards at the other end of the room which is not that case and has not been opened in about six years, and behind that end of the room there is a wall with four items on it and not one of the four is time. The box under the floor is a different size from the case and a different colour, and the lid of it has not been off, and there is no reason anybody has ever given for it to be on that floor at all. The lid of the case is up at the back and does not go down while anybody is at it, and what comes out of it in a morning is a stack with the edges squared on it and the corners gone soft from the carrying, and it is soft at those corners this morning as it is every morning.

She came in twenty minutes early and she was in the room before anybody in it, and she stood at the near side of it with the open book on the end of the counter in front of her and the stack in the crook of her arm, and at about the first hour a man came up the four flights with a bag over his shoulder and stood at the near end of the boards a yard off the case with the strap still round his forearm. The light came in across the boards and lay between the two of them and neither of them was standing in any of it. The stair went quiet behind him when he stopped and it did not make any noise again for the rest of that hour, and the boards under both of them are old enough to carry what a boot does to them, and both of them were standing very still on it.

"You have not put it down for three weeks."

"I have not."

"There is a case at this end of these boards."

"There is."

"You are standing in front of it."

"I am."

---

**There is exactly one thing I have ever wanted in this room and I have wanted it for about a fortnight, and it is to say one true sentence out loud where somebody is working, in the ordinary way, at the ordinary hour, with a second pair of ears in the air of it.**

And the only person on this floor who has never once been asked anything is a man with a bag on his shoulder who has come up these stairs four times a week for eleven years and has not been told what is in the bag, and he is standing at that end of this room right now and he is not going anywhere for as long as it takes a sheet to be squared.

And I am not going to do it, and the reason is the whole of what I have worked out in about a fortnight and it is not a form and it is not a column and it is not going in a book.

I have not been asked one thing in two years. That is the only thing I have that is worth anything. If I say a true thing in this room where he is working then I am a woman who wanted one single thing out of a man who has never had to give one, and there is no arrangement in this empire that separates that from a woman who put something in a man's reach. I have not asked him for anything and I am not going to start with the one thing in twelve volumes that actually costs him something.

And he is at that end of this room with his back to a case and a strap round his arm and the only thing either of us has done this morning is stand where we stand, and if I open my mouth in the next hour then that is the one time out of two years, and I am not going to spend the whole of the two years on it.

---

He worked at the far side of it with his back to her, because the near side of a case is where a person stands and she was standing on that, and he did that for the whole of that morning and he did not turn round and he did not put the bag down. He put the stack down on the boards at his own end and squared it and took the next one out of the case, and he did the four things to it in the same order every time, and the fourth of them was the heel of his hand along the near edge of it.

"I have never wanted one single thing out of you in two years."

"I know you have not."

"I did not start coming in twenty minutes early for you. I have done it for two years and that is all there is in it."

"I know that as well."

"Then you know that if I said one thing out loud in this room this morning it would be the first thing anybody on this floor had wanted out of anybody, and I am not going to do it, and nothing in this building is going to credit me for stopping."

"No. You are not going to be thanked for it by anybody in this building."

She put the stack away where it goes and she shut the book and she did not go out at the first hour and she did not go out at the second, and the room emptied behind her end by end and she was the last one in it, and the wall with the four items on it was behind her the whole time and she did not go over to it. There is a drawer under that wall and it stays shut, and she has not had her hand near it, and she went past that end of the room while she was putting the stack away and did not stop at it.

At about a quarter past the bag went out of that room and down those stairs, and the boards under the far end of them stayed shut, and the wall behind them has four items on it and not one of them is time.
