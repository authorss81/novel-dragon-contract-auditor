# Chapter 0595: Thirty-Five Weeks Down A Lane, And The Front Of A Ground Floor Done Toward The Step She Was Standing On

She had come down that lane for thirty-five weeks and she had wanted one thing out of every one of them, and this week the thing she wanted was to be in that passage while a person was in it.

It is the second day of the first week of the third month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the second hour and about the sixth.

---

She came down that lane, which is about as long as the table in the room behind her, and at the bottom of it a passage runs on to two steps and a turn. The lane is wet on the side of the middle and she came the wet side of it, and there is nothing standing on it at which either of them could stop, and the whole of it goes down onto the passage at the step at the turn. The tin is on the top one of those steps, lid down, corner to the wall, and the front of that ground floor is the run of boards between that step and the front door, and it is being done from the turn end of it.

She came down the lane at about the second hour, which is not her hour, and went along the passage and stood on the step below the turn. The boards under her were dry and there was a wet line along the edge of them where the cloth had stopped, and she had the cloth of her own day over her shoulder and she took it off and folded it in her hands while she stood there. From there the turn is at her left hand and the door of that house is a long way off at the other end of the run of boards, and the tin is up one step from her on the level above with its lid down and its corner to the wall, and she did not look at it while she was standing there and she had looked at it on the way in.

The whole of that passage could be seen from where she was standing, and the whole of it could be seen from the step at the front door, and there was nothing in it but her. She stood with the wall of the turn on her left shoulder and the front of the house on her right, and the rain came off the boards at the turn and went past her on both sides and did not come in on her at all, and the skirting on the far side of the run is painted once and the paint has lifted off it in one band at the level a cloth would reach, and the line of it goes the whole way to the turn.

---

She had the sentence. It had been ready since the third week of the seventh month and it had come down that lane in nine years, and she had said it out loud once, in that passage, in the ordinary way, at the ordinary volume, to a woman who was counting a week of a man's money into a tin, and that was a thing said in the air of a room to a person who was working. She had said it at the ordinary hour with her own hands full and her back to nobody, and the woman counting had not stopped and had not turned round, and the sentence had gone into the air of the room and had stayed there and had never come down.

And I worked that out in a fortnight and I have not gone near it since. A sentence said out loud over somebody who is working takes the only thing that person has, which is the standing there, and it takes it for good. The only thing that woman has is that nobody in that house can produce her for anything, and I am not going to take it off her for a sentence about a column I have not ruled in nine years.

Thirty-five weeks. And there is one way of saying it and there is no other, and the way is another person in the room.

---

The woman at the bottom of that stair came through the passage from the back of the ground floor at about the fifth hour with the cloth over her shoulder, and she stopped, and she looked at the step below the turn where the other one was standing, and the marks on that front were about two inches apart and they went from the far end toward the near one. She stopped with her hand on the door frame and she did not come any further into the passage than the frame, and the two of them could see the whole length of it between them.

"You are standing where the boards come to."

"I know where the boards come to. I have watched them come to for about six weeks."

"You have not been down this lane for a fortnight."

"I have not been down this lane for a fortnight."

---

**There was a person working in that passage at about the fifth hour on a wet morning and I had a true thing ready that I have been carrying down a lane for thirty-five weeks, and I did not say it, and the reason I did not say it is that I got it, exactly as I got it thirteen weeks ago with ten minutes of a cloth.**

And it is worse this time. Thirteen weeks ago she came through from the back and did ten minutes of that front and went out, and neither of us said a word, and that was a cost I paid. This morning she came through from the back and there is nowhere in that passage I can stand that is not in front of her. She has worked out the same thing I have worked out, and she is going to work those boards from the turn end down toward the front door, and she is not going to stop for me, and I am not going to ask her to.

A second pair of ears is the only thing there is and the price of it is the one thing the pair of ears has, and I have been the one who paid it and I am not going to be the one who takes it off somebody else to get a sentence said.

And I have been standing there about an hour with the cloth folded in my hands and the whole length of the passage in front of me, and there is nobody going to put anything in it for me, and I put myself at the wrong end of it and I am going to stay at the wrong end of it.

---

She put the cloth on the boards and began at the far end, at the rate she does everything, and the marks came up out of the wood about two inches apart behind her, and she did not say one word while she did it. She came to within about three foot of the step the other one was standing on and she did not stop and she did not go round, and the cloth went over the boards on either side of that step and the sound of a floor being done filled the passage up the way it had not been filled in about eleven weeks.

And then it was done, and she picked the cloth up off the boards by the front door and folded it over her arm the way she folds it every day of her life, and she stood there about as long as it takes to fold a cloth, and she did not look along it on her way out, and the door shut about as long ago as that happens.

The marks on the front of that ground floor are about two inches apart and they go from the far end to the near one, and the passage is empty, and the tin is on the top step at the turn.
