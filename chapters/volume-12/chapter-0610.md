# Chapter 0610: A Coin Put On The Second Tread Of A Stair By A Woman Who Has Not Been Above It In Twenty Years

She had left that door with nothing under it for five weeks and this morning she put a coin on the second tread of her own stair, and the difference between the two places was about nine inches and cost her the twenty years she had spent standing below them.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the sixth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the first hour and about the fifth.

---

The passage runs from the front door to the turn and the whole length of it can be seen from the step outside the front door, because the front of that ground floor is done from the turn end and worked back toward the near end, and that is the only reason anybody in this house can stand at the bottom of it and know the whole of it at a glance. Nine years of one man's rent is in a tin on the top step at that turn. The tin has a lid and the lid is down and one corner of it is against the skirting in a finger's width of space that has not been cleaned out since before she came. It has never held a sheet of paper. It has never been anything but money.

Three keys hang on a board by that front door and not one of them has a name on it. Behind the boiler on the ground floor there is a key in a tin, and a number has been cut into the back of that key, and the number is the only record anybody anywhere has kept of the three rooms those keys open. She has never copied it out. She has never once been asked for it.

The marks on those boards were put there by the woman at the foot of that stair. They run from the turn end toward the near end, about two inches apart, made with the edge of a boot heel over some number of years, and they are the only thing in that passage that is not nine years old or twenty years old, and the tin is at the far end of them and the boards are wet along the line where the cloth stops.

She has been at the foot of that stair about twenty years. Her boots have never been on anything above the second tread, and the second tread is the highest thing her feet have ever reached, and she is fifty-one and there is nobody in this house who could say that out loud with any confidence, because there is nobody in this house who has ever asked her.

---

The woman of about thirty-five came down that lane for the fiftieth week running, at about the fourth hour, and she was carrying nothing.

She came the length of the lane the way she comes it, which is fast and on the wet side of the middle, and she did not slow at the step. She stood on the step at the front door with the cloth of her own day in the crook of one arm and looked up the stair.

She looked up it for about as long as it takes to fold a cloth once. The stair goes up out of the sight of anybody standing where she was standing, which is the whole point of where she was standing, and she could see the bottom four treads and the newel and the dark above the newel where the turn took the light away.

"You have not been on that step in twenty years."

"I have not."

"That is the highest you have ever been, and you have never once been past it."

"No."

"You put a coin on it this morning."

"I put a coin on it this morning."

And then she went out at the front door and she did not go up, and she did not ask one thing about it, and the door shut behind her the way that door shuts, which is without much in it, and the passage was empty again from the turn to the near end.

---

**I have thought about nothing else for five weeks.**

Under that door it was a habit. A habit is a thing a person can be shown having. I worked that out with the flat of my hand on the lid of that tin about a month ago, standing at the turn in the dark before the lamp was lit, and I would rather have twenty years of nothing that anybody could produce about me than one habit that goes four times a week for the rest of my life. That is what I said to myself. That is what I did. There has been nothing under that door for five weeks and there is going to be nothing under it.

And on the second tread it is a place. A place is a thing a person cannot be shown having or not having. A coin on a tread is a thing that is there whether anybody looks at it or not. It does not follow me out of a door. It does not say anything about what I do on a working morning. And a woman who has never been above the second tread in twenty years, standing on it once to put a coin down, is a thing that happened and cannot be undone and does not go in anybody's book.

He would have to come down that stair to get it.

---

He came down those stairs twice in about two years. I know that because I am at the bottom of them twice a day, and the second time was about six months after the first, and both times he came down at an hour when the house was empty and both times he went along that passage in the dark and did not touch the boards. He has told nobody in this house that he came down either time. I am not going to be the person who tells him I know, and I am not going to tell anybody else, and there is nothing in nine hundred buildings that takes a woman for a stair that stayed quiet at four in the morning.

He has said out loud that there is not going to be a third.

And his door has been standing open a hand's width for a fortnight now, and I found that out because the light comes under it at the fourth hour and the stair is at the end of my own front room. I could put my hand flat on that door at the fourth hour and feel the draught off it. I have not done that and I am not going to.

---

She stood at the foot of my stair and she did not come up.

That is the one person in this house who could have said one true thing out loud in that passage this morning, at the ordinary hour, at the ordinary volume, in the ordinary way, while a person was working in it, and there is nothing else in this empire for putting a thing into the world without anybody being asked anything, and what it costs the person who is standing there is the one thing that person has, which is that nobody could ever show them having been anywhere in particular.

I have twenty years of that. She has four rooms going by the week and a day-book of her own hand with four headings over four columns in it and a fifth that has not been ruled in nine years. She came down that lane for the fiftieth week and she had the sentence ready, and she stood on my step and looked up my stair and she did not say it, and she is not going to be thanked for not saying it and she is not going to be asked about it, and I have not got a word to put in her mouth about it and I would not put one there if I had.

Fifty weeks. She has come down that lane fifty times with something to say and she has said it not once, and every one of those weeks has cost her the only thing she owns, and she keeps paying it.

---

The cloth comes down those boards from the near end. I get to the turn about the fourth day of a week, and on this morning I got there, and the second tread is at the far end of the run and I went up it on my knees with the cloth, which is not the same as standing on it, and I put the cloth over the tread and the coin under the cloth came away under the cloth.

About nine years of one man's rent is on the top step and it is staying there. There is a door on the floor above that stair with nothing under it for the fifth week, and there is a coin on the second tread of that stair about the middle of the tread, and the marks on the boards run from the turn end toward the near end about two inches apart, and the whole of that passage can be seen from the step at the front door, and this morning there was one woman in it and she was on her knees.
