# Chapter 0610: A Coin Put On The Second Tread Of A Stair By A Woman Who Has Not Been Above It In Twenty Years

She had left that door with nothing under it for five weeks and this morning she put a coin on the second tread of her own stair.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the sixth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the first hour and about the fifth.

---

That passage runs from the front door to the turn and about nine years of one man's rent is in a tin on the top step at that turn, lid down, corner to the wall. One floor up from the second of those stairs there is a door, and behind that boiler there is a key in a tin, and a number has been cut into the back of that key which is the one record of three rooms anybody anywhere has kept of them. The front of that ground floor is being done from the turn end of it, so that the whole of that passage can be seen from the step at the front door, and there is nothing in it this morning but the tin and the marks on the boards.

She has been at the foot of that stair about twenty years and her boots have never been on anything higher than the second tread.

---

The woman of about thirty-five came down that lane for the fiftieth week running, at about the fourth hour, and stood on the step at the front door and looked up that stair, and she stayed there about as long as it takes a cloth to go round a hand twice.

"You have not been on that step in twenty years."

"I have not."

"That is the highest you have ever been and you have never once been past it."

"No."

"You put a coin on it this morning."

"I put a coin on it this morning."

And then she went out at that front door and she did not go up and she did not ask one thing about it, and that door shut about as long ago as that happens, and the passage was empty again.

---

**I put that coin under that door every working morning for about two years and then I stopped, and I have thought about nothing else for five weeks, and this morning I put one on the second tread of my own stair and that is the whole of the difference and it is a difference of about nine inches.**

Under that door it was a habit, and a habit is a thing a person can be shown having. I worked that out a month ago with the flat of my hand on the lid of that tin, and I would rather have twenty years of nothing that anybody could produce about me than one habit that goes four times a week. That is what I said to myself and that is what I did, and there has been nothing under that door for five weeks.

And on the second tread it is a place. A place is a thing a person cannot be shown having or not having, and a coin on a tread is a thing that is there whether anybody looks at it or not, and a person who has never been above that tread in twenty years standing on it once to put a coin down is a thing that happened and cannot be undone and does not go in anybody's book.

And he would have to come down that stair to get it. He came down those stairs twice in about two years and told nobody in this house that he had done it either time, and he has said out loud that there is not going to be a third, and I have never gone up past that second tread in twenty years, and neither of us has ever once been on a stair in this house at the same time, and nobody is going to say one word to either of us about any of it.

She came down that lane for the fiftieth week and stood at the bottom of my own stair and she did not come up. That is the one person in this house who could have said one thing out loud in that passage this morning, at the ordinary hour, at the ordinary volume, in the ordinary way, while a person was working in it, and there is nothing else in this empire for putting a thing into the world without anybody being asked anything, and it costs the person who is standing there the only thing that person has, which is that nobody could ever show them having been anywhere in particular. I have twenty years of that. She has four rooms going by the week and a book of her own hand with four headings over four columns in it and a fifth that has not been ruled in nine years.

She did not say it. She stood at the foot of my stair and looked up it and went out at the front door, and I am not going to put a word in her mouth about that, and I have not got one to put there anyway.

---

There are marks on the boards at the front of that ground floor about two inches apart, and they run from the turn end of those boards toward the near end, and the second tread of that stair has one coin on it about the middle of the tread, and there is a door on the floor above that stair with nothing under it.
