# Chapter 0597: Ninety Foot Of A Shed Floor Walked Down To A Count That Had Been Short For A Month

The count at the far end of that bench had been short for about a month and she had not asked about it, and this morning she came all ninety foot of that floor to be at that end of it when it was made.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the third month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour.

---

She is fifty-one. A class put her at the back of the bay eleven years ago and has not moved her since, and for six weeks the chain along the front of that bench has gone at one rate under her hand without once slowing for her. That bay is about nine foot by eleven. The far end of the bench, where the count is made, is at the back of it, and the end by the door, where the girl works, is about nine foot from it, and the ninety foot of open floor is between that bay and the wall it is against. The bench itself is a length of stone with a chain going along the front edge of it that goes the length of the stone and comes back, and the wood of the two frames under it is grey at the place where forearms have gone over it for eleven years and dark at the two ends.

A boy of seventeen is at the far end of it, and he came out of another place a year ago on an engagement that is written down, and there is a silence on that as well. He stopped reading the tops of the sheets two springs ago and has not gone back down to them. He stands at the end with his weight on one hip and he does the four things to a sheet in the same order every time, and the fourth of them is the heel of his hand along the near edge of the pile, and it is the only one of the four that is a habit and not a piece of the work.

The count on that end has been short twice a week for about a month.

---

She came down that floor at the rate she walks. She did not halt at the front of it and she did not lay a hand on the wood. She went along it, clear of the back of the bay where the count is made, and carried on to the end by the door, and stood there about as long as it takes to square a sheet. The boards of that shed carry a sound, and the light comes off the roof of it about a hand's breadth up the wall and stops there, and the whole of those ninety foot of it had her boots on it, and the chain along the bench went on at the one rate it goes at for the whole of the walk.

He never once looked along nine foot of bench. He had a sheet in his hands and he turned it over and put it down on the pile and squared it with the heel of his hand and picked up the next one, and he did that four times while she was standing at that end, and he never once looked along it.

The girl had a sheet about a third of a way down and she finished what she was doing with it and set it on the top of the pile at that door end, squared it, and took up the next, and she did not turn round, and she had her back to ninety foot of the bay. The light does not reach either end of the bench, and the two of them worked at the two ends of the bay in the two places where it stops, with the whole of the rest of the floor between them and nothing on it.

Nothing was put on the bench that morning.

---

**I said one true thing out loud in this bay in the third week of the third month, at the ordinary volume, in the ordinary way, to the boards in front of me, about a girl of seventeen at the end by the door. There were two people in that bay at that hour and neither of them turned round, and one of them was at the far end of that bench where the count is made.**

And I have worked out in a month what that armful has been doing at the other end of those nine feet. He has carried every armful past the end of it for about a month so that there would be nothing there for anybody to put a thing down by, and a boy who does that has done it on purpose. No form anywhere in this city will take a boy of seventeen for having arranged that a thing should not arrive in front of a girl, and nobody in that shed is going to put it to him and nobody is going to give him anything for it.

And the count at his end is short twice a week, and I cannot ask him one thing about it. If I ask him one thing about that count then I am a foreman who has noticed a count, and a foreman who has noticed a count has been looking at the far end of a bench where a thing was said about a girl, and there is no way of looking along that bench that a person in this shed cannot be shown having looked for.

And I walked ninety foot of my own floor this morning to stand at the door end of a bench and put nothing on it, and there is nobody in that shed who knows how often I have done that.

---

She went back up the ninety foot at the rate she came down it, and she did not stop at the back of the bay on the way, and she has not put a bundle at that end of it since the third week of the third month and she is not going to.

What she has instead is the ordinaryest thing in that shed and the only one that has ever cost anybody anything: a second pair of ears is the only thing in this empire that can put a sentence into the world without anybody being asked anything, and it costs that person the only thing they had, which was that nobody could ever show them having been anywhere in particular.

And it has been in use at the far end of it every working morning for about a month, in nine feet of a bay that size, at the ordinary hour, and she put it there. Nobody in that shed is going to be told that she knows, and she is not going to say a second one out loud in that bay, and the girl at the end by the door is never going to be given one thing to turn down, and the boy at the other end of it is never going to be asked.

The count at the far end of the bench is made at about the sixth hour and it is short again, and there is a paper on a board by that door that has not been filled in in eleven years. The chain on the bench went at one rate the whole of that morning, and neither end of it was looked at by anybody in that shed.
