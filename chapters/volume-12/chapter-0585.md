# Chapter 0585: The Front Of A Ground Floor Started At The Other End, And A Woman Who Is Not Going To Be Seen Standing At It

She had done the front of that ground floor for about a fortnight now, and in the last week of it she had started at the far end instead of the near end, and this morning she was at the far end of it with the cloth and the marks were coming up out of the wood about two inches apart behind her. The line where the cloth stops moves along with her, and the front door of that house stands at the other end of the run with its back to her.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the twelfth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the second hour and about the sixth.

---

She has been at the foot of that stair about twenty years and no boot of hers has ever been on any tread of it above the second, and the third one up is no different from the one below it and there is nothing on that half of that stair for a person to be fetched for. The second tread is the highest thing in that house she has ever stood on and she has never gone past it, and nobody in it has ever come down and asked her why.

A week's money comes out of a hand in the morning and goes into a tin behind a boiler, and that is the only place in that house where a person's money has ever been kept, and it has not been on paper since before she came, and in twenty years she has put a slip of paper into nobody's hand and is not going to start. There is one number cut into the back of a key and that is the whole of the record of that house, and it was cut there before her. The boiler stands at the back of that floor and the tin is behind it, and she has not put a hand near it since before the spring that is gone.

There are three keys on a board by that front door and there have been three on that board for about nine years, and none of them has a name on it and nobody has ever come down to that ground floor and asked her for one of them. That board is up at the height of a man's own head with a nail at each end of it, and the three of them hang with their bows the same way up, and she has never once had a hand on it.

The back of that ground floor is where she has spent her mornings for twenty years, and the boards back there are different boards, and there is no light in that room except what the well gives it. From the back of that ground floor she cannot see the window of any room on the other side of it, and that is not a thing she has wanted to do and not a thing she is going to do. What the well gives does not get out of that room, and that is the whole of it, and it is what she has wanted out of the house for twenty years.

For about eleven weeks she did not touch the front of that ground floor at all, and then she did it for ten minutes in front of a person, and she has not stopped since, and she has moved the start of it to the far end so that the whole of that passage can be seen from the step at the turn and there is nobody in it. The whole run of it is boards that have gone soft along their edges and hard underfoot, and the grain of them stands up in ridges you can feel through the sole of a boot, and the skirting is painted once and the paint has come off it in one long band at the level a cloth would reach.

---

The woman of about thirty-five came down that lane at about the fourth hour for the twenty-fifth week running and went along the passage and stopped where she stops, which is not on the boards. She came down the wet side of the middle of that lane at a walk that is not slow, and she had her own day's cloth over one arm, and she came the whole length of the passage and did not slow at the step.

"You have started at the other end."

"I have."

"It will not be the same floor."

"No. It will be the same boards."

"You will not be able to be seen at it."

"No."

"You are doing that on purpose."

"It is the only way I do anything."

---

**Twenty years of being the cheapest person in that house and I have spent eleven weeks of it finding out what the price was. A person standing at the front of that ground floor can be produced for that hour of that morning, and I stood there on purpose in front of a stranger, and nobody in that house is going to thank me for it and nobody in that house is going to put it to me either. I knew what it would cost before I did it and I did it anyway, and the part I did not know was that it would only cost one thing.**

And a person who cannot be found cannot be given anything. That is the arrangement and it has held for twenty years and it is the only thing in this house that was ever worth anything to me.

The far end of that floor is finished in about a quarter of an hour from the back door and the near end takes the rest of the morning, and the whole of that passage is empty for the whole of the rest of the day, and I am going to go on doing it that way until there is nothing left in this house that could put me anywhere. I have got a fortnight of it behind me and the marks come up the same two inches apart every time, and in another year there will be more of them on those boards, and not one will have my name anywhere near them.

---

The cloth went along and came back and the marks came up out of the wood about two inches apart, and the woman of about thirty-five stood on the step below the turn with a folded sheet of her own paper under her arm and did not come into that ground floor, and the two of them did not say one word about the fortnight or the eleven weeks or the ten minutes. She put her cloth back on her shoulder and went along the passage and out at the front door, and the boards behind her were dry before she had got to the turn.

The door at the front of that house has a lamp on a table by it that has been lit at the sixth hour every evening for about twenty years, and it went on at the sixth hour, and the tin is behind the boiler and the key is on the board by the door and neither of them was touched. Her hand was on the cloth the whole of the morning and it is on the cloth now, and that is the whole inventory of that house at the sixth hour and none of it is hers.

---

There is a lane a person comes down about once a week and a passage about as long as a table at the end of it. It runs from a front door to a turn, and a person at one end of it can see all of it, and that is the only thing either end of that passage is for. From this week there is going to be nothing at all in that passage at any hour of any day. The one person who came there for it is not going to be told why, and the one who emptied it is not going to be thanked for it, and neither of them can be shown anything about the other.
