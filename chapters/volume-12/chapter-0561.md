# Chapter 0561: The Top Step At The Turn, And A Sheet Of Her Own Folded Under A Tin

She had stopped saying the four things about the lid, and she had begun carrying a sheet of her own down that lane in her hand instead of the book, and the reason for it was a wet board.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the sixth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the third hour and about the sixth.

---

The tin is on the top step at the turn.

It has been on that step for about as long as the woman who stands at the bottom of that stair has been at the bottom of it, and about nine years of one man's rent went into it, and there is no paper anywhere in that house that says so.

The lid has been coming down straight on it every morning for about a fortnight. Before that it had been coming down crooked for about a month. It is still not on a piece of paper.

She wants it off the boards.

She has wanted it off the boards for about a fortnight and has not said so out loud to anybody, and there is nowhere on that lane at which she could say it out loud to anybody, and she has worked out that wanting a thing a person has not asked for is not a reason anybody has to come down a lane.

---

She came down at about the third hour with the sheet folded in three across her arm and stopped on the step below the turn, which is the only step in that passage a person can stand on without being in the way of the door.

"There is water on that step," said the woman of about thirty-five.

"There is water on that step in the winter," said the other one.

"It is not the winter."

"It comes up through the boards from the wall. It has done since the house was new."

The woman at the bottom of that stair was not at the bottom of that stair. She was at the top of it, and she had been at the top of it before either of them said anything, which is the whole of what is known about her week.

---

She put the sheet down on the boards and got her fingers under the near side of the tin and lifted it about an inch and pushed the sheet under it and set the tin down on the sheet and squared the sheet at the corners with the heel of her hand.

She did that in about as long as it takes to put a coin into an apron.

Then she stood on the step below the turn with her arms down and waited, which she has not done on that lane before, and she is not going to say that she was waiting.

The woman at the top of the stair came down two steps and put her hand flat on the side of the tin and worked it back and forth about a quarter of an inch, which loosened nothing, and then she took hold of the near corner of the sheet and pulled.

The tin did not move. The sheet came out from under it in a long scrape and about two inches of its fold had gone over wet.

She folded it in half the other way and put it on the top step face down, where she had picked it up from, and squared the edges of it with two fingers, and did not look at the woman standing on the step below the turn.

"You have put that under it."

"It was on wet boards."

"It has been on wet boards for nine years."

"That is not the same as it being wanted on them."

---

**I have come down that lane for five weeks with nothing in my hands and this week I have come down it with something, and the something is a sheet of my own under a tin that is not mine in a house that is not mine, and the woman it is standing on is the only person in this lane who goes through that passage, and she has just told me the boards have been wet for nine years and she is right.**

The woman at the top of that stair went back up the two steps and put her hand on the lid and left it there for about as long as it takes a lid to stop rocking, and the lid did not rock.

She did not ask where the sheet came from. She has not asked that woman one thing in five weeks and she is not going to, and there is a version of asking that consists of a pair of eyes on a folded sheet, and she did not do it.

---

The woman of about thirty-five went back up that lane without stopping anywhere on it.

The sheet is on the top step at that turn with the wet side up and the fold gone, and it is not hers any more in any way that would help her, and it is not that woman's either, and the whole of what it is now is a wet fold of paper on a step at a turn in a house where nobody has ever asked anybody anything.

She has been five weeks in a passage she does not live in and she has got nothing that can be shown and she has left a thing behind.

---

She came down that lane the week after with nothing in her hands and the book shut under her arm, and she has worked out that she is not going to put a sheet under anything else in that house.

That is not a decision she made standing in her own kitchen. It was made between the top step and the step below it, over about as long as it takes a tin to be rocked and stopped, by a woman who has never once in twenty years asked anybody anything and who is not going to start with a sheet of paper.
