# Chapter 0586: The Room At The Top Of A Stair, And A Satchel Set Down At The Far End Of Those Boards

He had gone up that stair four times a week for eleven years with a satchel on his shoulder and set it down at the near end of those boards, and in the last fortnight he set it down at the far end twice, and this morning he set it down there a third time at about the first hour. The strap goes over the left shoulder and it has gone over that shoulder on every one of those four flights for eleven years, and he has never once gone up on the other side.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the twelfth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the first hour.

---

The stair has no other way up. There is a case at the near end of those boards and about four hundred blanks a year come out of it and go down four flights inside somebody's coat, and there is a box of about four hundred blanks under the far end of the boards that is not the case and that has not been opened. Nobody has accounted for it in about six years. It is a different size from the case and it is a different colour, and it stands under the far end of the boards where nothing has to be moved for it, and nobody in that building has ever given a reason for it being there, and nobody has ever been asked for one.

He has been handed a thing in that satchel every week for eleven years and has got nothing back for it and has never once been told what was in it, and it has never once been a courtesy between him and anybody in that building, and he has never asked. It is not a parcel and it is not a wage, and he has never once put a hand into the satchel, and there is nobody on that floor he could ask about it without it turning into a thing he had asked about.

---

There is about a minute of carrying in that stair and about eight hundred of them in four years, and he knows the fourth step from the sixth because the fourth one has a hollow in it. He has gone up it four times a week since he was young and has never once come up it with anything in his hands that was not put into them at the bottom of it. He goes up all four flights on the same side because of the bag, and the fourth step is the only one of the whole climb that gives under a boot, and he has never once gone up it trying to find out whether it would.

At the near end of those boards there is a woman of twenty-four who has been coming in twenty minutes early for two years and has never wanted one single thing out of anybody on that floor in all that time, and at the far end of two joined tables there is a clerk of about twenty-nine with a book open in front of her and a pen lying on the wood. Neither of the two of them has ever waited on him and neither of them is going to. The pen has not been in that clerk's hand at any hour of this morning, and the wood round about her book has been worn pale in a ring by books being set down and lifted again off it.

The woman of thirty-four was at the far end of those boards where she has been for eleven years and she did not get up and she did not move her chair. Her own two hands were flat on the wood in front of her and there was nothing in either of them, and the boards at that end of the room were bare from the wall to the table and had been for about eleven years.

"That satchel has been on that floor for eleven years."

"It has."

"You have never once asked what is in it."

"I have not."

"Not once in eleven years."

"Not once in eleven years."

And she went on with what she was at, and she has said the shape of a thing out loud in that room four times in eleven years and has not been asked one thing back about any of them, and she is not going to say a fifth. And she has not asked him what is in the satchel and she is never going to ask him what is in the satchel, and if she did he would tell her the same thing he has told nobody, which is that he does not know.

---

**A person who is handed a thing every week for eleven years and has never once been told what is in it cannot afterwards be shown to have received it, and cannot be shown not to have received it either, and there is nowhere in this empire to put a person who has had nothing. I have worked that out in a fortnight and I have been the other sort since the first week I came up these stairs, and the other sort is not possible this month.**

I put the bag down at the near end of these boards for eleven years because that is where I have always put it down, and the near end is not a place. It is a habit, and a habit cannot be found, and neither can the man in it. For eleven years a thing has gone into the satchel at the bottom of four flights and come out of it at the top of them, and the man carrying it has been the same man every week, and I could not have told you on any morning of those eleven years which of them was the one that mattered.

The far end is where the woman of thirty-four sits, and she has said out loud that she is not going to be thanked for any part of any morning. I put the bag down there this morning at about the first hour, on purpose, where anybody coming in could say a man had left a thing at that end and gone, and I stood nine foot off it, which is further than anybody in the room has to stand to see a satchel, and waited while a sheet got squared.

---

Nobody came to that end of those boards. The count went on at the near end and the woman of thirty-four went on with what she was at and did not look along the length of the room, and about as long as it takes to rule a line nobody had picked it up and he put the strap over his shoulder himself and took it back down four flights the way he has taken it down every week for eleven years. The lid of the case was up at the back the whole of the time and the stack came out of it the same way it comes out every morning, and it went down those four flights inside somebody's coat with its four corners gone soft off the carrying.

There is a box under the far end of those boards and a case at the near end of them and a wall board behind with four items on it and not one of them time, and none of the three of them was touched and none of them was looked at, and the drawer under that board is shut and is not going to be opened. The board itself is soft and dark, and things have been written on it and taken off it again, and the four that are on it now are not in a line, and nobody has ever put it to the board what order they went on in.

Eleven years of four times a week went up and down that stair this morning with him in it, and the strap went into the shoulder it goes in. He stood nine foot off a satchel at the far end of those boards until a sheet had been squared, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that takes a man for having put a thing where somebody might have taken it, and there was not one person on that floor who did. Four flights and the strap back over the same shoulder, and a minute of that is a long time to be standing still in a room with a bag in it that nobody has taken.
