# Chapter 0605: A Coin That Did Not Go Under A Door, And A Woman Who Went Up One Stair To Stand Beside A Tin

She had put a coin under that door every working morning for about two years and this morning she had it in the front of her apron and did not go up the second tread.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the fifth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the first hour and about the fourth.

---

That passage runs from the front door to the turn and the whole of it can be seen from either end of it. The tin stands on the top step at that turn, lid down, corner to the wall, in the finger's width of space between the nosing of that step and a skirting that has been there since before her, and about nine years of one man's rent is in it and not one sheet of paper has ever been in that tin.

She has been at the foot of that stair twenty years and the second tread is the highest thing her boots have ever been on. On the floor above the second of those stairs there is a door, and a coin has gone under it at the first hour on every working morning for about two years. Behind that boiler there is a key in a tin with a number cut into the back of it, and three keys hang on a board by that front door, none of them with a name on it.

---

The woman of about thirty-five came down that lane for the forty-fifth week running, at about the fourth hour, and went along that passage and did not stop on the step she always stops on, which is not on the boards. She went up.

She went up the two steps and stood on the top one, on the boards, beside that tin, and she did not touch it, and she stood there about as long as it takes to fold a cloth once.

"You have not been on that step in twenty years."

"I have not."

"You have not asked me one thing about that tin in twenty years either."

"You have not asked me for anything."

Then she went down those two steps and went along that passage and out at the front door, and she did not look at that tin again on her way out, and the door shut about as long ago as that happens.

---

**A habit is a thing a person can be shown having, and I worked that out about a month ago with my hand flat on the lid of that tin, and I have put that coin under that door every working morning for about two years and every single one of them was mine, and this morning I have it in my apron and I have not gone up.**

Here is the reason, and it has not been said to one person and it is not going to be. That door has not been opened in about two years. A coin under a door that has not been opened is nothing. A coin under a door that has been opened is a thing that was on that floor at the hour the door was opened. There is no arrangement in nine hundred buildings that separates a coin somebody put there weeks before from a coin that was there when the door came open, and the second of those two is a fact about a room and the first one is a fact about a woman. A woman who has been on that stair at the first hour four times a week for two years is a woman anybody could produce.

He is not coming down those stairs. He has not come down them in about two years and he told nobody in this house that he has done it either time, and I have never gone up past the second tread in twenty. And he has had that door standing open a hand's width for a week now, and I found that out because the light came under it at the fourth hour and the stair is at the end of my own front room, and I am not going to be the thing that is under a door when a door in this house comes open.

Twenty years of being the cheapest person in this house bought me one gap and I have spent it on a habit, and a habit is the one thing in this matter that can be produced against a person without anybody asking that person anything, and I would rather have the gap.

And she came up those two steps today and stood on my tin and did not touch it, and that is the first time in twenty years that a person in this house has been further up that stair than I have. I have not asked her one word about it and she did not say one word about it, and there is a man on the floor above me whose door is standing open, and neither of the three of us is going to say a word about that either.

---

The top step at that turn has a tin on it and the whole of that passage is empty. There is a door on the floor above the second of those stairs and there is nothing under it this morning, and it is the first working morning in about two years that there has been nothing under it, and about nine years of one man's money is on the top step and it is staying there.
