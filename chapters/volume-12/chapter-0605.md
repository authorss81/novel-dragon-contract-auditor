# Chapter 0605: A Coin That Did Not Go Under A Door, And A Woman Who Went Up One Stair To Stand Beside A Tin

She had put a coin under that door every working morning for about two years and this morning she had it in the front of her apron and did not go up the second tread.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the fifth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the first hour and about the fourth.

---

The passage runs from the front door to the turn and the whole of it can be seen from either end of it. The tin stands on the top step at that turn, lid down, corner to the wall, in the finger's width of space between the nosing of that step and a skirting that has been there since before her, and about nine years of one man's rent is in it and not one sheet of paper has ever been in it.

The boards of the passage are old enough that they have gone soft at the edges and hard in the middle, and they have been washed so often that the grain stands up in ridges you can feel with a heel through a thin sole. The skirting is painted and the paint has gone off it in a long band at the height of a cloth, and where the cloth stops the boards are dry and where it has not stopped they are not, and that line is the only division anybody makes in that house on an ordinary morning.

There is a smell off the passage that is water and coal and something that has been going on in a corner for a year. The light comes in at the front door and stops about half way down and does not reach the turn until late in the day, so that for most of the morning the top step and the tin on it stand in the shade with a bright piece of the near boards in front of them.

She has been at the foot of that stair twenty years and the second tread is the highest thing her boots have ever been on. On the floor above the second of those stairs there is a door, and a coin has gone under it at the first hour on every working morning for about two years. Behind that boiler there is a key in a tin with a number cut into the back of it, and three keys hang on a board by that front door, none of them with a name on it.

---

The woman of about thirty-five came down that lane for the forty-fifth week running, at about the fourth hour, and went along the passage and did not stop on the step she always stops on, which is not on the boards. She went up.

The lane is wet on the side of the middle and she comes down the wet side of it at a walk that is not slow. She had the cloth of her own day in the crook of one arm and she came the length of the passage at the rate she comes it and did not stop, and she went up the two steps and stood on the top one, on the boards, beside the tin, and she did not touch it, and she stood there about as long as it takes to fold a cloth once.

She put her hand flat on the lid of it for the length of a breath and took it off again, and the lid did not move, and that is all she has ever done with it in the whole time she has been coming down this lane.

"You have not been on that step in twenty years."

"I have not."

"You have not asked me one thing about the tin in twenty years either."

"You have not asked me for anything."

Then she went down those two steps and went the length of the passage and out at the front door, and she did not look at the tin again on her way out, and the door shut about as long ago as that happens.

---

**A habit is a thing a person can be shown having, and I worked that out about a month ago with my hand flat on the lid of the tin, and I have put that coin under the door every working morning for about two years and every single one of them was mine, and this morning I have it in my apron and I have not gone up.**

Here is the reason, and it has not been said to one person and it is not going to be. That door has not been opened in about two years. A coin under a door that has not been opened is nothing. A coin under a door that has been opened is a thing that was on that floor at the hour the door was opened. There is no arrangement in nine hundred buildings that separates a coin somebody put there weeks before from a coin that was there when the door came open, and the second of those two is a fact about a room and the first one is a fact about a woman. A woman who has been on the stair at the first hour four times a week for two years is a woman anybody could produce.

He is not coming down those stairs. He has not come down them in about two years and he told nobody in this house that he has done it either time, and I have never gone up past the second tread in twenty. And he has had the door standing open a hand's width for a fortnight now, and I found that out because the light came under it at the fourth hour and the stair is at the end of my own front room, and I am not going to be the thing that is under a door when a door in this house comes open.

I have thought about it the way I think about the cloth. The cloth comes down these boards from the near end and I get to the turn on the fourth day of a week, and the day I do not get there the boards are wet where I stopped and dry where I got to, and no person anywhere could put me at the turn on a day I did not stand there. Twenty years of that is not cleverness. It is a woman doing a floor.

Twenty years of being the cheapest person in this house bought me one gap and I have spent it on a habit, and a habit is the one thing in this matter that can be produced against a person without anybody asking that person anything, and I would rather have the gap.

And she came up those two steps today and stood on my tin and did not touch it, and that is the first time in twenty years that a person in this house has been further up the stair than I have. I have not asked her one word about it and she did not say one word about it, and there is a man on the floor above me whose door is standing open, and neither of the three of us is going to say a word about that either.

---

The top step at that turn has a tin on it and the whole of the passage is empty. There is a door on the floor above the second of those stairs and there is nothing under it this morning, and it is the first working morning in about two years that there has been nothing under it, and about nine years of one man's money is on the top step and it is staying there.

The front door is shut and the boards from the near end to where the cloth stopped are wet and the rest of them are dry, and the second tread has got nothing on it and has had nothing on it since before the light came up. Nobody in this house came past it this morning and nobody is going to come past it this afternoon, and there is nothing in nine hundred buildings that takes a person for a stair that stayed empty.
