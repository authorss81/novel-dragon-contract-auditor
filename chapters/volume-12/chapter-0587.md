# Chapter 0587: A Landing Four Foot By Six, And A Man Behind Four Inches Of Wood Who Has Not Stood On It

She had been coming up that stair at about the fourth hour for about eight weeks, and in the last fortnight she had come up at the first hour as well and stayed a little while both times, and this morning he stood in the middle of that floor for about as long as a coin takes to travel under a door and did not go to the door. She had the coin in her hand and it went under the door before she said anything, and he was not at the door when it went, and a coin's travel under a door is not a long thing.

It is the second day of the first week of the first month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the third hour and about the sixth.

---

There is a landing at the top of that stair about four foot by six and there is a mat on it that is the landing's and not his. Its near corner stands up where the boards have lifted at the joint, and it has been about three months since he gave something for that mat and he has not been down that lane since. The mat is square and flat and it came off a cart with four other things on it at the end of a lane, and he carried it up under his own arm, and nothing about the face of it would tell a person that anything had ever happened to it.

About four foot of that landing is not dark at the first hour and none of it is not dark at the fourth hour, and the window in the room behind that door is shut with its catch turned and has been for about two years, and there is a lamp standing on the floor at the end of that bed that has never been lit. The room is about eleven foot by fourteen and the bed stands against the wall and is not made up, and the window has a stool under it, and a chair is set over against it facing four feet of plaster with the window at its back, and the coat is on a nail in the middle of those four feet.

---

**A man who stands still in a place cannot afterwards be shown to have been anywhere else, and I have spent about ten years being unfindable by never once standing still, and I stood in the middle of that floor this morning for the length of a coin going under a door and I know exactly what I was doing and I could not tell you the name of it. I have had ten years of never stopping and one morning of stopping, and the two of them do not go together, and I am not going to find out which of them I am going to lose.**

She is on that landing at the first hour and at the fourth hour and she has been at the fourth hour for about eight weeks. I am on this side of four inches of wood and I have not been on that landing in about two years, and the two of us are the only people in that house who have ever said a true thing out loud, and neither of them is going to be entered anywhere for it.

There is no way of standing in a place and being unfindable at the same time and I worked that out in a fortnight and I have not worked out which of the two I would have to put down, and I am not going to work it out standing in front of a person. I have got about two years of a room behind that door and about ten years of not stopping, and the two of them have never needed each other before this month, and I am not going to be the one who finds out which of them to give up standing in front of a person.

---

The light goes off that window at about the fourth hour and the fourth hour is a different quarter of an hour every week of the year, and the last hour of every working morning in that room has been done in the dark for about two years, and he put that there himself and has not taken it back. The light that does get in at all comes in as one piece, lies down by the stool, and is gone again inside a morning.

A coin comes under that door every working morning at about the first hour and goes about four inches along the boards and stops, because there is nothing on that floor to stop it. There is one of them on that table and there is nothing in that room it can be spent on, and he put a second one back under that door about six weeks ago and did not go down that stair afterwards, and the one on the table is his and is not going to be spent. There is a jug on the table with a dry ring inside it where there was water, and beside it a glass lying face up with the top left corner gone off it, and one coin lying in off the edge of the wood by that much, and neither the jug nor the glass has been moved this morning.

Her boot came on the second tread at about the first hour and stopped on the mat and the corner went down under it and came back up. She was not on the mat long, and about four foot of the landing was not dark while she stood on it, and she went on up and he stayed where he was.

"You have not opened that door since before the spring that is gone."

"I have not."

"Not once in about two years, and I have not knocked, and I am not going to, and nobody in this house is going to be thanked for that."

"That is not the arrangement."

"That is the arrangement and it is the only one there is."

The coin went under the door and the boot went on down past the second tread and the stair went quiet, and the corner of the mat was up again behind her. She came down at the rate she comes down and she did not hurry on the second tread, and he was somewhere in the middle of the room with his hand on nothing.

---

She came up again at about the fourth hour and stood on that landing and he stood in the middle of that floor and neither of them said one word. The light went off the landing while she was on it and went off the room behind that door at about the same time, and the gap under that door did not change, and it has not changed twice now and it is not going to change. She came with her hand in the front of her apron and she had nothing at all in the other one, and neither of them put a hand on anything the whole time she was standing there.

He did not take the latch of that door and he is not going to take it, and the gap under it has not changed twice now and is not going to change a third time. She stood on a mat that is the landing's and came down that stair at the rate she comes down it, and he stood in the middle of a floor he takes by the week and stayed where he was, and that is the whole of what has happened between the two of them this month. About a fortnight, and about four foot of landing and four inches of wood, and not one word at any hour of any of the mornings, and there is no column in this city that takes a coin put under a shut door.
