# Chapter 0584: The Sill At The Near End Of That Corridor, And A Pot Moved Four Inches Along It In A Fortnight

He had been at that sill nineteen years and the pot had been at his right hand for the whole of it, and in the last fortnight he had moved the pot four inches along that stone towards the near end, and he could not say why and nobody had put it to him. The pot is the same pot it has always been and the sill has not moved and neither has he, and the four inches are the only difference anybody could see in that corner of a building for a fortnight.

It is the second day of the second week of the twelfth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour.

---

The sill is about four feet long and it takes the weather off a lane. The tin on it was cut out of another one by his own hands in a winter before he came to that floor, and there are two pens in it and they are the same pen, and it hands one straight back and takes the other straight out, and it has never once had an opinion about which of them went in. One cut edge of it is a shade longer than the other and the two of them do not lie flat together, so there is a hair of daylight under the longer one on a bright morning, and it has been like that since before he came.

He signed with his right hand for nineteen years. For about two months now the hand that goes into that pot is whichever one goes, and about half the time it is the other one, and he can find no rule for it and has not been asked for one. There is no reason in the arm for it. The right one got there first and went in for the whole of nineteen years, and a man who has done a thing with one hand for nineteen years does not notice which of the two of them he is reaching with.

Something in the order of nine hundred sheets leave that building in a year, one at a time, and before he puts a pen to the foot of any of them he reads the head and the date and the number at the top of it. That is the whole of the care he has ever added to anything and there is not one other thing in his working life that he takes that much care over. He lays the nib down across the top of the line and not along it, and the mark comes out under the number and never over any of the writing above it, and now and then the words between the head of a sheet and the foot of it are in a hand that is not the man holding the pen.

Four people in this matter cannot get the sense out of a paragraph. He is not one of the four, and has said so out loud on that floor, more than once, in the ordinary way.

---

There is a bench he can reach that sill from without standing up, and he has not got up off it once in nineteen years so far as anybody in that building knows. The date at the head of the sheet in front of him is the date and the number at the head of it is the number, and he turns the pen round to him and signs underneath, and nobody in that building has ever watched him do it. The stone is at the height of his forearm where he sits and his forearm lies along it all morning, and an arm that lies on the same four feet of stone for nineteen years learns the cold of it, and he has never once said so out loud on the floor.

The daylight in that corridor comes in over a wall about five foot high and goes about a foot down the far side of it, and by the fifth hour there is nothing on that sill that is not the colour of the stone.

The pot is the only object in that room anybody has ever repaired, and there is nothing else in it to look at except a window at the far end of that corridor and a door at the back of that floor and a girl of nineteen about four foot off him four mornings in a fortnight. He cut it out of another one with his own hands in that same winter and closed the join with his own thumb, and it is the only work he has ever put into anything in his working life, and the join has not opened since.

The girl of nineteen came along that corridor with an armful on her hip and stopped at the far end of that sill, and stopped there, and stayed there. She came the whole way along that run and then she did not go on, and the armful went down on the stone at the end of it, and she put her hand flat on the side of it the way a person steadies a thing that is not going anywhere.

He signed three sheets and did not look four foot along the stone, and the pot took the pen back out of his hand and gave him another, and those three went down that corridor on the armful that was already there and out of that building one at a time in the ordinary way. The three went across four feet of stone and off the end of it, and the armful was heavier by the time the third one was on it, and he had not looked up once while they were going.

She was still at that end of that sill when the fourth went out, and she had not said one word, and he had not said one word, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that takes either of them as the one who was standing at the end of that sill. The fourth one went past her hip and off the end of the stone, and a sheet going four foot on a wet morning does not stop for anybody.

---

**There is a person at the far end of my stone four mornings in a fortnight and a man with two hands at the near end of it, and neither of us can be shown to have come here on purpose, and that is worth having, and there is nowhere in nine hundred buildings to enter a person as the one who stood somewhere. I have not been the person who was doing anything in this corridor for nineteen years, and I have never once spent a minute of what that has been worth, and nobody in that building is going to be thanked for a fortnight of moving a pot.**

I moved this pot four inches in a fortnight and I have gone over it every morning since and I cannot get a reason out of it that would survive being said out loud. There was no room made by it. There was no person asked about it. There is no second copy of a thing in this empire that has as much in it as that pot and a man with two hands.

And it was not for her. I do not know that anybody was going to stand at that end of that stone. I moved a pot and about three feet of that sill has been empty since, and if a person stands at the empty end of it, that is a thing about the person and not about the pot, and there is nothing in this building that would let me be the one who put her there. I can tell you to an inch where that pot sits and I cannot tell you why it sits there, and that is the whole of what a man knows about himself after nineteen years.

---

She said nothing and he said nothing, and at about the fifth hour she carried the armful on down the corridor and out, and he signed eleven more sheets with whichever hand went into the pot, and the day's total went out of that building at the end of it and said nothing about any of this. One of his forearms was on the stone the whole of that time and the skin on it had gone the colour the stone goes at the fourth hour, and he did not look at it.

The pot is at the near end of that sill and about three feet of that stone has been empty at the far end of it for a fortnight, and he could not tell you what put it there or why. He signed fifteen sheets this morning with a hand he could not afterwards name, and the two pens in it are one pen, and nobody came and stood on the empty half of that sill. There was wet stone and a bench and a man on it, and the sheet says nothing about any of it, and there is a place where it could be said and it is not going to be said in one.
