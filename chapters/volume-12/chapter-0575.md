# Chapter 0575: Two Coins On A Table In A Room That Had None, And One Of Them Put Back Under A Door That Was Not Opened

He had picked the coin up off the boards on the first morning, and it had been on that table since, and on the second morning another one came under the door and he had two.

It is the second day of the first week of the tenth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the first hour and about the third.

---

He gave what he had for a mat at the end of a lane about three months ago and he had not been down that lane since, and there is not enough in that room to have a lamp lit or a plate washed every day of a week, and both of those have been going on about four days out of seven for about two years.

The first coin came under the door at about the first hour and he picked it up off the boards and put it on the table on the other side of the floor, face up, and he had it on the table at the second hour and at the third.

Then the second one came under at the first hour on the morning after that and went about four inches along the boards and stopped, because there was nothing on that floor to stop it, and he had two coins in his hand and nowhere in this city to put one of them.

---

The mat on that landing is about three months old, it belongs to the landing and it belonged to the landing by the fourth morning, and its near corner stands up where the joint is and has done for about two months.

She came up at about the first hour and she was on the landing before her hand was down.

"There is one of those every week."

"I have it."

"You have not opened that door."

"No."

The corner went over under her boot and she lifted it and the coin went under flat with the flats of two fingers, and she put the corner back down.

"I know it is down," she said.

He had never said one word to her about the mat and he said one now, through about four inches of wood, and then neither of them said anything else, and her boot came off the mat last, on the step.

---

**I have two weeks of one man's rent on a table in a room where there is no money, and there is nothing I can do with either of them, and I am a man who has not been given anything by anybody in about ten years, which is the whole of what I have been.**

A person who cannot be shown having received a thing cannot be shown having wanted one either, and that has been the arrangement for about ten years and it has cost me nothing at all, and on the first morning this month a coin came under that door and I picked it up, and there is no putting that back. It is out of my hands and it is on my table and I know what it is and I cannot be shown not to know what it is, and I cannot give it to anybody, and I cannot put it under a door, and the only thing there is to do with a coin you have been given is to have a second one and give it back.

That is what happened this morning and there is no other version of it.

---

He waited until her boot had gone down past the second tread and the stair had gone quiet, and then he took the door and turned the rail of it and opened it about a foot, which is as far as that door will go with a landing behind it.

The mat was on the landing. The corner was down. About four inches of the landing was not dark at that hour.

He got down on one knee with the coin flat on the boards and pushed it, and it went under the door and lay against the other one, and the two of them were a coin and a week and about two years.

Then he stood in his own doorway for about as long as it takes a coin to travel under a door, and the stair went down to the ground floor and did not come back up, and he did not go down it.

---

There is no form anywhere in this city that takes a man for putting a coin back under a door, and there is not going to be one, and nobody in that house is ever going to be told that there were two of them and that he chose the one that had been on his table for a day and a night.

The mat is on the landing and the corner is down and the lamp is on the floor at the end of that bed and is not going to be lit. There is one coin on that table that came out of that door and is his and is not going to be spent. He has not gone down that stair, and he is going to be on that landing at the first hour every working morning for as long as he is in that room, and what is on either side of that door is going to stay on its own side of it.
