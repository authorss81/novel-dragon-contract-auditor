# Chapter 0615: A Tin Of Oil Put Back Nine Foot Off That Bar By A Man Who Has Not Said Why

That tin has been standing on those flags about nine foot off that bar since two people each moved it, and this month the man in the felt apron put it back in the place he took it out of, and neither of them has opened it.

It is the second day of the third week of the seventh month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the second hour and about the sixth.

---

She is at this end and not on any roll and has never been one of the clerks in that building, and there is a bar across the stone in front of her and a rack behind it and about four hundred yards of cold flags going away from this end, and nobody has gone the length of them in a working day in four years, including her. A number has been in her head since the spring of her fourth year and it is written down nowhere and is not going to be, and nobody in that building has asked her for it in four years and nobody is going to this month.

---

He came up the stone at about the fourth hour, the way he has come up it four times a day for forty years, and he stopped about nine foot off that bar and looked down at the tin and then went and picked it up.

He carried it nine foot back along those flags and set it down on the flat stone where it stood before either of them touched it, square to the edge, and he put his hand flat on the top of it for as long as it takes to put a coin down, and then he went back down the stone and did not say one word.

"It is back where it was."

"It is."

"You put it there."

"I did."

"You took it from there. That was in the spring and I saw you do it and I did not say anything then either, and now you have put it back, and that is twice that a thing has been moved on that floor by a person who was standing in the room when it was moved, and there is nowhere in nine hundred buildings that a third of that goes."

---

"I am not going to open it."

"I did not ask you to open it. I have asked you nothing this month and I am not going to start, and I have not asked you anything in four years either, and you have never once asked me for that number, which is the only reason I am standing at this end at all."

"I know what it is the only reason for. I said a thing to you on this floor about a year ago and I have not stopped saying it to myself since, and I have never once come up here and asked you for it, and that is not going to change this month."

"I know it is not."

"It is going to stay exactly where it was, and that is the whole of what I have done this morning, and you can go back to the other end of that floor and I will not say one word to you about it, and neither will anybody else in this building, because there is nobody in this building to say it to."

---

**A thing that has been moved once is an accident and a thing that has been moved twice is a place, and that is not a thing anybody made up, it is just what two hands do to a floor.**

It stood on those flags at that spot about four years and then a man in a felt apron picked it up and carried it nine foot and set it down again, and from that day there has been a place on that floor at nine foot off that bar, and everything he does on that stone happens in a place, and a person can be shown standing in a place.

And this morning he carried it back nine foot and set it down square to the edge and put his hand flat on it, and the place is gone, and what is there instead is a thing that two people can be shown having picked up and put back, which is worse, because a place is at least only about being somewhere.

That is the refusal, and it is made in the ordinary way at the ordinary hour, and it needs no form and no heading and no column and no person to have asked him for it, and there is nobody in this empire who could put a question to that man about it and I am not going to be the one who tries.

I would like that tin off this floor. I have wanted it off this floor since the spring of my fourth year and there is nothing whatever in the working of this end that wants it there, and I am not going to move it a third time, because a third time is mine.

I have been at the end of this run for four years so that there would be nothing anybody could produce about me, and I am not going to produce myself for the sake of a tin of oil and a flat stone.

---

The tin of oil is on the flat stone about nine foot off that bar with the lid still on it, and there is one square on the top of that lid where the dust of four years is broken, and nothing else on that floor has been touched today.
