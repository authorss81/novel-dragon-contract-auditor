# Chapter 0569: The End Of A Bench At A Quarter Of An Hour Before The Lane Wakes, And An Armful Carried Past It

She had been coming in about a quarter of an hour early for four mornings and standing at the end of that bench, and there had been nothing at the end of that bench for all four of them.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the eighth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the third hour and about the fourth.

---

She is seventeen.

Nobody in that shed has asked her one thing in about a year and nobody has ever wanted one single thing out of her, and she has not been given the chance to turn anything down, and there is nothing in this empire that could put that choice in front of a girl her age.

She has worked out what that costs, and it is this: a person who has never been given a thing cannot afterwards be shown to have refused one.

Not a bundle. Anything. A thing at the end of a bench, an armful, a word. If nothing is ever put in her reach that she could turn down, then there is nothing she can be shown to have turned down, and she is that for the rest of her life, and there is no column anywhere that could take her as anything else.

She has been at the end of that bench about a year and nothing has been put in her reach in any of it.

---

Then a bundle came to the end of that bench about a fortnight ago and she carried it on to the end by the door.

And a bundle came on the next morning and she carried that one too.

And the woman who puts work at that end came and stood at the front of that bench for about a quarter of an hour, from the other end of it, and neither of them said one word and neither of them looked along it.

She worked out over about four days that she had wanted that bundle and had carried it past anyway, and that wanting it and carrying it past are both true, and that the second one is the one anybody could see.

---

This morning she came in a quarter of an hour early and stood at the end of that bench and there was nothing on it.

There was nothing on it the morning before and the morning before that, because the woman who puts work at that end had not come down the length of that floor since about the sixth morning, and there is nobody in that shed who comes down that floor any more than she does.

She stood at the end of that bench for about as long as it takes a sheet to be squared.

---

He came in behind her with an armful.

He is seventeen as well and that end of that bench is his whole working day, every day of it, and there is nowhere else in that shed for him to be. He can make sense of a paragraph and he is not one of the four who cannot.

He did not stop. He went along that bench at the same rate he goes along it and he did not put the armful down at the end where she was standing and he did not look at her and he carried it on past her to the end by the door and set it down where arms get set down.

---

**He has carried it past me four times because he is the only other person in this shed who knows I can put a thing back, and he would rather the woman never found out. I have been standing at this end of this bench a quarter of an hour early for four mornings for a thing to be put where I could turn it down, and the one person who could put it there without anybody knowing is the one person who keeps it from being there.**

Neither of them said one word about it and neither of them is going to, and there is no form anywhere in this city in which a boy of seventeen is entered as the one who kept a bundle off the end of a bench, and there is none in which a girl of seventeen is entered as the one who wanted it put there.

---

He worked at the end by the door and she worked at the end by the door and the armful went out with the rest at the sixth hour, and the end of that bench stood empty all morning with a girl at it until about the fourth hour and then not.

Nobody in that shed is going to be told that she had come in early. Nobody is going to be thanked for a bundle that was carried past four times or for a quarter of an hour standing at the end of a bench, and nobody is going to be sent for.

The chain went along at the front of that bench the whole of that morning at the rate it has gone for eleven years, and the light came off the roof of that shed about a hand's breadth up the wall and stopped there, and the end of that bench stood empty all day with nobody at either end of it and nothing on it at all.
