# Chapter 0602: An Armful Put Down At The Far End Of That Bench By A Girl Of Seventeen

That count has been running a couple of sheets light twice a week for about six weeks, and this morning it came out even, and nothing had been put on that bench all morning to make it come out even.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the fourth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour.

---

That bay is about nine foot by eleven. The chain along the front of that bench goes at one rate under a woman's hand and it has not slowed for her in about eleven years. A class put her at the back of that bay eleven years ago. The count is taken at the back end of that bench, the girl works at the other end of it, the two of those are about nine foot apart, and the floor between that bay and the wall it stands against is ninety foot the length of it.

The chain has a wooden handle at the end of it and it comes down on the wood with a small knock every time it is drawn back, and anybody standing anywhere else in this shed can hear it and has not heard it for a long time. The bench is a plank on two trestles, planed so often along its front edge that the edge has gone round. Nine foot of the plank is between the two women who work at it, and the two of them have never once had to speak to get an armful past one another.

There is one window high up on the long side of that bay and it does not open. The light off it comes down at an angle and reaches the boards at the far end for about an hour and then gives them up, and dust stands up in it while it is there. Somebody sweeps the floor up to the bench and not under it. The line where the sweeping stops and the line where the light stops are in about the same place, and nobody in this shed has ever said so out loud and nobody is going to start.

A boy of seventeen is at the far end of that bench. He came out of another place a year ago on an engagement somebody wrote down for him, and a silence is sitting on that engagement as well, and he gave up reading the tops of those sheets two springs ago and has not gone back to it.

He is not slow. He is not careless either. He carries more in one armful than the girl carries, and he can get an armful up onto a pile without putting a hand on it twice, and there is nothing anybody has ever seen him do wrong at that end of the bench. The one thing he does there that is not work is that he goes past it.

---

The boy carried the armful past the end of that bench and set it on the boards at the front of the bay and squared it with the heel of his hand. He has carried every armful past that end for about six weeks so that there would be nothing there for anything to be put down by, and this morning he put one down there himself, and the count at the far end of that bench came out right for the first time in about six weeks.

He came down the bay with it held up against his chest and his chin on the top of it, and he did not look at the far end of that bench while he was carrying it, and he set it down the way a person sets a thing down when they mean to leave it there. Then he took his hand off it and the hand went back into his pocket, and he stood at that end for about as long as it takes to square a sheet, and then he went and took the next one up.

She came to the end of that bench with the next armful on her hip and she stopped, and she put it down on the same boards at the same end.

She stops where she stops without paying for it. Her feet finish before she has decided anything, and then she is standing square to the boards with her hands off the armful and her shoulder dropped, and she looks at what she has put down the way anybody looks at a thing they have just finished. The boards are old and have been wet and dried more times than there is anything to say about.

He did not look along nine foot of bench.

---

**I have been standing at the front of that bench for eleven years and I put one true sentence out loud into this bay in the third week of the third month and there were two people in it and neither of them turned round, and one of them was at that end where the count is made. I worked out in a month what that armful had been doing at the other end of those nine feet and I could not put one thing about it into any form in this city, and I walked ninety foot of my own floor about a month ago to stand at that end and I put nothing anywhere.**

I got to that end and I stood there with my hands at my sides looking at the boards and the boy came past me with an armful and did not stop and did not look at me either. There was nothing to see once I was standing on it. That is what a month of working it out comes to, and I have not told anybody that either, and there is not one column in nine hundred buildings for a foreman who has stood at the wrong end of her own bench and come back.

The count has been short twice a week for about six weeks and the boy at that end has been the one carrying past it. A boy who does that has done it on purpose, and there is no form anywhere in this city that will take him for arranging that nothing should arrive in front of a girl, and nobody in this shed is going to put one word to him about it and nobody in this shed is going to give him anything for it.

This morning he put an armful down at that end himself. That is the whole of what happened in this bay today and it is the end of six weeks of a boy arranging the end of a bench so that nothing would be put there in front of a girl, and he has stopped arranging it, and the count is right, and a count being right is a thing about a bench and not about either of them.

And about an hour later the girl carried the next armful down and set it down on the same boards at the same end of the same bench, and the boy did not look along nine foot of bench while she did it.

---

She stayed at that end about as long as it takes to square a sheet and then she picked the two armfuls up together and carried them out of that bay one sheet at a time, at the rate she carries them, and the boy at the other end went on with what he was doing and did not turn round.

He went on stacking. He will go on stacking this afternoon and tomorrow and the day after, and at the sixth hour somebody will take the number off that bench and carry it away, and it will be the right number, and none of that has anything to do with him and he will not be told any of it.

The count at the far end of the bench is made at about the sixth hour and it is right again, and the paper on the board by that door has been left blank for eleven years, and not one word has been put to that boy about any of it and none is going to be.
