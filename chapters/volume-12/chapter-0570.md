# Chapter 0570: A Tin On A Top Step And A Woman Who Has Found Out What An Empty Room Is For

She had been saying true things out loud to an empty room for about a fortnight, and it had cost her the only protection she had and had bought nothing whatever.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the eighth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the third hour and about the sixth.

---

The room at the back of her own house has stood empty since before the spring and she has not let it and is not going to.

She went into it on the first morning of that fortnight and said out loud, standing in the middle of that floor, that she had not ruled a fifth column in nine years because she did not want to rule it. She said it to the floor and to a window with a catch on it and to a door she had shut behind her, and it took about as long as it takes to write a day down, and then she came out and shut the door.

She did it on about seven mornings out of that fortnight.
It is about as big as the two rooms she lets put end to end and it has nothing in it at all except the boards underfoot and one window and the door she shuts behind her, and she stands in the middle of it and says one sentence to the boards and comes out and shuts the door behind her, and the door takes the sentence out of it the way a door does. There is a catch on that window and she has never once turned it, and a shut door in the house is a thing she does about twenty times a day and a shut window is a thing she has never done in there, and she worked out inside the first fortnight that the difference between the two is the whole of what an empty room is.

And what it did was nothing at all, which is the correct result and which she had worked out in about nine years without ever having to say one of them out loud to find out.

---

A room with nobody in it is a room with nobody in it. There is no one in it to hear, and a thing heard by nobody has not been said, and a thing said to nobody has not been said, and she keeps a book in her own hand with four headings over four columns and a fifth one that has gone unruled since she was twenty-six and she is not going to be asked about it and there is nobody in this empire she could ask.
She has been down the lane for a fortnight of mornings since the fourth morning of the third week of that month, and the other one has been at the boards at the front of the ground floor on every one of them, and there has been a person doing her own work within reach of her own mouth on every one of them, and she has not put the sentence to her once. That is not a thing she is going to be able to put into the four columns that are ruled and she is not going to be able to put it anywhere else either, and there is no heading in this empire that takes two women not asking each other a question on a step at a turn.

The whole of the instrument is the second person and the second person has to be working.

---

She came down that lane at about the third hour on the tenth week of it and went through the passage and the tin was on the top step at the turn and the boards were wet again.

"It has been a fortnight."

"It has been a fortnight."

"You have not said anything different."

"I have not said anything different."

The woman at the bottom of that stair went on down that passage with the cloth in her hand, at the same rate, and did not look along it on her way, and she is not going to be asked what happened on the fourth morning of the third week of that month because there is nowhere standing on that lane at which anybody could ask her.
The marks the cloth leaves on the boards at the front of the ground floor are about two inches apart and go the whole length of the front, and a person with her weight on the boards one tread below the turn can see the whole of that run of them from end to end and there is nothing in it this morning except a woman working at the far end of it. She had said two sentences in the passage in a fortnight and both had been said to a woman who was on her way somewhere else with a cloth in her hand, and she has worked out since that a thing said to somebody who is on her way somewhere is a thing said to a person who was not there for it.

---

**I said a true thing out loud in that passage with a woman in it and she was putting a week of money into a tin and she did not stop and she did not turn round. There are two people alive who were in that passage. That is a refusal in the world and it is the first one in nine years that has been anywhere at all, and I have had nothing for it, and there is not one column in this city that either of those two women can be entered in.**

And here is what it cost, and she had not known it that morning and she knows it now.

Before that morning there was no room in this empire she could be put in as having said a thing. That was nine years of having said nothing out loud that belonged to her and it was the only thing she had that could not be taken off her.

Now there is one.

And the other half of it is that she is never going to know whether the tin went over the rim on the fourth morning of the third week of that month because she was counting out a week of a man's money or because somebody nine years of not saying anything was talking in her own passage.

Both of those are inside about half an hour of her having said it out loud, and there is no form anywhere in this city that separates one of them from the other.

She is not going to be able to find out by going down that lane every week until one of them has worn off, because the only way either of them could come to an end is if the woman at the bottom of that stair told her, and that woman has not asked anybody anything in twenty years and is not going to be asked.

---

**And I am going to do it again, and not in that passage and not in an empty room, and in any room in this city where a person is working and nobody wants one single thing out of me, and I will not know whether it took and I will never know, and I am not going to stop to find out.**

She will go down that lane every week for as long as that house stands.

That is the cost and it is a small one and it is hers: from now on there is exactly one room in this empire she is in, and it is somebody else's ground floor, and she is going into it on a working morning to be in it, and nobody in that house is ever going to be told that.
She has been going into a room at the back of her own house for a fortnight to say one sentence to a floor, and from this week she has a passage with a step at the end of it and a woman working at the far end of it, and the two of them are twenty years apart and neither woman knows that the other is standing in the other one on the same morning of a working week. A room with nobody in it is still a room with nobody in it and she has been standing in one for a fortnight, and it did not cost her that, because nothing was ever going to happen in it anyway. It cost her the other one. That is the whole of what a thing said out loud costs in this empire, and it is a fortnight of standing in an empty room for a thing that is going to be standing in somebody else's passage for the rest of her life.

The tin is on the top step at the turn and it is going to be on that step when she is not in that lane.
