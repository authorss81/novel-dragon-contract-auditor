# Chapter 0592: A Satchel That Went Up Four Flights And Was Not Put Down Anywhere On The Top Of Them

He had set that satchel down at the far end of those boards three times in a fortnight and nobody had taken it, and this morning he came up the four flights with the strap over his shoulder and did not put it down at all.

It is the second day of the second week of the second month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the first hour.

---

The floor of that room is boards laid end on end, and it goes the whole way from the door at the bottom of the four flights to the wall at the back, and everything in the place stands on it. A case at the near end of the boards gives up about four hundred blanks a year, and they go down four flights inside somebody's coat, and the lid of it is up at the back and stays up while anybody is at it, and what comes out of it on an ordinary morning is a stack with its four edges squared off and its four corners rubbed soft, and it is the corners that go first, because that is what a coat does to them.

Under the far end of the boards there is a box that is not the case and that has been shut for about six years and that nobody has ever counted. It is not in the way of anything, and there is no reason in the world for it to be on that floor, and it is the only thing in the place that has gone six years without a hand on it.

The count on it is done at the far end of the room. The woman of thirty-four sits at that end and has not shifted off it in eleven years and has never once wanted anything out of anybody who works there. Two tables are joined at the other end, and a clerk of about twenty-nine sits at those with her book open and her pen down on the wood beside it. The pen is not in her hand. The book is open at a page with a line of figures down the side of it and the wood of the table round that book is bare where book after book has been put down and picked up again.

He has gone up those stairs four times a week since he was a young man, and every single thing he has ever carried up them was put into his hands at the bottom of them. He knows the fourth of the four flights from the sixth, because there is a hollow in the fourth one. The stair turns at every landing and the walls of it are brick to about the height of a man's chest and plaster above that, and there is a window at every turn with a wire across it, and a man going up with a bag on his shoulder goes up on the same side of every stair because the bag goes over the left one.

For eleven years a thing has gone into that satchel four times a week and gone down those four flights inside somebody's coat, and he has never once been told what is in it, and nothing has ever come back.

---

He came up the fourth of those flights and took the strap off his shoulder and held the satchel against his side instead of putting it down, and then he stood at the near end of the boards about a yard off that case with the strap still round his forearm, in the way of a floor, for about as long as a sheet takes to be squared. The stair behind him went quiet and nobody came up it after him, and the light off the top window lay down the room in one long strip and he was standing at the edge of it and not in it.

The woman of twenty-four was at the near end with her book open and her hands empty, and she had been coming in twenty minutes before the hour for two years without once wanting a thing off anybody who works up here. She had the near side of the case behind her and the boards under her feet and the whole length of the room in front of her, and she did not get up and she did not turn round, and she put the flat of one hand on the open book and left it lying there.

"Put it down by the case."

"I know where it has been put down for eleven years."

"Then put it down there."

"No."

"You have carried that up those stairs four times a week for eleven years and you have not put it down once in three weeks."

"I have not."

"There is no room on that floor to stand up in."

"There is about as much floor as there was last week."

---

**A man who is given the same thing every week for eleven years and is never told what is in it cannot afterwards be shown to have taken it. That is the whole of what I have got, and I have had it since the first week I came up these stairs, and it has never cost me a thing, and it is the only reason a man with a bag on his shoulder for eleven years has never once been in a position.**

And there is a second one and it came to me in the third week of this month and it is the same shape from the other end. A man who puts a bag down at the far end of a floor where nobody comes is a man who can be produced in that floor at that hour. A man who does not put it down can be produced in the same floor at the same hour. There is no arrangement in this empire that separates the two, and the only thing a man has against either of them is to be in a place, and being in a place is the whole of what I have given up.

And the strap is round my forearm and the leather has gone soft along the top of it where my hand goes, and I have got my thumb hooked over the buckle out of the way of it so that it will not make a noise, and I have not put it down for three weeks, and there is a place along the top of the strap where it has turned over on itself that will take the mark of a thumb for as long as I keep it there.

---

She worked at the far side of that case for the whole of that morning with her back to him. That was not a courtesy and she did not say that it was. The near side of it is where a person stands, and he was standing on it, and she moved round to the other side and stayed there, and the count at the far end of the boards was done by a clerk of about twenty-nine who has never wanted one single thing out of anybody. The two of them worked at it from opposite sides and neither of them said one word, and the sheets came out of it two at a time and went into the satchel, and the count went on at the far end of the room without either of them breaking the rhythm of it.

At about a quarter past the satchel went over his shoulder and he went out and down the stairs, and the strap went into the shoulder it goes in.

The strap goes into the shoulder it goes in, and about halfway down the fourth of those flights his hand went up and found the place where it had been sitting for eleven years, and the place was there, and it had been there yesterday, and it will be there at the first hour tomorrow. There is no form anywhere in this empire that takes a man for a mark on his own shoulder.
