# Chapter 0557: A Lid Put Down Straight On A Tin For The First Time, And A Stair That Did Not Go Up At The Sixth Hour, And Nobody Went Up It

She had been shutting the lid on that tin straight for about a fortnight, and the lid had been coming down crooked on the top of it for about a month before that, and on the fourth day of that run the stair did not go up at the sixth hour and she stood at the bottom of it.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the fifth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the fifth and the seventh hour.

---

She has been at the bottom of that stair for about twenty years and has not been above the second of those stairs once in that time.

A rent comes in once a week out of a hand and goes into her own pocket and has never been written down anywhere in that house. What is scratched into the back of that key is the whole of what the building has ever had to say about a person sleeping in it. The key is not on the board by that door and is not in that tin, and she has not looked for it and is not going to.

She carried that tin up two steps and round a turn herself, about a month before this, and no one in that house was in a position to see her do it. The boiler at the back of that room still holds the warmth of where it used to stand.

---

About nine years of one man's rent is in it and it has not been counted in six years and she has never counted it and there is no reason on earth to count it.

That is the whole of what is in there and the whole of what is not in there, and she could stand in front of that tin for a month and tell you nothing that a person could show, and she has been standing in front of it for about a month.

What she has been doing instead is taking about as long over the lid as it takes to get a coin out of an apron, and putting it down straight, and lifting a finger off it before she takes her hand away, in case it wants to go again.

A tin with a crooked lid is a tin somebody has been in a hurry with. A tin with the lid shut on it is a tin a person has done something to, and there is no form anywhere in this empire in which a woman at the foot of her own stair is entered as the one who shut a lid.

---

**I have shut the lid on about nine years of a rent that has never been on a piece of paper in this house, and I have never counted it, and I am not going to, and nobody is going to thank me for the lid and nobody is going to be told there is a lid to be thanked for. A thing with a lid on it is the first thing in twenty years I have done in this room that a person could look at.**

The woman of about thirty-five came down that lane at about the fifth hour and went through the passage.

She has done that every week for longer than either of them would say, and neither of them has ever said a word to the other about the man who lives above the second stair. She keeps four rooms by the week in a house of her own and keeps a book in her own hand, and has not ruled a fifth column in nine years, and has said out loud once that she is not going to.

"The lid is down."

"It is down."

"You have shut it."

"I have shut it."

Neither of them asked the other one anything else. She went up the lane and did not stop on the step and there is nowhere standing at which either of them could have asked, and it is not going to be made.

---

At about the sixth hour the stair did not go up.

It has gone up at about the sixth hour and come down at about the seventh every working day for about two years, and she knows the sound of it the way she knows the sound of her own coin on a stair, and this morning it stayed where it was.

She stood at the bottom of it.

Not at the foot of it — at the bottom of it, which is the thing a person stands on, and she had not been on the second of those stairs since before she can remember, and she was not on the second of them this morning either.

She stood there for about as long as it takes a brush on boards and she did not go up, and there is not one form in nine hundred buildings that a woman at the foot of a stair could fill in about a stair she did not go up, and she has never once gone above the second and she is not starting at fifty.

---

At about the seventh hour the coin came under that door in the ordinary way and she took it in her hand and put it where she puts it and did not look at the top of the stair on the way past, and neither of them said a word.

Nobody in that house is ever going to be told that the stair did not go up on that morning. There is a key to the top room in that building somewhere and she is the only person who knows that it is not on the board and not in that tin, and what is scratched into the back of it is the whole of what that building has ever had to say about anybody being in it, and she has not put a thumb on it since a spring that is gone.

The lid was shut and straight when she came past it that night, and she took about as long over it as it takes to get a coin out of an apron, and nobody came down that stair to see it, and nobody is going to be thanked for it.
