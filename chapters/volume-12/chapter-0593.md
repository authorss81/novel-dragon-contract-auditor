# Chapter 0593: A Hand Put Flat On The Lid Of That Tin, And Nine Years Of A Man's Money Staying On One Step

She had put her hand flat on the lid of that tin on the top step three mornings in a fortnight and had not lifted it, and this morning she had her hand on it before the stair had finished making noise behind her.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the second month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the first hour.

---

That passage runs from the front door to the turn and you can see all of it from either end of it. It has a door at the near end and a stair at the far end, and the stair comes up out of the sight of anybody standing at the near door, and the whole length of it is boards that have gone soft at the edges and hard in the middle, and the grain of them stands up in ridges you can feel with a heel through a thin sole. The tin stands on the top step at the turn, lid down, with about nine years of one man's rent in it, and there has never been a scrap of paper anywhere in that. It is the only thing in the passage that has not been put there this morning, and the boards round the foot of it are not marked in any way at all.

Twenty years at the foot of that stair and her boots have never been on anything higher than the second tread, and the third is a tread like any other, and there is nothing on the upper half of that flight a person could be fetched for. The front of that ground floor is being done from the far end now, and the effect of that is that a person on the step at the turn can see every inch of the whole length of the passage, and there is nothing in any of it this morning except the tin. There is a key in a tin behind that boiler and a number cut into the back of that key and there are three keys on a board by that front door and none of them has a name on it. The board is up at the height of a man's head with a nail at each end of it, and there have been three keys on it for about nine years, and the back of the ground floor behind that door has no light in it at all except what the well gives it.

---

There is a door on the floor above the second stair, and a coin goes under it at about the first hour on every working morning, and she has been putting it there for about two years, and this morning she had come down off the top step with her hand still shut and she had not gone up the second one. She was standing in the passage with her back to the stair and her fist against the front of her apron, and the stair behind her had gone quiet, and she did not go up it.

He has not come down those stairs in about two years. She has not been up past the second one in twenty. There is a week in this month in which neither of them was on a landing at any hour, and there is no form in nine hundred buildings that takes a woman for having put a coin under a door four times a week, and there is nothing in this house that has ever taken a man for the coin being there.

She lifted it with her knee, the way she has lifted it for about two years, and she had both hands on it and she stood on the top of the stair with it against her chest, and she could have carried it into the back of that ground floor and put it down behind the boiler and there would have been nothing on that step at all. It came up off the boards heavier than it looks and the lid shifted on it once and she got a thumb under the rim again before it went.

She set it down again on the boards she had lifted it off, corner to the wall, into the finger's width of space between the nosing of the step and the skirting that has been there since before her. Then the lid went down flat and she took her finger off the edge of it. She put her heel on the nosing and looked down at the tin and then went down the two steps at the ordinary pace, and the boards under the near end were still wet along the line where the cloth stops.

---

**I have wanted that tin off the step for about a fortnight and I cannot have it, and the reason I cannot have it is that a tin in a new place is a thing a person can be shown having moved. That is the whole of what twenty years has been for. Nobody in this house can produce me for anything, and if I carry that tin into the back of that ground floor this morning then for the rest of it there is a thing in that house that I did, and I will not know what it is for, and I will not be able to put it down.**

**And there is the other thing, and it came to me on the way down off the top step. A coin has gone under that door at the first hour every working morning for about two years and I have put every one of them there. That is a habit, and a habit is a thing a person can be shown having. I have spent twenty years on the ground floor of this house so that there would be nothing anybody could produce about me, and what I have got instead is a habit that goes four times a week, and it goes in coin.**

And I am not going to stop it and I am not going to tell him what it is for and nobody in this house is going to be told that I have been on that step.

And I have just proved to myself that I can do it. That is worse. For twenty years I have been the cheapest person in that house and the whole of what that bought was that a thing in that room was never mine to move, and this morning I stood on the step with about nine years of a man's money against my chest and I put it back in the gap it has been in since before I came.

---

For the thirty-fifth week running the woman of about thirty-five came down that lane at about the fourth hour, went the length of the passage, and stopped on the step she always stops on, which is not on the boards, and looked up at that top step. She was on the wet side of the lane and she came the wet side of it, and she had the cloth of her own day over her shoulder, and the passage she came into was empty from the turn to the near end.

"That has not moved."

"It has not."

"It is the only thing in this passage."

"It is."

"You have been at the foot of that stair for twenty years and you have never once been in the passage at the hour I come down it."

"I have not."

And she stood on her step for about as long as it takes a cloth to be folded once and then she went along it and out at the front door, and she did not look at it again, and neither of them said one word about the eleven weeks or the ten minutes or the thirty-five.

That tin is where it has always been on the top step at that turn, and her hand lay on the lid of it for about as long as it takes a sheet to be squared, and nine years of a man's money is inside it and it is going to stay there.
