# Chapter 0596: About Four Foot Inside A Door At The Back Of That Floor, And A Book With No Line In It For Her

She had not been in that room for about a month, and this morning she stopped at the end of that sill at about the third hour, put the armful down, and went on to the door at the back of that floor and stood about four foot inside that threshold.

It is the second day of the second week of the third month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the third hour and about the sixth.

---

The second copy of that week's minutes goes in on the older woman's own hand now, once a week, in that room, with the door standing open. It is a table at the far end of the room under the window, and the copy is written out from the other one and read back against it, and the door is propped where it has been propped for nineteen years. The pen goes along the line of the day and stops at the end of the week, and she writes a week's copy straight through in one sitting without lifting the pen off the paper, and the corner of the last page is soft where a thumb has been at it. There are nineteen years of that book and the day goes at the head of a line and nothing else does, and the girl of nineteen put about half of them in over four years and nobody ever said one word to her about where any of them went.

Under a window about two foot wide the pane has been out for about four years, and there is a shelf under it, and on that shelf there is a question that does not finish with the older woman's own name written underneath it, and it has been there about eleven years, and nothing has ever been set down after it. There is no pane in the opening at all and it takes the weather straight on, and the sill under it takes the weather straight on as well, and the paper on the shelf has been on it through all of that for eleven years.

She went in four foot and stopped, and the armful stayed on the stone at the far end of that sill where she had put it, in the open, in a corridor about as long as a table. About three feet of the sill at that end has been empty for a fortnight and the armful was on it, and the rest of the stone was bare, and the man at the near end of it had not moved his head.

"You have not come in here for about a month."

"I have not."

"There is a week in that month you came and there is a week in it you did not, and I am not going to say which was which."

"I would not have known."

"No. You would not have known, which is the whole of it."

---

**I gave up the only thing I ever had in a month that is gone, which was that nobody in this building could ever show me having been somewhere in particular. I got a piece of stone about three foot long at the other end of a corridor instead, and I have been at that end of it every morning for about fourteen weeks. I have worked out that standing somewhere often leaves you with a habit, and a habit is a thing that can be produced against you, and a place is not, and that there is a difference between the two and it took me a fortnight to find it.**

And this morning I have put a sheet of a pair of arms down on that stone in the open and gone four foot into the room at the back of this floor. There is a person in this room and she is doing the week's copy on her own hand with the door standing open, and I am a second pair of ears in here. I have put myself here on purpose and nobody in this building is going to be told one word about it, and it is not going to be put to me afterwards, and there is nowhere in nine hundred buildings to put a thing of that kind down.

And the armful is out there in the corridor in the light where anybody coming along that run could see it, and the two of us are four foot apart with a door open between us and no more than that, and the whole of it is that I have lost the one thing I had and bought a place, and the price of a place is that it does not follow you, and I have not been able to work out yet whether that is a gain or a bill.

---

"There is a book on that table and there is no line in it anywhere that has you in it."

"I have not asked to be in it."

"I know you have not. I am telling you that there is not one and I am not going to make one, and that is not mine to give and it is not yours to want, and I have been keeping that book for nineteen years and I have put about four hundred names of people in it and not one of them knew that was what they were for."

"What they were for."

"What they were for. That is the whole of what I have to say about it and I have said it, and you are not going to ask me the other half and I am not going to be asked the other half by anybody in this building for as long as I am in it."

She turned the leaf of that book over and ran two fingers down it to square it against the table, and the girl of nineteen stayed about four foot inside that threshold and neither came further in nor went out. She kept the two feet of floor behind her and she kept the door frame on her left shoulder, and the older woman did not come round the table and did not ask her to come to it, and the two of them stayed like that until the older woman had finished the line she was on.

"There is a man at the end of that corridor and you carry an armful past him forty times in a day."

"I carry it past him."

"You stop at the end of that sill."

"I do."

"You have not said one word to me about it, and I am not going to ask you for a word about it, and you are not going to tell me, and that is the arrangement and it is going to hold for as long as I am on this floor."

---

She went back out of the room and along the corridor and picked the armful up off the stone where she had left it, and went on down to the door and out into the lane with it, in the ordinary way, one sheet at a time, and the man at the near end of that sill signed four more and did not look along the stone. She went the whole length of the run with the last of it against her chest and she did not look down the corridor on her way to the door, and the boards under her were dry all the way to the step.

The second copy of that week's minutes went in about an hour later on the older woman's own hand, with the door standing open, and there is no line in that book anywhere with the end of the stone in it.
