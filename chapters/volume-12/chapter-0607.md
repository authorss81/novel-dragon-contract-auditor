# Chapter 0607: A Jug Emptied And Filled At The End Of A Landing And Brought Back Into That Room By A Woman Who Was Not Asked

He had let the water in that jug go off rather than empty it, on purpose, about two months ago, and this morning she carried it out to the pail at the end of that landing and brought it back full.

It is the second day of the first week of the sixth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the second hour and about the sixth.

---

The door has stood open about a hand's width for four weeks now. The pail is at the end of the landing outside it, and the landing is about four foot by six with a mat on it that is the landing's, and the near corner of the mat stands up where the boards have lifted at a joint. The pail is the only water in this house. The bed is against that wall and is not made up, and the stool is under the window, and the chair is where it has been since a spring that is gone with its face to four feet of plaster and the coat is on the nail in the middle of them.

The landing is cold the whole length of it and colder at the end where the pail is, because that end has the stair coming up out of it. The paint on the wall along there has gone along in a band where shoulders and pails go past twice a day, and there is a dark place on the boards in the corner behind the pail where a pail has been stood wet more times than there is anything to be said about. A nail is in that wall about the height of a hand with nothing on it and a second one below it with nothing on that either, and there are two more further along that have had something on them and had it taken off.

The pail is galvanised and it has a rim turned over at the top and a handle that comes up in one piece off two sides. It is about two thirds of the way up and it is the only water in this house, which means that everybody in this house comes past the end of the landing twice a day and has done for a long time, and it also means that a man in the room behind the door has had two inches of nothing to put his hand into for a month.

She has been coming up the stair at the first hour and about the fourth hour for about twenty-one weeks.

---

"There is nothing in that jug."

"There has not been anything in it since before the last month."

"It has been standing on the table with a dry ring in the bottom of it."

"Yes."

"You have not carried it out."

"I have not."

---

She took it off the table and went out through the door and along the landing and emptied it into the pail and filled it at the pail, and brought it back in, and set it down on the table where it had been standing all along, and the jug was about two inches short of the top.

She takes a full jug with two hands and holds it against her body on the way in, and she has got the way of it where her hip takes the weight and the hand nearest the door stays on the handle. The water goes up over the heel of her hand and comes back down again and there is a wet place on the front of her apron before she is through the door, and she does not put the jug down at any point between the pail and the table, and nobody in this building will ever be told which of the four seconds that took.

"There is more in that pail than there was."

"There is."

"You have not touched this table for a month except to put that glass down on it."

"The glass came with the room."

"It did not come with the room. It came from somewhere and you have not told me and I have not asked you, and that is not what I am on about."

---

**Two months. That is how long that jug has stood on the table with a dry ring in the bottom of it and that is the whole of what I did about it, and in either of those two months I have not gone near the pail once. I had it worked out the way a person has a thing worked out when there is nobody in nine hundred buildings to hand it to: a man who changes that water is a man who can be shown in that room every morning of his life, and a man who lets it stand is a man nobody in this house would want one single thing out of, and I picked the second of those two on purpose and I have not looked at it since.**

And this morning a woman who has never once wanted one single thing out of me in about twenty-one weeks of coming up the stair twice a day emptied that jug and filled it and carried it back in, and there are about two inches of water in it. There is now a thing in the room that a person can be shown having done, and the person is her, and she did it for me. She did not ask me one word about it, and nothing in this house is going to say one word to her about it either.

I had it worked out that I am going to put a sheet of paper on the table. There is nothing written on the sheet except four printed things along a strip at the top of it and not one of the four is a name, and it has been in the pocket of that coat about two years, and a man who puts it on the table in front of a person cannot afterwards be shown to have been nowhere near any of it. I have got as far as a latch and a door standing open a hand's width and a person who came into this room and stood in front of that coat and went out again.

And now there is a jug of clean water on the table that she filled, and the table is not only mine, and I am not going to put a piece of paper down on a wet jug a woman has just carried in from that landing, and I have not got as far as it, and I have not gone backwards, and the jug is there.

---

That jug was on the table at about a foot back from the edge with about two inches of water in it, and the glass lies face up beside it, a corner of it gone, and the door is still standing open about a hand's width, and the water in that jug is the first clean thing that has been put on the table in about two months.

She went down off that landing the way she came up it, and the boards went quiet behind her, and the water is on the table and the table is not his any more in the way it was an hour ago, and that is not a thing anybody in nine hundred buildings can enter a man for.
