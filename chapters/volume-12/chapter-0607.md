# Chapter 0607: A Jug Emptied And Filled At The End Of A Landing And Brought Back Into That Room By A Woman Who Was Not Asked

He had let the water in that jug go off rather than empty it, on purpose, about two months ago, and this morning she carried it out to the pail at the end of that landing and brought it back full.

It is the second day of the first week of the sixth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the second hour and about the sixth.

---

That door has stood open about a hand's width for four weeks now. The pail is at the end of the landing outside it and that landing is about four foot by six with a mat on it that is the landing's, and the near corner of that mat stands up where the boards have lifted at a joint. The pail is the only water in this house. The bed is against that wall and is not made up, and the stool is under the window, and the chair is where it has been since a spring that is gone with its face to four feet of plaster and the coat is on the nail in the middle of them.

She has been coming up that stair at the first hour and about the fourth hour for about twenty-one weeks.

---

"There is nothing in that jug."

"There has not been anything in it since before the last month."

"It has been standing on that table with a dry ring in the bottom of it."

"Yes."

"You have not carried it out."

"I have not."

---

She took it off that table and went out through that door and along that landing and emptied it into that pail and filled it at that pail, and brought it back in, and set it down on that table where it had been standing all along, and the jug was about two inches short of the top.

"There is more in that pail than there was."

"There is."

"You have not touched this table for a month except to put that glass down on it."

"The glass came with the room."

"It did not come with the room. It came from somewhere and you have not told me and I have not asked you, and that is not what I am on about."

---

**Two months. That is how long that jug has stood on that table with a dry ring in the bottom of it and that is the whole of what I did about it, and in either of those two months I have not gone near that pail once. I had it worked out the way a person has a thing worked out when there is nobody in nine hundred buildings to hand it to: a man who changes that water is a man who can be shown in that room every morning of his life, and a man who lets it stand is a man nobody in this house would want one single thing out of, and I picked the second of those two on purpose and I have not looked at it since.**

And this morning a woman who has never once wanted one single thing out of me in about twenty-one weeks of coming up that stair twice a day emptied that jug and filled it and carried it back in, and there are about two inches of water in it. There is now a thing in that room that a person can be shown having done, and the person is her, and she did it for me. She did not ask me one word about it, and nothing in this house is going to say one word to her about it either.

I had it worked out that I am going to put a sheet of paper on that table. There is nothing written on that sheet except four printed things along a strip at the top of it and not one of the four is a name, and it has been in the pocket of that coat about two years, and a man who puts it on that table in front of a person cannot afterwards be shown to have been nowhere near any of it. I have got as far as a latch and a door standing open a hand's width and a person who came into this room and stood in front of that coat and went out again.

And now there is a jug of clean water on that table that she filled, and that table is not only mine, and I am not going to put a piece of paper down on a wet jug a woman has just carried in from that landing, and I have not got as far as it, and I have not gone backwards, and the jug is there.

---

That jug was on that table at about a foot back from the edge with about two inches of water in it, and the glass lies face up beside it, a corner of it gone, and that door is still standing open about a hand's width, and the water in that jug is the first clean thing that has been put on that table in about two months.
