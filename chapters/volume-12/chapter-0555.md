# Chapter 0555: The Shed On The Slade, And A Reader Of Seventeen Who Reads The Top Of Every Sheet And Then Stops

He had begun reading the top of the sheets that came to the end of that bench, the head of them and the date at the head and the line at the top, before he did anything at all with them, and it had been a fortnight, and this morning he stopped.

It is the second day of the first week of the fifth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fifth hour.

---

He is seventeen. He has been at the end of that bench since he came out of somewhere else and nobody in that lane has ever wanted one single thing out of him and nobody has ever once said a word to him about any part of it, and the terms he is on at the end of that bench are a written engagement and a silence and he has not broken either.

He can make sense of a paragraph. There are four people in this matter who cannot and he is not one of them and never has been. The man at the front of that bench is not one of them. The woman of fifty-three who keeps minutes nine miles off is not one of them. Nobody in that shed is going to ask him one question about a number because nobody in that shed has ever asked him a question.

---

The sheets go down that lane in armfuls and come back at the other end and go on other people's hands and the two of seventeen at the end of that bench have the far end of them, which is where a bundle sits for about as long as it takes a person to look at it.

There is a strip printed along the top of every one of them and four things printed along the top of that, and not one of the four is anybody's name, and he has read those four things about nine hundred times without once learning anything he did not know in the first week.

What he began doing was reading the top.

Not the work. The head of the sheet, and the date at the head of it, and the line at the top, and whether the two of them belonged to the same job.

---

The reason is about a fortnight old and it took him until the third morning to get to it and he has not said it out loud to anybody and is not going to.

There is no form anywhere in this empire in which he is entered as the one who read the top of a sheet. There is not one either in which he is entered as the one who did not, and between those two there is nothing at all, and he has worked out that the moment a person reads the top of one sheet and the head of it says something about the sheet that came down the lane the week before, that person cannot afterwards be put back as the one who never read the top of anything.

It is not a rule anybody made. It is just what a thing is, and he is seventeen and can make sense of a paragraph, and he got to it about as fast as it takes a bundle to come down a lane.

---

He read the top of about one a day for a fortnight and he found nothing.

That is the whole of what was in them. Not one head out of place in about as many sheets as a fortnight of them comes to. There was nothing in a single one of them to find, and he kept reading, and on the fourteenth morning of that run he put a sheet down square on the bench and squared it along the edge with the heel of his hand and stopped reading the top of anything, and there was nobody in that shed who had asked him to and there is nobody who is going to notice that he ever did.

---

The girl beside him had put the bundle back three feet along that bench twice in a fortnight and neither of them had said one word about it and neither of them was going to.

He had seen her do it. He is at the end of that bench every working day of his life and there is nowhere else for him to look, and he had seen her get her hands under the near edge of it and lift it about four inches and set it down about three feet along, in the middle, where hands go all day, without her face changing.

And he had worked out what that was, at about as fast as it took him to work out the top of the sheets, and what it was is that the woman who puts work at the end of that bench wants the girl to be able to put it back, and the girl is able to put it back, and the woman is going to find out that she can.

And he worked out then, about a week ago, that he would rather the woman did not find out, and that he would rather the girl kept doing it, and that there is not one thing in this empire he could have said to anybody about any part of that.

---

**A person who reads the top of a sheet is a person somebody could show having read the top of the one before, and there is nowhere to be put back as the one who never did. A reader of seventeen has given up the only thing in that bay that was his own because there was nothing wrong with a single head in about a fortnight of them. The girl beside him is putting a bundle back three feet and he is the only other person in that shed who has seen it and neither of them is going to say one word about it.**

He carried the next armful down that bench at about the same speed as the last one.

He has not been spoken to this week. He is not going to be thanked for the fortnight and he is not going to be sent for. Nobody in that lane is going to be told that the two people at the end of that bench have both worked out what the other one is doing about a bundle of ordinary work. Neither of them is one of the four who cannot make sense of a paragraph, and the chain went along at the front of that bench the whole of that morning under a hand that had been on it for eleven years and did not stop once for either of them.
