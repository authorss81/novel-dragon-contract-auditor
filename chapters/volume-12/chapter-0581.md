# Chapter 0581: A Girl Of Nineteen Standing At The End Of That Sill Four Mornings In A Fortnight, And An Older Woman Going Straight Past Her

She had carried the armful past the end of that sill for four years without stopping anywhere on that floor, and in the last fortnight she had stopped at it four times, and this morning she stopped at it again with the weight of it on her hip and let it stand there while she got her breath.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the eleventh month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the third hour and about the fifth.

---

The sill is at the near end of that corridor and it is about four feet long and it takes the weather straight off the lane, and a tin stands on it with a pen in it and there is a second pen in the same tin and they are the same pen.

He is about sixty-one. He came to that sill at forty-two and has not moved off it since, and he has never once asked for anything and nobody has ever once asked him, and this morning it was the other of his two hands that went into that tin, which is what has been happening for about two months, and he cannot account for it and nobody on that floor has put it to him.

She carried the armful past that end of the floor about forty times in each direction in a working day for four years. Something in the order of nine hundred sheets leave that building in a year, and a mark at the foot of every one of them is made by one of that man's two hands, and once in a while the writing above that mark belongs to somebody else, and he is the only person alive who has ever known which, and it is on nothing at all.

---

She put the armful down on the stone at the end of that sill and stood with her hand flat on it and waited, and she was not waiting for him.

He signed four sheets in the time she was standing there. He did not look along four feet of stone to find out what was at the end of it, and he would not have known what to do with the answer if he had, and the pot took the pen back out of his hand the same way it takes it out of the other one.

Then the door at the back of that floor opened, and it had stood open about nine mornings out of twelve for a fortnight and about one person had come through it in all that time.

---

The woman of fifty-three came out into that corridor and did not come to the end of the sill.

She came as far as the middle of it, and she was carrying nothing, and she stopped about four foot off the stone where the armful was standing.

"You have not asked me for anything."

"I have not asked you for anything."

"You are standing at the end of that sill with a thing on your hip."

"Yes."

"That is not going to be anything."

"That is not mine to say."

---

**I gave up the only thing I had in a month that is gone, and what I have instead is that I stop at the end of a sill and a man four foot off me signs, and neither of us can be shown to have come to it on purpose. That is worth having. Nobody in this building has ever offered it to me and nobody is going to, and I am not going to be thanked for working it out on my own in a fortnight, and I have not said one word about it to anybody and I am not going to.**

There is one other person on that floor who has stood where a thing is said. She came out of that room four times in about a year and said a true thing at the end of that sill each time and was answered not once, and she has been at that sill nineteen years and she has never had one minute of it for nothing.

And this morning she came as far as the middle of that corridor and would not come the last four foot, and she is not going to come the last four foot again, and I am not going to ask her why, and there is nobody in this building she could be asked by.

---

She went the length of that corridor and into that room without shutting it, and stood at the window in there, which has had no pane in it for about four years, with her hands down, and then she came out and shut it.

That is the fifth time in about a year that door has opened and the first time the person behind it did not come to the end of that sill.

The girl picked the armful up off the stone and went on down the corridor with it and did not stop anywhere else on that floor.

---

The second copy of that week's minutes has to go in once a week and it goes in on the older woman's own hand this month, every week of it, in a room at the back of that floor with the door standing open, and the girl of nineteen does not go into that room at all.

She has been in it twice in about six weeks and both times she came out of it somebody in that building could have said she had been somewhere in particular, and she cannot get either of those mornings back, and there is no form in nine hundred buildings that puts a thing back.

She is nineteen, and she has stopped at the far end of that sill four mornings in a fortnight, and the other mornings she carries the armful past it at the speed she has always carried it. There is nothing at that end of that stone but a girl who is not going to be told she has worked anything out, and there is nothing at the other end of it but a pot with two pens in it that are the same pen, and not one word has passed between the two of them in four years.
