# Chapter 0574: Seven Weeks Of A Bloom On The Front Boards Of That Ground Floor, And A Woman Not Going Back To Them

She had not been to the front of that ground floor for seven weeks and had worked out in the first of them that the boards at the front of that ground floor are the only record in that house of a thing she has ever changed her mind about.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the ninth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the third hour and about the sixth.

---

She has been at the foot of that stair since she was a young woman and the third tread is a tread like any other and no boot of hers has ever been on it.

That is the only stair in the house, and the whole upper half of that flight is out of sight from the bottom of it, so a person could be on the floor above for the whole of a morning and nobody down there would be any way of knowing it.

A rent comes out of a hand once a week and goes into a tin and has not been on a piece of paper since before she came, and she has put a receipt into nobody's hand in the whole of it. What that house has to say about anybody sleeping in it is a number cut into the back of a key, and it was cut there before her.

She has not gone up past the second of those stairs in twenty years and she is not going to on the strength of seven weeks.

---

A cloth goes over a board and the board keeps a mark of it, and the marks on the front of that ground floor are about two inches apart, and there have been no new ones in seven weeks.

That is the whole of what has happened in that house in seven weeks and it is the only thing in it that has happened, and there is a woman who has not been in that passage for seven weeks to be seen not doing it.

The wood under a mark goes a shade darker than the wood beside it, and seven weeks is long enough for the top of the front to lose the last of them in the grain, and there is a woman who could put her hand flat on any part of it and say which week it was and has not been near it since the first of the seven.

She swept the back of that ground floor at the first hour every morning for twenty years, where the boards are, and then she came along the passage with the cloth and did the front, and that was the whole of her day in that house until about the sixth hour.

Five weeks ago she stopped at the turn.

---

The woman of about thirty-five came down that lane at about the fourth hour on this morning and went along the passage and stopped on the step below the turn.

"That front has not been done."

"It has not been done."

"You do the front."

"I have not done the front for seven weeks."

The woman at the foot of that stair had the cloth in her hand and she came through the passage from the back of that ground floor at the rate she comes through it, and she did not stop, and she did not put the cloth down.

"I am going along this passage," she said. "That is the whole of what I do in it."

She said it going, with her hand on the cloth the whole of it, and she came the length of the run with her face to the boards and the step below the turn went past her shoulder.

And she went along it and out at the front door, and the door shut about as long ago as that happens.

---

**A woman who comes down a lane every week for about thirteen weeks has put herself in that passage, and I have got to the front of that ground floor seven weeks ago and I have not been able to go back to it. The front of that ground floor can be seen from the step at that turn, and there is one person alive who comes down that lane, and she has been in that passage about thirteen weeks. She is never going to be asked a thing about it and she is never going to say one.**

The whole of what I have is that nobody can show me in that passage. That is twenty years and it is the only thing I have ever had that was worth anything, and it is not a thing anybody can be shown having, it is a thing nobody has been able to enter me in.

Seven weeks is forty-nine mornings and I have never once had to count the days, because the front counts them for me and it stopped, and not once in the forty-nine have I gone up those two steps to the turn, and that is the whole of what the seven weeks has cost me and the whole of what it has bought.

And a person who is standing in a room when a thing is said in it cannot afterwards be produced for any room she was not in. I have worked that out in seven weeks and I have worked it out about the one woman in this empire who could ever have been in that passage when a thing was said in it, and she was, and she does not know it, and she is never going to find out and she is not going to be asked.

So the boards at the front are not going to be done, and that is a record, and a record is the one thing I have not got and cannot get rid of.

---

She left the cloth on top of the boiler at the back of that ground floor and did the boards back there with her hands, at the back, out of the sight of the step at the turn.

The back of the ground floor is a long way from the front door and it is where the work is, and there is more floor round the back of the boiler than there is in front of it, and there is a place on the top of that boiler where the cloth has been laid down and taken up again so often that the dust has gone out of it.

There is a tin behind that boiler with a key in it and a number cut into the back of that key, and her hand has not gone after that tin since a spring that is gone, and it did not go after it this morning either.

The top step at the turn is bare. It has been bare for seven weeks, and the tin is on it with the lid flat down on it, and there is nothing else on that step in that house and there is not going to be.

The bloom on that front is not dirt, and no cloth in that house will lift it, and it is a shade greyer this week than it was the first of the seven, and there is nobody in that house who is going to be told what it is a record of.

The stairs behind her go up to a floor where a coin goes under a door at the first hour of every working morning, and she is not going to go up them, and she is not going to be asked about them, and there is no form in this city that takes her as the one who stopped cleaning a floor because of who comes down a lane.

She will be at the back of that ground floor at the sixth hour with the cloth over her shoulder, and a person coming down that lane is going to see a floor that has not been done and is not going to be told anything at all about it.
