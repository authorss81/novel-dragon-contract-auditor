# Chapter 0566: A Coin Under A Door And A Mat With Its Corner Turned Down, And A Man Who Does Not Open It

He had been putting the corner of that mat flat every morning for about a month, and in the fourth week of it the corner was down again and it had been trodden over rather than scuffed.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the seventh month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the first hour.

---

A mat on a landing takes a week to become a landing's mat and it takes a corner to say so.

He had worked that out on about the fourth morning. The middle of it had gone down and gone flat and had stopped being the thing he had carried up under his arm, and the near corner had come up about two inches off the boards, and in the fourth week of it that corner had been put over and flattened out of the way of a boot.

Somebody in that building stands on that corner on the way past, every morning, at about the first hour, and has been doing it since before he had the mat, in the space where the mat now is.
He had watched the corner go over on morning after morning and had never once been in the passage when it went, and the going of it is over before a person standing at the top of that flight would have taken her hand off the rail, and there is nothing on the landing a person can hear over. His own door was shut behind him and there was a shut door between his hand and the stair, and the room behind him had a table with its far edge into the wall and a bed against the far wall and a chair facing a piece of blank wall, and none of that was any use to him at the first hour.

---

She came up at about the first hour and she was on that landing before her hand was down, and she stepped on the corner of it and it went over, and she lifted it, and the coin went under the door flat on the boards with the flats of two fingers.

"It is the fourth week."

"I have it."

"I have not come up for the mat."

And then she did not go down.
She stood on the landing with her hand still down at the height of her own knee and the corner of the mat under her own boot, and she had come up one flight and had not come up it for a mat, and the whole of that is one shut door. The steps are stone under a boot and they do not give, and she has gone up and down them on every working morning for about two years and has never once been up there a quarter of an hour either side of the hour.

---

He had his hand flat on the door and his thumb up under the rail of it, and he neither turned it nor took his hand away.

"I have not come up for the mat," she said again, "because if I come up for the mat then this is a thing about the mat."

"It is a mat."

"It is a mat and it is the fourth week of it and I come up here at the first hour and there is a coin, and that is the whole of what I do on this landing and that is all I do on this landing."

The coin was under the door. She had said everything she had come up that flight to say and she had said it twice, and she stood there about as long as it takes a hand to stay flat on a thing.
The rail was cold under his thumb and it was the only cold thing in the room at that hour, and he could hear her on the landing the way a person hears a person through a door they have stood behind every working morning of their life without once opening it. There was about as long as it takes to wipe a hand down a skirt and he could have turned that rail in any part of it, and he has gone over it since, at night, more than once, and turning it would have put a door between two people in a form that does not exist.

"That is the week," she said, and she went down the stair behind her.

---

**She would not let it be about the mat. I have wanted a second person in this room who had not come for me for about ten years and I have had a person on that landing twice in about a month who would not give me the reason I wanted, and she is right not to, and I am not going to be thanked for hearing her say it and I am not going to be asked about it.**

Nothing in this city would take a man of thirty-eight for standing behind a door, and nothing in it would take the woman at the foot of that stair for saying what she came up that flight to say.

He did not open the door and he did not say anything at all through it, and she was gone before he had decided anything, which is the only way that has ever gone.
He stood with his hand on the door for a while after the stair had gone quiet, and then he took it off, and it was about as warm as the back of his own hand, which is the only heat in the room at that hour and there is no fire in it. The whole of what that building had become in about a month was a woman on a landing who had told him twice what she had not come up for, and he has still not worked out what a person does with being told a thing about his own landing.

---

He worked out the rest of it after that, standing in the middle of that floor with his hand not on anything.

At about the first hour of every working morning for about two years there has been a brush on boards at the other end of about four foot of air, at the back of the house behind, and it has gone at one unvarying rate for the whole of that time, and one morning of it lasts about as long as washing a plate, and the catch on that window has been turned for the whole of that time and he has never once gone to it.

He has been treating those two as the same thing for about two years. They are not the same thing at all.

A brush on boards through a shut window is a person who is not coming in. A coin under a door is a person who has come and gone and is not coming in either. Neither of them is a second person in the room, and the one on that landing has twice refused to be anything else, and she was right to, and the whole of what he has learned in the last month is that a plate and a mat are about as far apart as that.
He has been in the room about two years and the window in it has been shut the whole of it and the catch on it is turned, and there is a press beside the door, and there is an inch of wick in the lamp where the bed ends and he has never once put a light to it.

---

The corner of the mat was down when he came out to the door at about the sixth hour and he stepped round it.
The light on the landing at the sixth hour is a different light from the one at the first hour and nobody is standing on it, and the landing was the same four foot by six it has been every morning of that month, and he was out of it and back behind his own door in about as long as a coin takes to go under one. He has looked at the corner from a foot off it and has not touched it and is not going to, and a mat a person steps on every working morning of her life is not a thing anybody can be careful with.

He is not going to take it up. It is on a landing and it is going to be trodden over at the first hour of every working morning by a person who has never been told it is his and is never going to be, and there is no form anywhere in this city in which a man of thirty-eight is entered as the one who put a mat on a landing to keep another person's boots out of the wet.

She will be on that landing at about the first hour tomorrow and the day after that, and it will be under her boot before it is under his eye, and he will be behind that door with the catch turned.
