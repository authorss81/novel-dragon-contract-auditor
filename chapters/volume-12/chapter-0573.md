# Chapter 0573: A True Thing Said Out Loud In That Bay, And A Girl At The End Of The Bench Who Would Not Turn Round

She had worked out in about six weeks since she left a bundle at the far end of that bench that she had put it there to be looked at and had not wanted a girl of seventeen to look at it, and this morning she wanted something worse, and she wanted it out loud.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the ninth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour.

---

She is fifty-one and the back of that bay is where a class left her eleven years ago, and she has not walked to the front of it in about six weeks, and the chain that goes along the front of that bench has been going at one rate under her hand for the whole of that six weeks and has not stopped once for her.

The bench runs the width of the bay with the count taken at the far end of it and the girl working at the end by the door, and the chain goes along the front of the bench the whole length of it and comes back, and nothing about any of that has ever been said out loud in that bay.

The girl is at the end by the door. She is seventeen and she came out of somewhere else a year ago and there is a written engagement on it and a silence on that too.

Ninety foot of that floor is between the two of them and the whole of it is open.

There is nothing standing on any part of the open floor between them. The boards of the shed carry a sound, and the floor is the one place in the building where a thing said out loud can go somewhere and arrive nowhere.

---

What she wanted at about the fourth hour was for that girl to turn round.

She had not wanted a bundle looked at. She wanted a person turned about, in a shed, on a working morning, once, and there is nothing in eleven years of that bay that has ever got it and she had come to the front of that floor knowing it.

She had come down the whole of the floor twice a month through the spring and stood at the front of the bench with her hand laid flat on the wood, and she left it there until the sixth hour. On two of those mornings the shed's own looking did not get done for an hour, and the count for the day came out one short, and it was not said to anybody and it was not asked about by anybody. She has not walked the length of her own floor since, and there is nobody in the building who would remark on a foreman standing at the back of a bench.

And then she got it another way, and she has not worked out since whether she chose it or it arrived.

She said it at the ordinary volume, in the ordinary way, to the boards in front of her, the way a person says a thing about the work that is in the room.

She did not lift her hand off the chain while she said it, and the nine foot of bench between her and the girl was open the whole of the time, and the boards in front of her took what she said and carried it to the far end of the bay and no further.

**"That girl has not been asked one thing in about a year and she has never once had the chance to turn anything down, and there is nowhere in this city to put the fact if she gets it."**

---

The girl did not turn round.

She was holding a sheet about a third of the way down with both hands and she finished what she was doing with it, and she put it down on the top of the pile at the end by the door, and the top of it went under about a foot of another sheet, and she squared it with the heel of her hand, and she picked up the next one.

She did it in about as long as it takes to square a sheet, and she did it with her back to ninety foot of that bay, and she did not stop doing anything for the whole of it.

Nobody on that floor said one word. There is a boy of seventeen at the far end of that bench, where the count is made, and that bay is about nine foot by eleven, and he is nine foot from the girl and about nine foot from where she was standing, and a thing said at the ordinary volume in a bay that size is in the air of it while it is being said.

He can read a paragraph and take what is in it. Four people in this matter cannot do that. He is not one of them, and nobody in the shed is ever going to ask him to say so. He is at the end of the bench and he goes on with what is in front of him, and he did not stop doing anything while she was talking either.

---

**I have said out loud, in that bay, on an ordinary morning, at the ordinary volume, one true thing about that girl. She was at the end of the bench with her back to me and she was working, and she has not turned round and she is not going to. There is no form anywhere in this city that takes a woman of fifty-one as the one who said it, and none that takes a girl of seventeen as the one who was there when it was said.**

The whole of what I wanted was a thing put where she could put it back, and I have gone about that for about six weeks with a bundle and a hand on a bench and ninety foot of floor, and what has happened instead is that I have found the one instrument there is for saying a true thing where a person is working, and it works, and I did not choose it, and I have chosen everything else about that shed for eleven years.

A second pair of ears is the only way a sentence gets into the world without anybody being asked anything, and I have wanted that girl to have a choice for about a year, and this morning I have made her the second pair of ears for a sentence about herself, and she is seventeen and she was not asked and she is not going to be asked and she is never going to find out what she was standing in the air of.

That is the cost of it and it is mine and it is not hers, and I could not give it to somebody else instead, and there is no other instrument.

A bundle is the whole of what I had to work with and it does what it was made to do. What it does is put a thing down where a person can get at it. What it will not do is turn anybody round. I worked that second one out in about six weeks, standing at the end of a bench with a bundle in my hand, and the whole time the other way of doing it was in the same room with me, and I did not know I had it until I had used it.

---

She stayed at the front of that bench about as long as a sheet takes to be squared and then she went back up the ninety foot to the back of that bay.

The paper on the board by that door has not been filled in. It was on that board before she came to that bay, and she has not taken a pen to it in eleven years, and she did not take a pen to it this morning either.

It has been standing there in reach of a woman with her hands full for the whole of eleven years, and it is the only surface in that bay a thing could be written on, and nothing has ever been written on it.

The chain went along under her hand at the same rate it goes at. The boy at the far end of that bench squared what was on it with the heel of his hand and did not look along the nine foot of it, and the girl took the armful out at the sixth hour the way everything in that shed goes out, and the top of every sheet in it was under about a foot of another sheet.

---

She is not going to say a second one of those out loud in that bay, and that is not a rule and there is nobody to make it one, and it is hers and it costs her the whole of the thing she has been reaching for since before the spring.

The girl was given nothing this morning that she could put back, and she was given a room where a true thing about herself was said in the ordinary voice, and she is seventeen, and nobody in that shed is ever going to be told that she heard it, and there is nowhere in this empire a column that will take either of the two of them.
