# Chapter 0580: Ninety Foot Of A Shed Floor Walked Down And Back With Nothing Put On The Bench

She had not come in a quarter of an hour early for about a month, and in the last fortnight she had stopped standing at the far end of that bench at all, and this morning the woman came down the ninety foot and put nothing anywhere.

It is the second day of the second week of the eleventh month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the third hour and about the fourth.

---

She is seventeen, and nothing in that shed has ever wanted anything out of her, and nobody has ever put a choice in her reach that she could turn down, and there is no machinery anywhere in this empire that could put one there in front of a girl of that age.

There have been two things put at the far end of that bench in about two months. She carried the first one the nine foot to her own end. The second one is a fortnight gone and went out with the rest of that floor and she has never found out who put it there.

And the woman who puts work at that end has stayed at the back of that bay since the morning she said it out loud about seven weeks ago.

---

She came down that floor at about the fourth hour, all ninety foot of it, at the rate she walks.

She did not stop at the front of that bench. She did not put her hand on the wood. She came along it, past the far end where the count is made, and on to the end by the door where the girl works, and she stopped there for about as long as a sheet takes to be squared.

The girl had a sheet in both hands and she was about a third of the way down with it and she did not stop and she did not turn round.

Nothing was put on that bench that morning. The woman went back up the ninety foot at the same rate and there was no bundle at the far end when the girl went along there later.

---

**She came all that way and stopped at my end and said nothing, and she has not said anything out loud in this bay since the morning she said it about me, and I have wanted a thing to be put at that other end for about a year and there are only two ways anybody in this shed has ever got one.**

Stand at the front of that bench for a quarter of an hour first, and then put it down so that it looks like ordinary work. Say something out loud in here while a person is in it, and then put it down.

She did the first of those once. She did the second of those once, and it was about me, and I am seventeen and I was not asked and I am not going to be asked and I have never found out what I was standing in the air of.

And this morning she came ninety foot and did neither, and she has priced both of them now, and I am not going to be given one at the end of that bench by anybody in this shed as long as I am in it.

---

A boy of seventeen has the back of that bay to himself and the count is made at his end, and the count has been short twice a week for about a month, and he gave the tops of those sheets up in the spring before last and has not gone back to it, and he read nothing this morning either.

The two ends of that bench are nine foot apart and they have been nine foot apart for as long as either of them has been in that shed.

He looked along it once, at about the fifth hour, and she was at her own end with her back to him, and he went on with what he was on, and neither of them said one word about a woman who had walked the length of that floor and stopped and gone back.

There is a paper on a board by that door that has not been filled in and is not going to be, and there has been a chain along the front of that bench for eleven years, and a girl of seventeen went along it at about the fifth hour and stopped at the far end of it.

**She is going to be at that end of that bench at the hour, every working morning, in the open, where anybody coming into that shed can see her standing there and say she was never anywhere else. She worked it out about a month ago, she gave up a quarter of an hour over it, and she has put herself back on purpose, and this morning she put her hand flat on the wood and left it there about as long as it takes a sheet to be squared.**
