# Chapter 0589: A Girl Of Nineteen At The Far End Of That Sill At The Third Hour, And A Door That Stayed Shut

She had stopped at the far end of that sill at the third hour every morning for about seven weeks, and that was the hour the woman of fifty-three had come out of that room four times in about a year, and in the last fortnight the door at the back of that floor had not opened once. The wet place in the middle of that run dries about as fast as it wets, and by the third hour there is about a foot of it gone dry, and that is the part of the stone she puts the armful down on.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the first month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the fourth hour and about the seventh.

---

The pot is at the near end of that sill now and it was at his right hand for nineteen years and he moved it four inches along that stone about a fortnight ago and could not say why, and about three feet of that sill has been empty since, and a girl of nineteen has been standing at the empty end of it every morning at about the third hour. He is on a bench at his end of that run with the stone up at the level of his forearm where he sits, and about three feet of it is the length of a girl standing still, and he has not looked along it at any hour this morning and has not asked.

She has carried the armful past that end of that floor about forty times in each direction in a working day for four years and she did not stop at any of them, and she has not stopped going past it either. She stops instead, and then goes on, and about three feet of stone and a pot with two pens in it are the whole of what is on the other side of her. That stone is low enough for her to put a hip against and high enough to keep an armful off the floor, and about forty goes in a day is not a heavy thing to carry and it is heavy enough to notice that a person has stopped.

---

That corridor is about as long as a table, and there is a door at the back of that floor and a window in the room at the back of it with no pane in it, and a person standing at the far end of that sill is in the whole of that corridor and cannot be anywhere else in it. She has known that for four years and had no use for it until about two months ago. There is no pane in the opening and it takes the weather straight on, and the stone underneath takes it as well, and a paper on the shelf in it has been standing there for about eleven years.

About nine hundred sheets go out of that building in a year and about nine of them are not what the person above him wrote, and nobody in that building has ever asked her one question about a number and nobody is going to, and she is not going to say one word to him about which ones they are. There is a man at the near end of that stone whose mark is at the foot of every sheet that goes out of that building in a year, and the two of them have never once put those two things together where the other one could hear it.

He signed two sheets and did not look along that stone, and then he said it, and it is the first thing either of them has ever said to the other. The tin had taken the pen out of his hand and put the other one in it, and he said it to the stone about a foot in front of him and not to her.

"You have not set it down at this end before."

"No."

"You used to go past."

And he turned the next sheet round to him and signed the foot of it and did not say one word after that, and it took about as long as a sheet takes to be squared, and the door at the back of that floor stayed shut the whole of the rest of that morning. The two sheets went off the end of that stone on the armful she was standing at, and she did not turn her head while they went, and they were about four foot apart with a wet place between them the whole time.

---

**What I had until about two months ago was that nobody in this building had ever wanted one single thing out of me, and I put that down and it has not come back, and what I have instead is that somebody who stands in a place cannot be produced for anywhere else afterwards. That is worth having. It is also the only thing about me that anybody has ever been able to notice, and this morning the man at the other end of that stone noticed it, and there is nothing in nine hundred buildings that will take me back to a girl who carried an armful past a sill.**

And a habit is not a place. A habit is a thing a person can be shown having, and a place is a thing a person cannot be shown having or not having, and the whole of what I came here for was the second of those and I have just been given the first. A habit of about seven weeks is not a place, and about three feet of stone is not a habit, and I have got one of the two now as well as the other and I did not go out and get either of them.

So I have about three feet of stone at the near end of it and a man who signs, and I am going to be at this end of it at the third hour tomorrow and the morning after, and I am not going to say one word to him about it and he is not going to ask me and neither of us is going to be thanked. He said one thing to me this morning about four foot of stone and it is the only thing either of us has ever said, and there is nowhere in nine hundred buildings to put a woman who has said the first thing.

---

She carried the armful on down that corridor and out of that building one sheet at a time in the ordinary way, and the tin took the pen out of his hand and put the other one in it, and every sheet that went out of that building that year carried one of that man's two marks at the foot of it. About four foot of the stone went down that corridor with her on it and it was bare again by the time she was at the door, and it will be bare by the time she gets to it tomorrow morning as well.

The second copy of that week's minutes went in on the older woman's own hand behind a shut door at the back of that floor, and the girl of nineteen was nowhere near it, and that door has not been opened since the week before last. Nothing was set down on that shelf under the window with no pane in it, and a girl of nineteen is going to be at the far end of that sill at the third hour tomorrow. About seven weeks of mornings is not a habit in anybody's book, and there is not one form in nine hundred buildings that takes a girl for standing at the end of a piece of stone about four foot long.
