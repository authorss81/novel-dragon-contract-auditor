# Chapter 0591: Four Feet Of Stone Looked Along For The First Time In Nineteen Years, And A Woman At The Far Window Of That Corridor

He had looked along four feet of that stone twice in a fortnight and this morning he looked along it a third time, and the whole of what was at the other end of it was a window and a person standing at it with her back to him.

It is the second day of the first week of the second month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the second hour.

---

The tin is at the near end of that sill and there are two pens in it and the two of them are the same pen, and it has been at that end of that stone since a winter before he came to that floor. About three feet of that sill has been empty at the far end of it for a fortnight, and the weather comes off the lane onto about four feet of stone, and by the fifth hour there is nothing on any of it that is not the colour of the stone.

He has been at that sill for nineteen years. He turns the pen round to him and signs underneath the foot of a sheet, and before he puts the pen to it he reads the head and the date and the number at the top, and that has been the whole of the care he has ever added to anything in his working life.

He had not looked along that stone once in nineteen years. That is not a decision he made. It is what a sill is.

---

The door at the back of that floor opened on the back of about a fortnight in which it had not opened once, and that fortnight had come after about a year in which it stood open nine mornings out of twelve and nobody went through it.

The woman of fifty-three came out of it, went the whole length of that corridor without stopping at the stone he was sitting at, and stood at the window in the end wall with her hands down, looking out.

"That is the end of this corridor."

"It is."

"You can see the whole of that sill from here."

"You can."

"I said I was not going to stand at that end of it. I did not say I was not going to look at it."

He signed two sheets and did not look up, and the tin took the pen out of his hand and gave him the other, and the two of them went down that corridor on the armful already standing at the far end of it and out of that building one at a time in the ordinary way.

---

**A man who does not look along four feet of stone cannot afterwards be shown to have seen what was at the end of it. That is the whole of what I have had for nineteen years and I have not once had to spend it, and there is no part of me that had to decide to.**

And this morning a woman who has been at the back of that floor for nineteen years walked past my stone to the far end of that corridor and stood at that window, and it turns out that the thing I have been holding is not a thing about the stone. It is a thing about the looking. A man at a sill who never looks is a man who cannot be shown to have noticed anything, and that has been worth having at nineteen years of a mark at the foot of a sheet a day, and nobody in this building has ever wanted one single thing out of me.

And now there is a person at the end of that corridor who is in the whole of it and cannot be anywhere else, and I have looked, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that will take a man for having looked along four feet of stone at about the second hour on an ordinary morning.

---

She came back along that corridor about as long as it takes to square a sheet and went past the far end of that sill and stopped about four foot short of it, which is where she stopped the last time she came out of that room, and she did not come the last four foot and she was not going to.

"You are not going to say anything about that window."

"I am not."

"You said that before I got to it."

"You said I would."

And she stood there about as long as it takes to square a sheet and she went into that room and shut it, and the second copy of that week's minutes went in on her own hand about an hour later with the door standing open.

---

At about the third hour a second armful came down that corridor and stopped where one has stopped every morning for about nine weeks, at the far end of the stone, three foot short of the tin, and he signed a sheet and did not look along the stone to find out whether anybody was standing at the end of it.

That is the arrangement and it is the only one there is. Neither of them can be shown to have asked the other for a thing and neither of them is going to be thanked for the other end of about four feet of stone being what it is.

There is a stretch of that stone at the far end of it that has had nothing on it for a fortnight, and this morning somebody was standing on it for about as long as it takes to square a sheet, and then the stone was bare again.
