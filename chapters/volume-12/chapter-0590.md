# Chapter 0590: The Room With The Sill In It, And Two People Four Feet Apart And Not One Word Between Them

He had been reaching into that pot with his other hand for about two months, and in the last fortnight he had stopped keeping track of which one went in, and this morning he found that he could not have said afterwards which one of them had gone in and went on signing until the light went off that window.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the first month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the second hour.

---

There is a door at the back of that floor and it has been shut for a fortnight, and a sill about four feet long runs along the near side of that corridor, and a pot is at the near end of it with two pens in it and the two of them are the same pen.

That building sends about nine hundred sheets out in a year and every one of them carries his mark at the foot of it, and once in a while what stands between the head of one and the foot of it was written by somebody else altogether, and he is the only living man who has ever known which, and it is on nothing at all and has been in his head since the spring that is gone.

---

The girl of nineteen came along that corridor at about the third hour with the armful on her hip and stopped at the far end of that sill, which is where she has stopped every morning for about eight weeks, and about three feet of bare stone is between her and the tin.

"You are at this end before the sheets come down."

"I am."

"That is not your end."

"It is not."

And that tin took the pen back out of his hand the way it has taken one out of it twice a day for nineteen years and gave him the other. He went on signing at that sill and she carried the armful on down the corridor and out at the end of it the way she has carried armfuls out of that building for four years.

---

He has signed about nine hundred feet of paper in a year and about nineteen sheets on an ordinary working day, and he turns the pen round to him and signs underneath every one of them. That is the care he takes and it is the whole of it, and it is the reason he is the only man alive who knows which ones, and the knowing is on nothing at all and there is no column in this empire that will take a man for it.

Four people in this matter cannot get sense out of a paragraph and none of the four has ever been asked one question about a number. He is not one of the four and has never been, and the man of about fifty-five with a chain at the front of a bench nine miles off is not one of them either, and the woman of fifty-three who keeps the minutes in the room at the back of that floor never was, and the reader of seventeen never was either.

**I have stood at that sill for nineteen years and there has never been anybody at the other end of it, and there is a girl there now every morning at the third hour, and I said one thing to her this morning and I am not going to say a second. Whoever stands in one place cannot afterwards be produced for any other. She has that. I have had it since I was forty-two and it has cost me nothing because nothing has ever been wanted out of me.**

And the two of us have not said one word to each other in four years except twenty words this morning. I cannot be shown to have asked her to move and I cannot be shown not to have asked her, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for a man at a sill and a girl at the end of his stone, and that is the whole of what has happened in that room this month.

I am not going to stop signing and I am not going to start anything about a hand. The other one has been going into that pot for about two months and I cannot account for it and nobody in that building is going to be asked and nobody is going to be thanked.

---

The door at the back of that floor stood open about as long as it takes to square a sheet and nothing came through it, and the second copy of that week's minutes went in on the older woman's own hand about an hour later. On a shelf under a window with no pane in it there is a question that does not finish and her own name is written underneath it, and it has been there about eleven years, and nothing has been set down after it this month.

About nine hundred sheets went out of that building that year and not one of them says a word about a pot moved four inches along a sill in a fortnight, or about a girl standing at the other end of it, or about a man who could not say afterwards which of his own hands had been in the tin.
