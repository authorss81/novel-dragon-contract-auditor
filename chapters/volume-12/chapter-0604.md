# Chapter 0604: An Armful Put Down Three Foot Off A Tin At The Near End Of That Sill, In The Open

She had stopped at the empty end of that sill every morning for about eighteen weeks and this morning she carried the armful the whole length of it and put it down at the other end.

It is the second day of the second week of the fifth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the third hour.

---

There is a tin at the near end of that stone with two pens in it and the two of them are the same pen. About three feet of that sill has had nothing on it since a pot was moved four inches along it, and the weather comes off the lane onto that stone. The door at the back of that floor has been standing open about as long as it takes to square a sheet on a morning for three weeks now, and the second copy of that week's minutes goes in on the older woman's own hand behind it.

About nine hundred sheets go out of that building in a year and every one of them carries that man's mark at the foot of it, and about nine of them are not what the person above him wrote, and he is the only living man who knows which nine, and it is on nothing at all.

---

She set the armful down about three foot off that tin with the near ends of it squared against the wood the way a person sets down a thing that is going to stay. She did not stop at the end of it. She stopped three foot short of that tin, in the open, between him and the door at the back of that floor, and stood there while he signed the foot of a sheet with his other hand, and the other hand was on the stone.

He did not move it.

She has been at that building four years and she has carried about forty armfuls down that floor in each direction in a working day and she has never once stopped at any of them until about eighteen weeks ago.

---

**I gave the only thing I had about two months ago, which was that nobody in this building could ever show me having been anywhere in particular, and I got a piece of stone about three foot long at the far end of a corridor instead. I have been at that end of it every morning since, and it turns out a habit is a thing a person can be shown having and a place is not. I have handed both of those over and got one of them back this morning and it is the wrong one.**

This morning I put an armful of sheets down three foot off that tin with his other hand on the stone about a foot from the edge of it. A man who signs with his other hand and a woman who puts an armful down in front of that hand are two people in one corridor at about the third hour, and there is no form anywhere in nine hundred buildings that takes either of them for it, and I cannot be shown to have put it there by accident, because an armful that is going somewhere else does not stop three foot short of a tin and get squared up.

And he did not move that hand out of the way. He could have put it in his lap. He has been putting it in that pot for a long time now and nobody in this building has ever said one word to him about it, and this morning there was a person about a foot off it and he signed the foot of a sheet with it in front of her and he did not put it away.

I am not going to say one word to him about any of that. I have not said one word to him since a morning about a year ago when four lines passed between us at that end of that stone, and I am not going to start, and he is not going to ask me, and neither of them is going to be thanked for the other three foot of stone.

---

The door at the back of that floor came open and the woman of fifty-three came out into that corridor and went the whole length of it and stopped about four foot short of that sill and did not come the last four foot, and she did not look along the stone, and she went back into that room and shut it.

That armful stood on the near end of that stone for about as long as it takes to square a sheet and then she picked it up and carried it out into the lane one sheet at a time, and the tin swapped that pen for the other one the way it has twice a day for nineteen years, and nothing has ever been set down on the shelf under that window with no pane in it.
