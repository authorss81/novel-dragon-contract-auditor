# Chapter 0571: A Lamp In His Hand On The Landing Side Of A Door, And The Last Hour Of The Light Done In The Dark

He had had a lamp on that floor for about two years and had never lit it once, and in the last fortnight he had carried it up off the floor at the end of the bed to the table and back again three times, and it was in his hand at about the first hour on this morning.

It is the second day of the first week of the ninth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the first hour and about the fifth.

---

The lamp came with the room. It was standing on the floor at the end of that bed on the morning he came into it and it is a lamp with a reservoir and a wick and an inch of wick left in it, and in about two years the only thing that has come through that door is a coin that goes flat under it and stops, and so there is nobody that lamp belongs to but the floor it stands on.

He had it in his hand because he had been carrying it to the table, and the table is the other side of that floor from the bed, and the door is between the two of them, and a man carrying a thing across a floor does not decide anything about it.

---

A boot came on the second tread at about the first hour and he stood in the middle of that floor with the lamp in his hand about as long as a coin takes to go under a door.

"It is dark in there."

"It is dark in there."

"It is not dark out here and it has never been dark out here."

"There is a window in it."

"I have put a coin under it for two years. I know what is in it."

The mat on that landing had its near corner standing up where the boards have lifted at the joint. She stepped on it and it went flat, and she got the coin under the door with two fingers and set the corner back down on top of it.

"The corner will be down by the time I come back," she said.

He did not open the door and she did not knock, and that is the whole of the two of them at that door in about two years, and the last thing he heard of her was the step.

---

**She was about four minutes off that landing and I had a lamp in my hand. A man who lights a lamp is a man who is saying out loud, to a room, that he is going to be in it after dark, and there is no form anywhere in this city that takes a man for that, and there is not going to be one, and a man who has done it has admitted in a room that he is the only one in it.**

I have been the other sort for about ten years. I have gone into no register in this city and asked nothing of it and it has asked nothing of me, and the whole of that was so that there would be nothing anybody could produce.

I put the lamp down on the floor at the end of the bed and squared it to the wall with the heel of my hand, and I stood up, and the door was shut.

---

The light goes out of that window at about the fourth hour and it has been going a little earlier every week for a month, and there is no other light in that room at all, and about four foot of air and a back wall stand between the outside of that window and the other side of it.

He did the rest of that hour at the back of a chair that faces four feet of plaster and is not going anywhere, and the brush went on boards at the other end of that well at the rate it goes at every morning of about two years, and he listened to it go, and he did not go to the window about it.

The dark gave him the whole of what he had come to it for, and it is a small thing and it is this: standing in that room with no light in it, he could not tell whether there was a person in the room behind that wall or a room with a person gone out of it. There is no way in this city of settling that which does not begin with a light being put on.

---

He is not going to light it. It will stand on the floor at the end of that bed tomorrow and the day after that, and the light will go at about the fourth hour, and he will do that hour in the dark, and a boot will come on the second tread at about the first hour and go down again.

And of the two of them, neither one is ever going to be able to be shown what the other one was doing at the other end of about four feet of air, and he did not put a light on, and nobody in that building is going to know that he had it in his hand at the first hour and put it down again.
