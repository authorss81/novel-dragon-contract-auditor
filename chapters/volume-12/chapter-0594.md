# Chapter 0594: A Jug Filled At The End Of A Landing At The Fourth Hour, And A Woman Standing In Front Of The Pail

He had carried that jug out to the pail at the end of that landing and back four times in a fortnight without once bringing it back full, and this morning he carried it out and did not bring it back, and the jug was full.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the second month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the third hour and about the sixth.

---

It is a plain earthenware jug about a quart, and it was standing in the corner by the door on the morning he came into that room, and there was about two inches of water in the bottom of it and there has been about two inches in it ever since, and it has gone the colour of the room.

The lamp is on the floor at the end of that bed and has never been lit in about two years. The glass is face up on the table at about a foot back from the edge. There is one coin on that table and it came out of that door and it is his and he is not going to spend it. The pail of water is at the end of that landing, which is about four foot by six and has a mat on it that is the landing's, and that pail is the only water in this house.

---

He took the jug out on to that landing at about the fourth hour, after the light had gone off his own window and off the landing, and the two of them had gone at the same time, and he had the jug in his hand at the end of it and the pail was the width of four foot off him.

She was standing on that mat. She has been coming up that stair at about the fourth hour for about twelve weeks, and she has stood on that landing and looked at the foot of that door and there has been nothing to see through the gap under it, and neither of them has ever said one word about it.

"The pail is behind you."

"I know where the pail is."

"It is behind me."

"Yes."

"You have had that jug out here four times."

"I have."

"Four times, and every one of them at about this hour, and you have brought it back not full every one of those four times."

"I have."

---

**A man who has been unfindable for about ten years gets that by never once standing still in a place, and a place and being unfindable cannot both be had, and one of the two has to go. I worked that out in a fortnight. I have not got as far as which one of the two I would have to put down, and I am not going to get that far on a landing with a person on it.**

There is about a quart of water at the end of that landing and it is four foot off that door, and it has been four foot off that door for about two years. I have been on the other side of four inches of wood for two years with a room in here that has a lamp in it and a glass in it and one coin in it.

I have wanted that water for about a fortnight and have carried that jug out and back four times without filling it, because the pail is on the landing, and the landing is where the mat is, and the mat is the landing's. A man who goes out on to that landing at the fourth hour is a man who has been on that landing at the fourth hour.

---

He did not go round her. That would have meant a hand past her and about nine inches of a landing between them and neither of them would have been able to account for it afterwards.

"That is not going to be full by the time I go down those stairs."

"It will be full when it goes back in."

"You are not going to stand there until I go down those stairs."

"No."

And she did not move, and he stood on that landing holding a jug in the dark for about the length of a coin going under a door, and then he took it back in and shut the door and left the jug on the table standing about two inches short.

She came down past the second tread a minute afterwards and the stair went quiet, and he waited until it had been quiet a while. Then he took the jug out again and filled it at the end of that landing and carried it back in and put it on that table beside the glass. There is water in it that came out of a pail about a quarter of an hour after the light went off both those windows.

The glass is face up on that table about a foot back from the edge and the jug is beside it, and he did it again the next morning, and again the morning after that.
