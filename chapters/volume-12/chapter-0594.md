# Chapter 0594: A Jug Filled At The End Of A Landing At The Fourth Hour, And A Woman Standing In Front Of The Pail

He had carried that jug out to the pail at the end of that landing and back four times in a fortnight without once bringing it back full, and this morning he carried it out and did not bring it back, and the jug was full.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the second month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the third hour and about the sixth.

---

It is a plain earthenware jug about a quart, and it was standing in the corner by the door on the morning he came into that room, and there was about two inches of water in the bottom of it and there has been about two inches in it ever since, and it has gone the colour of the room. The bed is against the wall and is not made up, and the blanket is pushed up at the head end of it the way a blanket goes when somebody has sat down on the edge and thought better of it, and the stool is under the window. The table is a deal thing with its far edge into the wall so that a hand will not go behind it, and the lamp is on the floor at the end of that bed and has never been lit in about two years, and the boards under it are blackened in one small place where somebody got a match off the iron once and then thought better of it. The catch on that window has been turned for about two years, and what light comes in at the third hour comes in in one piece and lies down by the stool and gets no further than that in a morning.

The glass is face up on the table at about a foot back from the edge. There is one coin on that table and it came out of that door and it is his and he is not going to spend it. The pail of water is at the end of that landing, which is about four foot by six and has a mat on it that is the landing's, and that pail is the only water in this house. The mat is worn through in the middle of it and the near corner stands up where the boards have lifted at a joint, and the pail stands clear of that corner with its handle out, and the shape of a foot is worn into the middle of that mat and it is going to be there tomorrow.

---

He took the jug out on to the landing at about the fourth hour, after the light had gone off his own window and off the landing, and the two of them had gone at the same time, and he had the jug in his hand at the end of it and the pail was the width of four foot off him. The stair was behind him and the room behind that, and there was nothing in the passage but the two of them and the pail, and he could hear the water shift in it when he moved his weight from one foot to the other.

She was standing on that mat. She has been coming up that stair at about the fourth hour for about twelve weeks, and she has stood on that landing and looked at the foot of that door and there has been nothing to see through the gap under it, and neither of them has ever said one word about it. She came up the way she comes up, which is without any hurry in it at all, and the stair did not make a noise worth the name, and she had stopped square in the middle of the mat with her hands in the front of her apron.

"The pail is behind you."

"I know where the pail is."

"It is behind me."

"Yes."

"You have had that jug out here four times."

"I have."

"Four times, and every one of them at about this hour, and you have brought it back not full every one of those four times."

"I have."

---

**A man who has been unfindable for about ten years gets that by never once standing still in a place, and a place and being unfindable cannot both be had, and one of the two has to go. I worked that out in a fortnight. I have not got as far as which one of the two I would have to put down, and I am not going to get that far on a landing with a person on it.**

There is about a quart of water at the end of it and it is four foot off that door, and it has been four foot off it for about two years. I have been on the other side of four inches of wood for two years with a room in here that has a lamp in it and a glass in it and one coin in it.

I have wanted that water for about a fortnight and have carried the jug out and back four times without filling it, because the pail is on the landing, and the landing is where the mat is, and the mat is the landing's. A man who goes out on to it at the fourth hour is a man who has been on it at the fourth hour.

And I have got the weight of it in my hand now and I have not put it down, and there is a person four foot off me who has not gone down the stair, and the two of us are standing in the dark on the same boards over the same water, and the only thing in this house that either of us could be shown out of is a quart of it.

---

He did not go round her. That would have meant a hand past her and about nine inches of a landing between them and neither of them would have been able to account for it afterwards.

"That is not going to be full by the time I go down those stairs."

"It will be full when it goes back in."

"You are not going to stand there until I go down those stairs."

"No."

And she did not move, and he stood on the boards holding a jug in the dark for about the length of a coin going under a door, and then he took it back in and shut the door and left the jug on the table standing about two inches short. He stood in the room with his hand still on the lid of it, and the room was as dark as the landing, and the coin on the table was not there to be looked at either, and there was a shape of light by the bed that was the window and nothing else.

She came down past the second tread a minute afterwards and the stair went quiet, and he waited until it had been quiet a while. The room had gone from one kind of dark to another while he was standing on the landing and he could find the table by the shape of the jug on it, and he stood in the middle of the floor for a while with nothing in his hands before he went out. Then he took the jug out again and filled it at the end of it and carried it back in and put it on that table beside the glass. The pail was about a quarter lighter than it had been and the handle was wet where his hand had been on it, and neither of them ever said one word about that either. There is water in it that came out of a pail about a quarter of an hour after the light went off both those windows.

The glass is face up on the table about a foot back from the edge and the jug is beside it, and he did it again the next morning, and again the morning after that.
