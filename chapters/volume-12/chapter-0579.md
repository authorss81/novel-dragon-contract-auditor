# Chapter 0579: A Stair Not Gone Up At The Fourth Hour In Twenty Years, And A Landing Going Dark In Front Of A Shut Door

She had begun going up past the second stair at about the fourth hour, and she had been doing it for a fortnight, and she had not decided to start and she was not going to stop.

It is the second day of the first week of the eleventh month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour.

---

Twenty years at the foot of that stair, and the first hour of every working morning is the hour the coin goes up, and no other hour of any day has ever had her on that flight.

The bottom half of the flight is hers and the top half is not, and there are treads above the one she stands on, and she had been on the same tread of the stair for twenty years and had not once had a reason to go higher.

She does the boards at the back of that ground floor now and has done for about twelve weeks, and there is no lamp in that back room and there has not been one, and the light comes up out of that well the way light comes up out of a well, and from the back of that ground floor you cannot see the window of the room on the other side of it.

The gap between those two back walls is about four foot of air and it is open the whole way along, and there is no way of standing in it and no way of seeing across it, and a person on one side of it at the first hour and a person on the other side of it at the first hour are two people who have never been in a room together.

From the landing you can see under the door.

That is the whole of it and it took her a week to get to the bottom of it.

---

She came up at about the fourth hour and the corner of the mat was up about two inches and she did not step on it this time.

She stood on that landing about a yard off that door with her hands down and looked at the foot of it, and the light in the room behind that door was the same as the light on that landing, and there was nothing to see, and she had come up a flight of stairs to see it.

The stair behind her had gone quiet under her the whole time she stood there, and nothing else in the house was making a sound at the fourth hour, and she was aware of the whole of that landing under her own boots and of nothing at all above her.

The light went while she was standing there.

It goes off that landing at about the fourth hour and it went a little earlier than it had gone the week before, and the landing went with it and the stair behind her went with it, and the gap under that door did not change at all, because there was nothing in that room for it to change.

She had about as long as it takes to fold a cloth of the light left and she spent the whole of it standing where she was, and the dark came up off the boards of the landing and off the walls of the stairwell together, and she did not go to the door and she did not put a hand on it.

---

"There is a window in it," he said, through the wood.

"I know there is a window in it."

"I have a lamp."

"You have not lit it."

Neither of them said anything else, and the door stayed shut at both ends, and that is the whole of that stair on that morning, and a landing is four foot by six and a woman standing on it is a woman who is on it.

A landing that size has one door at one end of it and a mat on the floor of it and nothing else on it at all, and the whole of the room on the other side of the door is about a foot of wood away from her and has never once been any use to her.

She has stood on that landing at the first hour on and off for about two years, and once at the fourth hour, and there is no form anywhere in this city that takes a woman as the one who came up a stair to find out whether a room was lit.

---

**I went up those stairs to find out whether there is a light in that room, and I have found out that there is not one, and that is no information at all, because a room with a man in it doing the last of an hour in the dark and a room with nobody in it are the same dark, and I have come down those stairs with the one thing I came up them for and it is the thing I had before I started.**

There is a mat on that landing and there is a coin under that door and I have stood on that landing about four times in twenty years and not one of them was about either of those.

I did the sums on the stair on the way up it. There is a mat on a landing and a coin under a door and a door with a man behind it, and none of those three can tell me whether the room is lit, and I have gone up a flight of stairs a fortnight running to find out about a thing that is not one of the three.

I have given up the only thing I ever had, which is that nobody can produce anything about me, and I have got a piece of information about a man behind a door, and I am not going to be able to be shown having come up that stair, and he is not going to be able to be shown having been behind that door, and neither of us is going to know any more about that room in a week than we know this morning.

---

She went down that stair at the rate she comes down it and did not stop on the third tread.

The tin is where it has been since before she came, and the boards at the front of that ground floor have marks on them about two inches apart. There is a lamp standing on a floor at the top of that house that has never been lit, and there is a key in a tin behind a boiler with a number cut into the back of it, and none of the three of them has anything to do with a woman standing on a landing at the fourth hour.

The front of the ground floor was done for ten minutes about three weeks ago and has been done on and off on most mornings since, and the mat at the top of the stair belongs to the landing and not to her, and she puts her boot on one corner of it every working morning at the first hour and has done for about a month without knowing that she does it.

The light in that room was the same as the light on that landing in the end, and that was the whole of what she came up there to find out.
