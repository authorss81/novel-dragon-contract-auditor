# Chapter 0608: The Room Taken By The Week, And A Man Putting One Piece Of Paper Onto A Table

He had not put a hand into that coat in about two years and this morning he put one in, and it came out with one thing in it and not with the other.

It is the second day of the second week of the sixth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour.

---

There is a jug on the table with about two inches of clean water in it that a woman carried in from the pail at the end of that landing a week ago, and a glass face up beside that jug with the top left corner gone off it, and one coin about four inches in from the edge of the table which came out of that door about two years ago and is not going to be spent on anything. There are about four feet of plaster on that wall and a nail in the middle of them that went in crooked, and the coat was on that nail until about as long afterwards as it takes to square a sheet.

The jug is about a foot back from the edge and the water in it is clear enough to see the grain of the wood through, and there is a skin on it that neither of them has broken. The glass is face up with the missing corner at the far side from him, and there is a pale ring under it where it has not moved, and the coin is between the two of them and about four inches in, and it does not roll when the door is shut because the table is level.

The nail went in crooked and it is about the height of a hand and it is in the middle of four feet of plaster with nothing on either side of it. Where it went in the plaster is a different colour, and about the size of a hand, and it has been that colour since before the coat was on it.

The two things have been in the inside pocket of that coat on opposite sides of one seam for about two years. One of them is a leaf out of a bundle folded twice, and a line has been struck through whatever used to stand at the foot of that line, and the crease a third of the way down that leaf is worn through. The other one is the fourpence sheet. There is a printed strip along the head of it and four short things set across the top of that strip, and none of the four is a name, and it came out of a case four flights up on a wet morning for fourpence about two years ago.

Both of them go into the flat of a hand and the seam is a machine seam with the thread gone at two places. A thing has been folded and unfolded on the other side of that seam often enough to have left a mark, and a thing that has never been touched at all in about two years has left no mark except one of dust.

---

He went to the nail and took the coat down off it and unbuttoned the inside of it and put his hand into the pocket and brought out the sheet and not the leaf, and he did not look at the leaf and he did not put a hand back in there, and he carried the sheet to the table and put it down in the middle of it and squared it against the wood and took his hand away.

The coat weighs what it always weighs and he carried it in his other hand the whole way across the room and did not put it down anywhere, and the sheet is thin and it lies down on the wood without a crease in it and it makes almost no sound. Squaring it took the heel of his hand once. He took his hand off it and left it flat and he did not step back from the table and he did not go near the door.

She came up that stair at about the fourth hour and came in through the door that stands open about a hand's width, and she came the length of the room and stopped at the near side of the table.

She stops where she stops about a yard off a table with her weight even on both feet and her hands down at her sides. She did not come the last of the way in. She looked at the sheet and then at the coat over the chair and then at the sheet again, and she did not say anything about the coat.

She put her hand flat on the sheet.

She kept it there about as long as it takes to fold a cloth once and she did not turn it over and she did not read anything except the four things printed along the top of it, and then she took her hand off and went to the door.

"There is nothing written on that."

He did not say one word.

"There is nothing written on that at all."

"I know what is on it."

And she went out along that landing and down past the second tread and the stair went quiet behind her, and the mat on that landing is the landing's and its near corner stood up where the boards have lifted at a joint, and she did not knock on the way out and she has never knocked.

---

**I have carried those two things on opposite sides of one seam for about two years and no hand in this city has been in that coat and I have never told one person that I am carrying two things. This morning I put one of them on a table in a room with a second person in it and I did not take it back. I chose it, and I chose it over the other one, and that is the whole of what I did.**

The other one is the one anybody would put on a table, and it is still in the pocket of that coat, and it is going to stay in the pocket of that coat. A week is a long time in a city of about nine hundred buildings, and a leaf with a line struck through the foot of it is worth a great deal more to somebody than a sheet of paper with four printed things along the top of it, and I have known which of the two of them that is since before the spring that is gone. The coat went back on that nail about as long afterwards as it takes to square a sheet.

This one has nothing written on it. That is what I have bought and what I have had for two years, and that is what I put down.

And what it costs is the only thing I have got. A man who cannot be shown to have been anywhere near a thing is the only kind of man there is in this matter and I have been that man for about ten years by never once standing still in one place. There was a sheet of paper on a table in the room this afternoon with a strip printed along the top of it and four things printed along the top of that which are not a name, and a man in this city can be shown to have gone to the top of four flights and paid fourpence for it. That is all of what can be shown. I gave that up on purpose, in a room, in front of a person, and I gave it up for a piece of paper that says nothing, and I did it because a thing that is in the world is worth having and nothing has ever been in the world on my account.

And she put her hand flat on it and did not turn it over and did not take it and did not say one word about what it was for. She read the four things along the top of it and there is not one of them a name, and then she said it out loud, in the ordinary way, that there was nothing written on it. She was not asking me for anything, and no form anywhere in this empire would take a man for putting a piece of paper down on a table in front of another person, in either direction, neither as the one who put it there nor as the one who did not.

---

The sheet is where he squared it against the wood, with the printed strip along the head of it facing the wall, and the glass is face up beside it, and the light went off the room a quarter of an hour after the stair had gone quiet, and the paper was lying in the middle of the table in the dark when the room got cold.

The coat went back on that nail before the light went, and the pocket went back the way round it had been, and the door stood open about a hand's width all the way through, and the jug and the glass and the coin were where they had been before any of it, and there is not one form in nine hundred buildings in which any of this is entered.
