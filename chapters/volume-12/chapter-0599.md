# Chapter 0599: A Jug Of Water Two Days Old Standing On A Table Beside A Glass, And A Woman On A Landing Who Did Not Knock

He had filled that jug at about the fourth hour every morning for a fortnight and this morning he did not, and the water in it was two days old and had gone off, and he was not going to empty it either.

It is the second day of the first week of the fourth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is between the first hour and about the fourth.

---

The jug is on that table at about a foot back from the edge and the glass is face up beside it with the top left corner gone off it, and there is one coin on it that came out of that door at the first hour about four inches in and stopped, and it is his and it is not going to be spent. The bed is against the wall and is not made up. The stool is under the window and the window has been shut with its catch turned for about two years. The chair is where he dragged it a spring that is gone, with its back to the window and its face to four feet of plaster, and it is still there. There is a lamp on the floor at the end of the bed that has never once been lit, and a dark place on the boards under it where somebody struck a match off the iron and gave up.

There is a film on the water in it. It has not been skimmed and it has not been poured out, and it has gone over into a smell that is in that room this morning and was not in it a fortnight ago, and the water is still in the jug and the jug is still where it has stood every morning for a fortnight, and a man who empties a jug is a man who has been in the room. The glass beside it is face up and has been face up for as long as the jug has been there, and there is a chip out of one corner of the glass that has been there as long as the jug has, and the coin is four inches in from the edge of the table and has not moved in two years and is not going to move this morning.

The pail at the end of that landing was about two-thirds full this morning and it has been about two-thirds full for two days.

---

She came up that stair at the first hour and went back down it, and she came up it again at about the fourth hour, and she has been doing that for about thirteen weeks, and the mat on the landing has its near corner standing up where the boards have lifted at a joint and it is the landing's mat and not his. The landing is about four foot by six and there is nothing on it but the mat and the pail, and the mat has worn through in the middle of it and taken the shape of a foot, and the shape will be there tomorrow, and she stands clear of the lifted corner every time without appearing to think about it. She came up the first time and stood a while and went down again without going near the door, and he was in the room and did not go to it either, and the two of them were in that house at the same time for about as long as it takes to square a sheet and neither of them made a noise.

A boot came on the second tread and stopped there, and he did not go to the door, and it carried on down the rest of that flight and the stair went quiet behind it. The stair is narrow enough that a person going up it has a hand on the wall if they want one, and she did not want one, and where she stopped the boards creaked and then did not. There is a tread on that flight where a person can stand and be out of the sight of anybody in the room at the top of it, and she used that one, and he has never once asked her why and is not going to.

"There is more in that pail than there was two days ago."

"There is."

"You are not coming out for it."

"No."

"That is two days."

"That is two days."

"And the jug on your table has not been emptied."

"That is not the same jug."

"It is the same jug. You carried that jug out on to this landing the other day and you have not carried it out since."

---

**I had a quart of clean water in it every morning for a fortnight and it was the only thing I have ever done in this city that anybody could be shown having done, and I have let it go off in the jug rather than empty it, and that is the whole of a decision I made about a fortnight ago and I have not told one person.**

A man who changes the water in a jug in a room he takes by the week is a man who is in that room every morning. That is what it is and there is no other reading of it and it took me ten years to have something worth being entered in and I gave it up over a pail at the end of a landing.

And a man who leaves two days of water standing in a jug is a man nobody in this house would want one single thing out of. I would rather be that man in a room I do not intend to be in for a week longer than the week costs than be a man with a jug changed every morning in a city of about nine hundred buildings where nobody could produce him for anything.

And she came up this morning at the first hour and went down again and came up at the fourth hour and stood on the mat, and she has never once asked me for a light or for a word said out loud, and I have got a jug of two days old water standing on the table in front of me and I am going to leave it where it is until she has gone down those stairs and until the stair has been quiet a while.

---

She did not knock. She has never knocked and she is not going to, and she stood on that landing with the light gone off it about as long as it takes a coin to travel under a door, and then she came down past the second tread and the stair went quiet and did not come back up. He stood in the room in the dark after that and did not go to the door, and the pail at the far end of that landing had more in it this morning than it had two days ago and neither of them ever said one word about where the extra came from.

The last hour of the light went off the floor at about the fourth hour and went off the landing with it, and the jug was standing on that table in the dark with two days of water in it, and he did not turn the lamp on, and he did not go out to the pail.
