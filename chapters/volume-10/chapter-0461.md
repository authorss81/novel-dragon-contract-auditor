# Chapter 0461: The Counter At The Top Of Four Flights, The Fifth Time Up It, And A Sentence Said To The Boards Instead Of To Him

He put nothing on the boards, and that is what he has done on the four other mornings he has come up that stair, and the fifth time is not different from the other four in any way a person at that counter could enter.

**Marek Kest. Thirty-eight, holding nothing, at no stage, with no office and no fund and no list and no leave and no certification and no post and no commission and no warrant, and no heading over his name in any building in this empire.** He has refused the reading and he does not exercise the thing he is able to do, and the reason he gave out loud for not reaching a stage was given once, in about nine words, and he is not going to give it again this month and nobody at that counter is going to ask him for it.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the fifth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the fifth hour.

---

He had come up wanting two things, the way he had come up the last four times.

The first of them was a heading over a name. There is no column in that building that would take a name of his, and there has not been one since a bar came off him on an application of one line which he wrote himself, and a bar cannot be struck off that was never granted and a certification cannot be withdrawn that was never held, and a man with nothing behind him is a man with nothing behind him. He wanted it anyway. He has wanted it since before the fire.

The other of them was the fourth thing on a board on that wall. Four items stand on that board and not one of the four is a clock, and the last of them is the one that nobody who works that floor has ever been able to account for. **It is in the hand of the woman at the far end of those boards, it went up there about four years ago, and she put it there herself, and no one of the three of them has asked her about it and she has not asked herself.**

---

Marn Ottery watched him come the last of the floor without straightening up, the way she has watched him come the last of the floor four times.

He came the last of it with his weight on the front of his feet and his hands down, and the sound of his boots went the length of the boards and was given back the way it is given back every morning, and nobody at the near end stopped what they were doing for it.

"You have not brought a page this time and you have not brought a hand either."

"No."

"You brought a hand in a month that is gone and you put it on those boards and asked for a second of a thing that was not on them, and I made you one, and it took about ninety seconds, and I have not said one word about that since and I am not going to say it now." She did not put her hands flat on the wood. "**What you have come up five times for is nothing you can put on a counter, and I know what it is, and I am not going to do the thing you came up here to have me do, and you did not ask me for it, and I want that in the room while it is in it.**"

He did not say anything to that.

"I have said a shape out loud in this room three times and this is not it, and nobody in this building is going to be asked whether this one and those three are the same, and nobody in this building is going to be thanked for any part of this morning including the part where I do nothing."

---

Then she said it, and she said it to the boards and not to him, and it took about as long as it takes a clerk to square a sheet.

"**A second holder is a person who can be shown holding a thing and is not a party to it.** Fourpence is not mine to put my hand over and it is not anybody else's, and the words stop being mine the moment they are in type, and once a thing is in type it is a thing any person in this empire may walk out of this room holding. That is the whole of it and there is nothing underneath it. Nothing that has been handed to a person in this room has ever been called back, and there is no instrument in this matter except that one, and it has been standing in this room for eleven years and it costs fourpence and about ninety seconds."

"You have said that to a room."

"I have said it to a room with a man standing at the end of it who is not going to be thanked for having been up that stair five times without paying for anything, and to a clerk who is not going to write it down, and to a counter with about four hundred people going out of it in a year." She put one hand flat on the boards and left it there. "**I am not going to be thanked for saying it and neither is he going to be thanked for standing in the room while I said it, and that is the arrangement and not a courtesy, and if anybody in this building thanks anybody this week the arrangement stops standing before the sixth hour.**"

---

The clerk of about twenty-nine turned her head as far as it goes, which is not far, and said one thing and went back to the sheet in front of her.

"A person who has stood in a room while a sentence like that was said is a person who can be shown having stood in it. That is not the same as a person who can be shown holding the thing and it is not much smaller, and I am not writing it down and neither is anybody else, and the not writing is going on at the rate it has always gone on and I am not going to remark on the rate."

"You told me in a month that is gone that you had set yourself a limit on it, and you have been over it twice this morning."

"I have said a thing I have said before in a different month, and I am not counting, and I am not going to start."

Nobody at the far end of the tables had said anything else all morning, and the light off the lane had come round to the end of the room by then, and the clerk of about twenty-nine did not move her head again for the rest of the hour.

---

At the near end, the case came up out from behind the boards, and about four hundred people in a year went out of that room one at a time in the hand of somebody who was not doing anything anybody had asked them to do, and this morning was one of the ordinary ones.

Tamsin Rook put her hand flat on the lid of it and left it there while a man of about fifty put fourpence on the boards, and took a blank out for him, and watched him fold it and go down. Then a woman of about thirty did the same. Then nothing for eleven minutes, and then a young man with a cord across his chest did the same.

The lid of the case went down and came up and went down and came up and the fourpence went into the same place every time, and nobody counted it and nobody asked where it had been, and the queue at the near end of the boards was nobody's queue.

**None of them was asked what it was for. Not one of them, and not by any of the three people behind that counter, and that is not because the three of them are careful. It is because there is nowhere in this empire for a person to be told what a thing is going to be used for, and the not asking is not a courtesy, it is a shape that politeness makes when there is nothing to be polite with.**

---

At about half past the five a courier came up the four flights with a satchel on his shoulder, and he put a folded thing on the boards, and nobody told him what was in it, and nobody at that counter ever has and nobody ever is going to, and that is the whole of the trade and it is why the trade has outlived everything else about that floor.

Marn Ottery put the word and the number on the top of that folded thing in the ordinary way, and they went on that and on nothing else in the world. The courier took the bag off his shoulder and then put it back on, and went down the four flights, and there is no form anywhere in this empire in which a man could be entered afterwards as the one who carried a thing up a stair and was never told what it was.

---

**The man at the end of the boards went down the four flights at about the sixth hour with two things in the inside pocket of a coat and neither of them out of the pocket, and nobody counted them. There is no document anywhere in this empire with a line on it for the person who counts what another person is carrying, and there is not going to be one.**

He wanted two things when he came up. He had gone down with neither of them, and he had gone down with a third thing that had not been in the room when he came into it. That was a sentence said to the boards by a woman who was not going to be thanked for it, and he was the only other person in the room, and from the fourth hour this morning there is a woman at that counter who could be shown saying that a second holder is a person who can be shown holding a thing.

He went down the four flights with the two things still in the inside pocket of the coat and the third one where he had no use for it, and the boards came down and the case went in behind them, and the four went back up on the wall where they have held for eleven years. **She said it to the boards and not to him and he was the only other person in that room, and a sentence said in a room with one other person in it belongs to that other person from the hour it is said, and he carried it down four flights in the open, and there is no form anywhere in this empire in which a man is entered as the one who left a building carrying a sentence he did not ask for.**
