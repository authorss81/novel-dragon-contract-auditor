# Chapter 0451: Four Things Along The Top Of A Blank, A Knife In A Drawer, And About Four Seconds

The boards came down at the second hour and the case came up out from behind the near end of them and stood on the counter with its lid up, and about four hundred of the things inside it go out of that room in a week in the hand of somebody who is not doing anything anybody asked them to do.

The stair at the back of that room has no door at the foot of it, and there is a hundred feet or so of floor between the near end of the boards and the top step, and a board is on the wall at the top of that stair with four items on it and not one of them a time. **A drawer under that board has been shut for eleven years and there are four things in it and two of those have never once been opened.** Under the far end of the boards, out of sight of the door, there is a box holding about four hundred blanks that were bought in a quantity and never filled in by anybody.

**The box is not the supply. The case on the counter is the supply, and not one of the three people on that floor has ever been told where the case is filled.**

**Marn Ottery has the other end of those boards. She is thirty-four, she is not a lawyer, and she has been at them for eleven years, and she is the oldest of the three of them and the only one who has never left that floor.** Across the room from her, at the far end of two tables that have been pushed together, a woman of about twenty-nine keeps a book open with a pen lying in the middle of it, and on the second of the two days there is a man in as well who is not a lawyer and has never said he is.

It is the second day of the first week of the third month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the second hour, and the hours at that counter are the second to the sixth.

---

She took one out of the case and turned it over in her hand, and there was nothing to unfold.

**There is a printed strip along the top of every blank that counter has ever sold. The strip is the same strip. The four things printed along the top of it are the same four things, and they are on every one of the about four hundred that go out of that case in a week, and no week in this matter has ever produced one of them coming back with a fifth thing on it.**

"There is a knife in that drawer," Marn Ottery said, to the boards.

The clerk of about twenty-nine did not look up. A book was open in front of her and a pen was lying in the middle of it, and the pen had been lying in the middle of it since the second hour.

"There is a knife in a drawer at this counter," Marn Ottery said. "It takes about four seconds to cut a line into the strip on a blank if a person wants one cut. I have never wanted one. I am telling you the shape of it because you work at the other end of these tables and nobody in this building is going to tell you."

"You have never wanted one, and you have just told me there is one. Those are not the same thing, and I have been at that end of these tables for about four years, and I know the difference."

"I know it as well. That is the whole of why I said it and not anything else."

---

"That is not what a knife in a drawer is."

"It is a knife in a drawer. It is also about four seconds, and you have just had both halves of that before the third hour of a morning. I did not ask you for either of them and I am not going to ask you for the rest of it."

"I have not said one word this morning and I am not going to start at the second hour of the third month." The clerk of about twenty-nine turned her head as far as it goes, which is not far. "**I will give you the one thing I know about it, and it is not mine to give you and I am giving it to the room. A line cut into that strip costs about four seconds and there is no column over it, and there is nothing in this empire a mark with no column over it can be laid against, and it would be the same line on every sheet this counter sells. That is the first thing either of us has worked out that a person can do at this counter and not be a party to it, and neither of us is going to be thanked for noticing it, and there is nobody in this building who could thank us if we were.**"

---

Marn Ottery had both hands flat on the wood.

She had put them there three times in eleven years, two of the three had been followed by a sentence, and the third had been followed by a sentence that has been said on that floor once and has not been said since. **There is no form anywhere in this empire in which a clerk is entered as the one who has said a thing out loud on that floor a fourth time, and the thing she had in her mouth at about the third hour of that morning was the one she has not said, and there is no form in this empire in which a sentence a person has not said is entered against her either.**

"I am not going to say it."

"I did not ask you to, and I am not going to, and I have not said one word this morning that was aimed at a sentence of yours."

"You said *you are not going to cut a line in one* in a voice that was standing there waiting for something. I have heard that voice about four times in eleven years, and three of those were followed by a thing I have never had to take back from anybody."

---

A man from the yard was on the stairs at half past the second, a strap across him and a satchel off it, and he has been up and down that stair since before the woman at the far end of the boards came to it.

He put the satchel down on the wood, took a folded thing out of it, set that down as well, and stood there while Marn Ottery wrote the word and the number at the top of it in the ordinary way and put them on that and on nothing else.

A courier is nobody's man in a matter like this one. **He could have been told what was on that folded thing and there is no place on that floor where a person tells a courier anything, and that is the whole of the trade and it is why the trade has lasted as long as the stair.** He has not asked her one thing in eleven years, he was not told about the knife, and he was not told what the four things along the top of a blank are. He went down those four flights with the satchel on his shoulder and nothing in his hands, and there is no form anywhere in this empire in which a man is entered as the one who carried a thing up a stair and was not told what it was.

---

A man of about twenty-six was at the near end of those boards for about the length of a sheet being squared.

He is paid for a corner and not for a page, and he has been paid for that corner for about two years, and he put nothing down this morning. **He has never once in that time put fourpence on that counter, and nobody at that counter has ever told him he does not have to, and nobody has ever told him what a blank is for either, and both of those are the same arrangement and he is inside it.**

He bought nothing and he said nothing, Marn Ottery did not tell him what she had in her hands, he did not look at the boards, and he went down the four flights with the corner still in his coat.

---

Tamsin Rook was at the near end with the case behind her, and she had been in twenty minutes before the hour, and nobody on that floor has ever put the twenty minutes on anything, and there is no form in this empire that would take them.

**She is twenty-four, and nobody on that floor has ever put a question to her in the whole of the two years she has stood at that counter. She made an undertaking two years ago and has not brought it into use on a single day since, and it has not been retired either, and the day and the month of it are not said out loud on that floor and are not said out loud anywhere anybody can be shown.**

She did the eleven things in the order she does them. At about the third hour she took a blank out of the case and set it on the boards in front of a woman of about thirty-five, who folded it and put it inside her coat and went down. Nobody said anything to that woman, the woman said nothing to anybody, then a young man of about nineteen did the same, and then a man of about fifty did the same. That is an ordinary morning at that counter, and it has been an ordinary morning there for as long as anybody standing at it can remember.

---

At about half past the fourth the clerk of about twenty-nine squared the sheet in front of her and said the last thing she said that morning, and she said it to the sheet and not to anybody.

"A line cut into a strip is on about four hundred sheets a year and nobody can say whose it is. That includes the person who cut it. There is no form for cutting one and no form for reading one out and no form for asking, and I worked that out in about four minutes this morning and I am not going to work it out again."

"I am not going to cut one."

"I know you are not. **I said it out loud in a room so that it would be in the room, and it is in the room now, and I am not going to say it a second time, and neither of us is going to find out this week whether a mark with nothing over it is a thing anybody can be shown doing.**"

---

**The counter shut at the sixth hour, the case went in behind the boards, the four went back up on the wall board in the places they have held for eleven years, and the drawer underneath stayed shut with four things in it and two of them never opened.** The box at the far end was where it was with the same dust on the lid that was on it at the second hour. The strip along the top of a blank is the same strip and it was not cut this week, nobody in that room was thanked for noticing that there is a knife in a drawer, and the sentence Marn Ottery had in her mouth at about the third hour is still in her mouth, and she is not going to say it again this month or any month.
