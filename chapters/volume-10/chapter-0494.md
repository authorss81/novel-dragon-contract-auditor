# Chapter 0494: A Third Page That Does Not Come Out Even, And A Woman Who Would Not Rule A Fifth Column

The counter is where it has always been, at the bottom of that stair, and the day-book is where it always is on the shelf behind her, and the lamp on that table is not lit until the sixth hour.

That house turns over about four rooms a week, and the girl in the one at the back is into her second season of it. She is about thirty-five and she keeps that book in her own hand. Four headings stand over the first four columns of it, and in nine years of keeping it she has never drawn a line and set a fifth above that. Ruling one is the single thing in that house she would have given anything for, and she has never once put a pen to it.

He is about thirty-four, and the hand he is paid for is a better one than the work requires. A list of four days a season is the whole of the reason a search in this city can be relied on to hand him back. Findable was the safest thing about him for two years. It has not been the safest thing about him for about a month. His rate is a rate and not a kindness, and he works eight hours at it, and the pile in that building has been a hand's width thinner than it should be since a week that is gone.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the first month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fourth hour.

---

He came down and he did not go to the counter first, and that is the part she thought about afterwards.

He stood about nine foot off it for a while with his hand in the inside of his coat, the way a man stands who has decided something and is letting the deciding finish, and then he came the nine foot and put the page down on the wood.

Not the book. The page.

The page on the wood was a loose sheet with nothing printed over any part of it, and the day-book stayed shut on the shelf behind her with its spine squared off even with the edge of it.

It is a page of that week's work, and the figures in the second column do not agree with the figures in the first one, by about the width of a thumbnail, on three lines. He has shut a gap of that width with the point of a pen in about four seconds ever since he was nineteen. There is no column anywhere in this empire for a job a person has refused.

"There is a second one of those," she said.

"There is a third one of those."

"Then there are three of them in that building. Not one of them is on a book. Not one of them is in a drawer. They have gone out of that room in a pile with the rest of the week, and the person who knows which three is you, and you have known which three for about a month, and nobody has done anything about it." She did not put her hand out to it. "**You have come down here to give me a third one. I am not going to thank you for it. I am not going to be the person you give it to. I am telling you that flat, and to your face, and there is no form anywhere in this empire in which either of us is entered this morning.**"

---

He did not pick it back up.

It lay on the wood between them and neither of them put a hand out to it, and it was still there when she turned round.

"I have got a number in my head," he said, "and it has been in my head since a spring that is gone. I am not going to tell you what it is. I am not going to put it on anything. I am not going to be in a room where anybody says one word about whether it is right. Nobody in that house has confirmed it and nobody in that house has denied it, and that is not politeness. A figure a man keeps in his head is a fact about that man and about nothing whatever outside him. The day it is on paper it stops being about him and starts being about a thing, and a thing can be carried down a stair and put inside a coat, and I have watched that happen to a person who was careful about it."

"That is a thing you have said to me once already. I did not take it up then and I am not taking it up now."

"Then here is the rest of it, and it is about a piece of paper and not about a number. **I have spent a month finding out that a second of anything can be handed to a person without anybody asking them one word about it. That is true, and it is the only thing anybody in this matter has got that works. And what I have found out this morning, standing on your floor, is that you do not need a second of anything for it to work. I have put three refusals into a pile where nobody would ever see them. This is the first one I have put in front of a person. You are now a person who has had one in her hand, or is going to. I have made you unlucky and I have not asked you, and I am not going to be thanked for it.**"

---

She looked at the page for about as long as it takes to decide against having an opinion about a column of figures, and then she turned round and took the day-book off the shelf, and she did not open it, and she set it down on the wood with her hand flat on the cover, and took her hand off.

"I have four headings over four columns in that book," she said, "and the fifth has not been ruled in nine years, and I am not going to rule it this morning, and I am not going to rule it this month. **A page whose two columns do not come out even is the first thing anybody has put in front of me in nine years that I could have used. And I am not using it. The reason is not modesty. The reason is that a page that does not add up has no heading over it. The second I rule a column for it, I have ruled a column. The second I rule a column, I have made a fifth thing in this house that a person can be entered against. There are four in there now and I have had them for nine years.**"

She put the page in the shelf and put the book back down on top of it, spine flush to the front edge, where it goes.

From the doorway a shelf is a shelf and nothing on the outside of it says what is standing on it, and a shelf in a house like this one has never been a place a person was asked about, and nothing in the room was altered by her having stood in it.

"It is in there," she said. "It is not in the book. The book is shut. I am not going to turn it round. Nobody in that house is going to know it is in there, including me in about a week. And there is no form in this empire for a woman keeping a thing in a shelf and not knowing she is keeping it."

---

He went up the stair and did not turn round at the top of it.

The lamp on the table came on after he had gone and nothing else in the room changed.

He is a man who can be found, and he has been found by a person with four headings over four columns and a fifth that is not going to be ruled. What findability costs goes on an account he has still never been given the name of, and he has known that for two years and has not once asked to see it. This month it has come to about a page with three wrong lines in it, and a woman at the bottom of a stair who will not thank him and will not open the book.

Nobody asked either of them one thing. Nobody at that counter is owed anything by anybody. No column has been ruled. Nothing has been written down. The pile in that building is a hand's width thinner than it should be, and the third page of it is in a shelf in another house, and two people in this matter are now in it together and neither of them can be shown the other.

It was the first thing she had ever put in that house on purpose.
