# Chapter 0479: Two Sentences On A Shipping Floor, A Tally Kept In A Man's Head For Nineteen Years, And A Courier Who Asks For Nothing

He has kept his own count in his head since he was about twenty-six and he has never had a book for it and never had anybody in that building ask him for it, and nobody asked him for it on that floor this week, and nobody is going to.

**He is about forty-five and he has the loading end of that floor, and about two hundred things go across it a day, and every one of them belongs to somebody, and a thing on that floor acquires a name by the second time it has been handled and that has been the ordinary shape of his working life for nineteen years.** The box at the end of that floor is the one object out there that nobody could be charged with anything about until a fortnight ago, because it had not moved, and it has not moved since, and it was not opened this week and it is not going to be.

It is the second day of the first week of the tenth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fourth hour.

---

The young man came the length of that floor and stopped about nine foot off him and said it, and it was ten words, and it was true.

The floor between the two of them went on carrying the ordinary traffic behind the sentence and neither of them stopped what he was doing for it, and the loading end went on being loaded the way it is loaded every hour of an ordinary day.

"**A copy of a copy cannot be traced back to anybody.**"

The man of about forty-five did not turn round and did not answer it and went on with what he was doing for about as long as a sheet takes to be squared, and when he did speak he said it to the floor and not to the young man.

"**I am not going to tell you that is wrong, and I am not going to say it again after you, and here is the whole of what I am doing about it. From this morning that sentence exists in this lane twice over: once in the head of a young man and once in the head of a man of forty-five, and neither of us can produce it and neither of us can be shown with it, and that is the shape of the thing you have just described being true in a lane in this city with nobody in it.** I did not choose to be the second and I am not going to be thanked for it and you are not going to be thanked for having put me in it, and neither of us has put one thing to the other, and that is the whole of what has happened between us on that floor this morning and it took about four seconds."

---

"You said it was true and you have taken it."

"I have not taken one word of it and I am not going to, and I have just told you what it cost and I am not going to be thanked for the telling either." He put a thing down on the boards and picked up the next one. "**And there is a second one in your mouth and it is a different one, and I am not going to help you get it out and I am not going to tell you to keep it in.**"

The young man waited about four seconds.

He waited there with the sentence still in his mouth and did not put it down and did not pick up another one, and the other man went on putting things down and picking up the next one at the pace he has worked at for nineteen years.

"**That makes it the only paper that fits anybody.**"

---

He put the thing down and it made a noise on the boards and neither of them looked at it.

The noise went into the boards and came back out of them a little flatter than it went in, and the thing stayed where it had been put down, and the next thing was picked up off the floor at the same speed as the last one had been.

"**Take that off this floor.** Not off me. Off this floor. A sheet with nothing printed on it can be set beside the wrong pair of figures and there is not one man in this empire who would be able to say it was set there on purpose, and I have been the one writing the name onto things on this floor for nineteen years, and I am not going to be standing nine foot away from a sentence like that when the wrong pair of figures turns up on this lane. About two hundred things go across here a day and every one of them is somebody's, and that is the only thing I have ever been able to give anybody on this floor, and I am not giving it away to a sheet that fits anybody at all."

"I have not taken it back and I am not going to."

"I know you are not and I did not tell you to and I am not going to ask you for it and I am not going to ask you for anything, and there is no form anywhere in this empire in which either of us is entered as a person who was on a floor when the other one said a true thing. **You have said two sentences to me and I have said one thing back and it was the one about me, and we are level, and we are not going to be level twice this month.**"

---

At about the fifth hour a courier came up that floor from the yard with a satchel on his shoulder. The young man put four sheets into it and kept his mouth shut, and the courier took them and went up the stairs the way he goes up them, which is the way of a man who has been carrying other people's paper up a stair for longer than either of the two men on that floor has been standing on it. **He has wanted to know what is in that satchel every working day of that time and he has never once put the wanting anywhere, and neither of the two men downstairs has ever told him one word about it, and there is no landing on that stair at which either of them could have.**

He did not look into the bag going up and he did not look into it coming down, and he has never once stopped on a landing to hold it, and nobody who uses the stair has ever opened one.

**Nothing was ever made out of that box by anybody, and the two men who made it chargeable in a lane with nobody in it about two months ago cannot undo it, and there was nobody in that lane who could have told the two of them what they were doing while they were doing it.** Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was sent for and the two of them went up and down the same floor all week with about nine feet between them and neither of them said one word to the other on any of it except the two sentences, and those two were said once each and neither of them is going to be said a third time in that lane.
