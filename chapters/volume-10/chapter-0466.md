# Chapter 0466: A True Sentence Said Out Loud In A Room, A Man Of Sixty-One Signing On, And Nobody Going To Be Told

The pot was at his right hand and the pen went into it and came out again, and that is the whole of what he does at that sill, and he did it about two hundred times between the second hour and the sixth, and he did not once look up off the sill in any part of that time.

**He is about sixty-one and he has stood at that sill as the second hand since he was forty-two.** He cannot make sense of a paragraph. Nobody who works that floor has ever found that out and he has not gone out of his way to be found out, and there are four people in this empire he is nothing to do with.

**About nine hundred sheets a year leave that building and his mark goes at the foot of every one of them.** In about nine of them in a year the page is not what came down out of the room where it was written, and he has been the only man alive who knows which nine, and he has had it in his head since the spring without once putting a line of it on anything. There is no place in this empire to enter a thing like that and a man of sixty-one with a pot for a pen is the last person anybody would go to looking for one. That is the ninth of the nine and it is on nothing at all.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the sixth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the second hour.

---

She came into that room at about the third hour and she was fifty-three and she had been in it nineteen years, and she keeps the minutes of it, and she advises nobody, and she has not asked that room a thing in about four years.

She had been about eleven years in that room when she put a question on the shelf under the window with no pane in it and wrote her own name at the foot of it, on purpose, so that she would be a person somebody could go to. **Nobody has gone to her. Nobody has asked her anything. It is still a question, it has never had anything said after it, and it is on a shelf in a room, and it is not going to be picked up this week.** She is not going to be asked about it by anybody, including the man at the sill, who could not read it if it were put in front of him.

She sat down and she wrote her line, which is which room stood empty and at which hour and for how long, and then she did not write anything else, and she did it again, and she had been doing it for four years.

---

Then she said it, and she said it to the room and not to the man, and there was nobody else in it.

"**A second holder is not a person who can be shown holding a thing, because a second holder is a person holding a copy, and a copy is not the thing.** That is the whole of it and I have carried it for about a fortnight and I am putting it in this room because this is the room and there is nowhere else in this building where a true sentence can be left. A man at the foot of about nine hundred sheets a year is in the safest place there is in this matter. He does not know that and he is not going to be told, and nobody is going to tell him, and I have not told him, and I have not asked him one thing about it and I am not going to."

Nobody answered it.

"A man who could read a paragraph could read a second of any of them, and there is a man at the end of a lane about nine miles off this one who can read a paragraph and has always been able to read a paragraph and is nothing to do with the four. **He has not been asked and he is not going to be.** I have had a true sentence in my hands for a fortnight and I have put it in a room and not in an envelope and not on a shelf. There is no form anywhere in this empire by which anybody could afterwards be shown as the one who spoke in a room and got no answer, and I did it anyway, and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to be asked about it."

---

He put the pen away and took it out again and did the thing his hands do between one sheet and the next, and his eyes stayed on the sill throughout it, and he said nothing, and nobody said anything to him, and there was no version of that morning in which either of those two things came as a surprise to anybody.

**He was not asked what a second holder is and he did not ask.** He was not asked what was on the sheets he signs, and nobody has asked him that in about nineteen years, and the thing he has by himself in his head has been on no paper since the spring and is going to stay off paper until he cannot hold it, and there is no form that would take it out of him and get it onto a page.

The woman of about nineteen came out of that room at about the fourth hour with an armful and stopped at the end of the sill and said the number, because a woman of nineteen who keeps minutes under a woman of fifty-three says a number out loud at the end of a floor every working day and has never once been thanked for the number.

"Ninety on the hook at the fourth."

He did not look up.

She stood there for about as long as a sheet takes to be squared, and she had a true thing in her mouth and it was the same true thing she had said in that corridor in a month that is gone. **A person who tells is a person who has to go on telling, and the woman at the table behind her had just put one into a room an hour ago, and if she put one in too then there would be two people in that building who had said a true thing out loud in the same month, and she would be one of them for the rest of her life.** She put the armful down on the ledge, and she did not say it, and she picked the armful up again and went back into the room.

Nobody answered her and nobody asked her and nobody thanked her, and the not being asked was still the only thing she had, and it was going to be that in a month as well.

---

By a minute past the seventh hour there was nothing left on the hook, and the hook emptied the way it empties, and the nine hundred went out of that building, one at a time, into nine hundred places where not one person knows what stands at the bottom of a page they have never read.

**He signed the ones that were square and he signed them the way he signs all of them.** One sheet in about two years came up that stair with the same mark on it twice, and he signed the top of it and did not look up while he did it. There is nobody in that corridor who could point at that sheet and nobody in that building who could point at it, and the one man who has seen it is the one man in the place who would not be able to find it again if he went back down that stair to try, and he has not gone back down it, and nobody has made a thing of it.

Not one person in that corridor was thanked for anything in it and not one person in it was sent for anything in it, and there was no version of that week in which anybody on that floor was asked anything by anybody, and everybody who worked it knew exactly what that was. The woman of about fifty-three wrote her line and entered one word of what she had said in that room and did not answer the number and did not ask about the ninth of the nine and did not tell the man at the sill one single thing. **He signed, and about nine hundred of them went out, and the ninth of the nine is not on anything, and the man who would be able to read a second of any one of them is about nine miles off and is not going to be asked.**
