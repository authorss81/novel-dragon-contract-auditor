# Chapter 0493: A Foreman Who Says Out Loud What A Girl Of Seventeen Has Not Been Asked, And A Man Behind A Chain

The lamp on that bracket does not come on until the seventh hour, and until it does, that shed is not a place a sentence can be said in.

Halla Wray. Fifty-one. A foreman, standing inside a box that a class put her in, and the form behind that class has gone on existing for longer than she has been in it without anybody once filling in a line of it.

The two of seventeen are at the back where they have been all week, and the girl has not been asked anything in about a year and is not going to be asked anything this month, and the boy with the book is a person nobody in that lane has addressed in a long time and is not going to be addressed this week either.

The man at the other side of the chain is about fifty-five, and a paragraph has never once been beyond him, and he is not the clerk of about fifty-three and no week is going to make him that. Put that man down among the four in this matter who have never got a working sentence out of a paragraph and he is out of that shed and back into it is not going to happen, and he told her so years ago, and it is the only thing he has ever threatened anybody with.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the first month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fourth hour.

---

She had not gone to the doorway, and that is the first thing that had changed in that shed in about two years. She did not say so out loud, and the man at the front bench noticed it the way he notices everything in that bay, which is to say entirely, and about three weeks afterwards.

She had a sentence that was not half finished. It had been whole since the week the girl went back to the back of that shed after a summer. It needed no frame and no light and no particular hour. She had been carrying it to that door out of a habit that had stopped having anything to do with it.

The doorway stayed at the side of the shed it has always been at and the sentence stayed where she had put it for the length of the whole week, and the floor between the front bench and the door went by the way the floor goes by every day of the year.

---

Then she said the thing that is the whole of that week. She said it to the flags, and not to the man at the front bench, and she said it once.

"**I am going to say one thing about the girl at the back, and I am not saying it to her. I have not asked her one thing and I am not going to, and nobody in this shed is going to be thanked for this, and I am not going to say it a second time this month. There is no form in this empire for a person who has been given nothing and does not want it. Not wanting a thing is not a thing that can be put down anywhere. So a girl who does not want a second of anything is a person nobody in this shed has anything to enter her under. That has been the safest place a person can stand in this matter for about a year.**"

She let that sit, and the chain went a small way and came back, and the two of seventeen at the back did not move through any of it.

"**And here is the other half of it, and I am saying it to the floor. That is not a shelter. It is the cheapest position there is. A thing that cannot be asked about is a thing that can be given to anybody, and anybody includes the girl at the back of this shed, and it includes me, and it includes the man at the front bench. There is nobody in this shed this morning who is going to be asked whether they would like one.**"

---

He said nothing for about as long as a sheet takes to be squared, which in that shed is about nine seconds and is the only clock in it.

The chain did not move in any part of it and the shed did not make a sound in any part of it except the sound the shed makes when nobody in it is saying anything, and the man at the front bench kept his head where he keeps it.

He is the only person in this matter who can read out of a printed strip and say out loud whose hand it is, and no sheet is going to be put in front of him in this lane, and nobody in this city is going to put one there either. When he spoke in that bay, four sentences, about a year ago, nobody took one of them up, and he had not wanted anybody to. Everything he has said in that shed since has gone to the floor rather than to her, and she has taken in every hour of it, and he has not once looked up out of that bay long enough to see that she has.

"I have not said that it is right," he said, to the flags.

"I did not put that to you and I am not going to. I want it entered that I did not, and entered by nobody, because there is nowhere standing in this lane at which entering anything is done."

"I have not said it is right, and I have not said it is not right, and here is the whole of what I am doing about it. **I can read a paragraph. I have always been able to read a paragraph, and I have never once been asked to read one about that girl, and I am not going to be, and I am not going to be thanked for not being.**"

---

And then he gave the rest of it to the floor, and it took him about as long as it takes a person to decide against saying anything at all, and he did not turn round.

"And the half of it I am saying once, and it is about me and not about her, and I have had it since a spring that is gone."

"**A man who cannot be asked anything is not a man who is safe. He is a man who can be given anything.** There is no form in this empire that would let anybody put one sheet in front of me and another one in front of the woman behind me. There is a reason for that, and the reason is that a man who has been asked nothing has got no line anywhere. So the not asking is not a protection. It is an open door with the hinges on the other side. I have been behind this chain for eleven years and I have worked that out, and I have said it to a floor with nobody in it, and that is the whole of what I have done with it. Nobody is going to thank me, and I would not take it."

---

The two of seventeen at the back did not move at any point in that hour and a half.

The light off the lane did not reach behind the bench at any point of the morning and nobody in the lane looked at either of them at any point of it, and the back of the shed went on being the back of the shed without anybody going near it.

Nobody went near the back of the shed in the whole of the hour and a half, and the ordinary morning went on going past the door of it, and the lamp stayed dark on its bracket until the hour it comes on by itself.

The girl did not say one word, and she is not going to be asked anything this month and she is not going to say anything this month, and nobody in that shed has ever told her that either of those is unusual. Nobody in that shed is going to speak to her, and nobody is going to send for her, and nobody is going to thank her. If a second of a second were put in front of her tomorrow, there would be nowhere in this empire for her to put the fact that she did not want it.

The boy with the book read what he reads. He has not been spoken to by anybody in that lane in a long time.

At about the seventh hour the lamp came on by itself, and there was a woman walking the floor for the rest of that night with her hands behind her, and the floor went past the same way it goes past every night of the year.

The second half of a sentence she began in a doorway about two years ago has still not arrived. The man at the front bench has not asked her for it and is not going to. Nobody in that lane is going to be told this month that a woman of fifty-one has said out loud what a girl of seventeen has not been asked, or that a man who can read a paragraph has said out loud that not being asked is an open door with the hinges on the other side.

She did not go to the doorway once.
