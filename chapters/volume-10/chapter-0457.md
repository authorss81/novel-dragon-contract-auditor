# Chapter 0457: The Fourth Time Up Four Flights, Fourpence Not Once On The Boards, And A Counter Shut At The Sixth Hour

The stair at the back of that room has no door at the foot of it, and the hours at that counter are the second to the sixth. He came up the four flights at about the fifth hour for the fourth time in his life and put nothing on the boards.

**Marek Kest. Thirty-eight. He holds nothing and nothing is on its way to him, and he refuses the reading, and he does not exercise the thing he is able to do. The reason he gave out loud for not reaching a stage is in about nine words, and he gave it once, and he is not going to give it again this month.** There is a leaf in the inside pocket of the coat on his arm, and a piece of paper with a printed strip along the top of it in the other pocket of the same coat, on the side the page is not on. He has not taken either of them out of it in this building or any other, and he has not told anybody he is carrying two things.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the fourth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fifth hour.

---

Marn Ottery was at the far end of those boards, and she watched him come the last ten foot of the floor without straightening up. The clerk of about twenty-nine was at the other end of the two joined tables with a book open and a pen in the middle of it. Tamsin Rook was at the near end with the case behind her.

The case was shut and the boards were up along the whole length of them, and the light off the lane came in at the far end and stopped about where the wall board is, and nothing on the floor made any noise at all.

"**This is the fourth time you have come up those four flights, and you have never once put fourpence on this counter, and a person who comes up a stair four times and buys nothing is the only person in this matter a practice can be built on.** I am not going to thank you for it, and I am not going to be thanked for saying it, and the fact of the fourth time is in this room and is not on anything."

"I did not come up here to be counted."

"You came up here to be counted, and it is not me counting you, and it would be the fourth time whether I counted it or not." She put her hand flat on the boards for about a second and took it off again. "**I have said the shape of a thing out loud in this room three times, and nobody has asked me one thing back about any of them, and I am not going to say it a fourth. What I have just said to you is not that. It is not a sentence, and nobody is going to be able to put it on a board.**"

---

He had come up wanting two things, and he was going to go down with both of them. One of the two was a heading over a name. The other was the fourth of four items on a wall that three people had looked at for eleven years.

He looked at the wall board while she was talking, and he did it for about as long as it takes to read four items. There are four items on that board and not one of them is a time, and the fourth of the four is the one none of the three of them can account for, and it has been there about four years.

"You have been looking at that wall since you came up," Marn Ottery said.

He did not say anything.

"I am not going to tell you what the fourth of those four is for, and I am not going to tell you that I cannot tell you. I have known both of those for about four years and I have not said either of them out loud in this room to anybody."

She put her hand back on the boards.

"**A person who has a name that fits in the space at the foot of a sheet is not a person whose thing is missing.** The woman at the other end of those boards worked that out in front of you in a month that is gone, and she said it once and nobody has asked her one thing about it since, and I have not been able to get at it in nine years because every way I have had of saying it is a sentence."

He did not say anything to that either.

Nothing was said there for about as long as it takes to turn a sheet over, and her hand stayed on the boards where she had left it, and nobody came down to the far end of the room while it went on.

"You want a heading over your name and there is not one in this building that would take it, and there is not going to be one. I have not got one to give you and neither has anybody on this floor, and you will not be thanking me for that, and I will not expect it of you, and neither of us is going to say so out loud again this week."

---

Tamsin Rook put her hand flat on the lid of the case at the near end and left it there.

"I am not going to serve you and I am not going to say why. You are not going to be thanked for coming up those flights, and I have not asked you one thing in the whole of the time you have been on this floor, and I am not going to start on a morning with a courier on the stairs."

"You do not have to serve me."

"I know I do not. That is not the half of it I am saying, and I am not going to say the other half of it on a working morning." She took her hand off the case. "**You have bought nothing off that counter four times and you have come up four flights four times, and there is no form anywhere in this empire in which a clerk is entered as the one who counted a man's four times. I have been standing at this end for two years and I have not counted anything and I am not going to start, and neither of us is going to find out this week what it would cost.**"

---

A courier came up at about the fifth hour with a satchel and put a folded thing on the wood, and nobody told him what it was. The man of about thirty-four who comes in on the second of the two days stood at the near end and looked at the counter and at nothing else on that floor. There were three people behind that counter and a man in the middle of the floor and a courier on the stairs, and not one question was put to anybody in the whole of that hour.

Then Marn Ottery wrote the word and the number at the top of the courier's thing in the ordinary way, and put them on that and on nothing else, and the courier went down the four flights with the satchel on his shoulder.

He went down the four flights a little after the sixth hour with two things in a coat and neither of them out of the pocket, and nobody counted them. There is no form anywhere in this empire in which a person is entered as the one who counted what another person was carrying. Nobody sent for him and nobody was sent, and he was not thanked.

He went down in the ordinary way with his hand going along the rail as it went, and the rail was cold enough in the middle of the run to be worth the hand, and there was nobody at the bottom of it and nobody was going to be.

**The boards came down at the sixth hour. The case went in behind them, the four went back up on the wall where they have hung for eleven years, the drawer underneath stayed shut, and the box at the far end of those boards was exactly where it had been at the second hour.** The strip along the top of a blank is the same strip it was at the second hour and it was not cut this week. There is still no column anywhere in this building that a man of thirty-eight with nothing behind him can be entered in, and there is not going to be one. The fourth of those four items is the one none of the three of them can account for, and it is still in Marn Ottery's own hand.
