# Chapter 0456: A Key With A Number On The Back Of It, Nine Years Of One Man's Rent Behind A Boiler, And A Name She Did Not Take

The lamp on the table by the front door was lit at the sixth hour, and it has been lit at the sixth hour for about twenty years. The ground floor is the ground floor: a passage, a stair off it, a door at the top of that stair that she has never been at, and about twenty years of nobody going up it and nobody coming down it.

**She has been at the bottom of that stair about twenty years and has never once been above the second step. She takes a rent. The rent goes out of a hand into a hand once a week, and nothing about it has ever been written down anywhere in that house, and nobody has ever come to that ground floor to be told one thing about what she is holding.** There is a tin behind the boiler with about nine years of a man's rent in it in coin, and the lid of it does not shut the way it used to, and she has never counted it and there is no reason on earth to count it.

**She is not the clerk of about fifty-three.** That building off the old river road is somewhere she has never been and would not know the inside of, and if the whole of what had been said in it were put in front of her she would not be able to say which room it had been said in, and nobody has ever told her anything and there is nobody in this city she could go to for it.

It is the second day of the second week of the fourth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fifth hour.

---

The knock came on the front door at about the fifth hour, and it was the knock of a man with something in his hands, and she knew that before she had the door a third of the way open.

He was about twenty-one. He had a cord across his chest and a bundle on his back and about seven shillings loose in his fist, and he had been three houses in two years, and one of the three had been his father's and the other two had not been anybody's fault.

"I am looking for a room and I have got until the end of the week."

"There is a room at the top of those stairs and there is a room at the back of that passage and there is a room over the coal. I take the rent and I do not write it down. I do not write anything down. The number of the room is on the back of the key and it is not on any paper, and I am not going to give you a receipt for it, because I have never given anybody a receipt for it in twenty years."

"**You have not written a name down in twenty years and you have taken the rent of about nine of them. The man upstairs and the man behind the coal and the man in the front room have all been in and out of that door, and none of them has ever been on anything, and that is not because you are careful. That is because a person who takes a rent for twenty years without a name is a person who cannot be served with anything.**"

Nobody said anything for about as long as it takes a person to shift a bundle off one shoulder.

"That is not a protection," she said.

"It is not. **It is not a protection because nobody would want to, and those are two different things, and the difference between them is the whole of what you have, and you are the only one in that house who knows which side of it you are standing on. You have known for about nine years. I worked it out in about the time it takes to count this money.**"

---

"There are three rooms and I have not got three written down anywhere either."

"You have not. I can see that from here, and I am not going to say one word about it again, and I am not asking you about anything. I have not asked you about anything, and there is nobody going to be asked anything in this house this evening, including me."

"Which of the three is the one you would put a person in."

"The one at the back of that passage. It has a window and the other two have not. I am not going to tell you that for your own good. I am telling you because I have been the cheapest person in this house for about nine years, and the rent is the only thing I have ever done, and I would rather somebody chose with a window than be the person who chose without one."

He shifted the bundle and looked at the stair and did not go up it. She did not ask him to and she did not tell him to.

Then he said his name out loud in that passage, in the ordinary way, the way a young man says a thing he has been told to say, and he said it to her and not to the room.

She did not take it.

"I am not going to remember it. I am not going to write it down and I am not going to say it back to you, and there is no form anywhere in this empire in which a woman at the bottom of a stair is entered as the one who heard a name. If you want to be found afterwards you will have to be somebody who can be found without me, and there is nobody in this house you can be found through tonight."

"I did not say it so that you would be in it."

"I know why you said it. Everybody says a name at a counter for the same reason, and it is not so that the person hearing it will use it."

---

The rent came out of his hand into hers at about the sixth hour, and it was one week of it, and it was not a debt, and it was never a debt. There is no form anywhere in this empire in which a woman at the bottom of a stair can be sued for a room she has let, and nobody in that house has ever been owed anything by anybody.

She gave him the key. There was a number cut into the back of it with a file, and that number was the only record of that room in existence, and it was not a record of anything else. She did not tell him the number of the room he was in, and he did not ask, and she did not go up the stairs to show him and she is not going to.

**Nobody was thanked in that house this evening. Nobody was forgiven. Nobody was sent for. Nothing was written down and nothing was resolved, and the rent is the rent, and the tin behind that boiler is where it was, and she is the only person in that house who knows what is in it. She has known for about nine years, and she said nothing about it to a young man with a bundle, and she is not going to say anything about it to anybody.**
