# Chapter 0452: Two Blanks Before The Fifth Hour, A Clerk Twenty Minutes Early, And Nobody Saying A Word Back

Tamsin Rook was in at twenty minutes to the fifth, and the twenty minutes are the twenty minutes.

**Nobody on that floor has ever wanted anything out of her.** She is twenty-four, she has stood at that counter for two years, and not one person in this building has put a question to her in any of it. Her undertaking is live and unretired and unused, and in two years it has not been brought into use on a single day, and the day and the month of it are not said out loud here and are not said out loud in a state record either.

It is the second day of the second week of the third month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fourth hour and forty minutes.

---

She did the eleven things in the order she does them. The room at the other end of the boards went on the way it goes. About the fifth hour the queue began, and the queue is not a queue either, because there is nobody standing at the end of it that a person can be shown standing at the end of.

**The only paper in that room is a thing that counter sells for fourpence, and she is not going to buy one of them out of that money with the money of that counter.** That was the whole of what she wanted at about the fifth hour. It is a small thing and it is the shape of everything.

She had been working towards something since the previous week. Not a decision. She had been watching.

---

Then Marn Ottery said it, and she said it out loud in the ordinary voice, and she said it to the case rather than to anybody.

"A copy has a queue and a time cost, and about four hundred people carry one object out of this room in a week. A person who has watched that can pick out which of them are carrying the second of a thing in about a minute. **The second of a thing costs fourpence and about ninety seconds, and it is the only object in this room that can be bought twice, and a person who has noticed that is not going to be told anything about it by anybody.**"

Tamsin Rook did not say anything.

She had the same thing and she had had it for about a week. She had got at it from the other end, and it had taken her until the fifth hour of a working morning to be sure of the shape of it. A woman at the other end of the same boards had just put the identical thing into the air in about forty words before she could put it into hers.

**That is what nobody in that room has ever done to her, and it is not a kindness, and she knew that inside about two seconds of hearing it.**

"You have not said it out loud," Marn Ottery said.

"I have not said it out loud."

"You are twenty-four and you have been at this counter two years and nobody has asked you one thing in that time, and I am not going to be the first. I have just said the thing you had, and I have said it to the case and not to you. You can take it or leave it, and it is not going to be said again this week."

"I am not going to take it and I am not going to leave it. It was mine before it was yours and I have got a better one. I am going to say the better one now, and I am saying it to the boards and not to you, and you are not going to be thanked for either half."

---

"You cannot pick them out in a minute."

"No."

"**I have watched about four hundred people go out of this room with one object in them and I could not tell you this morning which of them were carrying the second of a thing and which were carrying the first. Neither could you. Neither could the woman at the far end of those two tables. The reason is that they are the same object.** The strip along the top is the same strip. The four things printed on it are the same four things. The case gives out the one thing, and there is nothing in this building that can tell a first of a thing from a second of a thing."

"That is the answer and not a complaint, and I have not got nine words for it, and I am not going to stand here and be the second person in one morning who says something I cannot get back."

"I said it before you could say yours, and that was not done to you, and I would like it noticed that I noticed, and I am not going to be thanked for it by you and I am not going to be thanked for it by anybody who finds out."

---

A man of about thirty-four came up at about the fifth hour and put fourpence on the boards. She gave him a blank out of the case at the near end. He folded it and put it inside his coat and went down the four flights.

Then a woman of about twenty-eight came up and put fourpence on the boards. She was given the same object out of the same case. She folded it and put it inside her coat and went down the four flights.

**Two people. Fourpence each. The same object, the same morning, and Tamsin Rook did not say one word back to either of them, and neither of them said one word to her.** That is the ordinary way of that counter. It is also the whole of what she had worked out about it.

---

The clerk of about twenty-nine got up off her stool at about the fifth hour and went along to the case. She looked down into it for as long as it takes a person to count the top of a stack, and came back and sat down again.

Tamsin Rook watched her do all of it. The clerk of about twenty-nine knew that she watched it. Neither of them said one word about it, **and the not saying is the reason the two of them have lasted four years at the same two joined tables, and there is no form anywhere in this empire in which either of them is entered as the one who said nothing.**

---

At about half past the fifth a courier came up the four flights with a satchel on his shoulder. He put a folded thing on the boards and stood there, and nobody told him what it was, and he went down again.

**Nobody asked him anything, and nothing in this empire could have been put to him, and he has been a courier since the first year of this matter and he is going to be one in a month.**

---

The boards came down at the sixth hour and she went out into the stair with one hand on the cold rail and nobody on any of the landings. The twenty minutes had not been a decision when she came in and they were not a decision when she went out. They are not on anything, and there is no form in which the twenty minutes can be entered against anybody, and nobody in that building has ever asked her why she is in early, and she has not said.

**She got it right on the second landing, going down, in about a minute.** A copy is the only object in that room that can be bought twice. It is the only object in that room a person can buy twice, put inside a coat, and go down four flights with. It is the only one of the two she has put fourpence down for that anybody else in that building could have handed her as well. She did not say it out loud to anybody and she is not going to. Nobody is going to thank her for having worked it out, and there is nobody on any of those four landings she could say it to who would not turn it into a document by hearing it.
