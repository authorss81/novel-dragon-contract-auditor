# Chapter 0482: A Foreman Who Goes To A Door And Does Not Start It, A Man Who Crosses The Shed, And A Sentence Left Where It Stops

Nothing that gets said in that shed before the seventh hour could be said in a room anybody would call a room, and the lamp on the bracket is dark and will be dark for another three hours.

Halla Wray. Fifty-one. A foreman, standing inside a box that a class put her in, and the form that stands behind that class is the nearest thing this empire has to the thing that put her there. It is on the board by the door and it is older than she is in that class and it has not been filled in by anybody in all that time, and the only job it has left is the job of going on existing. The two of seventeen are at the back where they have been all week. Nobody in that shed put an offer to the girl or a condition on her this week, which is not the same thing as asking her and has never once been found out to be different in writing, and the boy with the book has not been spoken to by anybody in that lane in a long time and is not going to be spoken to now.

The man at the other side of the chain is about fifty-five, and the chain has been across the front of that bench for longer than she has been in that class, and a paragraph has never once been beyond him in his life. Put him in with the four people in this matter who cannot get sense out of a paragraph and he will not sit in the same shed again, and he has told her so, and it is the only threat that man has made in eleven years. He is not the clerk of about fifty-three either and no week is going to make him that.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the tenth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fourth hour.

---

She came in off the lane with the form still not in her hand and left it on the board, and she went to the front of the shed and put her back to the bench and said it to the flags on the floor.

"**I have got two halves and I am going to say them both and you are not going to answer either of them, and I want that entered before I start.** The first one is the one I worked out in the last month and I have moved it along since. A class is entered by a form. The heading over the column is the class. Every sheet that has ever come off that class carries that heading, and a second of anything carries the same heading, and that is why a second of anything I stand in is not a way out of it. **And here is the part of it I have not said out loud anywhere: a heading is the only thing that makes a mark into a thing you can be shown, and a mark with no heading over it is not that.**"

She let that sit.

The flags under her took the sound and gave back less of it than they were given, and she stood in front of the front bench with her back to it and did not move while the rest of what she had come in to say went out of her.

"You were not going to say anything and you are not going to now, and I have not said one word to you this morning that was aimed at you, and I am not going to be thanked for any of the two halves."

---

He shifted on the bench and the chain went a small way and came back, and he did not turn round.

"**Then here is the other half and I am saying it to the floor.** One printed band runs along the top edge of every blank that counter has ever sold, every one of them is the same band, the four things set along the top of it are the same four in the same order, and about four hundred of those sheets leave one building in a year. A line cut into that strip is not a signature. **It is a fourth item with no name on it, and it is the same fourth item on every sheet that counter has ever sold, and there is nobody in this empire who can say whose line any one of the four hundred is, and that includes the person who cut it.** A person cuts one and the line stops being theirs at the moment the knife goes down, and there is no column in this empire for a mark like that and there never has been one, and nobody in this lane is going to make one."

---

He said nothing at all about the strip.

Nothing was said in the bay for a length of time afterwards, and the chain across the front bench stayed where it was, and nothing was carried into the shed by anybody on any day after the morning and nothing was carried out of it either.

That is the whole of what that man did about it, and he had it, and he has had it for the length of time it takes Halla Wray to cross that floor twice. He is the only person in this matter who can read a line out of a printed strip and say out loud whose it is, and nobody in this lane is going to put a sheet in front of him, and nobody in this city is going to ask him for it, and he has not said one word of it in a room and is not going to start. He said four sentences in that bay about a year ago and nobody answered one of them and he never wanted an answer. Everything he has said in this shed since has gone to the floor instead of to her, and she has noticed all of it, and he has never once noticed her noticing.

"You have not said that it is true," she said, to the flags.

---

"I have not said that it is true and I have not said that it is not true, and you did not put either of those to me and I am not going to thank you for not putting it, and there is no form in this empire in which either of us is entered this morning."

"Then do not sit there with it."

"**I have been on this side of a chain for eleven years and I have watched about nine foot by eleven of floor go past this bench and I have never once been the thing that stopped it.** You have just told me the answer to a question I was not asked and you have told me the half of it that costs you nothing to say, and you have kept back the half that would have cost you something, and I know which half that is because I have been on the other end of a form for eleven years and I know what a person sounds like when they have put the useful part down first."

---

Then she went to the doorway.

She goes to that doorway about six times a year and she has stood in it about six times since the spring with a sentence that begins *It is not a person who* and stops there every single time, and the man at the front bench has never once asked her for the rest of it and has said so, in those words, and meant it.

She has walked to the frame at the side of the shed about six times a year and every one of them has been the same walk and the same not-beginning, and the door is on the side of the shed the light comes in on, and the frame is the brightest place in the bay before the lamp comes on.

She got as far as the frame and she did not begin it.

And about two minutes after that the man at the front bench got up, which he does perhaps twice in a month, and came the length of that shed to the board by the door and stood with his back to the form that is on it, about nine foot off her, and looked at the wall.

Nobody will ever be able to say which of the two of them left that sentence where it was. She did not begin it. He crossed a floor he did not have to cross, and he did it two minutes after she had gone to the door, and the order those two things happened in has never been spoken about in that shed by either of them and is not going to be. A sentence that stops halfway in a doorway is a sentence with two people standing in that doorway, and there was one person in that doorway this morning, and there has been one person in it on every other morning for about two years.

The doorway is the width of a person and it has been the width of a person for as long as the shed has been a shed, and it is the only place in the bay where a sentence can stop halfway and still be a sentence anybody heard, and it has been standing there with nobody in front of it for about two years except for her.

He went back to the front bench about a minute later and sat down and the chain went a small way and came back, and neither of them said one word about the two minutes, and the two of seventeen at the back did not move.

---

The board by the door kept its form on it, unfilled, and the form that put her in that class is not being asked to be void by anybody and there is nowhere standing in this lane at which the asking could be made. That chain has not moved a inch in eleven years and neither has the bench behind it, and the two at the back of that shed have not moved either. At about the seventh hour the lamp came on by itself, and for the rest of that night there was a woman walking the length of the floor with her hands behind her, and that was all it had, and it was enough to open it in the morning.

Not one word about a mark cut into a printed strip was said in that shed after the seventh hour, by anybody, ever, and the second half of the sentence in that doorway did not arrive, and he did not ask her for it and she did not give it to him, and the reader of seventeen was not spoken to and the girl was not given the chance to say no.

That band went on being printed along the top edge of every blank that counter sells, with the same four things set along it and not one of them a name, and about four hundred of them a year left a building in somebody's hand.
