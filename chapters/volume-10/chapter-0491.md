# Chapter 0491: A Sheet On The Boards That Was Not In The Case, And A Woman Who Would Not Put It Back

It is the second hour of an ordinary morning at that counter, the case is out, the boards are up, and there is a piece of paper lying on the wood in the middle of that floor that nobody put there.

Tamsin Rook is twenty-four and she has stood at that end of those boards for two years. In two years nothing has been left on them by anybody. Everything that comes onto that wood goes through a hand, and the hand is either hers or Marn's. The only things that have ever gone onto it without a hand are a broom and, once, a dog that came in off the lane, lay down on the middle of it, and had to be got off again.

It is the second day of the first week of the first month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the second hour, and the hours at that counter are the second to the sixth.

---

She had her hand on it before she had looked at it properly, and the stock told her, and then she looked at it properly anyway, because a person who has decided about a thing and has not looked at it is in a worse position than a person who has not decided.

It is the same stock, and the strip printed along the top edge of it carries the same four things in the same order, and not one of the four is a name.

There is nothing on the rest of the sheet at all, and that is the part that took her the length of a breath. A sheet that had come out of that case in the ordinary way would have gone down four flights in somebody's hand with fourpence against it in the tin. This one had nothing on it, and nothing under it, and nothing behind it. It was not warm. It was not cold.

---

Marn Ottery came along the boards from the other end. She is thirty-four and she has not left that floor in eleven years, and she is the only one of the three of them who never has. She did not touch it.

"**That goes back in the case, and it goes back in now. The case is open, and the case is where that stock lives, and a thing on my floor that is not in my case is the only kind of thing on my floor I cannot answer for.**"

"It goes back in the case," Tamsin Rook said, "and then in about three weeks it is at the top of four flights in the hand of somebody who has folded it once. By the end of the year it is somewhere else with a heading over every column of it, and not one word of this morning on any of it."

"That is what stock does. That is what it is for."

"**It is what stock does to a sheet nobody paid fourpence for.** You are talking about about four hundred of them a year going out of that case in a hand. And one morning in every one of those four hundred, the sheet that came off those boards is the one that comes off the case. There is not one thing printed on either of them that would say so."

Marn Ottery looked at the strip along the top of it for about as long as it takes a person to decide against having an opinion about it.

"There is a difference between a sheet nobody paid for and a sheet somebody made," she said. "One of those is a mistake. The other one is a decision. And if it is a decision, then it is not ours to put in a drawer."

"There is a drawer under that board and there are four things in it, and two of them have never once been looked at by anybody," Tamsin Rook said. "That drawer has not been opened in this room in eleven years. I am not going to open it for a piece of paper that arrived on a morning."

---

At the far end of the two joined tables the clerk of about twenty-nine had her book open in front of her and her pen down the middle of it, which is where it has lain every working morning for about four years.

She is the one person on that floor who could have taken a sheet in her hand and said out loud what it was a second of. She has said one thing in that room in about four years and it was said to another person. She has not said a second one and she is not going to say one this morning.

Nobody was waiting on her. Nobody on that floor has waited on her since before the man at the end of the boards had been in the building. That is the whole of what that end of those two tables is, and it is the reason she has got away with it for four years.

She squared the book against the table with the side of her hand, which is the one thing she does with it, and the pen stayed where it was, and she did not turn her head as far as it goes.

---

The twenty minutes before the hour came and went, and Tamsin Rook carried the sheet to the case and then did not put it in it.

She went down under the boards instead, on her knees, to the back, where the floor meets the wall behind the drawer, and she put it down flat on the boards of the floor there, and she pushed it back with the heel of her hand until it was against the wall and out of the light.

It is not in the case and it is not in the drawer, and it is not in her pocket and it is not in a hand, and it is not on a table where anything is ever stood up, and the three of those are the only places a piece of paper ever is in this matter.

"There," she said, to herself, and out loud, because the room had nobody in it who could be thanked. "**A third place, and there is no column anywhere in this empire for a third place. I have just made one. I have not been asked to make it and I am not going to be thanked for it, and I am not going to tell anybody on this floor where it is.**"

---

At the sixth hour the case went in behind the boards, and the four of them went back up onto the wall above it into the four places they have held for eleven years, and the fourth of those four is the one the three of them have never managed to account for, and it went up the way it goes.

Marn Ottery looked at the boards once before she went, and she did not go down under them, and she did not say one word about what was under them, and she has not said one word about it since and is not going to.

A woman of twenty-four went down four flights with a sheet under the floor of a room she has worked in for two years. Nobody sent her. Nobody asked her where it came from, and nobody is going to. The counter shut, and the case was in, and a third place had been made.

Nobody on that floor had ever made a third place, and she made one before the hour.
