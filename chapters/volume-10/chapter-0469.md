# Chapter 0469: A Man With A Fair Hand At A Door At The Bottom Of A Stair, What That House Is For, And A Rent Taken In Coin

There is a passage off that street and a stair off the passage and a door at the top of that stair that she has never once been at, and the front door of that ground floor is at the bottom of the stair with a lamp on a table beside it, and the woman who keeps that ground floor has been at the bottom of it about twenty years. **She has not been above the second step this week and is not going to be, and the three rooms above that stair are three rooms and not one of them is hers, and the one at the back of the passage belongs to a man who pays on the same day every week, and no one in that house is owed anything by anybody, and there is no way in this empire to serve any of them with anything.**

She takes a rent in coin, once a week, out of a hand and into her own, and it has never been on a piece of paper in that house and never is going to be, and a woman at the bottom of a stair cannot be asked what she carries in this empire because there is no form in it for asking, and nobody has come down that passage to ask her a single thing in twenty years.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the seventh month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the sixth hour.

---

He came up the street in the rain and put his hand on the frame of the front door and did not knock, and she knew who it was from about four foot off and she opened it.

The rain was coming off the step and it put the lamp on the table higher in the eye than it usually stands, and the passage behind her was dark the whole length of it, and the boiler was somewhere behind the dark making the noise it makes.

"You have not come up this street since a month that is gone."

"I have not. I have come up the four steps to my own door about two hundred times since then and you have never asked me one thing about them, and I have not given you one word about them, and I have not given you my name, and you have not asked me for it, and I am not going to start tonight." He had a rolled thing inside his coat and he kept his hand on it. "**I am going to say one true thing at your door and then I am going to go back down that street, and I am not going to say it to you twice, and I am not going to be thanked for it, and I am not going to be thanked for the two hundred times I have gone up four steps either.**"

"A second holder is a person who can be shown holding a thing. That is what it is and it is the whole of it, and I have worked it out about four times in the last month and I have said it out loud in two rooms and both of those rooms had other people in them. **Nobody can be shown holding a thing in this house.**"

---

He put his hand on the frame of the front door and did not come in.

"It is not because you are careful. I have watched you do it for two years and it is not care, it is arithmetic. There is no name on anything you have. The rent is coin and the tin behind the boiler is coin and the number on the back of that key is cut into the metal of it and it says which room and not who. **A person who can be shown holding nothing can be made to take a thing in about nine seconds, and that is what this house is for and it has been for twenty years, and you have known it for about nine years and you have never said it out loud to anybody and there is nobody on this floor you could say it to.**"

"Then you have said it for me."

"I have said it at your door in the rain and you have heard it and I have not asked you one thing and you have not asked me one thing, and that is the whole of what has happened at this door tonight, and there is no form anywhere in this empire in which either of us is entered as the one who heard a thing said at a door."

---

He took his hand off the frame and put it on the rolled thing inside his coat and did not take that out either.

"I had it in my head to leave this in that passage. Not with you. On the shelf in the passage, where it would be for whoever came in after me, and it is eight hours of a fair hand at a rate that is a rate and not a kindness, and there is a line in the fourth page of it that does not agree with the line above it, and I have not been able to make the two of them agree in about a fortnight."

The passage behind her had a shelf on it at the height of a person's hand, and the shelf had been on the wall since before she came to the house, and it was the only place in the house a person could leave something where the next one in would find it.

"Leaving it on that shelf is what you came up this street for, and you have not done it."

"**I am not going to leave it, and the reason I am not is the reason I came up this street, and it is that the second person to hold a thing in this house is the person who cannot be shown holding anything, and that person is you.** A thing on a shelf in that passage with a second set of figures in a different hand on it is a thing that two people can be shown, and one of them would be you, and you could not be shown not holding it because there would be nothing to be shown you were not holding. **I have been findable for two years and I have got a bill for it on somebody else's account, and I would not put a second bill on yours, and that is the whole of what I came up this street for and it took me about four seconds to say and I am not going to say it again.**"

She did not say anything to that for about as long as a person shifts a bundle off one shoulder.

She had the door open the width it opens to and no more, and she did not widen it and she did not narrow it, and the air off the step went past her into the passage behind her and stopped where the passage stopped taking it.

"You have come up this street to tell me what this house is for and you have told me a thing I have known for about nine years, and you have not asked me one word, and I have not asked you one word, and there is nobody in this city I could go to for the rest of it. Go down that street."

---

The man behind the coal came down about nine minutes later and put a week of it into her hand out of his fist. It was not a debt and it was never going to be one, and she is a woman who keeps the bottom of a stair and lets rooms off it, and there is nothing in this empire that can be laid against a woman for a room she has let, and nobody in that house is owed anything by anybody and has not been for twenty years.

His hand went back out through the front of the house the way it had come in, and the rain had come off the step by then, and the front of the house went quiet again in the way it goes quiet, and she was standing in it the way she stands in it.

He went up. Nothing has gone down that stair in about three weeks and nothing was going to.

**Nothing in that house was put down that week and nothing in it was taken back up, and the three rooms were let again on the same day to the same hands as the week before, and the lamp on the table by the front door is lit. The tin behind the boiler is where it is and the lid of it does not shut the way it used to and she has not counted it and there is no reason on earth to count it. Nothing was written down and nothing was resolved, and the stairs went up and nobody came down them, and the only thing that happened at that door in about a month was a man who can be found told a woman who cannot what she has been for twenty years, and neither of them was thanked, and neither of them said one word back.**
