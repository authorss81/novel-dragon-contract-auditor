# Chapter 0487: One Of The Four Sheets Going Down Four Flights Is Cut And The Man Carrying It Is Not Told, A Pen Lying In A Book, And A True Thing Said On The Second Step

There is a lamp at the foot of that stair and it is lit at the sixth hour, and a man with a strap across him comes up the four flights at about the fourth hour with a satchel on his shoulder, and he has been coming up them since before the woman at the far end of the boards came to that floor, and there is no landing on any of the four where anybody could stop him and say anything.

Nobody has ever told him what is in the satchel. He has never once asked, and he is not going to start, and it is not a courtesy between the two of them and has never been anything of the kind. There is no form anywhere in this empire in which a courier is entered as a person who was told, and there is no form in it in which he could be asked, and the two of those are the same absence and they are the reason the trade has lasted as long as the stair.

It is the second day of the first week of the twelfth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fourth hour.

---

About four sheets a week go across that stair in a satchel, in one direction or the other depending on the hour, and about four hundred people a year come the other way carrying a different object altogether and nobody on that floor has ever told any of them what it was for.

One of the four in the satchel this week has been cut into along the top edge. He did not know that and he was not told it and he did not look and the satchel was not opened and there is nowhere in this matter where anybody could have opened it. He carried it down four flights and a street and set it on a counter where nothing is sold and everything is set down and taken up again, and the man who took it off him did not say one word and neither did the man who put it there, and the strap went over his shoulder and the stair went up behind him.

The four sheets went into the bag flat, and nobody in the room could have said afterwards which of the four of them it was, and the courier did not look at the top edge of any of them.

---

At the far end of the two joined tables the clerk of about twenty-nine had the pen lying in the middle of her book where it has lain all morning, and she did not say a word, and she is the one person on that floor who could have said out loud what a cut line in a printed strip says. She has not said it in four years. The pen went on lying where it lies.

Nobody was waiting on her. That is what that end of those two tables is, and it has been that for about four years, and a man from the yard with a satchel is the least trouble of anything on that floor and always has been.

---

Marn Ottery said it to the boards, once, and not to him and not to the man at the near end.

"**A thing a courier carries cannot be recalled by anybody at all. Not by the man who put it in the satchel, and not by me, and not by anybody in this building, and there is no form in this empire for recalling one and there is not going to be one.** Here is the half of it that is worth saying out loud this morning, and I am not going to be thanked for it. This is the only trade in this matter in which the person who made the thing gives it up entirely. The fourpence goes in the tin and the maker's hands are empty and the maker gets nothing back, ever, and nobody has ever thanked one of them because nobody in nine years has ever found out there was a maker. **Everybody else in this building takes a thing and keeps it. A courier takes a thing and has not got it by the time he is down four flights, and he is the only person in the matter who cannot be shown keeping anything at all, and nobody has ever worked out what that is worth.**"

Nobody answered her. The man at the near end of the boards did not answer her and the clerk at the far end of the two joined tables did not answer her and had not been asked.

---

The young man at the loading end put four sheets into the satchel and kept his mouth shut.

He did it the way he has done it. He did not say the sentence he has said twice on that floor, and he was not going to say it a third time, and nobody at the loading end had asked him for it and nobody was going to, and about nine feet of floor went by between the two of them at the loading end and the satchel got to the bottom of that stair.

And then the courier said something.

He said it on the second step going down and he said it to nobody, in the ordinary voice of a man who has said nothing to anybody in eleven years and has got very good at not saying things.

"Nobody has told me what is in it and I have never asked, and I have worked out this month that I am the only person in this matter who is handed a thing every week and has never once got anything back for it, and there is nobody in this city who could show anybody that I have ever had anything. I am not going to ask, and I am not going to be thanked for not asking, and that is not modesty. It is that there is no form in this empire for a man who was given something and got nothing, and a man who says that out loud in a stair is a man somebody could be shown."

The stair took him down four flights and out of that street, and the lamp at the foot of it is lit at the sixth hour and was not lit when he went past.

Then a dog started up somewhere in the next street, and he stopped on the second step going back up with the satchel against his hip, and the young man at the loading end was still at that counter with the boards in front of him.

The stair went back to being a stair with him on one step of it and the other one of them where the morning had put him, and it made the same noise under him that it had made under him all morning, and that was all it made.

"You are not going to have said that to me," the young man said, without turning round.

"I have not said it to you."

"You have said it to a stair in a lane with nobody in it, which is the same as saying it to me and cheaper for you, and I have been at this end of this floor about two minutes and I heard none of it and I am telling you that I heard none of it, because that is the sentence and I am saying it while it is still true."

The courier shifted the satchel. The strap had gone into the shoulder it goes in.

He did not take the bag off and he did not turn round, and he went on up the four flights with the bag against him, and he did not use the hand that was not holding it for anything.

"Nobody has ever asked me for anything either," he said, "and I have not said that in a stair just now and I am not going to say it anywhere."

"You have not. That is the whole of what I have got and I am not going to ask you for the rest of it, and neither of us is going to be thanked on this floor."

He went down the four flights and out of that street and did not look back at the landing, and the young man went on with the boards in front of him.

---

The case went in behind the boards at about the sixth hour and the four items went back up onto the wall above it in the places they go, and the fourth of them is still the one the three of them have never had an account of. About four hundred of the things in that case go out of that room in a year in a hand nobody arranged. The strip printed along the top of every one of them is the same strip, and a line was cut into the top edge of one of them this week, and the person who cut it has a name and is not in this room and was not asked, and nobody on that floor was thanked for anything.

The man who went down those four flights with a satchel has said one true thing in a stairwell in eleven years and has just had a second one of the same shape handed back to him on a counter, and cannot be a person nobody said anything to again. The pen on that book is where it has lain every working morning for about four years, and this morning somebody stood at that counter and told him he had not heard anything, which is the first kind thing anybody on that floor has done for the pen in four years.
