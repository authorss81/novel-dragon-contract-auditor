# Chapter 0489: A Rent Taken In Coin On The Same Day Every Week, A Woman Next Door Who Goes Past, And A Tin Behind A Boiler That Did Not Get It

The lamp on that table has been lit at the sixth hour for about twenty years and it is lit at the sixth hour now, and the ground floor is a passage and a stair off it and a door at the top of that stair, and nothing came down that stair in that week and nothing was going to.

**About twenty years she has stood at the foot of that stair. She came as far as the second step this week and not one step past it, and the three rooms up there were all let again this week to the same three people they went to last week, and not one of the three is hers.** What she takes comes out of a hand and into her own once a week, it has never been on paper in that house, it is never going to be, and no form anywhere in this empire will put a woman at the bottom of a stair into a column, and nobody has come down that passage to ask her one thing in twenty years.

**She is not the clerk of about fifty-three, and the two of them have nothing in common but a trade of being left alone in a room.** That clerk has nineteen years of a life in a building off a river road that this woman has never been inside and could not find the door of. This woman has twenty years of a passage, a boiler and a table with a lamp on it. Put every word either of them has heard said this month in front of her and she could not tell you which building it came out of, and nobody has ever told her a thing, and there is nobody in this city she could walk to and ask.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the twelfth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is about the sixth hour.

---

The woman of about thirty-five went down that lane at about the third hour and passed the four steps without slowing. The woman who keeps the ground floor was at the passage window with the lamp not lit yet, watched her go by, and took no notice of her.

Neither of them said one word. It is not the first week either of them has let one pass the other, and it is not going to be the week one of them starts.

There is a second of a second in that woman's coat. **Nobody on this side of that lane could tell you what it is a second of, and the woman who made the first of the two is not a party to the second and could not be shown standing next to it when it was bought, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for asking either of them what either of them is for.** It came out of a case at the top of four flights for fourpence and it went down four flights inside a coat, and about four hundred of those leave that building in a year, and there is nowhere in this empire to enter the person who carries one.

---

**A mark with nothing printed over it cannot be laid against anybody, and a house at the bottom of a stair is the same kind of nothing, and the two of them are on this side of one lane and neither can be shown anything about the other.** She had worked that out over about nine years and had never said it out loud, because there was nobody in the house to say it to, and now there was one person in the house and he paid on the same day every week.

The man behind the coal came down about nine minutes after the hour and put a week of it into her hand out of his fist.

He did not say anything. He does not say anything. He has paid on the same day of the week every week for as long as she has kept that ground floor, and it has never been a debt and it is never going to be one, and no one in that house is owed anything by anybody, and nobody could serve a person in that house with a paper if they had one.

---

"You have not said anything in two years," she said, "and I have been at this end of a passage for twenty, and I have got one thing I have never said out loud to anybody and I am going to say it to a wall."

He went on up the stairs.

"**A thing with no column over it cannot be read out and cannot be asked about and cannot be laid against anybody, and a person at the bottom of a stair is the same thing and has been for twenty years, and the two of us are the only two in this lane who could be handed a piece of paper that would stick to nothing.** And I am not going to be thanked for saying it and I am not going to be thanked for the nine years it took me and I am not going to say it a second time. There is nobody in this house I could say it to twice to."

The man behind the coal did not turn round on the stair and did not answer and went up.

---

Then she stood in that passage with a week of a rent in her hand and did a thing she has not done in about nine years.

She did not put it in the tin.

**That tin has been behind the boiler for nine years and holds about nine years of one man's rent in coin, and the lid of it no longer sits the way it did, and she has never counted it and there has never been a reason on earth to count it.** She has put every week of it in there for nine years without deciding to, the way a person puts a thing where it goes. And this week she stood in that passage with it in her hand and worked out in about four seconds why.

A coin in a tin behind a boiler, in a house where nothing is ever written down, is the only figure in that house anybody could ever be shown. Nobody is going to arrive to be shown it. And the week somebody wants to know what is in that tin is the week that house acquires a heading over it, and it is never going to acquire one.

She put the coin in the pocket of her apron and went up to the passage window and stood at it, and the tin is where it is behind that boiler and nobody has counted it.

---

All three of the rooms up there went out again in that week, on the same day, to the same three people as the week before, which is the ordinary shape of that house and has been for twenty years, and a number cut into the back of a key is the only record of any of them in existence and it is not a record of anything else.

**The three rooms went out again on the same day to the same three hands, a week went by, and nothing was put down in that house and nothing was taken back up, and nobody was thanked and nobody was forgiven and nobody was sent for.** The young man from the shipping floor did not come to that door in that week and has not come to it in a long time and is not going to, and he said his last true thing at a door about four steps off in a week that is gone and nobody has come up a stair after him since. The lamp on that table is lit, and the apron is on, and the tin behind the boiler is where it has always been with nine years in it that nobody has counted.
