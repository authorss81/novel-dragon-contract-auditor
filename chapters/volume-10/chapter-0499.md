# Chapter 0499: A Pot That Took A Pen For Nineteen Years And Did Not Take One This Week, And A Woman Of Nineteen Who Did Not Write It Down

Nineteen years at that post, and a pot on his right that takes a pen and hands it straight back, and nothing else about the working life of a man of about sixty-one that anybody in this empire has ever needed.

A paragraph is beyond him, and the nineteen years have gone on not letting anybody on that floor find that out. He signs at the foot of about nine hundred of the sheets that go out of that building in a year. About nine of them in a year are not what the room above sent down, and he signed those as well, and he is the only person alive who knows which nine, and he cannot write one of them down and has never held a position in which he could.

None of this stock came out of the case at the top of four flights, and the printing along the top edge of one of these is not the printing on that stock. About four hundred that come down that wire in a year have somebody's knife in the top edge of them, and not one of them is in anything he keeps, and not one of them is in anything anybody else keeps.

It is the second day of the first week of the third month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the second hour.

---

About a sheet takes to be squared, and they come down the wire flat, and they come down the same way every time, and he has done it about nine hundred times a year for nineteen years.

This week he has been putting the pen down on the sill before he signs it, and he does it without stopping, and nobody on that floor has seen him do it.

Not far. About four inches to the left of where the pot is, face down. He signs with the other hand, which he has not done in nineteen years, and then he picks the pen up again and puts it in the pot, and the pot takes it and hands it straight back. About a sheet takes to be squared. Nine hundred of them a year do it that way.

He began it on the first day of the fourth week of the last month. He has not stopped, and he has not told anybody, and nobody on that floor has said one word to him about it.

The pot has been standing there all week with nothing to hand back more than it ever had, because the pen is on the sill and not in the pot. There is no wider account of that than there is of anything else in that building.

---

He cannot say why he did it.

That is the part of it that belongs to him and not to anybody. A woman of about fifty-three came out of a room she had not come out of in about four years and said a true thing at the end of that sill in a month that is gone. He could not have read it, and would not have said so if he could. About a fortnight after that he stopped going to the top edges with his left hand, and he gave up the only way he had of finding out a thing that was not written down.

About three weeks after that he put a pen down on a piece of stone.

If anybody in that building were to put one thing to him about it, he would not be able to read the answer, and he would not be able to say the answer. There is no form anywhere in this empire in which a man at a sill is entered as the person who began doing something differently on a particular morning.

---

The woman of about nineteen came out at about the third hour with an armful and stopped at the end of that sill, about where she stops.

She stood there as long as she was going to stand there, and he knew what it was, because he has known that corridor since long before she came to the end of it, and it is the one place in that building where anybody ever goes quiet on purpose.

She keeps the minutes under the clerk of about fifty-three. She said out loud once that a person who tells is a person who has to go on telling, and that the not being asked cost her the only thing she had, which was not being asked. She was not answered. No week since has anybody answered her, and no week is going to.

She looked at the pen on the sill, and then at the pot, and then at the pen on the sill again, and the two of those took about as long as a sheet takes to be signed.

She had a book under the armful, and she writes down what that room decides, and the room decides almost nothing, and what it does decide it decides twice. There is a question on a shelf under a window with no pane in it that carries no full stop, and it is not going to be printed out in any building in this empire, and the woman who wrote it made herself askable on purpose a long time ago, and nobody is going to ask her a second time.

She did not write it down.

That is the whole of what she did about it, and it was a decision, and there is no column anywhere in this empire for a person who has looked at a thing and not written it down. A thing that is not written down cannot be produced against anybody, and a person who has chosen not to write a thing down is a person who could be shown having chosen, and she is nineteen, and she has not said one word to anybody about it and is not going to.

She carried the armful back into that room without saying one word, and the book went with her, and it was shut.

---

About nine hundred sheets went out of that building that day, one at a time, and his mark is at the foot of every one, and somewhere near four hundred of them came off that wire with the top edge open, and not one of the people who carried any of them off that floor has the faintest idea what is printed along the top of what is now in their hand.

A pot on a sill took nothing back all day that it had not taken back for nineteen years before it. A man of about sixty-one has made one decision this month that no person in that building knows about, and no person in that building can read the reason for. A woman of about nineteen saw it and left her book shut. The two of them have not said one word to each other and are not going to, and there is nothing to be thanked for in either half of it.

A pen lay on that sill all day where a pot had been taking one back for nineteen years.
