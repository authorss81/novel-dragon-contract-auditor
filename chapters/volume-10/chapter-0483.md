# Chapter 0483: A Thumb That Has Known The Top Of Every Sheet For Nineteen Years, A Woman Of Fifty-Three Who Comes Out Of Her Room, And A Hand That Stops Going There

He has stood at that sill for nineteen years, and the pot on his right takes a pen and hands it straight back, and there is no wider account of the working life of a man of about sixty-one than that and there is not going to be one.

He came onto that sill nineteen years ago as the second hand. He cannot make sense of a paragraph, and nineteen years of taking care that nobody on that floor finds out is the whole of what he has done with that. About nine hundred sheets go out of that building in a year and his mark is at the foot of every one of them. About nine of them in a year are not what came down out of the room where it was written, he signed those nine as well, and he is the only person alive who knows which nine and he cannot write one down and has never been in a position to.

A sheet comes down the wire flat and it comes down the same way every time, and he squares every one of them with his left hand along the top edge, because that is the hand that goes under the corner when you square a thing on a table. He has done it about nine hundred times a year for nineteen years, and his thumb knows a thing his eyes cannot get at.

This building's stock is not the stock that counter at the top of four flights sells, and the band printed along the top edge of one of these is not that band, and a mark cut into it is not that mark. About four hundred of the sheets that come down to that sill in a year have been cut into, and he has never written one of them down, and he has never told one person in that building.

It is the second day of the first week of the eleventh month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the second hour.

---

The woman of about fifty-three came out of that room at about the third hour and stopped at the end of the sill, and that is the first time she has come out of that room onto that floor in about four years and the whole of that room knows it.

She advises nobody. She has not put a thing to that room in four years. Her own finding is on a shelf under a window with no pane in it: a question, no full stop, her own name at the foot of it. Nobody has gone to it. Nothing is going to be set down after it this month. And it would be no use on this floor, because the only man standing on this floor would not get through the first line of it.

"**I am going to say one thing on this floor and I have not said one thing on this floor in about four years, and I am not going to say it a second time and I am not going to be thanked for it.** A mark at the foot of a sheet and a mark cut in the strip at the top of that same sheet are on one piece of paper, and about half a year apart in the life of it — the strip was printed about half a year before the mark went at the foot of it. And neither one of them is a party to the other one. The hand at the bottom does not know about the knife at the top and the knife at the top does not know about the hand at the bottom. **And a sheet carrying both of them has no column anywhere on it at all**, and I have kept the minutes of that room for nineteen years and there is not one heading in nineteen years of them that either of those two would go under."

---

He did not look up and he did not put the pen down, and the pot went on taking it and giving it back, and about a sheet takes to be squared went by before he said anything, and what he said was not to her.

The sill under his eyes took the whole of the time she was standing at it and he gave her nothing off the end of it, and the sheets kept coming down the wire and going out the far end of the room the way they had been going all morning.

"I have not been asked one thing."

"You have not been asked one thing and I am not going to put one to you, and nobody in this building is, and I want it entered that I did not."

---

The woman of about nineteen came out at about the fourth hour with an armful and stopped at the end of that sill about where the other one had been standing, and said the number at the end of the floor in the voice she says it in, and it was not the number she had said the week before and nobody wrote it down.

And then she stopped at the end of that sill for about as long as it takes a person to decide against saying something, and he knew what the stopping was, because he has watched that corridor for nineteen years and it is the only place in that building where a person is ever quiet on purpose.

She had said a true thing in that corridor about a month ago. She had got nine seconds of it out and it had cost her the only thing she had that was hers, which was not being asked. He heard the stopping and he could not have told anybody what was in it, and she went back into that room with the armful and she did not say one word.

The armful went back into the room it came out of and the door of the room closed behind it the way it closes behind an armful every working day, and the corridor kept whatever she had not put into it to itself, and the sill went on being a sill with a man behind it.

"You are not going to say the other one," he said, to the sheets.

"No."

---

From about the fourth hour he squared those sheets with the heel of his hand instead of along the top edge.

About four hundred of them a year had come down cut, and his thumb had gone to every one of them for nineteen years, and from that hour it did not, and the sheets came down flat and the same and he signed them the way he signs them all, and the cut ones went past his left hand with his left hand not going to them.

Nothing about the sheets was different to look at and nothing about the man signing them was different to look at, and not one person in the building could have said afterwards which hand had stopped going where it had gone.

He gave up the only way he had of knowing a thing that was not written down. The reason he gave it up was a sentence a woman of fifty-three said at the end of a sill about two hours before it, and he cannot read a paragraph and could not have said what she was talking about and would not have said so if he could. Nobody in that building noticed, because there is nobody in that building who is looking at a man's left hand, and that is the whole of the price and he paid it without knowing there was one, and if anybody had told him what it cost he would not have been able to read it.

---

The hook was bare by the seventh hour and it emptied the way it empties. Nine hundred sheets went out of that building one at a time that day, and about four hundred of them came down that wire with the top edge open, and not one person who received any of them has the least idea what is at the top of the thing in their hand.

The hook came back to hanging empty and the sill did not move under him, and the pot stayed at his right hand where it has been every working morning of the nineteen years, and the corridor outside the room went on being a corridor people came out of with armfuls in.

The ninth of the nine is on nothing whatever and it stays in a man's head, and from the fourth hour this week so does everything else. A woman of fifty-three came out of a room she has not come out of in four years and put a true thing on that floor and got nothing back for it. A woman of nineteen stood at the end of that same sill this week and did not put hers out, and he heard the not putting it.

About four hundred sheets a year go on going out of a building with a mark in the top edge of each of them that belongs to nobody, and he was the only one in this city who could have felt it, and from the fourth hour this week he is not one of them.
