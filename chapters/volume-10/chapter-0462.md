# Chapter 0462: Two Joined Tables To Herself, A True Thing Said Aloud To The Room, And Four Words At The Top Of A Page

The far end of those two joined tables was hers, and this week they were not joined to a third one, and the far end was hers alone from the second hour, and nobody at either end of those boards had said one word about that and nobody was going to.

**She is about twenty-nine and she is not a lawyer and she has been at that end of those tables about four years. She said once, in a month that is gone, that she would not say a second thing, and she has not said one, and she is not going to say one this morning, and there has been a page in front of her all morning waiting for one.** A book is open in front of her and a pen is lying in the middle of it, and the pen has been lying in the middle of it since the second hour.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the fifth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the third hour.

---

Nothing came down in front of her for about two hours, and that is not a quiet morning and it is not an empty one. It is an ordinary morning at that counter and has been for as long as anybody standing at it can remember, and the whole of what it is comes through at the near end in ones and twos and goes out of that door and down four flights.

She wrote what she writes every day at the top of the page in front of her. Then she squared the sheet in front of her with the side of her hand, which is a thing she does about nine times an hour, and she did not look up.

---

Then Tamsin Rook said it, and she said it standing at the near end with her hand flat on the lid of the case, and she said it to the room and not to the woman at the far end of the tables.

"**The second of a thing and the first of a thing come out of the same case this morning, and there is nothing in this building that can tell them apart, and I have watched that be true about four hundred times in a week and I have never once been able to tell which was which.** That is the true half of it and I have said it in this room before and been answered by nobody, and this morning I am going to say the other half of it once and then I am going to go back to the case."

---

She did not answer it. She did not agree with it and she did not disagree with it, and she kept her eyes on the page, and the pen stayed lying in the middle of the book.

"You have not said anything."

"I have not said anything."

"**You have just said a true thing out loud in a room with me in it, and that is not the same as saying it to me, and I want that noticed, and you have noticed it and you are not going to remark on it.** I have said a thing in this room before and watched it go into the air and settle on the boards and stay there, and I have done it more than once, and I am not going to do it again this month, and this morning I have just handed you the reason without asking you for it."

"That is not asking me for anything."

"It is not. It is putting a fact in a room that you were standing in. There is a difference and you have known about the difference for four years and so have I, and I have never once said it out loud, and I have said it now because I am tired of being the only one in here who knows it." Tamsin Rook took her hand off the case. "**A person who has said a thing out loud in a room is a person who can be shown having said it. No form anywhere in this empire has a line for the person who was in the room, and no form anywhere in this empire has a line for the person who was not, and that is why nobody in this building is going to be shown anything and nobody in this building is going to be told anything, and that is the arrangement and not a courtesy.**"

---

She wrote nothing down. That is the whole of what she did with it, and there is nowhere she could have written it even if she had wanted to.

The pen went into the middle of the book and stayed there, and then she picked it up and used it, on the ordinary line, at the ordinary place, for the ordinary four words that go at the top of that page every working morning. She did not write one word of what had just been said in that room on that page or on any other. **The not writing went on at exactly the rate it has always gone on, and the rate did not change, and the reason it did not change is that she has never once written down a thing anybody said to her or near her or in front of her.** She was not going to begin it on a morning when there was a woman standing eight foot away who had just handed her the strongest reason there is.

"It could be written down and it would still not be a form, and it would still not be a document, because there is nowhere in this empire for a document of two clerks at a counter to go."

"I know that."

"It could be written down and then it would be a thing two people know and a thing two people have said, and there is still nowhere for it to go, and I have had my hand on that page for about half an hour this morning and I have not put it down."

"That is the first sensible thing anybody has said at that end of these tables in four years, and it is the reason I have not done it."

---

At about half past the fourth a man of about thirty-four came up the four flights and put fourpence on the boards and was handed a blank out of the case, and he folded it and put it inside his coat and went down again, and neither woman said one word to him and he said one word to neither of them.

A courier came up after him with a satchel and left a folded thing on the boards and went down, and nobody told him what was in it, and a man of about twenty-six stood at the near end for an hour and bought nothing and said nothing, and nobody told him he did not have to.

**Nothing in that room was asked anything. Not the man with the fourpence, not the man with the corner, not the courier, and not the woman at the far end of two joined tables who has been eleven years in a building where nothing gets asked, because nobody in this empire has anywhere to put down what a thing is going to be used for.**

---

She got up off her stool at about ten minutes before the sixth hour and shut the second of the two joined tables down, and stood with her hand flat on the edge of it for about as long as a sheet takes to be squared. She had said nothing and had written nothing and had agreed with nothing and had disagreed with nothing, and there is no form anywhere in this empire in which a clerk is entered as the one who did all four of those things in one morning.

**The drawer under that wall board stayed shut with four things in it and two of them that have never been opened in eleven years.** The box under the far end of those boards was where it had been at the second hour with the same dust on the lid, and the four went onto the wall in the same eleven-year places. **The fourth of the four is the one the three of them have never been able to account for.** It is in the hand of the woman at the far end of the boards, and she put it there herself about four years ago, and nobody has asked her.

The four words at the top of the page were the only thing on that page at the end of the day.
