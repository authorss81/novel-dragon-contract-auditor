# Chapter 0490: The Whole Of It Worked Out In A Lane With Nobody In It, A Young Man Who Says Nothing, And A Box That Is Still Chargeable

About two hundred things go across that floor in a day in ones and twos, and every one of them belongs to somebody, and by the time a thing has been handled twice on that floor it carries the name of the person it belongs to, and that has been the ordinary shape of one man's working life for nineteen years.

**He is about forty-five and he has the loading end of that floor and he keeps a tally in his head that has no book in it. Nobody in that building has ever asked him for it. Nobody is going to ask him for it this week and nobody has asked him for it in nineteen years and he has not offered it and there is nobody in that lane he could offer it to.** There is one thing at the far end of that floor that has never been anybody's to answer for, and the reason there is one is that it has stood still for two years, and it stood still this week, and nobody has put a hand on the lid of it since the morning the two of them made it chargeable in a lane with nobody in it, which is about three months ago now.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the twelfth month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the fifth hour.

---

He worked the whole of it out standing at the loading end with his hands in his pockets, and it took the length of time a floor takes to go past twice, and he said none of it out loud and there was nobody in that lane to say it to.

About four hundred sheets a year go out of a building in this city with a mark cut in the top edge of them and not one of those four hundred is anybody's. **There is more of that in a year than there is of anything else in this matter that cannot be laid against a person.

There is no form in this empire for reading one out, none for asking whose a mark in a strip was, and none for cutting one. All three of those absences are the same absence, and it is the only thing in this city that has got better at the one thing it does. **Two people in this city could stand in a room and say out loud whose a mark in a strip is, and they cannot do it in the same way, and neither of them is going to be asked. One of them can read it. He is on a bench behind a chain at the end of a lane and he has been able to read a paragraph all his life. The other one cannot read it and never could. He is at a sill in a building off a river road, he has been signing the foot of about nine hundred sheets a year since he was forty-two, and he finds out by hand whether one has been cut into, and he has stopped.** And I am not going to be one of the two and I am not going to be asked either, and I am going to go on putting a name on every thing that comes across this floor and I am not going to be thanked for it this week or any week.

Then he took his hands out of his pockets and went back to the loading end and said one of those out loud, flat, to a floor with nobody standing on it, and he said it the way he says a thing that is going to be true in ten years.

"**I have been writing names onto things on this floor for nineteen years and I have got good at it. That is the one thing this lane has ever been good at, and since a woman put fourpence on a set of boards in the tenth month it has got good at it faster, and it has got good at the half of it that does not say who did the doing, and I have been the one doing it, and nobody is going to thank me for the getting better and nobody is going to thank the floor.**"

---

The young man came the length of that floor at about that hour and stopped about nine foot off him, and he had not been to the end of that floor in about four months, not since the morning he walked away from it on purpose and said he was not going to come back to it, and he came back this week and stood there.

He said nothing at all about a mark in a strip. He said nothing at all about anything. **He has said two true sentences in his life on that floor and both of them were said to one man, and he is not going to say a third one, and nobody at that loading end is going to ask him for it, and about nine feet of floor went by between the two of them the whole time and neither of them put one thing to the other.**

"You have not said anything," the man of about forty-five said, to the floor.

"I have not."

---

"That is not me asking you for it and you know it is not, and I have not said anything either this morning and I have not said one word about that box at the end of this floor in about three months and I am not going to start now that you are standing on this end of it again."

"I know you are not. I have known it since a week that is gone."

"You have come back to this end of this floor and you have done it on purpose, and I have not told you not to, and I want it entered that I did not tell you not to. **Two men in a lane with nothing in it who have both been shown to be the kind of man who keeps a thing to himself is a worse thing than one, and I have had one at my age and I am not going to pretend to you that I have not.** And it is your floor as much as it is mine and you are not going to be thanked for standing on it and I am not going to be thanked for letting you."

The young man did not say that was right. He has said that to nobody. He is not going to say it to anybody.

---

At about the sixth hour a courier came up out of the yard with a satchel on his shoulder. The young man put four sheets in and said nothing at all while he did it, and the courier shouldered it and went up the stairs the way he goes up them.

There is no landing on that stair at which either of them could have told him one word. He cannot be told anything and he asks for nothing. He said one true thing in a stairwell a week that is gone, and there is nobody in that lane who knows he said it.

**Nobody has ever got anything out of that box, and the two men who put it where it stands cannot take it back off themselves, and there was nobody in that lane in the week they did it who could have seen what they were doing or said a word about it.** It is where it has been. It was not opened. It is still chargeable.

Nobody thanked anybody and nobody was sent for and nothing was resolved, and nobody at that loading end was asked anything and nobody was told anything they did not already know. **A knife has been in a drawer at the top of four flights since before the man thinking about it was on a floor anywhere, and it takes about four seconds, and this week it stayed where it is, and he has thought about it and he is not going to do it, and nobody in this lane knew that he had.** A young man came back to the end of that floor after about four months away and stood nine foot off him and said nothing at all, and that is the first time in three months either of them has got nothing, and the arrangement is still standing.
