# Chapter 0496: A Man Who Came Down Four Flights To A Floor He Has No Business On, And Three Men At The End Of It

A man who has been putting names onto things at one end of that floor for nineteen years can tell you this: the floor names people for you, and it does it by handling, and the second time a thing goes past a hand it has got a person on it. That is the whole of the trade and he is the only one on that floor who would say it out loud.

He is about forty-five and the loading end is his, and the count in his head has never once been in a book. Nobody has asked for it in nineteen years, he has never offered it, and there is nobody in that lane he could offer it to. The box at the far end of that floor is the one thing there that has never belonged to anybody's answer, and it is that because it has not moved in two years, and it did not move this week, and no hand has gone near the lid of it since two men made it chargeable in a lane with nobody in it.

It is the second day of the second week of the second month of the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after the year after next, and it is the third hour.

---

The young man was at the end of that floor and had been at the end of that floor about three weeks, which is longer than he has been at the end of it since a week that is gone. He had not said one word there and was not going to.

The man came the length of the floor at about that hour and said something that was not a question and was not for anybody, and then the young man stood with his back half turned for a while.

Then there was a third man at the end of it, and none of the three of them had said one word to each other.

Nothing was said to anybody and nobody turned round and the stretches of floor between them stayed the widths they were, and the box was looked at by nobody while all of that was going on.

He had come down four flights from a room he has lived in for about two years, and he had put nothing in anybody's hands, and he is not on a book anywhere in this city. The nine things that were set out against him at the other end of those boards are the nine things there still, and none of them is in his hands, and there is no heading over his name in any building in this empire. A man with nothing against his name is the only kind of man a search in this city reliably comes back holding.

Marek Kest came about nine foot off the end of that floor and stopped. He did not go any nearer. He did not touch the box, and he did not look at the lid.

The floor took his boots the way it takes the boots of everybody who stands on it, and the stretch between him and the loading end stayed exactly as wide as it was when he arrived, and he stood on it and stayed on it and was not asked to move and did not offer to.

---

"You have come to a floor where nothing is sold," the man of about forty-five said, to the floor, "and you have not come about the work, because the work is not ours. I have been at the other end of it for about four seconds and I have already stopped asking myself why, and I am not going to be thanked for stopping."

"I came to stand at the end of a floor I have never stood at."

"Then stand at the end of it. **And here is the thing I am going to say to the floor and not to you, and I am saying it once. A man who keeps a thing to himself is a number I can work beside. Two of them standing in a lane with nothing else in it is a number I cannot, and it is the worse of the two, and I have had the loading end of this floor for nineteen years and I am not going to stand here and tell you I have never been one at my age. There are three of them now. The third of the three is the only person in this matter that a search in this city reliably comes back holding, and he has come down four flights to stand nine foot off a box he has not opened, and I do not know what that is, and I am not going to be told.**"

---

Nobody said anything for about as long as it takes to square a sheet.

Men stood at the different ends of the floor and nobody crossed the middle of it, and the lid stayed on the box and no hand went anywhere near it at any point of the rest of the morning.

The young man did not turn round. Two sentences is all he has ever said on that floor, both of them to one man, and there is not going to be a third, and nobody is going to ask him for it, and there was a stretch of floor between the two of them the whole of the time that neither of them crossed.

Then the man of about forty-five said the other half of it, and he said it flat.

"**And here is what I am not going to do, and I want it said that I did it on purpose. There is a thing in this matter that could be put in front of you without anybody asking you one word about it. I have known what it is since the week a woman put fourpence on a set of boards four hundred miles away. I could do it in about four seconds, and there is no form anywhere in this empire in which a person who did it could be shown having done it, and that is the whole of why it works. I am not going to do it. I am not going to tell you why I am not going to do it. And if I did tell you, then you would be a person I had told, and that is a worse position than the one you are in now.**"

---

Marek Kest looked at him for about four seconds.

"You have just described a thing that could be put in front of me," he said, "and you have not said what it is, and I have not asked, and nobody in this lane is going to be thanked for the pair of us, and neither of us is going to be entered anywhere."

"No."

"**I am not going to take one, and I am not going to have one made for me, and I am not going to be in a room where somebody says one word about whether I should. I am not going to be thanked for turning down something you never offered, and I am not going to say one word about where I was in the last week of the year. There is a woman at a counter who is now in a position exactly like this one, and she has not been told that, and she is not going to be.**"

"That was not a refusal of anything I made."

"It was not, and I am not going to pretend it was, and I am not going to be thanked for a shape." He took his hands out of his pockets. "**And I want the other half of it said, because I have not said it out loud anywhere and I am saying it here to two men on a floor. For about two years the whole of what has been behind me is that I have never made a decision in front of another person in this matter. That is why a search in this city comes back holding nothing. I have just made one, in front of two of you, and it is the first one, and I have said it out loud, and neither of you is going to be thanked and I am not either.**"

---

He went back up four flights. He did not look at the box on the way out and he did not touch the lid of it, and he did not ask the young man one thing. The young man did not turn round and did not say anything at all.

The four flights took him up at the rate they take anybody up them and the door at the bottom of them was where it is and nobody was at the bottom of them, and the floor he had come down went on being a floor with men left at the ends of it.

The box at the end of that floor is still chargeable, and it was not opened this week, and nobody has put a hand on the lid of it. There are now three men in this matter who could be produced in three different rooms and who could not produce each other if they were asked. Nothing has been written down anywhere. Nobody was sent for. The third of them is the cheapest man in the city, and the only one of the three who has ever made a decision with another person in the room while it was being made.

The box was not opened and the third man went back up four flights.
