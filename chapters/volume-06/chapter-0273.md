# Chapter 273: A Hearing Room At The End Of A Passage That Smells Of Cold Stone, A Rail About Four Feet High, A Copy Of A Notice Of Six Numbered Lines, And The First Opinion A Man Of About Thirty Has Known He Was Having For Four Years

The cold comes up off the flags of a passage about four hundred yards long and it ends in a room with a rail in it that stands about four feet off the floor. The room he is in most of the time is at the other end of that passage, downstairs, with a shelf in it and a window about two feet wide and a pane out of it, and that is where he works, and he has been in this room four years.

He is about thirty and he has been in that building four years, and three of the other clerks are the woman of about fifty-five and the woman of about twenty-six, and for four years he has been the one of the three that nobody sends anything to.

This is the fourth day of the third week of the fifth month of the year after the year after next, and it is about the fifth hour, and the passage is colder than the yard it comes out of and always is.

---

**He has been up that passage about nine times in four years and this is the fifth time in four years that he has been in that room.**

The other four were to carry a stack. A rail is a thing a person leans on and the rest of that room is a table and two chairs, and about nine hundred sheets a year go through the room the other end of the passage and not one of them comes in here, and he has worked that out in about four minutes on the first afternoon and has not needed it again since.

He had it the other way round for four years and the other way round was comfortable. He had it that nobody wanted him in that room, and a man nobody wants in a room is a man who has not been put anywhere.

---

Being unasked is not the same as being left alone, and he has had them the same way round for four years, and it took him about four minutes at the rail.

**A person who is not asked is a person nobody has spent anything on.** A person who is left alone is a person whose leaving would cost nothing. Those are two different facts about the same four years and one of them is a wage and one of them is a room, and he has been being paid a wage about four years by a building that does not need him to be in it, and that is not a complaint and it is not a discovery either.

He has not been put anywhere. Nobody in that building has spent a thing on him, and nobody has been able to, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that says a clerk is to be put somewhere.

---

He put the copy down on the table on the wrong side of the rail and squared it, because that is what a person does with a thing they have been handed.

It is a copy. The thing itself is lying on a bench about four hundred and thirty miles down this river with a seal at the foot of it and nothing under the seal. Six numbered lines. Line two asks that no inference of any kind be drawn from the absence of any name. Line six declines to name whoever holds the office at all.

He can read it. He has read it about four times in four years because it was on a shelf on the ground floor and he is the one who puts stacks back, and he has never had a reason to read it four times.

"I am not going to say who that is," he said, to the room, at about the fifth hour. "**I want it written down that I did not say it and that I know what I am not saying, and I have known what I am not saying since before I came here.**"

---

And that is the whole of the four minutes, and it is not what he went up for.

**He asked a question out loud, in a room, of nobody.** Not of the woman of twenty-six, who was not in it. Not of the woman of fifty-five, who is eight feet of passage and a shut door away and has not asked him one thing in four years and would not have stopped him.

"Who is that for?"

The rail is about four feet high and the room is about as cold as the passage and there is nobody in it, and the room did not answer, and it is going to go on not answering, and there is nothing whatever at the end of that question that can come back and find him.

**A question asked of nobody is in nobody.** Not in a man, not in a rail, not in a minute, not in a head. He worked that out standing at a table in about four minutes and it is the first opinion he has known he was having in four years, and he did not have to decide to have it, and he has not been asked whether he meant it, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that would let anybody ask him that either.

---

And the other half of it, and the half that took the other four minutes, standing at the wrong side of a rail in a room that smells of nothing at all.

He is not a wall either. A rail is the only thing in that room that does not know which side anybody is standing on, and he has leaned on both sides of it in four years, once on each, and a person who can be on either side of a thing in a room is a person who is not being held by it.

**A wall keeps things out and he has been leaning on this one from the inside, and so has the man of sixty-one, and so has nobody, because there is nothing on either side of it that anybody in this building has ever been shown.**

---

Nobody is to blame and he is not going to name anybody, and he said that out loud too, at about the sixth hour, to the same empty room, and it took about four seconds.

"It is not the woman's doing and it is not the other woman's doing and it is not anybody's doing. **A thing that nobody decided and that goes on working is not a thing a person did to me, and the only person it is being done to is me, and I have had it four years and about nine weeks of it I thought I was being kept out of a room.**"

He said the second half of that to a rail and not to a person, and the rail is about four feet high, and he put his hand flat on it and it was cold, and he took it off again.

---

Nothing was asked anybody. The count of askings in this matter is six and it was six nine weeks ago and it is six now, and it is not his number and it is not in that building, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that says a man is told a count.

He went back down the passage with the copy under his arm and put it on the shelf in the second of the eleven books, second shelf down, where it was, and did not tell the woman of fifty-five that he had been in that room and did not tell her that he had asked a question of nobody, and she was at the window with the pane out of it and did not turn round and did not ask.

A girl of seventeen at the back of a bay about nine miles off this river is on a written engagement at the rate the list is set at, and she is not spoken to, and she was not thanked, and nobody is going to send for her.

The rail is still in that room. The count is still six. And a man of about thirty has an opinion for the first time in four years and has not told anybody, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that would let anybody ask him whether he meant it, and he is not going to say it out loud twice.
