# Chapter 255: A Chain On A Man, A Cut On A Wall That Is Nobody's, And A Reason Read Out Loud Where It Cannot Come Back

The tool shed is at the Slade end and it is about nine foot by eleven, with a bench along the north wall and a lamp in it that is not lit until about the seventh hour. It is about four hundred yards from a bay in which nine men work, and that is the whole of the distance between the only person in this empire you can say anything to and a bay full of men who can hear every word of it.

He is about fifty-five and he has a chain, and the chain is on him, and he cannot read a paragraph.

It is the second day of the first week of the first month of the year after the year after next, and it is about the sixth hour, and the lamp is not lit, and he has about an hour.

---

There is a cut in the wall at the height of his chest where he used to sit, and it is a cross with a bar through it, and it was cut by a man who worked in this shed nine years ago and who is dead, and it is not a mark anybody uses and it is not about anything and it belongs to nobody who is alive. It is not about the chain either. He has looked at it about four hundred times and he has never once been able to work out why a man who cannot read a paragraph would cut a mark like that, and he has never asked, because there is nobody left who was here when it was cut.

He has four seconds of a bay a week. That is what he is at the bay for, four seconds a week, at a time agreed with a foreman about nine years ago, and he has never been paid for any of it and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that pays a woman for knowing where a man is standing.

---

He is not one of the four, and he has said so out loud in a yard and in a bay and in a shed in nine years, and it is the standing sentence he has.

There are four in that bay who cannot read a paragraph and he knows all four and he has not asked one of them one thing in nine years and he is not going to start in a shed at the Slade end at the sixth hour on a wet afternoon. He has said why, in about four sentences, about four times, and it is that **writing him down as one of them would be a way for a list of four to go wrong.** There are four, and there are going to be four, and that is the whole of what he can do about it. If there were five then the four would be a class, and a class is a heading, and a heading is how a person becomes a company, and he is a person and he is fifty-five and he has a chain and he is not going to be the reason that happens to four men he works beside.

He works out loud to himself as he oils things, and the working out is the nearest thing to a book he has got, and it is about four seconds a week in a bay and every day of every week in a shed about nine foot by eleven.

He cannot read a paragraph, and he can add, and he has taught himself to count by hand in nine years, and he can carry a number in his head about as far as four hundred and not one step further.

He is also the only man on that bank who can be told a thing and have it not come back, and he has known that for nine years and has never once tried to make money out of it. Three people in this matter have tried to make use of it in about four years. Two of them found out that it is not a service. The third has not found out yet and is going to find out the same way, in about four minutes, and a man who is not findable is a man nobody can ask.

A man who cannot read a paragraph can be told a thing about a man who can read a paragraph, and there is no version of that in which the man with the chain is any use afterwards except as the place the thing was said. That is not a power. It is a hole. He has said the word *hole* out loud in a shed once, to himself, in the dark, about six weeks ago, and he has not said it since, and it is the truest word he owns.

---

She came in at about the sixth hour and did not shut the door, and it is a foreman of fifty-one and she has been behind a bench in a bay four hundred yards away for nine years and she has been in a box since the first week of the seventh month of the year after and nobody has asked her about it.

She had a coat on and she took it off and hung it on the nail by the door, which is a nail and not a hook, and she put her hands on the bench and stood there.

"I want to ask you to do a thing," she said, "and I am going to pay you for it, and I have thought about it for about four days and I am going to say all of that before you answer so that you know what you are being asked into."

---

He did not answer that for about four seconds, which is the whole of the time he gets in a bay in a week, and he gave her the four seconds here as well because nobody was counting and because about four seconds is what a person needs to be sure of a thing.

"I am not going to say no straight off and I want you to know what I am doing."

"All right."

"I am going to work out whether a man who is paid to read is a man who has been bought or a man who has been hired, and I have worked it out about nine times over nine years and I get the same answer every time, and I am going to say it in full before I say no."

"Go on."

"There is a hold on that cut that has to be read out loud to the whole of that bay, and reading it takes about four minutes, and a girl of seventeen at the back of that bay is on a written engagement at the rate the list is set at, and there are two people on that bank who could do it and one of them is seventeen." She did not sit down. "**A reader's day is between fourpence and a shilling in this empire and I have got that in my coat and I am offering you the top of it and I am offering it out loud in a shed because I have worked out that you are the only person I can say it to.**"

---

**And he said the thing, and he said it in one go without stopping, and it is the whole of why he has had four seconds a week in a bay for nine years and has not once used them for money.**

"I am not going to do it, and the reason is not that I cannot read a paragraph. A man who cannot read a paragraph can read four minutes out loud, and I have done about four of them, in about nine years, and every one of them was for a man who is dead. The reason is that a person who is paid to read is a person who can be told to stop." He put the rag down on the bench. "That is the whole of it. You pay a man to read and then one day there is a person in a room who says not this week, and he stops, and nobody can say afterwards that he stopped for a reason, because the reason is in the room and the room is not written down. And then a woman of fifty-one who was in a box is out of it, or a girl of seventeen is not on a list any more, or nine men know a thing they did not know, and none of it can be undone by anybody because the only evidence is a man who was paid."

---

She did not argue with it, and that was the thing he had expected and had got ready for and had not got.

"That is a better reason than I have got, and I have been trying for about four days to get at whether it is."

"It is not better and I have not got a better one either, and I have had mine for nine years and you have had yours for four days, and they are not the same age, and mine has never once been about money."

She looked at the bench for about four seconds.

"Then I am not going to offer it again," she said, "and I am not going to tell anybody I offered it, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that says a foreman has to report a thing she asked a man to do in a shed. **I have said it out loud to a man who cannot read a paragraph because it does not come back, and I worked out about four days ago that that is the reason and that I am not going to pretend to myself that it is the other reason.**"

He did not take that as a compliment and she did not offer it as one.

---

He let about four minutes go by before he said the other thing, and he said it because she had given him the four minutes and because there was nobody in the world he could say it to but her.

"There is a man comes to that bay. About four miles on foot. He has got a hand that writes figures out fair and he is on that list four times a season and about five weeks ago he asked you a question out loud in front of about nine men and he said in the doorway afterwards that he was not going to do it again." He did not look at her while he said it. "I have watched that man since and I have not seen him do it again and I do not think he is going to. And I want you to know that I have said that to a foreman, and that a foreman is a person who can be served, and that there is not one form anywhere in this empire for asking a person whether they will mind being told something."

---

She was quiet for about a minute, and then she said the thing back, and she said it out loud, and that is the whole of the cost of the evening and neither of them could stop it.

"A reason that has been read out loud is a worse kind of findable than a name," she said. "I have known that since a yard in the fourth week of the ninth month of the year after next and I have said it out loud in a bay, in front of everybody in it, once, and that was the last time there was anybody in this matter who needed to hear it." She put her hands flat on the bench. "**And I am about to do the same thing in a room with a rail in it nine miles off, because I have found a thing out and a person who has found a thing out is findable by having found it, and I would rather be findable than have not said it, and I would like it noticed that I have said that out loud to you first.**"

"Then you have made yourself worse and you have done it on purpose."

"I have made myself worse and I have done it on purpose and I have told one person about four days before I do it, and there is no form anywhere that requires me to and there is not going to be one, and the one person I told is a man who cannot read a paragraph, which means I have told nobody, and I have worked out that that is the correct use of him and I am not going to apologise for it."

---

She picked the coat up off the nail at about the seventh hour and did not put it on.

"Ask me nothing in a room," she said, "and do not tell anybody I came, and do not tell anybody I asked, and I know both of those are things you are now holding about a person and not about yourself."

"I am holding them about a person who is findable and about a thing she is going to do, and I have not got a form for either, and I am not going to make one up at six o'clock in a shed."

**And that is the whole of the price of the evening on his side, and it is smaller than hers and he did not ask for it and he cannot give it back, and he has not been thanked and is not going to be, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be thanked for a thing they were not asked to do.**

The lamp is not lit until about the seventh hour and they worked for about forty minutes after that in the light of it, and three feet away there is a cut in a wall that a man made who is dead and it is nobody's.

She did not say anything else about the hold and he did not say anything else about the four, and neither of them mentioned the four, because there is nothing to be said about the four in a shed and there is a finding in a book four hundred yards up that road which is not his and is not hers and neither of them improved it and neither of them will.

Nobody thanked him. A foreman of fifty-one did not thank a man of about fifty-five with a chain for telling her no, and he did not want her to, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be thanked for a refusal.

Four men in a bay four hundred yards away cannot read a paragraph and have still never been asked one question. **A season has closed and nobody decided that and nothing was resolved.** And there is a man in a shed who has said a true thing out loud to a person who can be found, and he is not findable, and that is the only reason he could say it, and the whole of the finding of this evening is that the only thing protecting anybody in this matter is a hole in them.
