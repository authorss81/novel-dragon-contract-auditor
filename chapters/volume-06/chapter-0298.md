# Chapter 298: The Number Is Not Six, And The Bill Is Not His, And He Cannot Pay It

The room over the chandler's shop has a table in it and a landing outside the door and four steps up to the landing, and a window about a foot wide, and about four feet of wall by that window with nothing on it. Downstairs the shop keeps tar and rope and lamp oil and the smell comes up between the boards all day, and in this month there is less of it than there is in the spring. He has been up and down those steps about nine times a day since he was twenty-three.

He is thirty-four or thereabouts and he is not standing in anybody's room this week. He has a house with a door on it and a wife and there is a second asking about a week old and it was four words long and it is in three people.

This is the fourth day of the fourth week of the eleventh month of the year after the year after next, and it is about the sixth hour, and the light has gone off the lane.

---

**He said it out loud, once, at about the sixth hour, to the room, and it is the first time in about eleven years he has said a figure out loud in a room where there was nobody in it, and it is the last time he is going to say it, and the number is not six.**

A count of things done and not a count of people. It did not move for about a hundred and forty years and it moved twice inside about a year, and the second time it moved it was him, and it does not care that it was him.

**A count that counted first askings only would be a count of people and not a count of askings, and a practice with two people in it is stopped by nobody, and the whole of the reason he stood in that room is in the second half of that sentence and not in the first.**

He heard the figure at second hand about forty-eight weeks ago and carried it from then until the fourth day of the third week of this month without saying it out loud to anybody once, and he has said it once, and there is no form in this empire that would let a man be handed a count of anything, and he handed it to himself and he is not going to do that again either.

---

And the rest of the week has been the ordinary week, and the ordinary week is where the price of it is.

He is findable. That is the whole of the cost of a second asking and it is the only part of it anybody can put a name on, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that puts a name on a man being findable, and the finding is a week old and it is the finding and not the asking that is the thing that cannot be taken back.

A clerk of about fifty-five in a room with a rail in it knows his face. A woman of about forty who has gone up a cold passage about four hundred yards long for about nine years knows his face. A building off the old river road has a step worn in front of the door and a woman of about thirty-five next door who saw a man standing at that step at about the sixth hour on the fourth day of the fourth week of the tenth month, and nobody has asked her anything and nobody is going to.

And none of that is the price. **The price is that a man who can be found can be sent for, and there is no form in this empire that would let anybody send him, and so there is no form that would stop anybody trying, and he has spent about eleven years being a man nothing can be held to and about a week giving the last of it away on purpose.**

---

And then he got to the part that took the rest of the hour, and it took him about four minutes, and it is the finding and it is the reason he is not going to be able to put it down.

The bill is not his.

He has a trade he can be refused and a room taken by the week and a house with a door on it and about four days a season on somebody's list, and there is no fund behind any part of it and there is no office and there is no paper anywhere in this empire with his name at the foot of anything except a line he ruled in a column in a day-book four miles off.

**A man who is findable is a man somebody can be made answerable to, and nobody in this matter would be made answerable by him being findable.** The only person a thing like this falls on is a person already standing in the gap, and the only people who stand in gaps in this matter are people who have nothing. About four hundred yards off and about nine miles off and about four hundred and thirty miles off there are three of them. He does not know which one it is going to be, and it is not going to be him.

He has not got a name for it and he is not going to go and find one out, and a man who goes and finds out what came of a thing he has done is a man who has been findable by that thing.

---

She came up about four steps at about the seventh hour with a plate in her hand, which is the second time since the first asking she has come up them, and she put it on the table and did not sit down.

About four steps and about a plate and about two years of a woman not sitting down at a table when she has come up with something in her hand, and the table has nothing on it at either end and the landing outside the door is about the width of two people.

"Are you going to tell me how it went?"

"No."

"Then I am not going to ask," she said, "and I want you to notice that I have said that out loud, and that there is nothing in this empire that would have made me ask you and nothing in it that would have let you tell me, and that we are two people in a room doing the thing we do."

"You are not going to ask me."

"No. And you are not going to tell me, and if you told me I would not thank you for it, and about a week ago I told you the whole of that and you did not thank me for that either, and I have not been thanked for it and I am not going to be."

---

He did not tell her, and she did not ask, and the not telling is not a withholding and there is no form in this empire that would let a person be told what a thing they have done is going to be used for, and the not asking is not a coldness, and neither of them is going to be thanked for either.

He has a figure in a column four miles off that came back under a line he ruled in the first week of the seventh month, in a hand that is not his and is not the old man's, and it is going to be added to and carried forward and be correct, and he does not know what came of it and there is no way whatever to find out, and that is the road being walked zero times.

Four miles off and about four hundred sheets a year and about nine hundred rooms, and a figure that is correct is the only kind of figure in this empire nobody has ever come back and asked about, and the finding cost him an afternoon nobody paid for and nine miles of road.

About four hundred and thirty miles down this river a foreman of fifty-one is still in a box in the first week of the seventh month of the year after, and a woman of twenty about nine miles off a building off the old river road is in a box, and the form that put them there has not been asked to be void by anybody and there is no procedure for it and never has been.

About nineteen people are still behind four hundred and forty foot of bank, and nobody has asked them again. A bill at Lowcross stands at nineteen pounds three and fourpence and is unpaid and nobody is liable. A girl of seventeen at the back of a bay on a written engagement at the rate the list is set at has not been thanked and is not going to be. About four men in that bay have never been asked one question, and were not asked one this week either.

Four hundred and forty feet of bank, with children inside it and about nineteen people behind it, and a bill nobody is liable for, and not one of the three is his and not one of the three has been paid, and a very long way of river between him and every one of them.

He put a match to the wick himself at about the ninth hour, which is a thing he does about four times in a season, and the lamp is the only thing in that room anybody pays for, and the count of what he has done this season is a figure in a column nobody is keeping, and he is not going to be handed it.
