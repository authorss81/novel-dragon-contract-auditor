# Chapter 254: A Day On Which Nothing Is Asked, And A Woman Of Twenty-Four Who Is Very Good At It

The room is rented by the week and it is two streets off a street that goes down to the river road, and there is a table in it and a lamp on the table and a board on the wall and a window that faces the back of another house, and about nine miles off in the other direction there is a counter at the top of a stair where she has stood every working day for two years.

She is twenty-four and she is a clerk at the fourth counter of the Notaries' Table in Auremar, and she is not a lawyer, and the room is where she keeps a book of her own that nobody knows about and that has no office on it.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the twelfth month of the year after next, and it is about the seventh hour, and the lamp is lit, and the day is over.

---

Nobody asked her one thing.

About nine people came up that stair and about four of them wanted something out of her and got it. A man wanted a copy and she made it and he paid fourpence and said thank you. A woman wanted to know whether a copy would stand if the thing it came from were lost, and it would not, and she said so without softening it and without charging her, and the woman went away without a copy and without an argument.

A boy of about fifteen wanted to know whether he could buy a blank and leave it, which he can, and she told him so, and he said his master had told him he had to hand it in filled, and she said then your master is wrong.

And a man of about thirty came up the stair, stood at the counter for about eleven minutes, and went down again without saying one word. The woman of twenty-four at the other end of the counter said afterwards that he had done the same thing and that he had done it on four days in the last month and that not one of the four had ever been spoken to. A person who stands at a counter is not a person who has come to a counter, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that says a clerk goes after anybody.

And not one person asked her one thing that was not a request. That is the ordinary shape of that counter on an ordinary day, and she is extremely good at it, and she has been extremely good at it for about two years.

She was in the room on the fourth day of the third week of the twelfth month of the year after next when a man of about forty-five came up that stair and asked the clerk of thirty-four a question about a line of writing on a wall behind the counter.

She was at the other end of the counter and she heard all of it. It took about nine minutes. The clerk said the shape of the question out loud before she answered it, which is a thing this woman has watched about nine hundred times. The man asked what the fourth line on the board was for, and the clerk said she did not know, and then the clerk told him the whole of the thing about a heading and a class and a company, out loud, in a room with four people in it. The man listened, and then he said he was not going to do anything about it.

**Nobody asked her one question about any of it, before it, during it or after it, and the clerk of thirty-four did not turn round, and the man did not look at her, and she has gone over that about nine times since and it is the day she counts from.**

She is not angry with either of them. She has worked out that being angry would be a way of asking somebody to have done it differently, and there is no form in this empire for a person to be asked whether they would mind doing a thing differently.

---

The book of her own is on the table and it is a book and not a copy of anything, and one page of it is ruled by hand in her own hand, and on that page there are two boxes.

Both of the boxes have the same name in them.

She has not ruled a third and she is not going to, and when she has been asked — once, by a clerk of thirty-four who asks the shape of a question out loud before she asks it — she gave a reason that did not say what the boxes are for, and that reason has held for about ten weeks and is going to hold.

"There is a day when I want to rule a third," she said, "and it has not been one of them, and I am not going to tell you how many there are going to be, and that is not a mystery. It is a private arrangement and there is not one form in this empire that lets a person ask about a private arrangement."

---

**And the two things that require her in any room he is in are two things, and she has never once put them together, and she is not going to.**

One is a paper that puts her in his rooms. It is dated and it has her name on it and it is a document and it can be produced.

The other is a watch, and the watch requires a person who is not from the office that cannot be named to be in any room he is in, and she is that person, and the watch has no name on it and no seal and no place it can be sent to, and it cannot be produced because there is nothing to produce.

She has about nine times in about four years gone over the two of them and worked out what would happen if they were one thing, and the answer is that about four hundred and thirty miles of road would come with them, and there is no fund behind that and no form for a fund anywhere in this empire, and nobody has ever been paid for satisfying a watch and nobody ever will be.

Nobody has ever asked her whether she would rather be somewhere else, and that is not a complaint and it is not a request, and she has not made one, and there is a rule about that from the twelfth month of last year which says that if she uses it then it needs a reason, and she has not used it, and a correct non-use is as good as a use.

She came close to putting the three of them in one sentence at about the seventh hour on this night and she caught herself doing it, and the catching took about four seconds, and she wants the catching written down because it is the only work she has done tonight that she is sure of.

The three of them are three of them. The paper is a document and the watch is not a document and the page in this book is in a book of her own with no office on it. If those three are ever in one sentence then there is a thing that requires her, and a thing that requires her can be served, and being served is a party, and a party is the only person who can be told what a thing is going to be used for. **She has not ruled a third box because the day she rules a third box is the day one of the three becomes the reason for the other two, and she does not know which one it would be, and a person who does not know which one it would be does not rule it.**

---

Then she did the thing she does on about four nights in ten, which is to sit at the table and find out what she is, in her own mouth, alone, about the seventh hour, with the lamp lit.

She had not expected to find anything and she found something in about nine minutes, and what she found was that being asked nothing is not the same thing as being left alone.

"Do you know what I have got?"

"You have told me three times tonight and I have not said no to any of them."

"A person who is not asked has not been given a duty. I have known that since about the seventh month of next year and I said it out loud in a room at that counter about ten weeks ago and I did not know until about four minutes after somebody asked me one question about a line of writing on a wall what it applied to. And it has never once applied to me, and I did not know that it did not apply to me, and I have worked out tonight why, and the reason is that the protection is the reason."

---

"A person in this empire is a thing it can only send papers to, and a company is a thing it can hold wages against and give letters to and take to law, and a person is a thing it can only send papers to, and the difference between those two is a heading." She put her hands flat on the open book, not on either box. "A question is the only thing in this empire that makes a person answerable. Nobody has ever asked me a question, and I have worked out that the reason nobody has ever asked me a question is that a clerk who advises nobody is a person nobody needs, and a person nobody needs cannot be made answerable to anything."

"So the not being asked is a wall."

"It is a wall and it is not one I built and it is not one I can take down, and I have been standing behind it for about two years and calling it being left alone, and it is not that. Being left alone is a thing somebody does to you. **A wall is a thing you get from what you are, and I have got one, and it is the only thing in my life that nobody has ever been able to take off me.**"

---

And then the second half of it, which is the half she does not like, and she said it anyway, and there was nobody in the room to say it to but a table and a lamp.

"I have wanted to be asked every working day since about the seventh month of next year, and I have been asked twice, and one of the two was a woman at my own counter about a line of writing and she did not charge me a shilling for it." She turned the page over and did not rule anything on the back of it. "**And the whole of what I have got is that nobody can make me answer for anything, and I have spent about two years being proud of that, and she has heard, second hand and out of a bay, that a foreman of fifty-one has decided to stop being glad of a thing nobody can check, and it turns out that is not only a thing foremen have.**"

She had been the price of a party out loud about ten times in four years and nobody had thanked her once, and she had not expected to be thanked and would like it written down somewhere that she did not, because in about four years somebody is going to read that a woman of twenty-four stood in a room and said a thing about a man and be paid for it, and they are going to assume somebody heard.

Nobody heard. That is the finding and it is the whole of it, and she has never said it out loud before tonight and she is not going to say it out loud again for about a month.

She did not cry and she did not put her hand over either box, and she looked at the two of them for about four minutes and she did not count them again, because she has counted them about nine hundred times and she knows the number and the number is not the thing.

---

There is a rule from the fourth of the first month that she made for herself and it is live and it has not been retired and it has not been amended, and tonight she looked at it for about four minutes and she did not exercise it, and the reason is a reason and not a shrug.

It needs something in the room to act on, and there was nothing in the room to act on, and a thing exercised without a reason stops being hers about four days after it stops needing one. She has watched about nine people in this city make a thing out of a habit in her time at that counter and every one of them ended up with a heading over it, and she is not going to be the tenth thing that happens to a room in this city.

There are four of them in the drawer under the table and not one has been opened, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that says a person has to send one of them, and she looked at the drawer and shut it.

---

The lamp was lit and the window was black and about four miles off somebody's dog went and stopped.

She had thought, on the four days before this one, that she would ask somebody one thing herself. She had the thing in the same place she keeps everything, and she had it ready, and what stopped her was that there is no version of asking in which a clerk of two years at a counter is a person asking and not a clerk collecting, and a clerk collecting can be told to stop and a person asking cannot. She is not going to find out which one she would be.

**Nobody was asked anything on the fourth day of the fourth week of the twelfth month of the year after next, and she was not asked anything on the four days before it either, and she is not going to be asked anything this week, and there is no form in this empire that requires a person to be asked and there is not going to be one.** A woman of twenty-four is sitting at a table with two boxes on a page ruled by hand and both of them have the same name in them, and she has not ruled a third. About four hundred and thirty miles of road is what it would cost anybody who wanted to see the rest of it, and there is no fund, and the road is not being walked. She is not waiting to be rescued and is not going to be, and nobody is going to thank her for a wall.
