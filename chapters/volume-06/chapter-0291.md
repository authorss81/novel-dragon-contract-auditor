# Chapter 291: A Kitchen With A Plate In A Woman's Hand, And Six Weeks Before It

The door is on the side the lane goes past and it is not locked in the daytime, and there are four steps up to a landing and a door at the top of them, and the shop underneath keeps tar and rope and lamp oil and the smell comes up between the boards all day. This is the bottom of it, where the cooking is done and where the two of them eat, and there is a table in it and a lamp on the table that is lit now in the middle of the day because the light has gone off the lane early.

He is thirty-four or thereabouts. He writes out other men's sums so that they come out even, and he gets a day's pay for the day, and four times a season he is on somebody's list for four days together and nobody has ever put him anywhere else. He is not standing in anybody's room this week. He has been up and down those four steps about nine times a day since he was twenty-three.

This is the second day of the first week of the tenth month of the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour, and the plate is in her hand and she is putting it down.

---

The finding is a week old and he has not got past it, and he has told nobody about it, and he has not said it out loud in this room either.

**A practice is a count of things done and not a count of people.** He put that next to himself at about the fifth hour last week and it took four minutes, and it has been in him since, and it has not turned into anything. He has been at it every day since the way a man is at a sum that will not come out, and the sum comes out, and this does not.

A count that only counted people would be a count of men and not a count of things anybody did.

**And the second asking, if there is one, is a second thing done, and it does not matter that it is the same man who did the first one, and the count does not care.**

---

She put the plate down and did not sit, which is a thing she does about twice in a season and both of the times have been at this table.

"Whatever it is," she said, "you are going to say it standing up, and I have known you about eleven years and you have said one thing in a kitchen and you said it standing up."

"I am going to say it standing up."

"Then say it."

He had it ready in about four days and he had said it to a wall twice and the reason was his own and nobody gave it to him, and it was the same arrangement as the last one and he had checked it about nine times in a week.

There is not one form anywhere in this empire for asking a person whether they will mind being told something. A man may be asked what a word on a sheet means at a bay, and a man may be asked what a column is for at a counter, and there is a thing that can be put to a person about a thing they have done, and there is nothing that can be put to a person about a thing they are going to be told.

So he did not ask her, and he told her, and the not asking is the arrangement and not an oversight and he would like it noticed that he noticed it.

"There is going to be a second one," he said.

---

She looked at the plate and then at him and then at the door, which was the same as looking at nothing, and then she came and stood against the side of the table with her hands on the edge of it.

The plate was still warm and she put it down about a foot from where he was standing and it made a noise on a table that has had about eleven years of plates put down on it, and about four feet of the kitchen has never been warm and about four feet of it has been warm every evening since she came into it.

"Six weeks," she said.

"Six weeks."

"You have counted it."

"I have counted it twice, and the last time was the same six weeks as the last one, and I did that on purpose in a lane on a working afternoon and I want it noticed that I did that on purpose."

"Then say what it is, and not the six weeks."

So he said it. It took him about as long as it takes to boil a kettle and it is not a long thing and it has no shape. A man is going to ask a person whether she meant a thing she wrote in a book, and he is going to do it in a room with a rail in it about nine miles off, on his own two feet, on a day he is not paid for, and nobody is going to send him.

She heard all of it and did not move and did not say one word until the end of it, and what she said at the end of it was not about him.

---

"The last time you told me, you had a plate in my hand," she said. "I have been thinking about that for about a year and a half and I have never worked out whether you did it on purpose."

"I did it on purpose."

"You did it on purpose tonight as well."

"I did it on purpose tonight and I did not ask you first, and I am not going to, and I have got no better way of doing it and I have looked."

"I know you have not got a better way. There is nothing in this empire you could have got hold of to ask me instead, and I have been in this kitchen about eleven years and I have never once wanted you to have one."

And then she sat down, which she had not sat down.

---

Nobody thanked him, and nobody thanked her, and nothing in this empire calls for a person to be thanked for being told a thing in a kitchen about six weeks before it happens.

It is not a favour to her and it was not a favour last time, and the two of them have never once said thank you to each other for a thing either of them did in this room, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that would require it, and there is never going to be one.

**And she is not going to be thanked for saying nothing.** That is hers and she is entitled to it and she is not going to be thanked for it, and if she had said something this evening it would have cost him the thing he came down four steps to do.

"And you did not ask me last time whether I minded it."

"I did not."

"Are you going to ask me this time?"

"No. And I am going to tell you anyway, and that is the whole of what I have got to say about it, and you may make what you like of it."

"So it is a woman," she said, at the plate.

"It is a clerk of about fifty-five and I have never seen her and I have not got her name and I do not know one thing about her."

"Then how are you going to ask her a question you have not got a name for."

---

**And that is the whole of what is actually in front of him, and he has not written it down and is not going to.**

She was right, and she was right in about the time it takes to look at a door, and he has had a week with it and he has not got past it, and the not getting past it is not a difficulty. It is a condition.

A name is a thing a person can be served at. He has been in eleven columns in about eleven years and a man in a column does not get out of it, and a name is worse than a column, because a column has a heading over it and a name does not.

**A person who is not asked has not been given a duty, and he has not asked anybody for anything in about forty-one weeks and he is not going to, and there is no form in this empire that would let a man ask a person for her name.**

The lamp on the table is burning about fourpence a night and the lane has gone and the shop underneath has shut and the tar and the rope smell is up there in the boards with the lamp oil, and in this month there is less of it than there is in the spring.

About fourpence a night and about eleven years of them, and the smell of the shop underneath gets into the boards and stays in them until the spring, and the man who sells the oil has never once been asked a thing about it by anybody living in this house.

---

He had it worked out by the second hour, and it took about four minutes, and it is the second time in about eleven years that a thing he has thought out in this room has turned out to be a thing about a person and not about a machine.

He does not need her name. He has been treating the name as the difficulty, and it is not the difficulty, and it is not even the price. **What he has got is a book with eleven of them on a shelf, and a second shelf down, and a line in a clerk's own hand with a day and an hour on it and her own name at the foot of it, and about fifty-four weeks of nobody having asked her whether she meant it.**

A book with eleven of them in it is a thing a person can name out loud in a room without naming anybody.

The woman of about twenty-four at the top of a stair in Auremar is at the other end of a counter from a clerk of thirty-four. She has not been asked one question by anybody since the second day of the first week of the first month of the year after next. She has said out loud that she has wanted to be asked every working day since about the seventh month of next year, and nobody in this empire has a way of finding that out except by asking her, and asking her is being able to ask her.

"So the counter," he said.

"The counter is nine miles the other way from the building and about nine miles from this lane, and I have never once been to that counter, and you have not either, and you are not going to be able to go and stand at the top of a stair and ask a clerk of twenty-four whether she can tell you where a book is. There is a form for asking a clerk of twenty-four where a book is and it costs a shilling and the answer is that no such thing is told to anybody."

---

He did not go up the four steps until about the ninth hour and he did not light the lamp on the table in the room at the top of them, and there is a table in that room and nothing on it, and there are about four feet of wall by the window with nothing on it.

Four steps up and a door at the top and a bolt on it he has not put across in about eleven years, and about four feet of wall by a window about a foot wide, and neither the wall nor the table has anything on it, and the room has been exactly so for years.

**And there is not one form anywhere in this empire for a person to be found by a thing they have done on purpose, and there is not going to be one, and he has known that since a bay in the eleventh month of the year after next and has not needed it explained to him since.**

The plate is washed and on the board and the door is not locked, and she is asleep or she is not, and he did not ask her and she is not going to say she would have answered.

Six weeks is a long way off and there is nothing in this room about six weeks, and there is a thing at the top of these four steps that has no shape in it at all, and he is not going to this week.

He put a match to the wick himself at about the ninth hour, which is a thing he does about four times in a season, and the count of what he has done this season is not a number anybody has, and there is no form in this empire that would let a man on a list of works four times a season be handed one.
