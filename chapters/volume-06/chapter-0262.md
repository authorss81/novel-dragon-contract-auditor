# Chapter 262: Eleven Books On A Shelf, A Window With A Pane Out Of It, And A Hand At The Foot Of A Sheet That She Has Been Entering For Nineteen Years Without Once Looking At

The office keeps eleven books on a shelf in a room on the ground floor of a building off the old river road, and the room has a window about two feet wide with a pane out of it, and at the other end of a passage that smells of cold stone whatever the season is there is a hearing room with a rail in it.

She is about fifty-five and she has been a clerk here nineteen years and she writes the minutes of this office and advises nobody and asks nothing, and she has never once been asked a question by anybody who was not a person who wanted something out of her.

It is the second day of the fourth week of the second month of the year after the year after next. There are three clerks in this building and she is the oldest, and the other two are a man of about thirty who has been in that room four years and a woman of about twenty-six who came in about four years ago and is not in this matter and has not been in it.

---

A man of about forty came in at about the fourth hour with a sheet he had been handed, and he wanted to know what a word in the body of it meant, and she told him she could not tell him that and told him why, and he said thank you, and she said that was the ordinary way and not a favour, and he went out and did not come back.

And then she did the thing she has not done in nineteen years, which took her about four seconds and is the whole of this day.

She put her hand flat on the last page and turned back until she was at the foot of the one he had left, and she looked at the foot of it.

There is a hand at the foot of every one of them. It is their own hand, and it answers for everything above it, and it is the part of the page that is not the entry.

She has entered about nine hundred of those a year since she came into this room and she has never once looked at the bottom of one, and the reason is nineteen years old and it is that the foot is not the entry, and a clerk enters the entry.

---

The hand at the foot of that one is a hand she has seen before, and she saw it about two weeks ago at the other end of that passage, standing up, on the wrong side of a rail, with a sheet in front of her that was not a document and a thumb on the rail and about nine minutes of a true thing in it.

He is sixty-one. He signs for people who cannot write and for people who are not in the room.

**She has been putting that hand into a book for nineteen years.**

A man of about thirty came in at about the fifth hour with a stack to put on the shelf and stopped at the window and did not say anything for about half a minute, and then he said the thing that is the only thing anybody has said in that room in a fortnight that was not a price.

"You have had that book open for about a quarter of an hour."

"I have."

"To the second of the shelf and not to the first."

"The first does not have a day on anything in it that I have ever wanted," she said, and he said that was a sentence, and she said that it was one she had made up in that room, and he said it was a good one and went and put the stack down.

**And that is how the man of about thirty found out what she was doing, and she has not decided yet whether that is a good thing or the other thing, and she has not written it down, and there is nowhere in that room she could write it down.**

She went and got the second book off the shelf and stood in front of the open window with it, and the pane is out of it and the wind comes in at whichever corner it likes, and she turned back through it, and it is not a thing you can count to an end, and she did not try.

It is not three sheets. It is not thirty. It goes back past the point where she began to be able to look at a hand and know things about it, which she noticed in the third week of the ninth month of the year after next, and it goes back before that into a stretch of pages she entered in a hand that does not vary, and she has no idea when it started, and there is no way at all of finding out.

A hand begins at the foot of a sheet without anybody deciding anything, and nobody in this building ever wrote down the day it began, and that is not a fault and it is the way the thing works, and it is the reason nobody in this empire can tell you when a practice started, only that it has.

---

"**You are looking at the foot of it,**" said the man of about thirty, from the other end of the table, and he had not been asked to look and could not have been stopped. "**Why?**"

"Yes."

"That is not the entry."

"That is not the entry," she said. "I have known that for nineteen years and I have had it for about two weeks now and I am not going to tell you what I have found, and you are not going to ask me."

He did not ask her. He turned a sheet round and squared a stack and did not look at her again for about four minutes, and she let him have all four of them.

"Then what happens if I do ask."

"Then I tell you about the pane, and you will think about the pane for the rest of your life, and the thing I have found will be in you instead of in me." She said it to the window, because the window is the only thing in that room that is not official, and then she said the other thing, and it took the two of them about four minutes.

"I will tell you what I can tell you because you have been in this room four years and you are going to be in it after me. About nine of those sheets in about nineteen years have not been what the person above them wrote, and the man whose hand is at the bottom of them has signed all nine and knows it, and he has never been asked, and he told me why about two weeks ago at a rail, and the why is that if anybody asked him he would tell the truth."

"You have just told me that."

"I have just told you that."

"Then I am holding it."

"**You are holding it, and you did not ask for it, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that says a man in a room has to hand a thing back, and I would rather it went round this building than went anywhere else, and I am not going to tell you that it did not cost me anything.**"

---

And that is the whole of the change and it is the change she is going to have to hold, and it is not the change anybody would have wanted.

There are three people in this matter who have said a true thing about a heading in a room and not in a book. A man of sixty-one at a rail. A man of about thirty at a table, four minutes ago, to a man who was not going to ask him.

And her.

**And hers is the only one of the three that is in a book.** She wrote a question in the second of the eleven books on the fourth day of the third week of the ninth month of the year after next, on her own motion, at nobody's request, with the day and the hour on it and her own name at the foot of it, and it is the only thing in nineteen years of that shelf anybody in this building has ever entered about what ought to have happened instead.

The other two are in a rail and in a man's head. **A thing in a head cannot be produced against anybody, and a thing in a rail is in a room, and a room is not a place a hand can be laid on.** Hers is on a shelf in a building off the old river road and it has been since the ninth month, and her hand is on about four hundred pages below it, and anybody who ever wanted to know when she began to be able to look at a hand and know things about it would find out from her own writing.

That is the price. She said it out loud in a room with a pane out of the window and she said that it is a burden and not a promotion, and she has said the first half of that sentence in this building before, and the second half is the one that is new, and it is that for nineteen years the whole of what she had was that she advised nobody, and she has now made herself the only person in this matter who can be held to anything at all.

---

**And a practice with one person in it is stopped by that person.**

He told her the rest of it in the second movement of a thing he said about nine minutes' worth of, and she did not understand the whole of it then, and she has understood the whole of it in this room with a book in her hands.

A practice with two people in it is stopped by nobody.

There are two of them now in this room, and there is one in this building about nine miles off at the top of a stair. And there is a woman in a rented room two streets off a street that goes down to the river road who has heard all of it at second hand and does not know these two exist. And there is a woman of twenty in a room about eleven feet by fourteen four hundred and thirty miles up this river who has said it in a different order and has never heard of this room. There is not one instrument anywhere in this empire that will put any two of them in a room together, and no form for asking a person to look themselves up, and no form for asking a person who works beside them.

And there is no register. A question written down in a clerk's own book is not a register and it cannot be posted and it cannot be asked at, and a man who said a true thing at a rail and did not want it written down is not in anything, and a man who said a true thing at a table and did not ask for it is not in anything.

**There are four or five of them now, in four or five buildings, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for finding out how many there are, and that is not a gap in a record.**

---

She is not going to enter a second line in that book, and the reason is the same reason and not a new one, and she said it to the man of about thirty before he left and he said he had not been going to ask.

A second line would be an answer, or the beginning of one. A minute with an answer in it is an order, and a clerk of this office has never entered an order in nineteen years, and there is no form that lets her.

She is also not going to tell anybody what a man of sixty-one said at a rail, and she is not going to have it written down anywhere, and she said the cost of that out loud too, in the ordinary voice, at about the sixth hour, to a room.

"A thing you said out loud in a room with four people in it is in four people and not in a book, and I have got the only one that is in a book, and I am not going to put a second one in beside it, and there is nobody I can hand this to, and I am going to be carrying it."

There is a copy of a notice in the second book in that room, with a day against it, and the notice itself is on a bench about four hundred and thirty miles down that river with a seal at the foot of it and nothing under the seal. Its second line asks that no inference of any kind be drawn from the absence of any name. Its sixth line declines to name whoever holds the office at all.

She has read the second of those lines about nine hundred times and she has never once said the name and she is not going to, and that is not discretion and she will not let anybody in this building call it that.

It is that about nine hundred entries a year would be very easy to stop if anybody in any one of them could be shown what is written at the bottom of it, and she has just spent a day finding out what is written at the bottom of about four hundred of them, and she is not going to be the one who says it out loud in a room with a rail in it.

The woman of about twenty-six came in at about the sixth hour to ask whether the room wanted shutting, and read nothing at all on her way past, and she is not in this matter and has not been in it, and she has been in that room for the whole of that time and has asked about four hundred questions in it and not one of them has been about the foot of a sheet.

**Nobody thanked anybody.** The man of about thirty did not thank her and she did not expect to be thanked, and a man of sixty-one who walked out of a building off the old river road two weeks ago is nine miles off in a lane and is not going to be told that a clerk of about fifty-five has been copying his hand into a book for nineteen years without looking at it.
