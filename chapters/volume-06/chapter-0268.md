# Chapter 268: A List Of Works In A Foreman's Own Hand On A Bench Nine Foot By Eleven, A Letter With A Heading On It And No Name, And The Difference Between A Wall And A Hole

The tool shed is at the Slade end and it is about nine foot by eleven, with a bench along the north wall and a lamp in it that is not lit until about the seventh hour. It is about four hundred yards from a bay in which nine men work, and that is the whole of the distance between the only person in this empire you can say anything to and a bay full of men who can hear every word of it.

He is about fifty-five and he has a chain, and the chain is on him, and he cannot read a paragraph.

It is the fourth day of the second week of the fourth month of the year after the year after next, and it is about the sixth hour, and the lamp is not lit, and he has about an hour.

---

**Four seconds of a bay a week, at a time agreed with a foreman about nine years ago, and he has never been paid for any of it, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that pays a woman for knowing where a man is standing.**

The four seconds are not a wage and they are not a favour and they are not a job. He is in a bay for the length of a breath so that a woman running a bay knows where a man is. The rest of the week he is in this shed with a bench along the north wall and a lamp in it that is not lit until about the seventh hour, and he oils things in it, and that is the whole of what this empire has given him to do with.

He has about four hours before the lamp and he has had about nine years of them.

There is a cut in the wall at the height of his chest where he used to sit, and it is a cross with a bar through it, and it was cut by a man who worked in this shed nine years ago and who is dead, and it is not a mark anybody uses and it is not about anything and it belongs to nobody who is alive. It is not about the chain either. He has looked at it about four hundred times and has never once been able to work out why a man who cannot read a paragraph would cut a mark like that, and he has never asked, because there is nobody left who was here when it was cut.

---

The list of works was on the bench when he came in, and it comes on the bench about twice a week, and it is in the foreman's own hand, and it is the only piece of paper in his life that decides where he is going to be standing.

Nine names under a heading. A day on it. She reads the day out to the bay in the morning because that is what a foreman does, and he has never once asked her what the heading says and he is not going to start.

**He has carried that page about four hundred times and he has never been able to read one word of it, and it is not a letter and it is not a bill, and it is the page that says he is on, and there is no other page anywhere that says that.**

He can add. He has taught himself to count by hand in nine years, and he can carry a number in his head about as far as four hundred and not one step further, and he has never wanted to know what the heading says, and he has wanted to know every working day for about nine years.

There is a bit of paper in the band of his hat with four marks on it and no name on it and no day on it. He made it himself out of a pencil that was nearly blunt, in a yard, in the third week of the sixth month of the year after next. **The four marks are not the four men and he is careful about that and has been since the yard, and he will not write down in which gang each of them works, because the day he does that it is a list.** He has had the list in his hat for about nine weeks and it has washed out twice and he has made it twice and he has not made it three times and he is not going to.

---

There are four in that bay who cannot read a paragraph and he knows all four and he has not asked one of them one thing in nine years and he is not going to start in a shed at the Slade end at the sixth hour on a dry afternoon. He has said why, in about four sentences, about four times, and it is that writing him down as one of them would be a way for a list of four to go wrong. There are four, and there are going to be four, and that is the whole of what he can do about it.

And there is a finding in a ledger four hundred yards up that road in a book-keeper's own hand that says something about it, and he has never been in the room where it was entered, and he does not know what it says beyond the shape of it, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that would let a man off a list of works go and read a ledger in a book-keeper's own hand. There is no fifth reason. There is not going to be one.

---

A man came into the shed at about the sixth hour with a letter in his hand, and he had a cart outside and about four more of them, and he had been asking after somebody to read it since the second hour.

"I will read it to you," said the man of about fifty-five, before the other man had got to the end of the first sentence.

"You will."

"I cannot read a paragraph. I can read four minutes of one out loud and I have done about four of them in nine years and every one of them was for a man who is dead." He put the rag down. "Say that again slower and I will do it and I am not going to charge you and there is nothing to charge you with."

---

The other man said it slower.

It is a short thing. A heading at the top, in a hand that was not the hand that wrote the body, and under the heading a line about a sum that is owed, and a day, and then four lines of ordinary words, and at the bottom a place to sign, which is not signed.

He read it out. He read it the way he reads anything, which is that he gives it back exactly as it was given to him and does not add anything and does not leave anything out, because a man who cannot read a paragraph who leaves a bit out is not a man who cannot read a paragraph, he is a man who has made one up.

**"Have I got that right?"**

"You have got that right," said the man with the cart, and did not say anything else about it, and has not come back to the shed.

The other man listened to the whole of it standing up. Then he said that was right, that that was the way it was, and that he had wanted to know whether there was a name at the top of it before he went back.

"There is a heading at the top of it."

"I know there is a heading at the top of it. I asked you because there is a heading at the top of it and no name, and I have got four of these, and about four of the four have a name and one has not, and I did not want to go back with the wrong one."

He put the letter in his coat. "**Is it a name or a trade.**"

"It is a heading and it is not a name and I could not tell you which of the two it is, because I cannot read it."

"That is the first honest thing anybody has said to me about one of these." He got his hat. "You have saved me about two hours, and it is not a thing anybody pays for, and I am not going to say thank you for it."

---

And the man of about fifty-five said nothing, and the man with the cart went out, and the door of a tool shed does not shut either, and he stood in about nine foot by eleven and worked out what he had just done, and it took him the length of a cup of water.

A person who is paid to read is a person who can be told to stop.

He was not paid. There is no form anywhere in this empire that pays a woman for knowing where a man is standing, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that pays a man for reading a letter in a shed, and the man in the cart did not have ninepence and did not offer it and did not know he was talking to the only person in this empire you can say anything to and have it not come back.

**So he could not be told to stop, and that was the good half.** The other half is that he could not be asked either. There was nobody who could ask him what he had read, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that will let a person ask a man what he said out loud in a shed on a dry afternoon. What he read is now in about nine people and not in him, he has got no hold on it at all, and there is no version of the next four hours in which he finds out whether that man got to the right house.

---

**And then he got to the other half of it, and the other half is the one he has had for about nine years and had the wrong word for.**

He said the word out loud to a shed with the lamp not lit in it, and he had last said it about six weeks before, in the dark, and had not said it since, and it is the truest word he owns.

A wall keeps things out.

A hole lets them through.

**He is not a wall.**

Three people in this matter have tried to make use of that in about four years. Two of them found out that it is not a service. The third has not found out yet and is going to find out the same way, in about four minutes, and a man who is not findable is a man nobody can ask.

And this afternoon a man with a cart used him in about ninety seconds without knowing there was anything to use, and the only reason the man with a cart used him is that he was the first one in a shed, and about four hundred years of this empire have worked out that a man who cannot be found will do a thing for nothing and will not be asked to account for it.

---

And he would not have it both ways, and he worked that out on the bench at about the seventh hour while the lamp was not lit yet, and it took him about four minutes and it is the whole of what the last nine years have been for.

If he could be asked, he would be a person who could be held to. He cannot be asked. That is the only protection anybody in this matter has, and he has it because he cannot read a paragraph, and it is not a virtue and he has never once said it was.

And a thing that cannot be asked is a thing things go through.

He is not going to stop being who he is. He cannot read a paragraph, and that is not going to change, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that could change it and none that would want to. He is going to go on reading four minutes out loud to anybody who asks him, in a shed, for nothing, and he is going to be the piece of a building that a heading goes through, and he would rather that than be a wall, because a wall is a thing you put up on purpose and he has not put anything up on purpose in nine years.

---

The four in that bay are still four and were not asked one thing this week and were not named and no reason about them was given, and a season has closed and nobody decided that and nothing was resolved.

A girl of seventeen at the back of that bay is on a written engagement at the rate the list is set at, and she was not spoken to, and she was not thanked, and nobody is going to send for her, and she is not going to start.

**Nobody thanked him.** A man with a cart said he was not going to say thank you and he did not say it, and a foreman of fifty-one did not thank him because she was not there, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be thanked for reading four minutes of a letter out loud in a shed for nothing.
