# Chapter 271: Eleven Books On A Shelf Nine Weeks After She Looked At The Bottom Of One, And A Stack From A District In Which There Is Nothing At The Foot Of The Page And Never Has Been

Eleven books stand on a shelf in a ground-floor room of a building off the old river road, and it is the only window in that room, about two feet wide, with a pane out of it, and the wind comes in at whichever corner it likes and has for nineteen years. Three clerks work in this building and she is the oldest of them, and the other two are a man of about thirty who has been in that room four years and a woman of about twenty-six who is not in this matter and has not been in it.

She is fifty-five or thereabouts and she has nineteen years in that room, keeping the minutes of an office, advising nobody, and being asked nothing by anybody who was not a person who wanted something out of her.

This is the second day of the first week of the fifth month of the year after the year after next, and it is about the second hour, and the stack is on the table with her because the stack always comes up at about the second hour.

---

A man of about forty came in at about the fourth hour nine weeks ago holding a sheet somebody else had given him, and wanted to know what a word in the body of it meant, and she told him she could not tell him that and told him why, and he said thank you and went out.

He has not been back. She has not looked at the door about nine times a day for nine weeks, which is her own arithmetic and not anybody else's, and a clerk who watches a door for a man who is not coming is a clerk with about four hours of a day gone, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that lets anybody pay for that either.

---

**The finding of the four days behind her is not finished, and it is not going to be finished in this room, and she has stopped expecting it to be.**

She wrote a question in the second of the eleven books on the fourth day of the third week of the ninth month of the year after next, on her own motion, at nobody's request, with the day and the hour on it and her own name at the foot of it. It is still the only thing in nineteen years of that shelf anybody in this building has ever entered about what ought to have happened instead. She has not entered a second line and she is not going to, and the reason has not changed and is not a new reason, and she said it out loud to the man of about thirty before he went out to the shelf and he said he had not been going to ask.

A second line would be the beginning of an answer, and a minute that carries an answer in it is an order, and not one order has gone into that shelf in nineteen years, because there is no form in this building that would let her write one.

---

The stack was about four hundred sheets and they came up from a district in a bundle tied with a string, and the ordinary work of the second hour is to open the bundle, count what is in it, put the day's number against the top of the bundle in pencil, and start.

She got to about the two hundredth of them and stopped for about four seconds, and then she carried on, and the four seconds are the whole of what happened in that room that day.

**On about one sheet in about nine, the thing at the foot of it is not a hand.**

It is a heading. It is the office, in the office's own words, in the same shape as every other heading, and where the hand ought to be there is a space the length of a hand and it is not signed and it is not going to be.

She has entered about nine hundred of those a year since she came into this room. She has never had to sign one of those, because there is nobody to sign it, and she had not until this morning understood that a person does not sign them by choosing not to.

---

There is no rule about it anywhere she has been able to find in nineteen years, and she looked, and she looked properly, for about four minutes, in the wrong book first.

Whoever began it did not write it down. A foot that nobody signs is a practice and not a rule, and a practice begins at a moment nobody chose and is still going at a moment nobody chose, and the only thing anybody can say about the day it began is that nobody wrote the day down, which is the whole of what she said out loud at a table about nine weeks ago and is not a new thing.

**And the reason it was never a rule is that nobody has ever been able to make anything of it, and the reason nobody has ever been able to make anything of it is the thing the man of sixty-one says at a rail, which is that there is no person in it to make anything of.**

She put that down as being said by somebody else and stopped, because she is a clerk and a clerk who puts a thing somebody else said into a book of minutes is doing the thing a man of about thirty did with a sheet, and she has watched that happen once this month and is not going to be the second of it.

---

And then she got to the other half of it, standing at the table with the bundle open, at about the third hour, with the wind coming in at whichever corner it liked.

For nineteen years the whole of what she had was that she advised nobody. Nobody could produce a minute against her because there was nothing in any of them about her, and she has said that out loud twice and meant it both times as a protection.

**Hers is the only one of three true things in this matter that is in a book, and it is the only one that can be held against anybody, and she worked that out about nine weeks ago and has been carrying it since like a person carrying a parcel she did not want.**

And on about one sheet in about nine, in a bundle tied with a string, on a table in a room with a pane out of the window, there is a page that cannot be held against anybody at all. There is nothing at the foot of it to hold it with. It is a thing about a thing, and it is about as safe as anything in this empire has ever been, and it is also a thing that could not be produced against the district that sent it, or against the office that kept it, or against her, if she lost it tomorrow, and there is no version of the next four hours in which anybody finds out what it was for.

---

She said it out loud at about the sixth hour, to the room, in the ordinary voice, and the man of about thirty was there and did not ask her anything, and she said the rest of it as well and not because he asked.

"About nine hundred of these a year come in under a heading into about nine hundred rooms, and every one of them could be stopped inside a year by one person showing anybody in any one of them what is written at the bottom of it."

He put a sheet down. "And who is the one person?"

"That is the part I have not got," she said. "And I have been in this room nineteen years and there is nobody in it who could tell me, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that would let me ask, and I am not going to enter a line about it either, because a line about it would be a line about a person and there is nobody in it to put a name to."

---

Nothing was paid and nothing was sent and nothing was thanked. The man of about thirty carried the rest of the stack up to the shelf about four minutes before the sixth hour and put it down square, and she counted it twice, and it was about four hundred and it was all of it.

The man who came in at the fourth hour nine weeks ago has not come back to that building and is not going to be asked to, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that says a man who has been told he cannot be told a thing has to come back and be told it a second way.

The wind came in at whichever corner it liked, and the pane is still out of it, and the second of the eleven books is on the shelf where it has been since the ninth month of the year after next, with one line in it that is a question and is not going to become anything else in this room.
