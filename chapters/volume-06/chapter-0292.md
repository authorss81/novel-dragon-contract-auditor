# Chapter 292: A Man Who Came Up A Stair And Asked Her One Thing, And She Did Not Answer It

The Notaries' Table in Auremar runs four counters and this one is the top of the flight, and the stair it stands on has no way up it other than the stair. The hours are the second to the sixth. Behind the counter there is a board with four things written on it and not one of them is a time. About nine people come up that stair in a day, and nobody stands at the bottom of it, because a stair has no door.

She is twenty-four and has been at this counter about two years and is not a lawyer, and the book that is on the table this morning is hers and is not a copy of anything and has no office on it and nobody has ever asked her what it is for. At the other end of the counter stands a woman of thirty-four who has been there eleven years, and a man of about thirty does the copies at the far end, and a woman of about twenty who has been there a year is at the back and is not in this matter at all.

This is the second day of the second week of the tenth month of the year after the year after next, and it is about the fifth hour, and there are about four people in that room.

---

**He came up that stair at about the fourth hour and he did not want a copy and he did not want a question, and about nine people a day come up it and he is the first one in eleven years who has come up it wanting nothing at all.**

She noticed the hands first, because that is what the counter is. A man who has been writing other men's figures out fair for about eleven years has the outside of the middle finger hard on the near joint of the right hand, and this one had it on both, and had it on the left, which is not a thing she had seen on a customer in two years.

Then she noticed that he was reading the board, and not the way people read a board, and that he had gone along the four lines and stayed on the second one, which is a question at a shilling.

"The second one," he said, to the board, at about the fourth hour.

"That is a question, and it is a shilling, and it is about your own business and not about anybody else's, and I have never once been asked to sell one and I have never once sold one."

---

"I have never bought one," he said. "I have wanted to know what it costs for about a year and there is nowhere else in this city you can find out, and I have not come to buy it."

"Then you have come up a stair for nothing, and about nine people a day come up it for something, and you are the first one in two years who has come up it for nothing, and I have not got a column for that and I am not going to invent one."

**And there was nothing whatever to be said to that, and it was said, and it was the sort of thing a woman of thirty-four at that counter has said in about eleven years, and it was not unkind, and it was the reason nobody has asked the woman of twenty-four one question in about two years and the reason nobody has thanked her for the price of it either.**

---

And then he asked her, and it was not about a word.

"Does anybody ever ask you anything?"

She had the answer ready in about as long as it takes to look at a page and she did not say it. She has wanted to be asked every working day since about the seventh month of next year, and a person who is not asked has not been given a duty, and she had never once known that it did not apply to her. About two years have gone on that at this counter. She has said the whole of it out loud to about four people, and not one of the four was anybody who could do anything about it.

And a man she has not seen before had come up a stair and asked her it in the plainest words anybody has used in this room in two years, and she did not answer, and the not answering took her about four seconds and she is not going to be told why by anybody.

"Are you all right?" he said, after it.

---

"No," she said. "And that is the first time I have said it and I would like it left where it fell, and I am not going to tell you anything else, and you have not asked me anything else, and that is the whole of what you have done in this room."

"Then I have done the one thing I came up here to find out whether anybody did."

"You have found out that a person said one word at a counter and went down four flights. **There is no form in this empire that would let me write that down, and if there were, I am not going to use it, and you are not going to be told a second time by me whether that is the right decision.**"

He put a shilling on the table. She looked at it and did not take it and he took it back and did not say anything about that either, and about nine people went up that stair that day and about four of them came down knowing something they did not know when they went up.

---

The rest of it is the ordinary day and it went the way the day goes.

A man of about fifty wanted a thing put back three years and could not have it and was not told why. A boy of about fifteen bought a blank for fourpence, stood at the wrong end of the counter for about eleven minutes with it in his hand, and went down without filling it in, which is the whole of what a blank is for and the only use it has. A woman of about thirty-two came up about nine people a day in a hundred wanting to know about a letter, and there is no such thing kept and no such thing told, and the woman said that was how it is done here and not a favour to her.

Nobody thanked anybody. The man of about thirty at the far end of the counter had not been spoken to in the whole of the hour and did the copies and is not a person anybody in this room is obliged to answer for anything.

---

And then she went down the four flights with her book under her arm and it was about the sixth hour, and the room she rents is two streets off a street that goes down to the river road and the landlord has never once been inside it, and there is a table in it and a lamp on the table and a board on the wall with the rent on it and nothing else.

There are two things in that book and neither of them is a record of this afternoon, and the first is a line in her own hand, put there in the third week of the third month, about thirty-four weeks ago, recording that a clerk of thirty-four said no on a stair and gave no reason, and the second is four lines off the fee board off a shelf, and the fourth of the four is in a hand she and two other people cannot account for.

A refusal written down by somebody else cannot be held against the woman who wrote it, because the woman who wrote it refused nothing, and it cannot be held for the woman who refused it, because holding it for her is putting her in it, and being in it is being served. She worked that out in about four seconds the day it was written and it has not changed and it is not going to.

And a man came up a stair this afternoon and asked her one question and she did not answer it, and the not answering is now in this book the way nothing is in this book, which is to say it is not.

---

The undertaking of the fourth of the first month is live and unretired and unamended and has not been exercised, and it has not been exercised for the fifth time, and the fifth reason is not about the room.

The fifth reason is the one nobody in this empire has ever had to write down. A person who has wanted to be asked every working day for about two years, and has said so out loud about four times, and is then asked one question by a man who came up a stair to find out whether anybody did, has not been asked. She has been used. There is no column for a difference like that, and a column is not a place a difference goes.

**She is the price of eleven years of unpaid reading at a counter and she is not going to be thanked for it and she said out loud about eleven weeks ago that she would like it written down that she did not expect to be thanked, and she has not been, and it is not written down anywhere except in this room and in her own head.**

The lamp is lit and the board on the wall has the rent on it and nothing else, and the window looks at the back of another house, and the man who came up the stair has gone down it and is about nine miles off by now and has not come back up it, and there is no form in this empire that would let her ask him whether he meant it, and she is not going to, and there is not going to be one.

Nobody was thanked today, and nothing was paid, and the second thing in her book of her own is still the second thing, and she is not going to rule a third line and is not going to send anything, and the book is shut and is under the table.
