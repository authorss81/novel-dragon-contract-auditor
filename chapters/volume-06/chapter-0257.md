# Chapter 257: Three Pieces Of Paper On A Bench, A Seal With Nothing Under It, And A Crease That The Wet Is Going To Take

The hired stone store stands off a lane about four hundred yards up the road from a bay in which nine men work, and it is locked, and it is hired by a Company whose whole money is four shillings a year and all of it is the rent of this building, at a shilling a quarter. There is a bench in it along one wall. There are three pieces of paper on that bench.

She is a foreman of fifty-one and she has been in a box since the first week of the seventh month of the year after, entered by a class, and nobody has asked her about it. About four hundred yards away there are nine men who know she is in one, and that is nine mouths and it is not a document and it is not going to be turned into one by anybody in this matter.

It is the fourth day of the third week of the first month of the year after the year after next, and it is raining, and she is in a store with the key because the key hangs on a nail inside and there is nobody to hand it to.

She had walked the four hundred yards from the bay in about six minutes in the rain, and she had not gone into the bay first, and that was on purpose. She had stood in the lane and looked at the light in the bay window and worked out that if she went in there first she would not come up this road, and that was the whole of the calculation and it took her about four seconds. It is about four hundred yards there and about four hundred yards back and there is no bus on it and nobody has ever walked it for her.

She came because the rain was on the third week and the store is dry and the key is hers on the day she keeps it, and that is the reason she came, and it is not a good reason, and she would like it recorded that she looked for a better one for about four minutes and did not find one.

The man who put that sheet in the post is about four hundred and thirty miles up this river and she has never seen him and has never been in a room with him. She has heard that there is a man who cannot be found, from a man who heard it from a man, without a document. She has not asked about the sheet and nobody has asked for her and there is nobody in this matter she could ask, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that would let a foreman put a question to a man four hundred and thirty miles away about whether a thing he sent arrived.

That is the arrangement and it is the only arrangement there is. Two ends of one sheet, four hundred and thirty miles apart, and nothing whatever joining them, and the only person on this side of it who could say anything at all is standing in the wet with her hand on a fold.

---

The first of the three is a notice of six numbered lines with a seal at the foot of it and nothing under the seal.

She has read the second line about nine hundred times. It asks that no inference of any kind be drawn from the absence of any name. She has read the sixth line about nine hundred times. It declines to name whoever holds the office at all.

There is a name at the foot of the first of those notices that this office put there once, and it is not on this copy, and she has never said that name out loud in a room and she is not going to on a wet afternoon in a hired building. **A document that proves the absence of a name is a thing an office can use, and about nine hundred entries a year in about nine hundred buildings would be very easy to stop if anybody in any one of them could be shown what is written at the bottom of it.**

The second of the three is a factor's letter of four lines. She has been in here about nine times since the summer and she has not been able to get one single answer out of it, and she is not a woman who sends letters anywhere, and there is no fund behind her and no form for a fund.

---

The third of the three is a sheet of clean paper with a day in it and no name at either end of it, folded with the crease at the bottom.

It came about four weeks after it was posted, which is a long time, and before that it sat on a sill in the wet for about four weeks in a room four hundred and thirty miles from here. The reason it sat there is that **a thing with no name on it cannot be served, and a thing that cannot be served on anybody cannot be opened**, and that is a machine and not anybody's fault.

She has not opened it. She could not open it, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that would let her, and a woman who opens it has nothing to show anybody afterwards, because a sheet with a day in it and no name on it cannot be produced against a man who has not been named in it.

---

**And she is the only person alive who could give evidence about that fold, and she has told nobody, and she is not going to.**

Not her foreman, and not a man in a lane, and not the man who sent it. He is about four hundred and thirty miles away and he cannot find out whether it arrived without being findable himself, and a man who asks a question in a room is a party from that day on, and being a party is the only way anybody in this empire can be told what a thing is going to be used for.

She has worked that out about four times in about four weeks and she has not said it out loud in front of anybody, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire for a foreman to be asked what she knows about a piece of paper.

And a crease is not a name. A crease cannot be served on anybody. A crease is the only thing about a man who cannot be found that has come four hundred and thirty miles, and it is going to be taken by the wet in about a year, and there is nothing in this empire that can stop weather.

---

**And there is nothing on that bench that a foreman of fifty-one can do anything with, and she knows all three of them and has known for weeks, and a person who is findable and a person who is a foreman are two different kinds of trouble and she is both.**

About nine men know she is in a box. She said so out loud in that bay in the second week of the ninth month of the year after next, in front of every man in it, and about nine men heard her, and not one of them has been asked a question about it since, and she did not ask them for anything when she said it, and she has not asked them for anything since.

A man in that bay said it to her on the fourth day of the fourth week of the eleventh month of the year after next, on his way out, and told her he was not going to do a thing again, and gave a reason. She has not asked him whether he meant it, and there is not one form in this empire that would let her ask a man whether he meant a thing he said out loud, and being asked would make him findable by her asking.

She stood at the bench for about nine minutes and did the ordinary thing, which is that she has come in about nine times since the summer and looked at the three of them and gone out again, and about nine times she has not touched any of them.

Today she touched the third one.

She did not open it and she did not turn it over and she did not look at what is in it. She put her hand flat on the outside of the fold and held it there for about four minutes while the rain came on the roof. The paper under her hand was not wet, and the edge of the paper at the crease was not wet either, and it will be.

"Well," she said, out loud, to a bench in an empty building. "**That is a fold and not a name, and it has crossed about four hundred and thirty miles to sit on a bench in a building that costs a Company four shillings a year, and it is going to go in about a year, and there is not one form in this empire that will let anybody say that is wrong.**"

---

And that is the whole of what she did, and it is the whole of what she is going to do about it, and it is nothing.

She had expected to do something. That was the part she had not said out loud in the lane and she said it now, to a bench. She had come up a road in the rain with about four hours in her and a foreman's ordinary week in her, and she had expected to be a woman who did one thing in a hired building on a wet afternoon, and the one thing had been four minutes with her hand on a fold.

A foreman runs a bay. A foreman reads a list of works and signs a day's tally and answers about nine ordinary questions before the lamp. There is no other part of it. About nine years of that is the whole of what this empire gave her to do with, and she had gone up a road in the rain expecting it to be more than that, and it was not, and **nobody is going to be the reason it is more than that, including her.**

She is a foreman of fifty-one and she settled three things about herself in the ninth month of the year after next, and none of the three has moved since, and she is not going to move one of them in a hired building on a wet afternoon, and there is nothing on that bench that could move one.

She is not going to send anybody. There is not one form anywhere in this empire for sending a person four hundred and thirty miles, and she has not got a fund, and a man who walks that road is a man who can be found at both ends of it. He is not going to be sent and he has not asked and nobody is going to ask him.

She is not going to answer the factor's letter, and she is not going to open the sheet, and she is not going to say the name at the foot of the first notice, and none of those three is a decision she has made today. She made all three of them on the day they needed making and today she is only standing in the wet confirming them.

She has a list of works in her own hand with nine names on it, and four of the nine cannot read a paragraph. She has not written one line about that on any piece of paper in this building or in that bay or in her own house. There is a finding in a ledger four hundred yards up that road that says something about it, and it is not hers and she is not going to improve it and she is not going to add to it. There is no fifth reason. There is not going to be one.

And she is a foreman and a foreman reads, and what she has read is a notice of six numbered lines, a factor's letter of four lines, and a sheet with a day in it, and that is everything this empire has ever put in front of her about a man who cannot be found, and it weighs less than a sheet of clean paper and it is four hundred and thirty miles of somebody else's decision.

---

There is a bill at Lowcross that has stood at nineteen pounds three and fourpence for longer than she has been a foreman, and it is unpaid, and there is no line for a bridge in that fund in nineteen years. She is not going to pay it and is not going to fund it and is not going to say who ought to, and there is not one form in this empire that would let her do any of the three.

And about nineteen people are still behind that bank, who said no again in the same words with the same reasons, and they have not been asked again and they are not going to be, and a guarantee stands offered and unanswered on about four hundred and forty foot of it with about sixty children under sixteen inside. She could not read it out in a bay and would not read the terms of it in a shed, and no child in it is named anywhere in this matter, and it is not going to be paid this month either.

---

She put the sheet back on the bench with the crease at the bottom, because that is the way it came and she has never been able to find out since whether that is the way it should be or only the way it was.

She put the key back on the nail inside and pulled the door to, and a hired stone store has a door that does not shut. The rain came on it for about another hour and then stopped.

On the way back she went the other way, past the end of the bay, which adds about four minutes and takes her past a gate she does not have anything to do with. There were two men about forty yards off doing a thing to a post and they did not stop and she did not stop, and one of them said good evening to her in the ordinary way about four hundred yards from a building with three pieces of paper in it, and she said it back, and that is the whole of what passed between them.

Nobody was sent. **Nobody is going to be sent.** There are about nine people four hundred yards away who know that their foreman is in a box and there are about nine hundred of the rest of this river who do not, and the three pieces of paper on that bench are the third of three and none of the three can be answered. The crease is not a name and a crease cannot be served on anybody, and the only person alive who could say that a fold was made by a particular man in a particular room about four hundred and thirty miles away is standing in the wet outside a building that costs four shillings a year, and she has told nobody, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that would let anybody ask her.
