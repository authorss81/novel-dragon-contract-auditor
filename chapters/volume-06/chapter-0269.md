# Chapter 269: A Return For One District With A Heading On It And A Column For The Answer And No Column At All For A Refusal, And A Foreman Of Fifty-One Standing Behind A Bench Watching Nine Men Fill Nine Columns In

Nine foot by eleven, a bench along the north wall, a lamp in it that is not lit until about the seventh hour. The water is down about where it has been. The hold against a Company whose whole money is four shillings a year has not moved since the fourth week of the fourth month of the year after, and nobody has asked it to.

This is the second day of the third week of the fourth month of the year after the year after next. Nine men are in that bay. A foreman of fifty-one stands behind the bench, and a girl of seventeen is at the back of it on a written engagement at the rate the list is set at. The men are a man of about twenty-eight, a man of about twenty, a man of about fifty who came in at about the eleventh hour, a man of about fifty-five with a chain, a man of about forty off the third gang, a man of about thirty off the second, Marek Kest, and a man of about thirty-four who is on that list four times a season the same as everybody else in the building.

---

**The ordinary part of that day had been ordinary for about nine years and she had done all of it in a hand that does not vary: the list of works read out, the day's tally written, the hold read, the number of the hold written, and about four ordinary questions answered before the lamp.**

The hand the form comes printed with writes the body, and the hand at the foot of it is hers, and about four of the nine cannot read either one, and the two have been on the paper about nine hundred times a year for nine years without once being the same twice and without once being different.

A man of about twenty asked whether a stone had to be re-cut if it was set the wrong way round. It does not and she told him so.

A man of about fifty who came in at about the eleventh hour asked whether the day's tally was the same as last week's. It is not and she told him so.

The man of about thirty-four came in at about the fourth hour, as he comes four times a season, and did not ask her anything at all, and filled in a line in about four seconds in a hand that does not shake, and went back out.

A carrier came up the lane at about the fifth hour with a return for one district, and she has never seen that particular paper before, and there are a handful of them a year and they are not the same shape as anything else that comes down this bank.

It is a single sheet. There is a heading at the top of it in a hand that is not a foreman's hand. Under the heading there is a column, and the column has a name against every row on it, and the men in that bay are nine of those names, and at the foot of each row there is a space with a day against it, and above the day there is a line to write in.

There is a column for the answer and there is not one column on that sheet for a man to write no in, and there never has been, and that is not an oversight and nobody in this empire has ever sent anybody a form to complain of it.

The column is ruled all the way down the same way as the day column above it, in the same hand at the same time, and there is no short one anywhere on the sheet with a line left out of it.

She read the heading to the bay in the time it takes to read one and she did not put a shape on it or a hat on it, and she is a woman who said the shape of a thing out loud in this bay once in the first month of the year after the year after next and is not going to do it twice.

And it went round that bay in about nine minutes, the way a thing goes round a bay. Not what was in the heading. What was in the heading is a thing four of them could not have told anybody. What went round was that there was a column, and that the column had to have something in it, and that about nine men had put in it whatever was under the same word on their own paper.

It went round in about nine minutes, and a bay nine foot by eleven goes round twice in that, and by the second time round the words had gone out of it and nobody in there was using words any more, only the shape of them.

Nobody made a party of anybody and nobody thanked anybody. It was not a thing anybody did on purpose. It was a thing that happened because a sheet came up a lane, and a foreman read a line out of it, and nine men filled nine lines in because that is what a foreman says on a day like that one.

"Then fill the line in," she said. "All nine of you. Your own hand if you have got one and not if you have not."

---

And the thing about it that she has not got a word for is that the sheet does not say what the line is for.

The man of about twenty came to the bench about four minutes later and asked her what went in the line, and she told him the truth, and she has told people the truth at that bench about four times in nine years and it never once got anybody anything.

"It is not on it. I have had the sheet about four minutes."

"**Then what is the line for?**"

"That is what I am telling you. It is not on it." She put the sheet down and squared it. "There is no line on that paper for a man to say that he does not agree with what is above it, and there never has been, and nobody has ever asked for one."

"Then what do I put in it."

"What you have to put in it."

"**And if I do not agree with it.**"

"Then what goes in the line is what goes in the line. I am not able to tell you that is wrong and I am not going to, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that would let anybody else tell you either."

---

He put in the two words that were under the heading on his own piece of paper and came back and sat down, and she saw him do it and she saw the face of the man of about twenty-eight go about four inches further down, and about nine men watched neither of them, and she watched both.

And the man of about twenty-eight, who has been in that bay about four years and who said one thing out loud in it in the first month of the year after the year after next and has not said anything since, put his hand flat on the bench after the other man had gone back to his place, and did not say anything either.

She looked at him and he looked at the wall, and that was the whole of that, and it went on for about four minutes, and neither of them said one word about it and neither of them has said one word about it since.

A girl of seventeen at the back of that bay has a written engagement at the rate the list is set at and she was not spoken to, and she was not thanked, and she is not going to start, and nobody is going to send for her.

**The man of about twenty wrote in a line what the heading on his own paper said under it, and he does not believe it, and he wrote it in about four seconds, and it is in a book somewhere in a town about four hundred and thirty miles up this river by now or by the end of the week.**

She could have asked him. That is the whole of it and she has been over it since about the sixth hour.

Asking a man what he wrote in a column under a heading is being served, and being served is a party, and a party is the only person who can be told what a thing is going to be used for, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that says a foreman may ask a man in her own bay what he has written in a line on a sheet with a heading on it.

So she did not ask him, and she is not going to, and it is not restraint. **It is that the cost of asking is that he would be in it for good and she would be in it for good, and the whole of what she has is that neither of them is in anything.**

---

The four rows that are filled in by somebody else's hand are the four rows of men who cannot read a paragraph, and about four hundred yards up that road there is a finding in a ledger in a book-keeper's own hand that has something to do with them, and it is not hers and she has not been in that room.

A man of about sixty-one signs at the foot of a sheet for people who cannot write and for people who are not in the room, and he is nine miles off in a building off the old river road, and he is the only one of three things on a page that can be asked anything at all, and in about nineteen years nobody in this empire has ever asked him one question about anything.

That is the answer to the four and it is not hers and she is not going to say it in a bay, and it is the reason there are four men in that bay and not none of them, and it is the reason none of them has ever been asked one thing in nine years, and there is nobody in about four hundred yards who could ask and there is no form anywhere that would let them.

A man of about fifty-five with a chain was in that bay on that day and did not fill in his own line, and did not ask about anybody's, and did not say one word about any of it, and he has not said one word about it since.

---

And then she signed the day's tally, which is what a foreman does, and there are three things on that paper: the hand that writes the body, which is a clerk's hand and cannot be asked anything; her own hand at the foot of it, which answers for everything above it; and a class, which is a heading, which cannot be examined and cannot lose. About nine hundred of these a year come in under one in about nine hundred buildings, and every one of the nine hundred is correct.

**And the return goes to one district.**

She has been in a box since the first week of the seventh month of the year after and it was entered by a class and nobody has asked her about it, and about nine men know she is in one and are not going to ask her, and she is not going to be asked about it today either, and she did not raise it and she is not going to.

**Nobody was rescued in that bay on that day and nobody was thanked.** The man of about thirty-four filled in his line in about four seconds in a fair hand that does not shake and said nothing to anybody and is on that list four times a season like everybody else and did not say he was going to do anything different on any day of his life.

A man of about thirty from the second gang wrote his line in and sat down and looked at the wall for about nine minutes, and he has asked one question out loud in this bay in his life and he has not asked another and nobody has asked him to and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be asked.

---

And she is going to go on doing the ordinary part tomorrow, and the day after, and about nine hundred times a year for as long as she is a foreman, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire for a foreman to put in a book that a man did not believe what he wrote in a line.

That is the whole of the second day of the third week of the fourth month of the year after the year after next. It is an ordinary bay, a sheet with a heading on it, nine lines filled in by nine men, and a woman behind a bench who knows what four of the nine wrote and cannot say so to anybody.

The lamp is not lit until about the seventh hour. The carrier took the sheet down the lane at about the sixth hour and did not come back up it, and the line the man of about twenty wrote in is about nine miles off in a room by now with a heading over it, and nobody will ever ask him what he meant by it, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that would let anybody.
