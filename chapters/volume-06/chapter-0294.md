# Chapter 294: Nine Miles As Far As The Lane The Building Is Off, And A Reason He Does Not Have

The lane the building is off runs down off the old river road. There is a lamp at the top of it about four hundred yards in from the road, and the building is at the bottom of the lane with a door on it that has a step worn in front of it. He has been at the bottom of that lane once, in daylight, in about the second month of the year after next, when he was sent to fetch a barrow and was made to wait four minutes in a room. He has never been at the bottom of it at night.

He is thirty-four or thereabouts, and what he does for money is write out other men's sums so that they come out even, and he is not on anybody's list this week and has not been for about nine weeks, and the day is his and nobody is going to ask him why he has spent it. He has a house with a door on it and a wife and a room over a chandler's shop with four steps and a landing, and there is a table in the room and nothing on the table.

This is the fourth day of the fourth week of the tenth month of the year after the year after next, and it is about the sixth hour, and the light has gone off the lane and the lamp at the top of it is lit.

---

He got as far as the bottom of the lane and stood in front of the door and did not go in, and it took him about four minutes to decide not to, and the four minutes were the whole of the journey.

The step is worn in the middle and not at either edge, which is what about nine years of a door used by everybody and never used by one person does, and he stood on the worn part of it without noticing, and about four minutes of a man standing on a step is a thing a woman next door could describe if anybody asked her.

The nine miles is not the thing. He has walked about four miles a day, most days, for about eleven years, and the nine miles took him the whole of the daylight and nothing hurt and he was not tired at the bottom of the lane.

What he had when he got there was a question. It is a good one and he has had it for about three weeks and it has got the shape it is going to have: a clerk of about fifty-five, a book with eleven of them on a shelf, a second shelf down, a line with a day and an hour on it in her own hand and her own name at the foot of it, and whether she meant it.

And what he had not got, at the bottom of a lane with a step worn in front of a door, was a reason.

---

A woman of about thirty-five came out of the house next along and shut her own door and saw him standing there and did not ask him anything and went in, and that is how the lane is at about the sixth hour, and he has not got anything against her and she has not got anything against him.

The reason he gave the last time is a good reason and it is the reason for a different thing.

**A man who has stood in one doorway and said a thing twice out of it does not go and stand in a third, because the second time cost him more than the first and he worked out in about a kitchen that he would not pay it again, and that has held him for about forty-one weeks and it is going to hold him for the rest of his life if that is all he has.**

But that is a reason for stopping. It is not a reason for going, and he has been treating it as both for about eleven years, because it is the only reason he has ever given himself about a room, and a reason for not doing a thing is not a reason for doing it.

**And a thing he has done once has never needed a reason from him, because the first time he did it he was not a person who did things twice and he did not owe anybody an account of the second one, and he has not been that person for about forty-one weeks and he is not going to be him again on purpose.**

---

He stood there and went through it the way he goes through a column that will not come, which is in the order, and there was nothing in it, and it took about as long again.

The first thing is that he does not know her name. The second thing is that he does not need it, and he worked that out in a kitchen a fortnight ago and has carried it since. The third thing is that he does not know what is in the second of the eleven books beyond what a man of about thirty told a foreman in a bay in the first month of the year after the year after next, and that what he does know is that a line is in it with a day on it.

The fourth thing is the one that stopped him.

**A man who stands in a lane at night outside a building off the old river road is a man who can be found, and he would not be able to say afterwards that nobody knew.** There is a woman of about thirty-five next door who saw a man standing there. About nine people a day come up a stair four miles off, and one of them saw a man come up it and want nothing. Neither of them can be asked, and both of them are a person.

He has been on nobody's paper in about two years. That is the whole of what he has and it is the only condition in this matter he has ever got himself into on purpose, and he was standing in a lane giving it up for a door.

About two years of nobody's paper is about two years of a man nobody can look up, and it is worth exactly nothing and costs nothing, and nobody at a counter could take it off him, and about four hundred yards of lane with a lamp in it is about as much as it was ever worth.

---

So he turned round, and it took him about four seconds, and there was nobody in the lane to see it, and he has not got past that either.

At about the seventh hour he was at the top of the lane and the lamp was still lit and there was nobody else on it, and he walked the nine miles back in about as long as he came and did not stop and did not go into a shop, and he got up the four steps about the ninth hour and there was nothing on the table in that room and the lamp was not lit and he put a match to the wick himself.

Four hundred yards in from the road the lamp is the only light for the rest of the lane, and a man walking away from a door at about the seventh hour is walking at the same speed he came at, which is the only thing about the day he has checked.

The cost of the day was nine miles and the whole of a day he was not paid for, and there is no form in this empire that would reimburse a man for a day he spent not going in a door, and nobody is going to hear about it, and it is the cheapest thing he has ever done and he is not going to be thanked for it.

Three weeks. He has got a question and he has not got a reason, and a reason is not a thing you can get in a lane, and the four minutes at the bottom of it were honest and he is not going to pretend to anybody, least of all himself, that he was ever going to knock.

And nobody thanked him. His wife is at the bottom of the four steps and he did not tell her he had gone and he is not going to, and there is still no form in this empire for asking a person whether they will mind being told a thing, and he told her the last one six weeks before it and is going to be held to the same terms for this one, and the not asking her is the arrangement and not an oversight.
