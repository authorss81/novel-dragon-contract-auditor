# Chapter 288: A Shilling A Question At The Top Of A Stair And Eleven Years In Which Not One Of The Questions Has Been About A Word, And A Woman Of Thirty-Four Working Out In About Four Minutes That The Price Of A Thing She Is Not Paid For Is Standing Four Feet Away From Her At The Other End Of The Same Counter

The fourth counter of the Notaries' Table in Auremar stands at the top of a stair, and there is no other way up to it, and it is open from the second hour to the sixth. Behind the counter there is a fee board with four things written on it and not one of the four is time. About nine people a day come up that stair, and a stair does not have a door at the bottom of it.

Marn Ottery is thirty-four and has been at that counter eleven years and is not a lawyer and has never wanted to be one. A woman of twenty-four is at the other end of it, and a man of about thirty does the copies, and a woman of about twenty who has been there a year is not in this matter at all.

This is the second day of the second week of the ninth month of the year after the year after next, and it is about the second hour, and there are about four people in that room.

---

Under that counter there is a box of about four hundred bought and unfilled blanks, and it has not been given a name, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that would let anybody give it one, and nobody in that room has proposed one in eleven years.

A man of about forty-five came up this stair in the fourth week of the fifth month for the fourth time and got one word and no reason at all in it, and she did not sit down and did not look at the board, and he went down. She has not thought about it since and is not going to.

---

**And this counter sells a question for a shilling, and there is one on that board, and in about eleven years not one person has ever bought one about a word.**

A man of about fifty wanted a thing dated back three years and could not have it. A boy bought a blank for fourpence and went down. A woman of about thirty-two wanted to know whether a letter had arrived, and the answer is that nobody is told that, and it is not kept, and the woman said that was the ordinary way and not a favour.

A question about a word is the only thing anybody in this empire has ever been able to make a person answer for, and there is one of those for sale on that wall for a shilling, and about nine hundred of them go out of about nine hundred rooms a year to about nine hundred people who are not given a choice about being given one.

Not one of them comes up this stair.

---

And then she got to the other half of the morning, at about the third hour, with the man of about thirty doing copies at the far end and the woman of about twenty-four at the other end of her own counter, and it took her about four minutes and it is the whole of what the second hour was for.

**Nobody pays her to read.**

A clerk at this counter is paid a day for the day. The day is for copies, and for a shilling a question, and for telling a man whether a thing would stand if the thing it came from were lost, and none of that is reading.

She has read in eleven years more of other people's money and other people's days and other people's signatures than she could put in that room, and she has been paid not one penny for any of it.

**And there is not one form anywhere in this empire that pays a woman for reading a room, and there is not one that lets anybody ask her to stop, and there is not one that lets anybody ask her whether she meant it.**

She has known that for eleven years and has never once had to think about it.

---

And the price of it is at the other end of her own counter, and she has had about two years of it, and she worked it out in about four seconds, and she is not going to do one thing about it.

The woman of twenty-four at that counter reads all day as well and is paid a day for the day, and for about two years she has not been asked one question, and Marn Ottery is the reason.

Not out of any decision. Because a person who reads in a room takes the questions in that room on, and the taking is what makes the asking, and if nobody ever asks her then nobody ever has to be able to tell her to stop.

And about four hundred and thirty miles from this stair a woman of twenty-four has worked out in a rented room that the price of that is not a wall, and she worked it out about ten weeks ago, and Marn Ottery has not been told.

---

**And the fourth time the man of about forty-five came up that stair he was told no, and the woman of twenty-four said out loud in this room that she was going to write it down in her own book, and Marn Ottery told her in about four seconds what she had done to herself, and did not stop her.**

That was twenty-three weeks ago. It is the first time in this matter that a refusal has been written down by a person who was not the person refusing, and the woman at the other end of the counter has not said one word about it since, and neither has Marn Ottery.

**And there is not one form anywhere in this empire that would let a clerk at this counter hand another clerk a sheet of paper with her own name on it and say, that is what you said, and I wrote it down.**

And there is not one that would let her ask whether she did.

If she did, then there is a piece of paper in this city with a clerk's name on the other side of it, and the clerk is Marn Ottery, and Marn Ottery does not know whether it exists, and has not asked, and is not going to, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that a person is to be told what is written about them in a room four feet away.

---

**And in this room there is a refusal and there is a piece of paper about nobody, and they have been four feet apart for eleven years and have never once been in the same question.**

The refusal is the one she gave on a stair in the third week of the third month. The other is four lines of a fee board, and the first three of them are in the hand that put them up, and the fourth one is not.

Three people in this empire cannot account for that fourth line, and one of them is about four hundred and thirty miles up this river and is not in this room and does not know this stair exists.

And there is not one form anywhere in this empire that would let anybody put those two things in the same question, and there has not been one for eleven years, and nobody in that room has ever wanted to.

The first of the three lines is a copy at fourpence. The second is a question at a shilling. The third is a thing she has never been able to work out and has looked at about four times a season for eleven years. The fourth is in a different hand and she does not know whose and does not know when it went up.

---

"Do you know what I have just done?" she said, to the man of about thirty, at about the fifth hour, and he said no, and she said, "I have found out that a person standing at a counter is not on the counter's side of anything, and that is a thing I have known for eleven years and never once had to use."

The man of about thirty did not say anything, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that says a man who does the copies has to answer a clerk of eleven years, and she did not want him to.

Nobody thanked anybody. The woman of about twenty at that counter has been there a year and is not in this matter and read nothing at all of any of it.

The counter shut at the sixth hour, and about nine people had gone up that stair and about four of them came down knowing something they did not know when they went up, and the fourth line is still on the wall behind her and will be on it next week, and the woman of twenty-four took her book and went down before Marn Ottery and did not say anything on the stair and was not asked.
