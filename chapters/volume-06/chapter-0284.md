# Chapter 284: Twenty-Nine Weeks Of An Undertaking, And A Fourth Night Of Not Doing It

She has the room by the week and the man she pays it to has never come past the door of it, and what he wants is on a board outside with a nail through it in a hand that is not hers, and the board says one thing and has said one thing for about two years. Out of the window is the back of another house and a dog about four miles off that goes off and stops and starts. The drawer with the four things in it is still in the bed, and it has no lock on it and never has had one.

She is twenty-four, a clerk at that counter, not a lawyer, and the book open on the table is the one with nothing written on the outside of it, and it has had two things in it for nineteen weeks.

This is the second day of the second week of the eighth month of the year after the year after next, and it is about the seventh hour, and the lamp has been on a while, and there is no more of the day coming.

---

About nine people came up that stair today, and four of them wanted something they could have and five of them wanted something they could not.

A man of about forty wanted a copy of a thing he is not a party to, and was told no before he had got the second sentence out, and said thank you, and went down, and she said that was the ordinary way and not a favour.

A woman of about twenty-eight wanted to know whether a thing would hold up if the thing it came out of were lost, and it would not, and she said so without softening it, and the woman said she had expected as much and had come anyway.

That is the whole of the day, and it is the product of a counter, and it is nine times a day, and it has been for two years.

And the two things in the book have been on that table for nineteen weeks, and she has looked at both of them about four times a week and has not torn either out. The first is a line in her own hand with a clerk's name on the other side of it. The second is four lines off a fee board of which the fourth is in a hand nobody can account for.

---

**She took it up out loud on a table in this room on the fourth of the first month, and it is still in force, and nothing has been done to it since, and she did not act on it tonight, and she is going to write down why, because this is the fourth night and the fourth separate reason.**

She wrote it out on the left-hand page in one line, without stopping, which is how long it took to be sure of it, and then she read it back and it was true, and it was not the reason she had given the two times before this one, and it was not a variation of either of them.

One line on the left-hand page and about four inches of it, and the pen has gone over the same paper about nine times a week for a couple of years, and has never once been the reason a thing got done.

The first time was that there was nothing in the room to act on. The second was that there was nobody in the room to act on it with. **Both of those are about what is in the room, and this one is not about the room at all, and that is why it took twenty-nine weeks and not four days.**

---

**An undertaking is a thing a person takes up out loud, and the only way anybody ever finds out whether they meant it is by asking them.**

In twenty-nine weeks nobody has asked her whether she meant the rule of the fourth of the first month. There is no form in this empire for asking a woman at a counter what she took up on a table in a rented room in the first month, and there is no second one that says she may be asked it by a person she did not send for. She has not sent for anybody and she is not going to.

She worked that out standing at the table at about the fourth minute, and it took about nine seconds, and what it gave her is not a reason to do the undertaking and is the opposite of one.

It is the same position as a clerk of about fifty-five nine miles off, in a room with a pane gone out of the window, who has a line in the second of eleven books with her own name at the foot of it.

She has had that line there since the ninth month of the year after next. No form in this empire will let anybody ask her whether she meant that either, and the two of them have never met, and there is not one instrument that would put either of them in a room.

"And so which of us is better off?" she said out loud, to a table and a lamp, at about the seventh hour, and there was nobody in the room to answer it.

She asked it to a table and the lamp, and the lamp had been on a while and had got warm at the top of the shade, and about four feet of the room was warm and the rest of it was not, and she has had the same arithmetic ready on two previous nights and has not once asked the question aloud.

**She has spent about two years being extremely good at not being asked anything, and she thought that was a wall.**

"It is not a wall," she said, and she heard herself say it twice over and did not mind. "It is the ordinary condition of a thing somebody took up on their own, and I have been calling it a wall because a wall is a thing you build, and this is a thing you are standing in, and I have been standing in it for about two years and calling it mine."

---

**And the fourth of the four things in the drawer is in the bed and has not been sent, and the two of them are the only things in this room that were taken up out loud and not carried out, and a resemblance is not a reason.**

She did not look at the bed when she said it and she is not going to look at the bed now.

The drawer is under the table and cannot be locked and never has been, and the four things in it are the only objects she has ever moved out of place in the room, and she has moved them about four times and put them back in the same order twice.

---

**And a thing not done on four separate nights for four separate reasons is not four decisions.**

It is one thing being not done. A woman who is extremely good at not doing a thing on purpose is a woman who is building it, and nothing in this empire would let her stop building it, and the whole of what she has got out of four nights and four reasons is the name of the thing.

She has got the name of it and she is going to keep it. The keeping of it is not a decision, and she is not going to pretend that having a name for a thing is the same as having done anything about it, because a fact is not a decision, and she worked that out about ten weeks ago, and it has not improved on being said once.

---

There are two instruments that call for her in whatever room he is standing in, and she has never once set them down in the same breath and is not going to start tonight, and she has spent nineteen weeks not putting them together and about two years before that not noticing that she was not.

One of the two is dated and carries her name at the foot of it and can be brought into a room and read out loud. The other is a watch, and it wants somebody who is not from the office that cannot be named, and it carries her name nowhere, and it cannot be brought into a room at all. She has never once had to hold both in her head at the same moment and she is not going to start on a night when she has just worked out that she is the price of a thing.

And there is a question she has not answered and is not going to answer tonight, and it is not in this room, and no instrument in this empire would let anybody put it in front of her, and that is the fourth time in about two years that the reason she has not answered a question is that nobody is able to ask it.

---

Nobody thanked her and nobody was thanked. The board by the door has said one thing for about two years and the dog about four miles off has gone off and stopped and has not come back.

She sat at that table for about four minutes with a book in front of her that has one thing about a person in it and one thing in it about nobody, and an undertaking that is twenty-nine weeks old, unretired, unamended, and not exercised tonight. The reason is on the left-hand page in her own hand, and it is the only one of the four that is not about the room in any way at all, and she is not going to do anything about it, and nothing an empire could print says a person may do anything about a thing she has worked out.
