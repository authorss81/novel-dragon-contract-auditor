# Chapter 259: A Landing, A Door, And A Man Who Came Four Miles To Say One Sentence Out Loud On A Step

The landing outside the room he works in has four steps and a door with a bolt on it and a window about a foot wide. The room is over a chandler's shop off a lane about four miles from a bay on the Slade Cut. He is on the list of works four times a season and this is not one of the four days and he is here anyway, because a man of about sixty has a hand that shakes and cannot do a fair one.

It is the second day of the first week of the second month of the year after the year after next. He is a man of about thirty-four and he has a trade, and the trade is a hand that writes other people's figures out fair, and he has a house with a door on it and a wife and he has said in one doorway in his life that he was not going to do a thing again, and it has been about nine weeks.

---

The work itself took about four minutes and it is the whole of what he does four times a season, and he has never told anybody it is the whole of what he does.

A man of about sixty came in at the second hour with a day-book under his arm and a sum at the bottom of it that he could not get out fair. A man of about thirty-four copied it out four times in a hand that does not shake, and the man of about sixty read all four back and said that the third one was the one, and he was right, and he said so. Then he said that a man who can do that ought to be doing more of it than four times a season, and he has been told that by about nine men in about four years and it has never once been a form of anything.

A man of about twenty came up the lane with an empty barrow and stopped at the foot of the four steps, and he had come about four miles, and he did not come up.

"I have got one thing to say and I have walked about four miles to say it because you are the only one anybody could say it to and I would rather say it on a step than in a room with nine men in it."

"Say it."

"A man in our bay asked a foreman a question out loud on the fourth day of the fourth week of the first month. A man of about thirty out of the second gang. He said the shape of it first and then he said the word, and the word was a word on a form, and about nine of us heard it and the foreman answered it, and she is a foreman of fifty-one and nobody has asked her anything since the box." The man of about twenty put his hands on the barrow. "Your name went with it. He said your name in the bay, and he said you came about four miles on foot the last time, and he said it out loud. **I would not have come at all if I had not been told that was the arrangement.**"

---

He put his pen down, and he did not say anything for about four seconds, and the four seconds were the ordinary four seconds of a man deciding what a thing has cost him.

"That is the arrangement," he said.

"That is what he said, and that is what I have come about four miles to tell you, and I have not been sent by anybody and there is no form in this empire that makes a man walk four miles to tell a man what a bay said."

"You have been paid for the day?"

"I have had my day and I have not been paid for this and nobody is going to pay me for this, because there is not one form in this empire that pays a man for carrying a sentence from a bay to a step."

---

"Is it going to go round the bay."

"I do not know and I did not ask and I am not going to ask, because a man who asks a man of about thirty from the second gang what he is going to do next is a man who has turned a practice into a thing with a head on it."

"He did not tell you his name."

"He told me his gang and he told me what he said and he told me the whole bay heard it, and that is more than I had about my own, and I have had mine about nine weeks and it is still the truest thing anybody has said to me." He picked the pen up and put it down again. ""And I am not going to find out his name. There is not one form in this empire for asking a man his name in a lane, and I have decided that on purpose, and I would like it noticed that I decided it on purpose and not in a temper.""

Then the man of about twenty went off down the lane with the barrow, and the man of about thirty-four stood on four steps and worked out what it was that he was feeling, and it took him the length of a cup of water, and it was not anger, and he has said since that he went looking for about four days for the anger and it was not there and he was glad and then he was not glad.

He did not like it, and that is the whole of the feeling, and he has not improved on it and he is not going to.

He went over the nine weeks afterwards, on four evenings, in the ordinary way, looking for the seam in it, and there was not one, and every time he came back to the same four facts. A man of about thirty from the second gang is not his friend and has never spoken to him. The asking cost that man something and he watched about nine men hear it and one of them said out loud that it was the best thing that had ever happened on that bank. Nobody sent him. And the word he asked about was a word on a form, and not the word he had asked about, and not a bigger word, and not a worse one.

**He could not find the difference and he has stopped looking, and what he has instead is a thing he is not proud of, which is that he would rather it had been a different man asking the same question about a different word, and that is exactly the shape of a practice, and he knows the shape of a practice now because he described one on a step.**

He could not have said why. That is the part that would not open. Try it four ways and every way ran into the same thing at about the fourth word.

If he said *he had no right*, then a man who asks a question in a room has no right unless somebody sent him, and he had not been sent either, and he would be saying that the two of them were the same and he has not decided that.

If he said *it is not the same thing*, then it is the same thing, because both of them put a question to a person about a word on a form out loud in a room, and he has gone over the two of them four times since the fourth day of the fourth week of the eleventh month of the year after next and he cannot get a difference out of it that is not about the size of the room.

And if he said *he should not have*, then a man of about thirty from the second gang has put a man of about thirty-four in a position, and a man who is in a position cannot get out of it and did not ask to be in it.

---

**And the reason is the one he gave in a doorway nine weeks ago, and he has to say it out loud on his own step to work out whether it still holds, and it holds, and that is the trouble.**

A practice with one person in it is stopped by that person. He stopped it. He stood in a doorway and said he was not going to and he meant it and he has not done it since, and it has cost him every week since the bay, being the man somebody comes up four miles to talk to, and he has paid that and it was worth it.

A practice with two people in it is stopped by nobody.

**And that is not a thing anybody has to do.** It is not an institution and it is not a decision and it is not anybody's fault. It is simply that there are two of them now, and each of the two can say *I only did it once*, and that sentence is true, and it is available to both of them at the same time, and the two of them will say it at the same time if they ever meet, and a thing that two people can stop by both saying a true sentence is a thing nobody stops.

---

He sat down on the top step with his back against the door and worked out the rest of it, and the rest of it took about four minutes and it is the part that frightened him.

**It is not going to stop at two.**

A bay is nine foot by eleven and there are nine men in it and all of them heard it. About nine hundred buildings in this empire each get one of these a year, and there is no form anywhere that can tell a man in a bay about a word he has not seen. And about four hundred and thirty miles up this river there is a woman of twenty in a rented room who says true things out loud to about forty books, and a foreman off a dye end who has said one true thing about himself out loud five times, and a boy of about nine with a slate, and there is not one form in this empire that will take a slate off a boy.

**And he is not going to be the reason a person four hundred and thirty miles up this river cannot stop something he never started.**

---

He said that out loud on a step at about the fourth hour with nobody listening, and it was the first time he had said it, and he has not said it since and is not going to improve on it.

And then there was the door, which is the actual cost and which he had not looked at for the length of the whole thing.

Behind that door is a woman he told on purpose in a kitchen six weeks before a bay in the eleventh month of the year after next, without asking her first, and he said at the time that he could have asked and did not, and that there is not one form anywhere in this empire for asking a person whether they will mind being told something.

He has now done the same thing twice, and the second time is cheaper and worse. A woman of about thirty-five came to his door about four days after the first telling and asked whether he was in on a day, and his wife said no. He has not told his wife about the woman with the wet hood either, and that is three things in about nine weeks that she is not in and knows nothing about. A man who tells a person about himself and then stops telling that person is not protecting her. He is managing her. There is not one form in this empire for managing anybody, and he has invented one, and he is the only person using it, and it does not work.

She will find out that a man came about four miles, inside about four weeks, because a lane is a lane and there is not one form in this empire that stops a man talking in a lane. He is not going to be the one to tell her first this time, and that is a decision, and it is a bad one, and he has made it on purpose on a step.

Then he said the other thing, because he had said it once in a doorway and the whole of the difficulty of saying a thing once is that there is no second time to check it against, and he wanted a second time.

"I am not going to do it again," he said. "That is the second time I have said it and it is more expensive than the first, and I am not going to say it a third, and I have worked out why it gets more expensive and I am not going to tell anybody, and my wife is going to be told nothing about a step."

---

Nobody thanked him for saying it and nobody had thanked him for the first one, and the man of about twenty who had come about four miles with a barrow was not paid for the walk and was not thanked on the way back and had not asked to be. **There is not one form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be thanked for asking a question, and there is not one that requires a person to be thanked for carrying one.** There never is going to be either.

Nobody is going to be sent. There is not one form anywhere in this empire for sending a person four hundred and thirty miles, and nobody in this matter has a fund, and the road is about six days and it is not being walked by anybody in either direction.

**And the count is six, and it moved once, on the fourth day of the fourth week of the first month of the year after the year after next, in a bay, by a person nobody sent.** The man who started it is not going to do it again and has now said so twice, and there is still no post in this empire whose job is to ask, and nobody was appointed, and nothing was written down.
