# Chapter 277: A Hearing Room With A Rail In It, A Single Sheet With A Heading At The Top Of It And A Seal At The Foot Of It And Nothing Under The Seal, And A Man Of About Sixty-One Who Says Out Loud The One Thing About It That Nobody In This Matter Has Ever Said

The cold comes up off the flags the whole length of that passage and there is no fire in either of the rooms at the end of it. The far one has a rail in it that stands about four feet off the floor. The near one is where the clerks work, with a shelf in it and a window about two feet wide. There are three clerks in this building and the third of them is a woman of about twenty-six who is not in this matter and has never been in it, and her place is at the table at the near end of that passage, and she came up it at about the second hour this morning with a day-book and has not gone back down it.

He is about sixty-one and he signs at the foot of sheets, and he has signed at the foot of about nine hundred of them a year since he was young, and he is the second of three hands on every one of them, and the second is the only one of the three that can be asked anything.

This is the fourth day of the third week of the sixth month of the year after the year after next, and it is about the sixth hour, and the rail is cold and has been cold in every month he has been in this building.

---

**A single sheet came up from a district on the second day of the first week of the sixth month, and it has been on the ground floor ever since, and it is the only shape of those about nine hundred that nobody reads out.**

It has a heading at the top in a hand that is not a clerk's hand. Under the heading there is a line that does about four things in about four lines. At the foot of it there is a seal and there is nothing under the seal.

Line two asks that no inference of any kind be drawn from the absence of any name. Line six declines to name whoever holds the office at all.

It comes about twice a year. It goes back. And in about four years of this building nobody has read one out loud in a room, and the woman of about twenty-six said that she is not going to be the first, and she was not being funny, and he believes her.

---

She was at the table at the end of the room with the day-book open and a pen, and she had got as far as the day, and she looked up at about the sixth hour and he had not said anything for about four minutes.

"I am not going to read it out."

"That is all right, and I am not going to argue with you about it."

"It is not all right, and I am going to say why, and you did not ask me, and I want that on the four minutes before I start rather than after."

She put the pen down. And that is the whole of what she did about it, and she has not said one word since, and she is not in this matter and never was.

"**Do you know what the seal is for?**" she said, after a while, and then, before he could answer it, "**Because I have signed underneath nine hundred of them and I have never once been told, and I have worked out in about four seconds that I am asking a man I have no business asking.**"

"No," he said. "And neither am I, and I have been underneath more of them than you have."

---

**A document about nobody cannot be answered, refused, held to a forgery, or produced against a person in any proceeding, because there is no person in it.**

He said it in about nine minutes and he said it the way he reads a thing out in about four minutes, which is that he gives it back exactly as it is and does not add anything and does not leave anything out, and this is the first time in about nineteen years he has said anything in that voice that was not on a page in front of him.

In the four hundred and thirty miles from here down this river a voice like that has never been in a room with anybody in it, and the rail was cold under his hand and the passage was cold behind him, and about nine minutes is the whole of what this afternoon is going to cost him and not one word of it is going to be written down.

"It cannot be answered, because answering is a thing persons do. It cannot be refused, because a refusal has to be given by somebody. It cannot be held to a forgery, because there is nothing in it of anybody's to have forged. And it cannot be produced against a person in any proceeding, because to produce a thing against a person you have to have a person to produce it against."

He stopped for about four seconds. "**And that is a shield and not a road.**"

---

About nine hundred of these go out of this room every year into about nine hundred other rooms, and in nineteen years there has not been one of the nine hundred that anybody has shown anybody what is written at the bottom of.

"I have been the bottom of it for about nineteen years. I have never shown anybody, because nobody has ever asked me, and if anybody had asked me I would have told them the truth, and I have thought about that for about nineteen years and I am not going to take it back."

"And then the other half of it, and this is the half that took the four minutes, and it is about four hundred yards of river and about nine miles of nothing else on either side of it."

There are about ninety-one households on about four hundred and forty foot of bank in a town about four hundred and thirty miles down this river, and there is a thing standing offered on it that has never been taken up, and about sixty children under sixteen are inside it, and not one of them is named on anything and not one of them was ever asked.

The bank is about four hundred and forty foot and there are about ninety-one households on it, and he has never seen any of them, and he has signed about nine hundred of a year for about nineteen years without once being asked to be shown where the foot of one of them went.

**The same shield that keeps ninety-one households from being joined to a duty keeps them from ever finding out what they owe, and those are the same fact and not two facts.**

He put his hand flat on the rail and the rail was cold and he took it off again. "I have been the bottom of that paper for about nineteen years. There is no name on it, so there is nobody to join, so nobody is joined, and the day somebody puts a name on that paper is the day about ninety-one households find out what they have been carrying since before I was in this room, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that says what a household does when it finds out."

Nine times in nineteen years is not many, and about four feet of oak takes a hand cold and gives it back inside about four seconds, and he has never once stayed on it, and he has never once told himself he was not going to.

---

The woman of about twenty-six wrote the day against it and put her mark at the foot of the page and the mark was hers, and she did not look at the sheet again after that.

"I am not going to say thank you for that," she said, "and I want it known that I am not going to, and I am also not going to ask you one question about it, and I have worked out in about four seconds that those two are the same decision."

"Then you have worked it out," he said, "and I have not, and I have been not working it out for about nineteen years, and the four seconds are worth more than the nineteen years and I am not bitter about that, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that would let me hand it back to you if I wanted to."

---

**The count of askings in this matter is six, and it did not move in the four weeks behind him, and he is not the one who did any of them and he is not going to be.**

The number is not his and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that says a man who signs at the foot of about nine hundred sheets a year is told a count.

Nothing was paid. Nothing was sent anywhere, and the road was not walked, and the sheet went back down the lane about four minutes before the seventh hour with a day against it and a mark under a seal that has nothing under it, and the woman of about twenty-six carried it to the bottom of the cold passage and did not come back up it.

Nobody was thanked. The rail is still in that room, about four feet high, and the window on the ground floor still has a pane out of it, and a man of about thirty-four with a fair hand is four miles off a bay and has not been told one word of this and is not going to be.
