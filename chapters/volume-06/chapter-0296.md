# Chapter 296: Four Hundred Yards Of Cold Passage, A Woman Carrying Something Up It, And The Reason He Had Not Got

He came down off the old river road at about the fourth hour and walked the lane to the bottom, past the lamp at the top of it, to the door with the step worn out in front of it. Inside that door the passage goes away from you for about four hundred yards and it is colder at the far end than at the near end in every month of the year, and there is no fire in either of the two rooms it comes out into.

He is thirty-four or thereabouts and he has a fair hand, and he is not on anybody's list this week. This is the second day of the second week of the eleventh month of the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour when he got to the bottom of the lane. It is the second time he has walked the nine miles in about three weeks, and no form in this empire would pay a man for either of them.

---

On the first of the two he got to the step worn in front of the door and stood in front of it and did not go in, and everything that happened after that happened about two hundred yards further on and he had no idea of it at the time.

The door is not locked and there is nobody at it. A building off the old river road with a passage in it and two rooms at the end of the passage is not a thing a man is turned away from, because there is nobody in it whose job that is.

A woman of about forty came up the passage from the door end carrying a scuttle and a lamp and a cloth, on her way to the far end, and she was not hurrying.

"Are you after the far room?" she said.

"I do not know what I am after."

"Then you are after the far room, because there is nothing at this end but a step and I have been going up this passage for about nine years and nobody has ever stopped at this end who was not going to the far end."

---

He went up it behind her and it is about four hundred yards and the flags are cold underfoot and there is a window about two feet wide somewhere in the middle of it with a pane gone out of it, and at about the two hundredth yard he stopped and the woman of about forty went on to the far room and did not look back and did not ask him anything, which is the ordinary way of a passage.

And that is where the reason was, and it took about four minutes, and he did not say a word out loud and nobody heard it and there is no form in this empire that would let a man say a thing like that out loud in a cold passage to a woman carrying a scuttle.

A practice is a count of things done and not a count of people, and he has carried the sentence for about three weeks without knowing what to do with it.

He has had that about three weeks and he has not got past it. What he had not seen until about the two hundredth yard of a cold passage is that he has been carrying it round a room, a lane, a kitchen and about nine miles of road, and has not once put it next to a person. The reason is that there is not one in any of those places who has done a thing on purpose and paid for it themselves.

**And there is one, and he knows the shape of him and not his name, and he is about four hundred and thirty miles down this river, and he did one in the first week of the first month of the year after the year after next in a bay with nine men in it and was answered in four minutes, and nobody sent him, and he is a man off a list of works like this one.**

---

So that is the whole of the reason, and it is not heroism and it is not a conversion, and it is four sentences long and none of them is a speech:

**A person who does a thing twice has made a practice, and a practice with one person in it is stopped by that person, and a practice with two people in it is stopped by nobody.**

**And he is not going to be the reason a person about four hundred and thirty miles down this river cannot stop it.**

And the count does not care that it is the same man who did the first one, and a count that only counted people would be a count of men and not a count of things anybody did, and that is the part he worked out in a kitchen three weeks ago and has carried since and had not once put beside himself until about the two hundredth yard of a cold passage with a woman carrying a scuttle in front of him.

He stood in the middle of a passage about four hundred yards long and it came on him that he had got a reason and no decision, and that those are two different things, and that the decision is not in the passage.

---

At the far end of the passage there are two rooms and he has never been in either of them and he is not going in either of them this week.

The rail room is the one at the end, and there is a rail in it standing about four feet off the floor. The other one is at the bottom of the passage, with a shelf in it and eleven books on that shelf and a window about two feet wide with a pane gone out of it. There is a woman at a table in that one with a page in front of her and a hand that does not vary, and she is fifty-five or thereabouts and has been in that room nineteen years.

He stood at the far end of the passage for about as long as it takes to look at a door and did not knock, and the reason he did not knock is not that he had not got a reason, and it is written down about a hundred yards back, and it is that a reason is not a decision and a man who has worked out a reason at about the two hundredth yard of a passage has not thereby decided to walk the last two hundred.

Nobody sent him. There is no fund and no list and no form and no carrier with a thing for him, and about nine people a day come up a stair four miles off and not one of them came up this one, and the whole of what brought him here is that he walked it twice.

He went back down four hundred yards of cold passage and out into a lane and stood at the bottom of it and looked at the step worn in front of the door, and then he walked nine miles back and did not stop and did not go into a shop, and he got up the four steps about the ninth hour and the lamp was not lit and there was nothing on the table.

**So that is the whole of the reason, and it is not heroism and it is not a conversion, and it is four sentences long and none of them is a speech:**

A person who does a thing twice has made a practice, and a practice with one person in it is stopped by that person, and a practice with two people in it is stopped by nobody. And he is not going to be the reason a person about four hundred and thirty miles down this river cannot stop it.

He is going to come back on the second day of the third week of this month, and it is a week away. Nothing in this empire can stop him and nothing in this empire can help him, and there is not one form anywhere that would let a man on a list of works four times a season be fetched, or asked, or required, and he is going on his own two feet on a day he is not paid for, and nobody is going to be thanked for any part of it, including him.
