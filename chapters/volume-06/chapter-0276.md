# Chapter 276: A Room Of Her Own Off The Same Dyers' Lane And About The Same Size As Another One, Nine Books For Nine Houses, A Knotted String In One Of Them, And A Woman Of Thirty-Five Who Has Found Out That She Would Rather Be A Company

She rents a room off the same dyers' lane and it is about eleven feet by fourteen, and it is not the room at the top of the lane with forty books on shelves made for taller books, and she has been in that room twice, and the two rooms being the same size is not a thing either of them has ever been troubled about. There is a bench along one wall and a table and no window that closes. She has kept the numbers of nine houses on that lane for nine years and nobody pays her a penny for any of them.

She is about thirty-five. There is a page in one of the nine with a knotted string in it that she put there herself, out of a piece of string off a parcel, in a warm room, in the twelfth month of the year after next.

This is the second day of the second week of the sixth month of the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour, and there are two houses on that lane that want a day a sum was wanted by.

---

**She has found the knotted string and she has found the woman of twenty, and she has not asked her one thing.**

The window does not close and the bench is under it, and the books are on the table and not on the bench, and there are nine of them and the table takes nine and no more, and if there had been one more than that she would have had to stack it on the bench in the light where anybody going past the lane could see it.

The knotted string was in a warm room in the twelfth month of the year after next and the woman of twenty put her own hand flat on the line that was hers and said that a column with no heading over it is a person and that she had got one and that it was not a wall.

The woman of twenty was in the room at the top of the lane about twelve weeks ago and asked for the numbers out of her own house and was refused, politely, in about nine seconds, and then asked four questions that were not about the money, and one of them was how a person would go about looking another person up, and she was told.

**And then she went up the lane, and it is twelve weeks, and the woman of thirty-five has not walked up it and has not sent anything and has not asked her anything at all.**

---

The reason is about four days old and it is not a reason she is proud of.

Asking a person a question is being served. Being served is a party. A party is the only kind of person in this empire that can be told what a thing is going to be used for.

And a woman of twenty on that lane has had a wage held since the second week of the third month of the year after and has told three of four women that she is not paid, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that would make it a small thing to be the fourth person in anything.

So she did not ask, and she is not going to, and the not asking is not restraint and it is not a wall either. It is that the woman of twenty has already been put in one thing by a class and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that will take a person out of a thing, and the woman of twenty said so herself about ten weeks ago about a book with her own name at the head of it, and that is the only thing in this matter that has ever come out of a room as a fact and not as a complaint.

---

Nine houses. Four of the nine asked about a day this week. The other five did not, and one of the five has not asked her anything since the second week of the eighth month of the year after, and she has not asked it either.

The one that had asked was the fourth house down, and it had come to the door and asked whether the day wanted was the day the money was wanted or the day the thing was wanted, and she said it was the day the money was wanted, and the woman said that she had thought so and would not have wanted to ask twice.

"**Do you have to be asked to be paid?**" the woman said, from the step, and she had not been owed anything, and she was asking because she wanted it settled that week.

"**No,**" said the woman of thirty-five, "**and I have not been paid for nine years, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that says a person who keeps nine houses' numbers is to be paid for them, and I have not asked to be, and I am not going to say here today why.**"

And nine years is how long she has kept the numbers, and in nine years she has worked out the whole of the arrangement, and it is about four lines long, and she has never said it in a room.

**A person is a thing this empire can only send papers to.**

**A company is a thing it can take to law.**

---

She said the second one out loud to the bench at about the fifth hour and then stood still for about four seconds, because of what came after it.

A bench takes the weight of a person sitting on the edge of it or standing on it, and she has been doing both for nine years, and the front edge of it is flat where nine years of standing has taken the curve off it.

A company is a thing that has wages held against it and letters given to it and a heading over it, and it can be sued, and it can be sued because it goes on being after everybody in it is gone. A person is a thing that has to be handed it. And that is the whole of the difference and it is a heading, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that would let a person take one.

Nine houses on a lane are nine houses on a lane. They are not a company. They have never been a company. And in about nine years not one of the nine has ever been able to be told what it owes to anybody, because the only way anybody finds out what they owe is by being taken to law about it, and a person cannot be taken to law about anything, and neither can nine of them.

---

And then she got to the half she did not know she had, and it took about four minutes at about the fifth hour, and it is the whole of what that room is for.

She has kept the numbers of nine houses for about nine years and nobody has ever paid her and nobody has ever asked her what she is for, and she has had about four hours a day for nine years of a thing that cannot be sued, cannot be written to, cannot be served, and cannot be produced against anybody.

And she has wanted to be able to be sued for about four days.

**That is the first thing she has ever wanted that she could get by being less of a person, and she has never once wanted anything else.** She said it out loud to a bench in a room about eleven feet by fourteen and there was nobody in the room to hear it.

Because a company can be taken to law and a person cannot, and being taken to law is the only way anybody in this empire ever finds out what they owe, and she has been the reason about four hundred and thirty miles down this river that nobody has found out, and she did it by being exact, and she would have done it anyway, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that says a person is to be pleased with that.

---

And a window is not a wall, and a wall is not a door, and a door is not a company, and all three of those are about the same thing and she has had about four days to say so.

A woman of twenty up the lane has one of about forty books with her own name at the head of it that she wrote herself, and said out loud that it is not a wall and it is not a door and it is not going to get her out of anything, and that she would say it again in front of anybody. And that is a person, and it is the only one of the forty that is anybody's.

And this woman of thirty-five has nine books and a knotted string in one of them, and about nine years of about four hundred and thirty miles of road that she has never once walked. She has not got one of her own at the head of anything and she is not going to write one, and the reason is that a name at the head of a page is a thing that can be asked about, and there is nobody she wants to ask her.

---

The third of the four women is told and the fourth is in a district, and this woman is not the fourth and is not going to be, and she is not going to say anything to the woman of twenty about any of it.

Nobody thanked her. There is not one form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be thanked for nine years of nine houses' numbers, and nobody on that lane has ever thanked her, and she has never once expected it and has thought about it more than she has said.

The two houses that wanted a day got their day, and about four of the nine want something by the end of the week, and the woman of thirty-five will do those four, and she is not going up the lane, and the lamp in that room is not lit until about the seventh hour and the day is not over.
