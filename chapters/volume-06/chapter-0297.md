# Chapter 297: A Rail About Four Feet High In It, Nine Minutes, A Woman With A Scuttle, And One Question

There are two rooms at the end of a passage about four hundred yards long off a flood terrace. The one at the bottom of the passage has a shelf in it with eleven books on the shelf and a window about two feet wide with a pane gone out of it, and the one at the end has a rail standing about four feet off the floor. The cold comes up out of the flags in every month of the year and there is no fire in either room. A door at the top end of the passage has a step worn in front of it and there is nobody at the door at any hour of the day.

He is thirty-four or thereabouts, and what he does for money is write out other men's sums so that they come out even, and there are four days a season he is on somebody's list and this is not one of them. He has a house with a door on it and a wife. He came nine miles on his own two feet and he is not going to be fetched.

This is the fourth day of the third week of the eleventh month of the year after the year after next, and it is about the fourth hour, and the woman of about twenty-six went down that lane with a rack at about the third hour and had not come back.

---

**Nobody sent him.** There is no fund, no list, no form and no carrier with a thing for him, and no instrument in this empire would let anybody send a man to ask a question, and about nine people a day come up a stair four miles off and not one of them had anything to do with this room or with him.

She was at a table at the far end with a page in front of her and her hand on it, and she is fifty-five or thereabouts and has been in that room nineteen years and writes the minutes and advises nobody and asks nothing.

He had it ready in about four days and he had said it to a wall twice, and the reason was his own and nobody had given it to him, and the reason is four sentences long and he has got it.

He said the shape of it first, because he had listened to two people say the shape of a thing and both of them were right to.

"I am going to ask you one question," he said, "and it is about a line in a book, and I have not got your name and I do not want it, and nobody has sent me and there is no form anywhere in this empire that would let anybody send me, and I am not going to be able to say afterwards that anybody arranged this. I would like it noticed that I said the shape of it before I did it."

"Say the question, then."

---

"There is a line in the second of the eleven books," he said, "on the second shelf down, with the day and the hour on it and your own name at the foot of it. **Did you mean it.**"

It took about a second and a half. She did not stand up and she did not put the page down.

"Yes," she said. "**Once is enough.**"

And that was the whole of it. Four words and then four words, and there was no name in either of them, and nothing in either of them could be used against anybody, and no part of it was going anywhere.

---

**A minute with an answer in it is an order, and a clerk of that office has never entered an order in nineteen years, and there is no form that lets her.** She is not going to enter one on the fourth day of the third week of the eleventh month of the year after the year after next because a stranger came up a passage.

So the four words are not in a book. They are in a woman of about fifty-five who has been in that room nineteen years, and in a man of about thirty-four, and in a woman of about forty who came up the passage with a scuttle at about the fourth hour to light the lamp in that room and who is not one of the three clerks and is not in this matter at all.

And there is not one form anywhere in this empire that will let anybody ask that woman of about forty what she heard in that room, and a question put in that building is a question put into a record, and the record is nineteen years of a woman of fifty-five copying a hand into a book, and there is no form for adding a stranger to it.

---

It went on for about nine minutes and it is not nine minutes of talking.

The woman of about forty lit the lamp in that room because it is not lit until about the seventh hour and she was early, and she did it while he was standing there and she did not look at either of them while she did it, and then she went down the passage.

After that it was four minutes of a clerk of about fifty-five telling him what a second shelf down in a room is, and about a hundred and a half of sheets a year, and how a man who copies a hand for nineteen years learns to read the hand and not the person, and she said all of it in the ordinary voice at about the fourth hour and none of it was about the line.

**He did not ask her the second thing.** There was a second thing and it is not asked, and it is not going to be asked in any room by anybody, and the reason he did not ask it is not a reason and is that there is no form in this empire that would let him be told what the answer would be used for, and no instrument anywhere in this empire lets a man find out what a thing is going to be used for before he asks it.

"Did you put it there because somebody asked you to?"

"No."

"Nobody asked you."

"Nobody asked me."

---

Then she said the thing that is the whole of what the day had in it, and she said it in about nine words, and she said it to a page.

"**There is no form in this empire for a person to be asked whether she meant a thing, and there is not going to be one, and I have known that for nineteen years and I have never once had to think about it, and it has taken a man of about thirty-four nine miles and about nine minutes to find out what I would not write down.**"

And she put her hand flat on the page and went on with it, and he was still in the room.

**She was not thanked. Nobody in that building thanked him and he did not thank her,** and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be thanked for asking a question, and there is never going to be one, and the not thanking is the ordinary condition of a thing anybody did and not a cruelty in it.

---

He stood in that room for about four minutes after and the rail was about four feet high and the lamp was lit and the woman of about twenty-six came up the passage at about the seventh hour with an empty rack on her hip and went past the door without stopping at it, and she is not in this matter and never has been and has not been told one word of the nine minutes and is not going to be.

**The whole of what is in this room is three people, and one of the three is a woman of about forty whose name is not in this matter and is not going to be written down.** One of the three is a clerk of about fifty-five who has just said that nobody is going to ask her a second time. One of the three is a man of about thirty-four, who came nine miles, and who worked out about three-quarters of the way down the passage on the way here that he is not going to be able to take any of it back.

He had not worked out the rest of it. The rest of it is about four hundred and thirty miles off and it is a man of thirty-eight with a mark on the inside of his wrist and a labour a gate can refuse, and he is not in this room and is not sent and does not go, and the road is walked zero times.

He went down four hundred yards of cold passage and out into a lane and did not stop at the step and walked nine miles back in about as long as he came.

---

The lamp in that room is not lit until about the seventh hour and it is now lit and the rail is standing about four feet off the floor and the second of the eleven books is on the second shelf down where it has been for about ninety-one weeks, and it is still a question, and it is not an answer and it is not an order, and a clerk of that office has not entered one in nineteen years and did not enter one today.

**The two people in the boxes are in the boxes.** The form that put them there has not been asked to be void by anybody, because no form in this empire has ever been asked to be void and there is no procedure for it, and the person who administers one is a clerk who is not a villain and has done about nine hundred of them correctly and has done this one correctly as well, and nobody in that room asked her about a box or a hat or a wage.

Nobody was thanked. Nobody was sent. Nothing was written down. The pane is still out of the window at the bottom of the passage and the rail is still four feet high and there is no instrument in this empire that could put four words spoken in a room on a shelf where about a hundred and a half sheets a year go, and there is not one, and there never is going to be one.
