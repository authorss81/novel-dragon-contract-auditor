# Chapter 289: A Rack Of Letters Going Down A Cold Passage Twenty-Two Weeks After Five Of Nine Went Into A Book And Four Went Back On A Shelf, And A Woman Of About Twenty-Six Who Has Counted In Her Head And Is Not Going To Write It Down

The cold comes up off the flags of a passage about four hundred yards long whatever the season is, and it ends in a room with a rail in it that stands about four feet off the floor. The other end of that passage is downstairs, and there is a shelf in it with eleven books on the shelf, and a window about two feet wide with a pane out of it.

There are three clerks in this building. She is the youngest of the three and she has been in this building about four years, and she is not in this matter and has never been in it.

This is the fourth day of the third week of the ninth month of the year after the year after next, and it is about the sixth hour, and there is no fire in either of the rooms at the end of that passage.

---

**Twenty-two weeks ago a carrier came up that passage with a rack on her hip and put five things on a table and put four back on a shelf, and she signed for the five, and the five are the only ones that exist.**

The mark she put at the foot of that page says the thing arrived and was received and could be produced afterwards against whoever the five were addressed to. It was in her hand and it was correct, and she entered about four hundred others that year in a hand that does not vary.

**The four went into a rack, and the rack went back on a shelf in a room with a pane out of the window, and they had not arrived anywhere and had not been received by anybody and could not be produced against anybody at all.**

There is no form anywhere in this empire that can put a person back into a thing they are written down as not having received, and nobody in this building knows they are written down at all. A thing written down as not having arrived is not a thing anybody goes and looks for.

---

And then she got to the part that took the twenty-two weeks, and she has never said any of it in a room, and there is nobody in this room to say it in.

She has not asked one question about it. Not of the carrier, who is paid ninepence at a lock and is on a list and has not been asked what she is carrying in about six years. Not of the clerk of about fifty-five at the other end of that passage, who has worked in that room for nineteen years and has never once opened the second of the eleven books, and who has been asked about four hundred questions in that room in four years and not one of them has been about the shelf.

**And not of the man of about sixty-one, who is in this building twice in a season on his own feet, and who is the only one of three hands on about nine hundred sheets a year that can be asked anything at all, and whom nobody in this empire has ever asked one question about anything.**

She has three questions for him. She wrote them down in her own head about nine weeks ago and she has not asked one of them and she is not going to, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that would let a clerk of this office put a question to a person who has come in on his own feet, and a question put in this building is a question put into a record.

"And which of those two is it?" she said, to the rail, at about the sixth hour.

The rail is about four feet high and it is about as cold as the passage, and it does not answer, and there is nobody else in that room.

---

**What she has done in twenty-two weeks is looked at the rack.**

Every day, at about the third hour, when it comes off the carrier's hip and goes on the shelf by the window. Not touched. Counted, in her head, the racks that go down that passage and come back with four on them and not five.

She has got the number. And she is not going to write it down, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that would let her, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that says a number heard in a room may not be written down in a book afterwards.

About nine hundred of them go out of that room every year into about nine hundred other rooms, and not one of them has ever been counted by anybody.

Four letters at a time. And the whole of the number is four letters at a time, because those are the only ones that come back with nothing in them. The five go into a book with her mark under them and are true. The four go on a shelf and are true as well, in a different way, which is the way of a thing nobody is going to do anything about.

And that number is the only measure this empire has of how many people are being held by a thing nobody can be asked about, and it is in the head of a woman of about twenty-six who is not going to say it out loud.

---

**And she has worked out that not asking is the whole of what is standing between her and those four people, and it is not a skill and it is not a decision and she did not choose it.**

She worked it out in about four minutes, and it took her about four minutes, and it was the fourth day of the third week of the sixth month.

A man of about sixty-one had said something in this room in about nine minutes, and she had not asked him a question about it and had not thanked him for it, and she has had the two of those for twelve weeks now, and they are the same decision, and she worked that out in about four seconds on the day.

She is a person and not a thing anybody can be held to, and that is the whole of her protection, and it is exactly the thing the four people have got and not one thing more, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that would let her make them more protected by telling them.

---

And there is nothing whatever to be done about any of that, and nobody in this building is going to do anything.

The sheet with a heading at the top of it and a seal at the foot of it and nothing under the seal came up in the fourth week of the sixth month and went back down the lane with a day against it and a mark under a seal that has nothing under it. It comes about twice a year. It goes back. Nobody reads it out.

And there is a woman four feet away at the other end of that passage who entered a question in the second of the eleven books on the fourth day of the third week of the ninth month of the year after next.

She entered it in her own hand, with the day and the hour on it, at nobody's request, and has had it there about twenty-four weeks, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that would let one clerk ask another clerk whether she meant it.

They have worked four feet apart for four years and have never asked each other one thing about the shelf.

---

Nobody thanked her. There is not one form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be thanked for signing for five things and not asking one question about four others for twenty-two weeks, and there is not going to be one.

The rail is still in that room about four feet high, and the window on the ground floor still has a pane out of it, and the second of the eleven books is on the shelf where it has been since the ninth month of the year after next, and a man of about thirty-four with a fair hand is four miles off a bay and has not been told one word of this and is not going to be.
