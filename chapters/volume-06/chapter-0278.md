# Chapter 278: A Room She Rents About Four Hundred Yards From A Bay, A Notice Of Six Numbered Lines That Came Up A Lane And Is Not Answerable, And A Woman Of About Twenty-Four Who Read It Out Loud In About Four Minutes And Has Not Been Asked One Thing

She rents a room about four hundred yards from a bay, and she has rented the same one for about nine years, and it is a room and not a house, and there is a bed in it and a table and a lamp on the table and about four feet of bare wall. The wall is bare because there is nothing to put on it. That is the whole of the furnishing and it has not changed in nine years and it is not going to.

She is fifty-one and she is the foreman of the Slade Cut, and it is an office with no office in it, being a thing nine families agreed to about eleven years ago, and she has been in a box since the first week of the seventh month of the year after, and it was entered by a class, and nobody has ever asked her about it.

This is the fourth day of the fourth week of the sixth month of the year after the year after next, and it is about the seventh hour, and the lamp is lit.

---

A notice came up the lane on the fourth day of the third week of this month, which is the day a copy of it was written down about nine miles off, and it is a notice of six numbered lines with a day entered against it, and there is a copy of one in a room about nine miles off with a pane out of the window, and it came up here because everything comes up here.

The bed is against the wall under the four feet of nothing, and the table stands about a foot off it, and she eats standing up at the table, and has eaten standing up for about nine years, and the lamp is on it because a rented room with four feet of bare wall has one place a lamp can go.

Six numbered lines. Line two asks that no inference of any kind be drawn from the absence of any name. Line six declines to name whoever holds the office at all.

She cannot read a paragraph. She has never been able to. She did the arithmetic of about sixty children on the back of her own hand with a stick in about nine years, and she knows what her own arithmetic is worth, and she has never once pretended it was worth more than that.

The arithmetic went on the back of her own hand in about nine years and the sixty children are on there in a sum she cannot read it now without putting the hand flat on the table, and about half of it has been rubbed off at the knuckle where her hand goes across when she is carrying a barrow.

So the notice went up the lane about four hundred yards on the fourth day of the third week, because it could not be read in that bay by anybody who was going to be paid for reading it, and because there is not one form anywhere in this empire that says a foreman may have a page read to her.

---

There are two people in that cut who can read a page and the first of them is on a list.

A girl of seventeen at the back of that bay is on a written engagement at the rate the list is set at. The rate is the rate and it is on the list and she is on it, and everybody in that building knows she reads, and it is a thing about her that is written down and can be looked up in about four seconds by anybody who has the list in front of him.

**Halla Wray did not send it to her, and she has not been spoken to, and she is not going to be, and she was not thanked, and nobody is going to send for her.**

The second one is a woman of about twenty-four who keeps a book at that end of the works, and she is not on any list in that building and has never been on one, and she came up the lane behind the notice on the day it came up and read the whole of it out loud in about four minutes standing at the bench in front of nine men, and she did it because the foreman could not and there was nobody else there.

Nine men in front of her and a sheet of six numbered lines and about four minutes of somebody else's voice, and nobody has read anything out in a bay in as long as she has been standing behind one, and about nine people were in there and not one of them wrote down that it had happened.

---

**And Halla Wray has had a week to work out what that is.** What it is, is that the only person in that cut she can use is the only person in that cut nobody can account for.

The girl of seventeen is on a written engagement and can be found by anybody holding a piece of paper. The woman of twenty-four is on nothing, has never been on anything, and cannot be found by anybody at all, and that is the only reason a foreman of fifty-one in a bay on a wet afternoon in the sixth month could hand her six numbered lines and stand there while she read them out.

A person who is not asked has not been given a duty. That is the sentence, and Halla Wray has known it since about the seventh month of next year and has said it out loud in that bay about four times in nine years, and it has never once cost anybody anything, and it is the only reason that woman of twenty-four has spent a week reading things nobody asked her to read and cannot be told what any of it is for and cannot be told to stop and has never been told that she could.

---

She has not asked her one thing, and she is not going to, and the not asking is not restraint and it is not a wall either.

Asking a woman of twenty-four a question in a bay is being served. Being served is a party. A party is the only person who can be told what a thing is going to be used for.

And Halla Wray has been in a box since the first week of the seventh month of the year after, and she is not going to put a second person in one in the sixth month of a later year because she was alone in a room with a lamp.

She is in a box and nine men know it and there is no form anywhere in this empire that will let her be asked about it, and she is not going to spend that on a woman of twenty-four who can read. "**And is anybody in that building going to ask you what you read it for,**" she said, to the wall, "**or am I?**"

"Neither," she said. "That is why I am telling you and that is why you will not be able to say I used you." "**She has not asked you, and you know she has not,**" she said, to the wall, at about the sixth hour, "**and I am telling you that I know, because a person who is not asked has not been given a duty, and I would like that written down in that bay in my own voice before anybody works out that I have been using you.**"

---

**And a man of about fifty-five with a chain went down a corridor about four hundred yards off a flood terrace in the second week of the fifth month of the year after the year after next with a lamp in his hand, and she sent him, and she has not asked him what was at the end of it, and she is not going to.**

There is a door about nine foot from the far end of that corridor and it has not been opened in nine years and there is no name on it and it is not on a list. He came back with the lamp not on the hook and said nothing about it and put it on a bench and stood in about nine foot by eleven while the day's tally was read out. Four seconds of a bay a week is what that man has had for nine years, and nobody has ever paid him for it, and nobody is going to ask him.

She has now made two people in that cut do a thing on her behalf in about six weeks, and neither of them was asked, and nine men know about the first and four people know about the second, and that is a worse kind of findable than a name, and she said that out loud to a lamp about four hours ago and to nobody else.

---

Nobody was thanked. There is not one form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be thanked for reading four minutes of a notice out loud in a bay for nothing, and there is not going to be one, and the woman of twenty-four did not expect it and Halla Wray did not give it.

The bench is against the north wall and about nine men had gone out of the bay by the time the light went, and a foreman of fifty-one was standing behind the bench on her own, which is the arrangement and has been for about eleven years, and the lamp in the bench was still cold and about nine men had gone and one had not.

The four men in that bay who cannot read a paragraph are still four and were not asked one thing this week and were not named, and no reason about them was given, and a season has closed and nobody decided that and nothing was resolved.

The girl of seventeen at the back of that bay is on a written engagement at the rate the list is set at, and she was not spoken to, and she was not thanked, and nobody is going to send for her, and she is not going to start.

A guarantee stands offered and unanswered on about four hundred and forty foot of bank about four hundred and thirty miles down this river with about sixty children under sixteen inside it, and it is not taken up and it is not withdrawn and nobody is going to be told anything about it this month. Halla Wray is going to do the ordinary part of the day tomorrow and the day after, and the lamp in that bay is not lit until about the seventh hour, and it was not lit when she came back, and nobody thanked anybody.
