# Chapter 251: A Tally Copied Out In A Fair Hand, And What It Is To Be Found By Your Own Question

The bay is about nine foot by eleven with a bench along the north wall and a lamp in it that is not lit until about the seventh hour, and the hired stone store is about four hundred yards up the road with a bench of its own in it, and those two are the only buildings on that bank that anybody keeps anything in.

He is a man of about thirty-four and he has a trade, and the trade is a hand that writes other people's figures out fair, and he is paid by the day for it and he is on the list of works four times a season like everybody else in that bay, and his name goes on the list in the same hand as the other eight and it is not his hand.

It is the second day of the first week of the twelfth month of the year after next. There are nine men in that bay and a foreman of fifty-one behind the bench and a girl of seventeen at the back on a written engagement at the rate the list is set at. There is a man of about fifty-five with a chain, and a man of about thirty from the second gang, and a man of about forty from the third, and a man of about twenty-eight, and a man of about twenty, and Marek Kest, who came in at about the fourth hour and can be told to stop by anybody with a wage in his hand.

**And a foreman of fifty-one who has been in a box since the first week of the seventh month of the year after said nothing to him at all, for the whole of the afternoon, and she has not said one word to him since a day in the eleventh month when he stood in a doorway on that bank and told a book-keeper of thirty-eight what he had come to do.**

---

He wrote the day's tally out from about the fourth hour on a sheet of his own that went in under the bench, and he wrote it fair, and he wrote the nine names and the day's work against each of them and the number of the hold where the water was, and the number of the hold has not moved since the fourth week of the fourth month of the year after.

A foreman reads a tally and a foreman signs it and the hand that signs it does not have to be fair, and the hand that writes it out fair is the one nobody ever looks at. He has known that for about eleven years and he had never once said so out loud until a man of about fifty-five with a chain said a version of it in a yard in a different month, and the version the man with the chain gave was better and he told the bay so at the time.

"Put the third one down as half," said a man of about twenty, from the doorway, about the fourth hour, and he put it down as half.

---

Nothing happened in that bay for the rest of the afternoon, and that is what a bay is for, and he had asked for about four years of it before anybody told him that asking for it was not possible.

Marek Kest put his hand into a bucket of stones at about the fifth hour and worked until about the sixth, and said one thing, and said it to the wall and not to anybody.

"There is a difference between a man who is paid and a man who can be told to stop, and the difference is about fourpence a day, and I know which one I am and I chose it out of my own hand and I am not going to be asked about it again."

Nobody asked him about it again. A man of about thirty from the second gang said *good* out loud, once, to the stones, and nobody picked it up. In about six years of standing beside that man this man of about thirty-four had never once heard him ask anybody anything, and he was not going to hear it begin with a man who works in a bucket.

---

He did not ask the four anything either, and there are four in that bay who cannot read a paragraph, and he knows which four they are, and he has known since a yard in the third week of the sixth month of the year after next when he wrote a number on the back of his own hand in a pencil that was nearly blunt.

**The number is gone.** It went in about four weeks, the way a number goes off the back of a hand in a wash. He looked for it on that hand every morning for a month and then stopped looking, and what he has instead is a bit of paper in the band of his hat with four marks on it and no name on it and no day on it. A thing in a hat is not a document and cannot be produced, and there is nobody in this empire that could take it off him and read it out in a room.

The four marks are not the four men. He is careful about that and he has been careful about it since the yard, and he will not write down in which gang each of them works because the day he does that it is a list.

---

The lamp is not lit until about the seventh hour and he does his own work in the light that comes in off the door, and the light goes at about the sixth hour in that month, and after that there is a lamp on the bank and it is a long way off a bay and it does not reach into one.

A foreman of fifty-one has said nothing to him at all, and that is the whole of what she gave him, and he wants it recorded somewhere that he noticed and did not once find it a strange thing to be given, and that a week ago he would have found it strange, and that it is not strange now, and that is the price and it is the second half of the price.

"You are still on the list," said a man of about forty from the third gang, at about the sixth hour, not meaning anything by it.

"I am still on the list," he said.

"You are on the list the same as anybody."

"That is the only thing I have ever wanted out of that page," he said, "and I have got it, and it took me about eleven years and one afternoon, and the one afternoon is the expensive half and nobody is going to refund it."

---

He came out of that bay at about the seventh hour and the lamp came on about four minutes after he had shut the sheet of his own away. He passed the hired stone store on his way and did not go in, because it is hired and locked and the key belongs to a Company whose whole money is four shillings a year and all of it is the rent of the building. He has no business in it, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that gives a man a business in a room he does not work in.

The bench in there has three pieces of paper on it and he knows that the same way everybody four hundred yards from it knows it, which is to say that he has been told there are three and he has not asked what they are and he is not going to.

---

The landing outside his own house has two steps and a door with a hasp on it, and the door is shut, and his wife had the lamp lit before he was up the stairs, which is a thing she does and has done for about nine years.

"You were in late," she said, and then she said the other thing, and she said it standing with a plate in her hand and she did not put the plate down first.

"A woman came to that door about four days ago. I told her you were not in. She asked were you in on the fourth day of the fourth week of the eleventh month, and I said no, and she said a boy from the Slade end had told her a man with a fair hand walked about four miles to a bay and asked a woman what a word meant, and I said I did not know anybody that did that, and she wrote nothing down and she said she would come again, and she has not come again."

---

He put his bag down in the passage and did not go into the room she was in, and he stood in the passage for about a minute and a half with his hand on the bag.

"Who was she?"

"A woman of about thirty-five with a wet hood on. I have not seen her before and I have not seen her since." His wife turned the plate over. "**And there is not one form anywhere in this empire that says a woman at a door has to say yes to a stranger, and she did not ask me a question about you, she asked me about a day.**"

"You could have said you did not know the day."

"I could have said anything at all and I would have been right and it would have made no difference, because she had the day before she came to the door. She did not come here to find out. She came here to be able to say she had been."

---

**Four times a season is the whole of what he does now, and he has not asked for more, and there is not one form in this empire for a man to ask for more work on a list he is already on.** He asked for a day of it for about four years and never got one, and what he has instead is four days a season on a page with his name on it in a fair hand that is not his, and the four is more than he had and less than he is worth and he has worked that out twice and has not said it out loud to anybody.

The trade is a strange one to have at thirty-four. It is not a trade anybody trains a boy into and it is not in any list of trades. It is that a man can write a figure out so that another man can read it back without guessing, and a foreman will pay a day for that out of a Company's money that is four shillings a year. A man who has the hand for it is not paid more than a man who has not, because there is no form anywhere that says what a fair hand is worth.

---

That is the whole of what it cost, and he has gone over it about nine times in a week and it does not get smaller, and there is nobody in it to be angry with, and that is the part he was not ready for.

A person who does a thing in a room is a party from that day on, and a party is the only person who can be told what a thing is going to be used for, and he understood all three of those sentences before he said them, in a kitchen, six weeks before, and he paid for them in about eleven words in a doorway and he was right about the price on the day.

**The price is not a paper.** He has been waiting since the bay for a paper and nothing has come, and there is no form in this empire that would send one, and a man who is findable does not need one. A woman with a wet hood can carry a day in her head four hundred yards and a boy from the Slade end can carry a description of a man's hands, and between them they have made him a person who can be looked up by a person who has nothing to gain and no office and no reason to be kind to him.

"I would rather it was a paper," he said. "A paper I could put in a drawer."

"Why?"

"Because a drawer shuts."

---

His wife did not answer that, and it was not the kind of question that wanted one, and she took the plate through to the room. He came in behind her about four minutes afterwards and did not say anything about the door for the rest of the evening, and she did not either. Neither of them mentioned that she had told him and he had not thanked her and that she was not expecting to be, and that there is not one form in this empire that would require it of her and there is not going to be one.

Nine years of this house, and about nine years of a woman in it who had not been asked one thing by anybody until a plate and a door one evening, and he has not been able to work out whether that is a good thing and he has stopped trying, because the answer is that he made it and he would make it again and it does not improve with being looked at.

---

He had said in a doorway that he was not going to do it again, and the reason was his own and not a foreman's, and he had said it once and had not improved on it since.

He went over it that night, in the ordinary way, on his own, the way a man goes over a thing he has said out loud once and does not intend to say twice. **A person who does a thing twice has made a practice, and a practice grows a heading over it on its own without anybody deciding anything, and a heading is how a person becomes a company, and a company is a thing this empire can take to law.**

It is still true. He has not found anything wrong with it since the bay and he has not gone looking with any real hope of finding anything wrong with it, and that is the part that has changed and it is not a small part.

A week ago the reason was an argument. A week ago it was a thing he believed in a bay and could hold up. Tonight it is a thing he has looked for the seam in, twice, on purpose, and has not found, and the not-finding is worse than the argument, because an argument can be dropped and a thing with no seam in it cannot.

---

Nobody thanked him, and he was not thanked at the time and he is not being thanked now, and there is a woman about four hundred and thirty miles up this river who has said out loud that she would like it written down that she did not expect to be thanked either, and he has never met her and is not going to.

The lamp on the bank is lit and about four hundred yards off and it does not reach into a bay. There are about four people in a bay who cannot read a paragraph and have still never been asked one question and there is a finding about that in a ledger in a book-keeper's own hand about four hundred yards up that road which is not his to improve and which he has not thought about improving. A foreman of fifty-one is in a box and a woman of twenty is in a box and a clerk of thirty-four has about four hundred bought and unfilled blanks under a counter and a clerk of about fifty-five has a question in a book about nine miles off with her own name at the foot of it.

**And the count has not moved since a bay on the fourth day of the fourth week of the eleventh month of the year after next, and it is not going to move because of him, and the whole of what he bought is that, and he does not get to be thanked for it and he would not know what to do with it if he were.**
