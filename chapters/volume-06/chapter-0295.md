# Chapter 295: A Cold Passage About Four Hundred Yards Long, And A Number In A Head That Has Gone Up

This building has a passage in it and the passage is longer than the building is wide by a good deal, and the flags of it come up cold underfoot in every month of the year, and there is a rail at one end of it standing about four feet off the floor. Downstairs at the other end there is a shelf with eleven books on it, and a window about two feet wide with a pane gone out of it, and nobody has put the pane back in about four years. Three clerks work in this building. She is the youngest of the three and she has been in it about four years, and she is not in this matter and has never been in it.

This is the second day of the first week of the eleventh month of the year after the year after next, and it is about the third hour, and there is no fire in either of the rooms at the end of that passage, and the clerk of about fifty-five is at the other end with a page and a hand that does not vary.

---

**What she has done in about forty-one weeks is looked at the rack, and every day at about the third hour, when it comes off a carrier's hip and goes on the shelf by the window, not touched, counted, in her head, the racks that go down that passage and come back light by one.**

She has got the number. She is not going to write it down, and nothing in this empire would let her write it, and nothing in it forbids a clerk setting a number she has counted in her own head down in a book afterwards either, and that is the reason she has not.

And the number has gone up since she counted it in the seventh month, and she has not said by how much and she is not going to, and a number that has gone up in about seventeen weeks is a number that is still going up while a woman of about fifty-five sits at the other end of four hundred yards of cold passage copying a hand into a book she advises nobody about.

---

About nine hundred of them go out of that room every year into about nine hundred other rooms in this empire, and the number of them that anybody has ever counted is nothing at all.

Four letters at a time, and the whole of the number is four letters at a time, because a carrier's hip holds five and comes back with the fifth one gone off it. She signs for five and the five go into a book under her hand. The other four go onto a shelf in the room below and stay there, and a thing on a shelf is true in the way a thing nobody will ever fetch is true.

And nothing anywhere in this empire keeps a figure on how many people are sitting inside a thing that nobody is able to be asked about, and the figure is in the head of a woman of about twenty-six who is not going to say it out loud, and there is no column in this building it could go in.

---

She has three questions for the man of about sixty-one. She wrote them down in her own head about forty-one weeks ago and she has not asked one of them and she is not going to.

He is in this building twice in a season on his own feet, and he is the only one of three hands on about nine hundred sheets a year that can be asked anything at all, and one person asked him one question about a seal, once, in this room, and got an answer, and about nothing else. The other two of the three hands are a man of about seventy who fills a column in and a clerk of about fifty-five who writes the minutes, and neither of the two can be asked about the foot of a sheet because the foot of a sheet is not theirs.

A question put in this building is a question put into a record, and being asked is being served, and being served is a party, and a party is the only person who can be told what a thing is going to be used for, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for serving a person with a question, let alone with that one.

So the three questions stay in her head, and they are not a grievance and they are not a debt and she has never written them down and she is not going to.

---

And then she got to the part that took the whole of the hour, and it is the thing she has been getting wrong for about forty-one weeks and she got it right in about four minutes on the second day of this week.

She had been thinking of the number as a thing that would be useful. That was wrong, and it was wrong in the ordinary way, and she had gone about four hundred yards of cold passage every working day for four years with it.

**A number in a book is a column, and a column wants a heading, and a heading over a number like that one is a heading over about nine hundred rooms, and about nine hundred is a class, and a class is a heading, and that is how a number that nobody counted becomes a thing with a name over it and a person underneath it and a wage against it.**

She is not going to give it a heading. She is not going to give it anything. She has got a number in her head and she is going to take it with her when she stops doing this, which is a thing she has worked out she will do, and there is no form in this empire that would let her hand it on and there is nobody she is going to hand it to.

And that is the same as the thing a man of about sixty-one said at a rail in the third week of the sixth month and I have had four feet of a room for that, and I am not going to say the rest of it in this passage because it is four hundred yards long and cold and there is nobody in it.

---

The rail is about four feet high and it is about as cold as the passage and it does not answer.

At about the fourth hour a carrier came up the passage with a rack on her hip and she signed for the things on it, and she has not said one word to the carrier about what she is carrying in or where it came from, and the carrier is paid ninepence at a lock and is on a list and has not been asked what she is carrying in about six years.

"Do you want the room at this end shut or the room at the far end?" the carrier said.

"The far end. And take the rack straight back down, and it goes back down the lane and it comes up again in the morning, and it is not late and it is never going to be late."

The carrier went down the passage and the cold came up after her and the rail stood in it.

---

And then there is the other thing in her, and it is three questions long, and she has not asked one of them in forty-one weeks and she is not going to, and nobody has ever come to this room and asked her whether she meant anything either.

There is a woman four feet away at the lower end of that passage. She wrote a question into the second of the eleven books on the fourth day of the third week of the ninth month of the year after next, on her own motion, at nobody's request, with the day and the hour on it. It has been on that shelf about ninety weeks, and it is the only thing in nineteen years of that shelf that anybody in this building has ever entered about what ought to have happened instead.

**And not one instrument in this empire would let one clerk ask another clerk whether she meant it, and there is no form for it, and there has not been one for four years, and the two of them have worked four feet apart for four years and have never asked each other one thing about the shelf.**

She is not going to ask her. She is not going to tell her. And there is nothing in this building that she is going to do differently in the morning, and the number in her head will be a little higher, and the broken pane has still not been put back in the window, and nobody thanked anybody and nobody is going to, and the woman of about twenty-six went down four hundred yards of cold passage at about the fifth hour and out into a lane and did not look back at the rail.
