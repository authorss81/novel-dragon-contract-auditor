# Chapter 274: A Room Off A Dyers' Lane About Eleven Feet By Fourteen, A Window With A Broken Light In It, Sixteen Books And One Of Them With Her Own Name At The Head Of It, And The Shape Of A Question Worked Out By A Woman Of Twenty Who Is Not Going To Put It Anywhere

The room is eleven feet by fourteen and it is off a dyers' lane in a furnace town about four hundred and thirty miles up this river, and it is hers by the week. There is a bench along one wall, a window with a broken light in it, and about forty books on shelves that were built for taller books, so that a woman of twenty has to reach up for the top of every one of them. The light went out of that window before she came into the room. She knows which of the forty is which by where her arm goes and has not looked at a shelf in about sixteen weeks.

The dye house pays her. The books pay her nothing at all. Eleven houses on that lane and four houses in three other districts, kept by her out of that room since she was seventeen, and not one of the fifteen has ever been asked what it costs her to keep it.

This is the fourth day of the fourth week of the fifth month of the year after the year after next. Her wage stopped in the second week of the third month of the year after and it is stopped now, and the word for it is held.

---

Ten weeks ago a woman of about thirty-five stood at the bench in this room and asked how a person would go about looking another person up, and Nell Kest told her, and the answer took about four seconds and it is the shape of a street and a name and a number.

And then the woman had asked the other half of it, which was what the book with her own name at the head of it is for, and Nell Kest had said that it is for her being in it and not for her being hard to find, and had never been able to work out which of those two is the more dangerous.

She has had ten weeks and she has not worked it out. What she has worked out is the other thing, and the other thing has taken the ten weeks, and it is the shape of a question.

---

**In this empire a question stops being a question and becomes a thing the moment there is a day on it and a name at the foot of it.**

That is not a rule anybody made. She has worked it out in about ten weeks out of about nine hundred of the same shape of paper a year and about four hundred years of the same arrangement underneath them, and the working out took her about four minutes, and the four minutes were on the second day of the eighth week, and she has not improved on it since.

A thing with a day and a name at the foot of it is a thing somebody can be held to. It is a thing somebody can be served with, and being served is a party, and a party is the only kind of person this empire can tell what a thing is going to be used for.

---

She had a pencil out and had it flat on the bench on about the second day of the eighth week, and she had it there for about four minutes, and she did not write anything on anything.

**It is not restraint and she has been careful about that for about three years.** It is that there is no way to write the first line of a thing like that and stop. There is no form on which a question sits at the top and a refusal sits at the bottom, and there is no sheet anybody could be handed with *this is a question* written on it. If she puts a day and her own name on anything at all then she has started something that will want a second thing on it inside about sixteen weeks, and the second thing will be a column, and the column will want a heading, and then it will be a machine in a room and not hers.

So there is no first line. There is only the whole of it or nothing, and she has had about three years of being very careful.

---

**And the cost of it is the wage, and she has known the cost of it since about the second week of the third month of the year after, and this is the first time she has said the cost out loud in a room.**

"If anybody in this empire could serve me with a question of my own that I had put in a book, then they would be able to tell me what my wage is being held for, and I have wanted to know that every working day for about two years."

She said it to the bench and the broken light in the window and the forty books on shelves made for taller books, and there was nobody else in the room, and she did not say it twice.

Nobody has ever sent her a paper that could be answered. Nobody has ever been able to. There is not one form anywhere in this empire that makes a woman of twenty who is not paid for keeping eleven houses' books into a person that anything can be put in front of, and the not being able is the only reason those four books are still hers, and the not being able is also the reason she has never once known what is being done with her money.

**That is one fact and not two facts, and she is the only person alive who has both halves of it and she has told nobody.**

---

The four that come every week are the same four. A day a sum was wanted by, and whether a thing wanted in a district wanted in the lane, and whether the third house down had paid, and whether the second one is still holding the book open at a page with a knotted string in it. That last one is the oldest of the four and it has been asked about four times and the answer has not changed in about thirty-nine weeks.

She did not say a sum out loud. She has never said one out loud in this room and she is not going to start, and the reason has not changed, and it is that a sum said out loud in a room is in nine mouths by the second hour, and there is not one form anywhere that says a number heard in a room may not be written down in a book.

A man came to the door at about the fifth hour on the second day of the second week and wanted to know whether a figure in the book at the top of the lane was a figure or a sum, and she told him it was a sum, and he said thank you, and she said that was the ordinary way and not a favour, and he went away.

"**And can I be down for a sum,**" said the woman of the third house down, from the step, about a week ago, "**or only the house?**"

"You are in it either way and you only get to choose which line, and neither of them is yours to be taken out of, and I have said that to nine people and four of them did not like it, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that says a person may be put out of a page their street put them on."

---

**Whoever it was that changed it is not coming, and she is not going to be the one who goes looking, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that would send anybody to look.**

That is the whole of what she knows about it and it is the whole of what anybody in this lane knows about it, and it is the reason she has not got a second copy of anything.

It is that a thing which was changed once by a person is now a thing that has no person in it, and there is nowhere in this empire to send a paper about a thing that has no person in it, and about four hundred of those go past this room a year in about nine hundred other rooms, and not one of them has ever been stopped.

The third of the four women came here about ten weeks ago. The fourth is in a district and there is a foreman off a dye end in this city with a piece of paper in the inside of his coat who has said no once on a stair about a thing that is not his.

**Nell Kest is not going to walk four hundred yards up a lane to the fourth one this week, and she is not going to put the fourth one in front of the third one, and the reason is not that the third one went well.** The third one asked her a question about herself. That is what happened here about ten weeks ago, and the fourth one has not been asked anything by anybody, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that says she has to be told.

---

The window with the broken light in it has a landlord in another city who has never seen the room, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that requires a woman who rents a room by the week to have a window mended.

The forty books are on their shelves. The second shelf down on the left has her own name at the head of it in her own hand, three years old, and it is the only one of the forty that is anybody's, and it is not a wall and it is not a door and it is not going to get her out of anything, and she would say it again in front of anybody.

Nobody thanked her and nobody was thanked. Her wage is still held, and about four hundred and thirty miles of road is what it would cost anybody who wanted to see the rest of this, and nobody is going up that road in this matter and nobody is being sent.
