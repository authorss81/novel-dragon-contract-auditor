# Chapter 258: A Bay With Nine Men In It, A Word On A Form Nobody Can Account For, And The Count Moving Once

The bay is about nine foot by eleven with a bench along the north wall and a lamp in it that is not lit until about the seventh hour, and the water is down about where it has been, and the hold against a Company whose whole money is four shillings a year has not moved since the fourth week of the fourth month of the year after.

It is the fourth day of the fourth week of the first month of the year after the year after next. There are nine men in that bay and a foreman of fifty-one behind the bench and a girl of seventeen at the back on a written engagement at the rate the list is set at. There is a man of about twenty-eight, and a man of about twenty, and a man of about fifty who came in at about the eleventh hour, and a man of about fifty-five with a chain, and a man of about forty from the third gang, and a man of about thirty from the second gang, and Marek Kest, and two more whose ages he has never got to know.

He is a man of about thirty from the second gang, and he has been in that bay about six years, and he is the man who said *about nine hundred of us* out loud in a bay in the eleventh month of the year after next when a foreman told nine men what a class was, and he has carried the number since and has not written it down.

---

**And there is a word, and he does not have the thing the word is on, and he has not got anybody to ask.**

He saw the word once, in the eighth month of the year after next, in a room about nine miles off a forge. He had been sent to fetch a barrow and had been made to wait four minutes while a man at a table filled in a sheet. The word was at the top of a line and there was nothing under it. He has never seen the sheet again, he has not got a copy, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that would get a man a copy of a sheet he was not a party to.

He has been at this since the eighth month of the year after next. He asked a foreman about it once, in the tenth month of the year after next, and she said no. He asked a man in a lane about it in the first month of the year after next, and the man was kind to him, and did not tell him, and he has worked out since that kindness is not a reason and he would like the reason.

He is not going to ask the four. There are four in that bay who cannot read a paragraph and he has not asked one of them one thing in his life and he is not going to start. There is a finding in a ledger about four hundred yards up that road about the four and it is not his to touch, and he has never been in the room where it was entered, and he does not know what it says beyond the shape of it. There is not one form anywhere in this empire that would let a man off a list of works go and read a ledger in a book-keeper's own hand.

He had also worked out, over the weeks since the eighth month, that he was going to have to stand in a room and say a word out loud, and that a man of about thirty from the second gang is a thing this empire can only send papers to, and that there is no post in it whose job is to stand in a room. He had looked for one for about nine minutes on the second day of the third week of the first month of the year after the year after next and had not found one, and he had said out loud, to a wall, that not finding one was not a reason not to stand in the room.

---

He had it ready for about four days and he had said it to a wall twice, and the reason was his own and nobody had given it to him.

The reason is that a practice is a count of things done and not a count of people, and he got that from a man of about thirty-four who said it in a doorway and did not say it twice, and he has not met the man and does not know his name and has never once wanted to. What he knows is that the man said he was not going to do it again, and he has had about eight weeks with that sentence. He has worked out that the man is either right or the man is going to be doing it again, and that in either case this afternoon in this bay is going to happen with or without him. The only question in front of him was ever whether he was the sort of man who would be in a room where it happened.

He decided, on the second night of those four days, at a table in a rented room, with nobody sent and nothing in his hand. He has not improved on it and he does not intend to, and he has not worked out whether the man with the fair hand was right, and he has worked out that it makes no difference either way, which is a colder thing to have worked out than the other.

"Is it going to be two," said a man of about twenty-eight, at about the sixth hour, not turning round.

"It is going to be one more than it was," said the man of about thirty from the second gang, "and I am not saying the number until I have said the word, and if I do not say the word then the number does not move, and that is the arrangement and I worked it out myself."

---

"There is a thing I am going to do and I am going to say the shape of it first, because I have listened to two people say the shape of a thing and both of them were right to."

"Why?"

"Because it is the only thing a man off a list of works can do that a clerk cannot, and it costs him nothing, and about nine men can hear it."

"Go on." She had not moved and she had not turned round, and that is the whole of what a foreman does when a bay is about to get past her.

"**It is not about a box and it is not about anybody in this bay, and it is not about the four, and I have not got the paper and I am not producing it and I never had it.** I am asking you a question about a word I saw once, in a room, in the eighth month of the year after next, and nobody sent me here to ask it and nobody in this building knows I am going to, and if anybody says afterwards that this was arranged then I will be able to say that I said the shape of it in a bay in front of nine men before I did it, and that is the whole of what I have bought."

"Four minutes, then," said the foreman. "The lamp is not lit."

"Foreman. What does the word *capacity* mean?"

---

Nobody in that bay made a sound. And the asking made a foreman of fifty-one findable in front of nine men, and she knew it before she had finished the word, and she answered it anyway.

"It means in what hat," she said. "**That is the whole of the answer and I am going to give you the rest of it because you gave me the shape of it and nobody in this bay has given me the shape of anything in nine years.** Put a man in a hat and the hat is the thing that is on the sheet, and the hat is what the column is under, and the column has a day on it, and the man has nothing in it at all. And a hat comes off, and it goes on a different hat next month, and the sheet does not know and the day does not know and the hat is the only part of it that anybody has ever read."

---

"You have just said out loud in a bay what a word on a form means, and about nine men have heard it, and that is the whole of what I have done and it is not enough."

"No. It is the whole of what you have done." She turned round, which she had not done. "**And I am now findable by a word instead of by a box, and I have been findable by a box since the first week of the seventh month of the year after, and I want you to understand that you have not made me safer and you have not made me less safe, you have made me a second time.**"

---

A man of about forty from the third gang said, out loud, that a man asking a foreman a question in her own bay was the best thing that had ever happened on that bank, and the man of about thirty from the second gang told him to be quiet, and the man of about forty said he was not being kind, he was being accurate, and that nobody had thanked anybody and he was not going to start.

"And about the count," said the man of about thirty from the second gang, to the bay and not to her. "**It is six.** It was not six in a bay on the fourth day of the fourth week of the eleventh month of the year after next, and it is six now. I want the number said out loud, and I want it said by a man who cannot read the sheet."

"Get on with it."

"I have worked out something about the number and I worked it out at about the fourth hour and I want it in the bay." He did not look round. "It is a count of askings and not a count of people. If it were a count of people then this afternoon would be nothing, because the man who asked the question in this bay a few weeks ago is not in this room and he is not going to be. **And if it is a count of askings then this afternoon is one, and it goes in, and a count that only counted first askings would be a count of men and not a count of things anybody did.**"

---

"It is the third one anybody has done on purpose," said the foreman. "Say that much or do not say the number at all."

"It is the first one anybody has done on purpose since the bay," said the man of about thirty from the second gang, "and it is on purpose, and I have not been sent, and the man it was before is not in this room and is not going to hear about it from me and will hear about it from about nine mouths inside a week, and I would like that said now while it is in the room."

Nobody said anything. A man of about fifty came in at about the eleventh hour and put his coat on a nail and did not know for about nine minutes that anything had happened.

---

**And the ordinary things went on, and there were more of them after the asking than there were before it, and not one of the nine men in that bay could have said afterwards which of the two hours had contained the word.**

A man of about twenty asked whether being asked a question out loud in a bay by another man in the bay made him a party. She told him that it did, and that a party is the only person who can be told what a thing is going to be used for, and that there is not one form in this empire for asking a person before you do that to them. He said he had not thought of that and she said that was the arrangement. A man of about forty from the third gang wanted to know whether anybody had read the number on the hold that week, and she told him that a foreman reads it every morning of her life and that it had not moved and that a number on a hold is not a document and can be produced by nobody. And a man of about twenty went out into the yard and did a thing with his own hands and told nobody that he had.

**Nobody thanked him.** A foreman of fifty-one did not thank a man of about thirty from the second gang and he did not want to be thanked and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be thanked for asking a question, and there never is going to be one.

He did not get anything out of it. He did not get his wage back, and he did not get the four out of a list, and he did not get a foreman out of a box, and the word is still on a sheet about nine miles off in a room he was made to wait in for four minutes in the eighth month of the year after next, and nobody has told him whether there was ever anything under it.

He had expected none of that and he had not been thanked and a girl of seventeen at the back of that bay was not thanked either and was not spoken to and is not going to start.

---

The lamp is not lit until about the seventh hour and it was not lit when he said the word, and the four in that bay were not asked anything, and the man of about thirty-four who asked the one before was about four miles away at that moment with a hand that writes figures out fair and a sheet of his own under a bench, and he was not sent anything and nobody sent him anything and he was not told.

**And the count of askings is six, and it moved once, on this day, because a man put a question to a woman out loud in a room about a word on a form, and it did not move because a figure changed anywhere, and there is still no post in this empire whose job is to ask, and nobody was appointed, and nothing was written down.**
