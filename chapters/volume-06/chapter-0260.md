# Chapter 260: A Rail In A Room At The End Of A Cold Passage, And A Man Of Sixty-One Who Signs At The Foot Of Every Sheet

A rail about four feet high runs across the hearing room at the other end of a passage that smells of cold stone whatever the season is, and the shelf with the eleven books on it is in a room on the ground floor behind you, and the window in that room is about two feet wide and has a pane out of it.

She is a clerk of about fifty-five and she has been in that room nineteen years and she writes the minutes and advises nobody and asks nothing. It is the second day of the second week of the second month of the year after the year after next, and a man has come in about nine miles with a sheet that has a heading over a column on it and his own hand at the foot of it, and he is sixty-one.

---

He has come twice in nineteen years and both times it was a season, and both times somebody had told him where to come, and both times he left before the sixth hour.

Nobody in this building asked him to come. A man of about seventy who cannot get about told a carrier in a lane in the third week of the first month, and the carrier told a man who knew where this room was, and that is the whole of the instrument. There is no form in this empire for asking a man of sixty-one to walk nine miles so that a clerk can put a hand on the foot of a sheet, and he came anyway, twice, in a season, on his own feet, and he has never once been told that he was expected.

He has signed at the foot of more of the sheets that go out of that room than anybody alive. He signs for people who cannot write and for people who are not in the room. **He is the second hand, and he is the only one of the three on a sheet who can be asked anything at all.** In about nineteen years nobody has ever asked him one thing. He has not been asked whether he minded, or whether he was sure of the sum, or whether he understood what the heading over the column was for.

He stood on the wrong side of the rail for about four minutes before he said anything, and then he put the sheet down on the rail itself.

"I am going to say one thing and it is going to be about nine minutes, and I am going to say the day first. I sign the day on about nine hundred of these a year and nobody has ever asked me what a day on one of them is for."

"Is it written down anywhere?"

"No."

"Then I will say it again. Go on. The second day of the second week of the second month of the year after the year after next."

"Thank you. That is the day, and I have said it, and I am a person who has said a day out loud in a room with a rail in it, and I know what that is worth because I sign the day on all of them and I have never once been asked."

---

"There is a column on that sheet for the answer, and there is no column on it for a refusal. I have known that for eleven years and I have never said it out loud in a room, and I have signed about nine hundred of them."

He put his thumb on the rail.

"Three hands and a seal. The first hand writes the body and it is a clerk's hand, and it cannot be asked anything, because a clerk in this building advises nobody. The second hand is mine and it is at the foot and it answers for everything above it. The third thing is the office, and an office cannot be asked anything either, and cannot lose, and can hold a matter for as long as it likes."

"The three of them come down to one, and the one is me. Nobody in this empire has ever asked the second hand, and I have worked out why. **The why is that if anybody ever did ask, then I would tell the truth.**"

---

"Why would you tell the truth?" she said, and she said it to the rail and not to him.

"Because I have signed about nine hundred of them and I have read every one that I could read, and I cannot read a paragraph. About nine of them in about nineteen years have not been what the person above me wrote, and I have signed those nine because I was not asked and I was not given anything to sign against. There is not one form in this empire that says a man who cannot read a paragraph may put his mark beside a sum and call it a disagreement."

He did not move.

"You would want to sign those nine differently," she said.

"I would want somebody to ask me about them, and I would want about four minutes to say no to about half of them and yes to the rest. There is nobody in this empire to ask, and there is not one form anywhere that says the asking is a thing that happens."

"And a man who signs about nine hundred things a year and is asked about nine of them would begin to be a man who signs about nine hundred things a year and is sometimes right, and there is no form in this empire for being sometimes right, and the machinery in this room has been built for nineteen years on the understanding that a hand at the foot of a sheet is a hand and nothing else. I have been that hand. I would not know how to be anything else and I am sixty-one."

---

"A class is a heading," she said.

"I do not know what a class is. I am a man who signs." He looked at the rail. "But I know what a heading is, and I know it better than I know a class."

"Correct?"

"Correct, and I have never found one wrong, and that is not a compliment to anybody. It means the machine is not the kind that breaks." He took his hand off the rail. "And that is the whole of what I have come in nine miles to say and I have said half of it, and I have got about four minutes, and I am going to say the rest of it standing up because I am not going to be asked to sit down and there is nobody here who is going to ask me to sit down."

---

"A heading is how a person becomes a company, and a company is a thing this empire can hold wages against and give letters to and take to law, and a man who is a person is a thing it can only send papers to." He said it in about nine seconds, standing at a rail, in a room that smells of cold stone, to a clerk of about fifty-five.

"I have worked that out at this rail about four minutes ago and I am saying it to you and not to the rail, because you have a book and I have not got one, and I am not asking you to put it in it."

"I am not going to put it in it."

"Then it is in a room and not in a book, and I have said that out loud on purpose. I am sixty-one and I sign things, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be thanked for saying a thing at a rail."

He put his hand on the rail again and did not take it off.

"I have one more thing and it is the half of it that is the worst half, and I have said the other half first because the other half sounds like a complaint and this does not."

"Say it."

"On every one of about nine hundred sheets a year there are three things that can be asked, and two of them are a clerk and an office, and neither of those is a person anybody can put a question to. That leaves me. **Nobody in this empire has ever asked the second hand, and I have been the second hand since I was forty-two, and I am going to keep being it until they stop.** I would like somebody to have asked me once. I have worked out that I would rather they had not than that they had asked me for something I could give."

---

She had not asked him one question, and she noticed herself not asking, and there is a difference between a clerk who does not ask because she is busy and a clerk who does not ask because she has worked out what the asking costs, and she had about nine minutes ago worked out exactly what it costs. She stood on the other side of a rail and she had not asked him one question, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that would let a clerk of this office put a question to a person who has come in nine miles with a sheet, and a question put in this building is a question put into a record. She has entered one record of her own in nineteen years and she is answerable for it.

"You have said nine minutes' worth of that standing up."

"I have."

"I am not able to ask you one question about it and I am going to tell you why, because you have told me why, and it is only fair. If I ask you, then you have to answer me in a room with a rail in it, and there is no form in this building that requires a person to answer in this building, and the moment I ask, that goes in a book somewhere with the day on it. And I have just spent nine minutes listening to a man tell me that the last time that happened to about four hundred people their sheet went back."

"Then do not ask me."

"I was not going to." She had her hand flat on the edge of the table. "**And I am also not going to write any of it down, and that is the cost, and I would like it said in a room, because a minute with an answer in it is an order, and a clerk of this office has never entered an order in nineteen years, and there is no form that lets her.**"

---

And then she said the thing that is the whole cost of the morning, and she said it to the rail, because he was still on the other side of it.

"That is an answer."

He waited.

"That is an answer to the question I wrote in the second of the eleven books on the fourth day of the third week of the ninth month of the year after next, on my own motion, with the day on it and my own name at the foot of it, and I have not read that line since about the fourth week of the tenth month because reading it is worse than not having written it." She had not moved away from the table. "And I am not going to write your answer down. **If I write it down then it is an order, and a clerk of this office has never entered an order in nineteen years, and there is no form that lets her, and a thing you said out loud in a room with four people in it is in four people and not in a book, and if it goes in a book it goes four hundred and thirty miles back down that road.**"

"You think I have answered it."

"I think you have answered a quarter of it, and in a room, and nobody asked you to, and that is worth more than the whole of it in a book and I have got no way of holding it."

---

He picked the sheet up off the rail and gave it back to her, and she entered it correctly, in a hand that does not vary, in about four minutes. It is the nine hundred and something of them and it is correct. The heading over the column is a heading, and the second hand at the foot of it is his, and nobody asked the second hand, and he is on his way home about nine miles and the road is not being walked by anybody for his sake.

**Nobody thanked him.** She did not thank him and he did not expect to be thanked and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that requires it and there is not going to be one. She did not write anything down, and that is the price she has paid for a morning, and a price is not a favour and she has not been given one.

The sheet went on the shelf in the second book with the others. The room on the ground floor is the same room it was at the second hour, and the pane is still out of the window, and the eleven books are on the shelf, and there is one line in one of them with a clerk's own name at the foot of it, and it is a question and not an answer.

**A man of sixty-one walked out of a building off the old river road with the only thing anybody has said in that building in nineteen years that was not a price, and it is in a room and not in a book.** A bill at Lowcross is nineteen pounds three and fourpence and unpaid, and about nineteen people are still behind that bank, and nobody was sent, and the road is not being walked. The second hand at the foot of every one of about nine hundred sheets a year is the only one of three that can be asked, and nobody in this empire has ever asked the second hand.
