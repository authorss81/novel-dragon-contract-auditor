# Chapter 263: A Day-Book, A Sum That Does Not Come Out, A Fair Hand That Is Not A Correct Hand, And A Woman With A Wet Hood At A Door On The Day He Worked Out She Would Come

Over a chandler's shop, off a lane four miles from the bay on the Slade Cut, there is a room he works in and a landing outside it, and the landing has four steps, a door with a bolt across it, and a window about a foot wide. He is on the list of works four times a season, and this is not one of the four days, and he is here anyway, because a man of about sixty has a hand that shakes and cannot make a fair one.

Thirty-four or thereabouts, and a trade, and the trade is a hand that writes other people's figures out fair. He is paid by the day for it. He has a house with a door on it and a wife, and in one doorway in his life he said he was not going to do a thing again.

It is the fourth day of the first week of the third month of the year after the year after next, and it is about the fifth hour, and the light goes off the lane at about the sixth hour in that month.

---

The day-book came in at the second hour with a sum at the bottom of it that the man of about sixty could not get out fair. He has the hand. It is not a bad hand. It is a hand that has been writing the same eight figures for about thirty years and has got them into a shape.

"I have got it wrong twice this week," the man said, "and both times the wrong is in the same place, and I have brought it to you twice and you have fixed it twice and it has come back wrong twice."

"You have brought it to me once."

"I have brought it to me twice," the man said, meaning himself, and put it on the table. "**Which of the four is it?**"

"**Is it wrong again.**"

"It is." He put his finger on the line. "**And you are the second one anybody has said it to?**"

---

He has done that four times a season for about eleven years and he has never once done it for nothing, and the four copies are the whole of what a fair hand is in this empire.

The man of about sixty cannot read his own figures by the second week of a month. That is not an old man's trick and it is not a thing he says about himself. It is a fact about the shape of a hand after thirty years of writing the same eight figures, and the shape goes, and the man goes on writing them, and about four weeks later the shape comes back in a way that is not the shape it was.

A man can be paid a day to fix that in about four minutes. There is no form anywhere in this empire that says what a fair hand is worth, and about nine men in four years have told him he ought to be doing more of it, and he has not asked for more, because asking is a form and there is not one.

He wrote it out four times, in a hand that does not shake, and it took about four minutes, and the man of about sixty read all four back and said that the third one was the one, and he was right, and he said so.

The four went on the table one on top of the other and he squared them at the near edge, and the third from the top had a corner turned over where the grain of the table had caught it, and the man of about sixty ran a finger along the edge and stopped on the turned corner and went back to the beginning of the third one.

And all four of them were wrong, and he had known that they were wrong before he wrote the first one, and he wrote four of them fair anyway, because that is the whole of what he is paid for and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that says a fair hand is a correct hand.

He has known it since he was about nineteen. He has never once said it out loud in a room, and he has never once been asked, and he has worked out over about eleven years that the reason nobody asks him is not that nobody thinks of it.

It is that a man who can write a figure out so that another man can read it back without guessing is the last man anybody in this empire would think to ask whether the figure is true. He is the machine's own reader. Nobody asks a man who is good at reading a page whether the page says what it ought to.

He got the man of about sixty his hat and his coat and said the thing he says about four times a season, which is that a man who can do that ought to be doing more of it, and the man said it, and he has been told that by about nine men in about four years and it has never once been a form of anything.

---

Then he was on the landing at about the sixth hour because the light had gone off the lane and the shop below had shut, and the four steps were cold, and he was not going in for about four minutes.

The light went off the lane at the flags first and the flags kept a colour for about four minutes after everything else had gone, and the smell of the chandler's shop came up through the boards of the landing and out along the seams between them, and he stood where he was and let all of it come up to him.

A woman of about thirty-five was at the bottom of them with a wet hood on, and she had been at the bottom of them before, and she had said she would come again.

It is about four weeks since he worked out on a step that she would come again inside about four weeks, because a lane is a lane and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that stops a man talking in a lane. She came on the day he worked out she would. He notes that and does not improve on it and does not find it comforting, and there is a difference between those two and he is not going to spend the evening on it.

"Stand still and I will go up," she said. "I only want to say one thing and it is quicker from the bottom."

"Tell it from the bottom."

---

**"There was a man come to this door about four miles on foot. On a second day of that week, early in it, with an empty barrow, and he did not go up. He asked whether a man had come to that door who walks about four miles on foot, who was in a bay on a day about five weeks back, and who has got a hand that writes figures out fair."**

The door behind him opened about halfway.

**"I said yes," said his wife, from inside it. "I said a man like that came about four weeks ago with a barrow and did not go up either."**

---

He did not turn round, and that is the part he went over afterwards and the part that took him the longest.

"There is no name on that door," he said, to the woman at the bottom of the four steps.

"There is no name on this empire," she said, and went off down the lane, and she was not thanked and she did not want to be and she has not come again.

She had been at that door about nine weeks earlier with a day in her head that she had got from a boy at the Slade end, and she had asked his wife about a day and not about a man, and she had written nothing down, and she had said that she would come again.

He had stood inside with his hand on a bag for about a minute and a half and had worked out that the day was the point, and that the day was already in her head before she came, and that she had come to be able to say she had been.

He had not improved on any of that and he is not going to. What he has learned in the nine weeks is that a description of a man travels by the same door a day travels by, and that the door belongs to somebody else, and that somebody else is standing in it answering.

**And the door was shut, and his wife was on the landing, and she had said yes.**

---

He has told her one true thing about himself on purpose, in a kitchen, six weeks before a bay in the eleventh month of the year after next, without asking her first. He said at the time that he could have asked and did not, and that there is not one form anywhere in this empire for asking a person whether they will mind being told something.

Then a man of about twenty came about four miles with a barrow and told him on a step that his name had gone out of a bay, and he decided on that step that he was not going to be the one to tell her first this time, and he made it on purpose, and it was a bad decision, and it has now been made for him by a stranger's mouth at his own door.

**And the thing he has learned, standing on four cold steps, is that his household is where he is findable.**

Not his name. His household. A description travels further and cheaper than a name does, and it does not have to be written down by anybody, and a person carrying a description only has to knock.

There is not one form anywhere in this empire that says a person has to stop answering a door. She has not broken anything. There was nothing to break. She answered a stranger about a day because the stranger had the day before she came, which is the thing a woman of about thirty-five with a wet hood said to him in a doorway about twelve weeks ago and which he wrote down at the time and has not thought about since.

---

**And he did not reverse it, and the reason he gives for not reversing it is not the reason he gave for it, and he worked that out on the top step at about the seventh hour and it took about four minutes.**

On the step in the second month he had a decision. He did not have a reason. He did not want to be the one who said it, and a man who has worked that out in about four seconds and has got nothing under it does not go back and put a reason under it, so he called it a decision and made it on purpose and told himself that a decision made on purpose is a different animal from a preference.

It is the same animal. It is a preference, and it was one, and it is going to keep being one, and the only thing that has changed is that he now has to hold it with the other thing in the same week.

---

He went over the rest of it after that, standing at the top of the four steps in the cold, in the ordinary way, and it took the length of a cup of water, and there is no anger in it and he went looking for about four days and it was not there.

**A practice with two people in it is stopped by nobody.** He has said that once, on a step, in the second month, and he is not going to say it a second time, and it does not need saying again because it has been true in every room he has been in since and he has not looked for a way round it once.

**And the number has not moved.** He knows that much. A bay, five weeks back, and a foreman, and a word on a form, and that is the last of it, and nothing has happened since and nobody has come up four miles again, and he has caught himself twice this week hoping that would turn out to mean something and both times it was not about the man in the bay.

A practice is not a number. A practice is a thing that keeps going whether or not anybody counts it, and the counting was never the part that kept it going, and he worked that out on a step about four weeks ago and has now worked out that he worked it out for the wrong reason. He wanted the number not to move because a number not moving is a thing he can be proud of, and a thing a man can be proud of is a thing that is his, and he has been making his own reasons out of that for about eleven years.

And the sentence about the woman four hundred and thirty miles up this river is on a step somewhere in another town, and he said it once and did not improve on it, and he is not going to say it a second time, and it is still there and he does not need to be near it for it to be there.

---

He is on the list of works four times a season and this is not going to be a sentence about money. It is the whole of what he does.

He found out in about nine minutes, about four years ago, that the answer to asking for a fifth is nobody. There is no form for a man to ask for a day on a list he is already on, and no form for a man to say that he is worth more than the day he is paid, and no form anywhere in this empire that a man can be handed which says he has offered. So he is four days a season. His own name goes on the list in a hand that is not his. About nine men have told him he ought to be doing more of it and he has agreed with all nine of them and asked none of them.

**Nobody thanked him, and nobody was paid for the walk, and a man of about twenty with a barrow who told him his name had gone into a bay is not going to be thanked by him and has not asked.**

The four men in that bay who cannot read a paragraph have still not been asked one question and were not asked one on any of the four days this week, and there is a finding about them in a ledger four hundred yards up that road in a book-keeper's own hand which is not his and which he has not thought about improving, and there is no fifth reason about them and there is not going to be one.

A girl of seventeen at the back of that bay is on a written engagement at the rate the list is set at, and nobody has asked her about the season and nobody is going to.

He went in and his wife had the lamp lit before he was up the stairs, and she did not say anything about it, and he did not either, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that requires either of them to.

He put the wet hat on the inside of the door where it has always gone and it dropped once on the boards, and his wife did not look at it, and the lamp was lit and the two of them had not eaten and were not going to speak about it either.
