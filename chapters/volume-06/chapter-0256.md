# Chapter 256: Eleven Books On A Shelf, A Window With A Pane Out Of It, And About Four Days To Decide What To Do About Having Asked

The office keeps eleven books on a shelf in a room on the ground floor of a building off the old river road, and the room has a window about two feet wide with a pane out of it, and at the other end of a passage that smells of cold stone whatever the season is there is a hearing room with a rail in it.

She is about fifty-five and she has been a clerk here nineteen years and she writes the minutes of this office and advises nobody and asks nothing, and she has never once been asked a question by anybody who was not a person who wanted something out of her.

It is the second day of the second week of the first month of the year after the year after next. There are three clerks in this building and she is the oldest, and the other two are a man of about thirty who has been in that room four years and a woman of about twenty-six who came in about four years ago and is not in this matter and has not been in it.

The shelf is a plank on two uprights and the eleven books stand on it in the order they were opened in, and the two of them opened most are at the end nearest the door because a clerk reaches for them without turning round.

The window has not been glazed since before she came and the hole in it is about the size of her hand, and in the winter the cold comes through and lands on the second book on the shelf and nowhere else in the room.

---

In about nineteen years not one of those eleven books has carried the name of a person who was in a box. There is one line in one of them that has a name at the foot of it, and she wrote that line herself, in her own hand, on the fourth day of the third week of the ninth month of the year after next, at about the sixth hour, and nobody asked her to.

It is in the second book. It is not an answer and it is not an order and it is not a minute, in the sense that the other things in that book are minutes. **It is a question with the day on it and the hour on it and her own name at the foot of it**, and it is the only thing in eleven books that anybody in this building has ever entered about what ought to have happened instead.

She wrote it on her own motion, at nobody's request, which is a thing a clerk may do, and there is no form for it and there is not going to be one.

---

And in about four minutes on the first day of that week she worked out why she had four days, and the four days are not hers either.

The second of the eleven books goes to the hearing room at the other end of that cold passage on the fourth day of the second week of the first month, twice in a year, the way it goes, and a clerk carries it and a clerk brings it back and the clerk who carries it does not read it and could not. That is not a rule anybody made. It is what has happened about nine times in nineteen years.

After that day it will be in a room with a rail in it and then it will be on the shelf again, and she will not be able to say whether anybody looked at the one line, and she is the only person in this empire who knows that the line is hers.

A finding is a thing a person has and not a thing a person has written down. She wrote it down, and writing it down was the price, and she has been paying it every week since the ninth month of the year after next without knowing what the instalment was.

---

**Two of them are in boxes and she is the only person in this empire who knows it, and she has not told either of them, and she is not going to, and that is not discretion and she will not let anybody in that building call it that.**

The first is a woman of twenty in a room two streets off a street that goes down to a river road, about nine miles from this building, with a wage that has been held since the second week of the third month of the year after. She said no out loud on the fourth day of the third week of the fifth month of the year after, in a hearing room with a rail in it, and the day went on the sheet, and a piece of paper with a seal on it told her that no further consent of that person is required. That sentence has been written out in this room about nine hundred times in nineteen years in a hand that does not vary, and it has never once been asked what it means.

The second is a foreman of fifty-one on a bank about four hundred and thirty miles down this river, entered in the first week of the seventh month of the year after by a class and not asked, and nothing has come with it, and nobody has been in a room with her about it since, and she has not been asked either.

**Neither of them is any further out of a box than they were on the day she wrote the line, and she has checked neither of them, and she cannot check either of them, and there is not one form in this empire for a clerk of this office to go and see.**

The ordinary work went on.

About nine sheets came in in that day and she entered all nine correctly, in a hand that does not vary, and on the front of each one there is a heading and under the heading the body and under the body a space with a day on it, and at the foot of each one there is a second hand.

Her hand does not vary because she was taught it in the first year not to vary it, and the reason given to her was that a hand which varies is a hand somebody can be asked about on the day it varies, and she has never found out whether that was true and has never tested it.

**The second hand is the only one of the three that can be asked.** The first hand is hers and it writes the body and it cannot be asked anything, because a clerk is a person who advises nobody and is a person nobody needs. The third thing on the sheet is the office, and an office cannot be examined, cross-examined, embarrassed, sued, fined or committed, and it cannot lose, and it can hold a matter indefinitely, and there is no person in it to say yes or no to anything.

The second hand is the person the sheet is about. It is their own hand, at the foot, and it answers for everything above it, and in about nineteen years nobody has ever asked any of them one question about anything.

A man of about forty came in with one on the second day of the second week of the first month of the year after the year after next and she read it, and he wanted to know what a word in the body meant, and she told him she could not tell him that and told him why, and he said thank you, and she said that was the ordinary way and not a favour, and he went out and did not come back.

The man at the foot of that one had signed it in a hand that shook on the downstroke. She saw it and she said nothing about it, and she has seen about nine hundred of them and she has said nothing about every one of them, and since the third week of the ninth month of the year after next she has begun to be able to look at a hand and know things about it, which is a different thing from reading a hand and is not a skill and has no form attached to it and cannot be entered anywhere.

She has not written any of it down. That is the whole of what the weeks since the ninth month have produced in that room, and she would like somebody to come in and ask her about it, and there is not one form in this empire that would let anybody come in and ask her about it, and she has looked for about four days.

---

The man of about thirty read over her shoulder on the first day of that week, which he has not asked to do and which she cannot stop him doing, and he said nothing at all for about a minute and then said the thing that has made the four days.

"You have written a question down."

"I have."

"Not an answer."

"Not an answer." She did not put her hand over it. "**A minute with an answer in it is an order, and a clerk of this office has never entered an order in nineteen years, and there is no form that lets her.** A question is the only thing I am permitted to put in that book, and I have put one in it, and there is nothing anywhere that will let me take it out again either, and I have looked."

"Where does it go."

"It goes to the other end of the passage on the fourth day of the second week and it comes back on the shelf, and that is the whole of its journey, and there is no form anywhere that lets me send it to a person instead of a room."

---

"So you have four days."

"I have about four days to decide whether to put a second line in it, and I am not going to, and I have known that for about nine days and I am telling you now so that you do not have to work at it."

"Why not?"

"Because a second line would be an answer, or it would be the beginning of one, and there is no third thing it could be." She squared a stack. "A person who has found a thing out is findable by having found it. That is the price I have not paid before and I am about to pay it. The whole of it is that I can no longer be a person who advises nobody, because I have advised myself, in writing, in a hand that can be compared with my hand for forty years."

"You were never a person who advised nobody. You were a person nobody had asked."

"That is the same shape and it is a different cost, and I would not know the difference except that I have now got a piece of paper with my name at the foot of it, and I cannot get the name off, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for asking a person whether she meant it, and I am the only person in this matter anybody could ask, and nobody is going to be able to."

---

That is a burden and it is not a promotion, and she said both halves of that out loud in a room with a pane out of the window on the second day of the second week, to a clerk of about thirty who is a person nobody needs and who has been in that room four years.

**And the reason she is not going to put a second line in it is not that she has nothing to add, and she wants that said in a room rather than in a margin.**

She has plenty to add. She could put a second line in that book tomorrow that would name a procedure, and it would be a good one, and she has drafted about four of them in her head since the ninth month of the year after next and has not written any of them down, and the reason is the same reason and not a new one. A second line is an answer, and an answer in a minute is an order, and a clerk of this office has never entered an order in nineteen years, and there is no form that lets her. A clerk who enters an order is a clerk somebody can be told what an order is, and then there is somebody at the end of it, and then there is a person to serve.

She is also not going to tell anybody, and that one she decided in about nine minutes and she has not changed it. **A person who has been told has been given a duty, and a person who has been given a duty cannot stop.** She has got nothing she can stop. She is a clerk who advises nobody and there is nobody in this building she could ask, and the one person in this matter who is answerable for a question is her, and she is going to go on being that.

Nobody thanked her. He did not thank her and she did not expect to be thanked and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be thanked for having found out a thing.

There is a copy of a notice in the second book in that room, with a day against it, and the notice itself is on a bench about four hundred and thirty miles down that river with a seal at the foot of it and nothing under the seal. Its second line asks that no inference of any kind be drawn from the absence of any name, and its sixth line declines to name whoever holds the office at all.

She has read the second of those lines about nine hundred times and she has never once said the name and she is not going to. That is not discretion. **It is that a clerk who puts a name into a room where a document proves there is not one has used the only weapon the office has against itself**, and about nine hundred entries a year would be very easy to stop.

The woman of about twenty-six came in at about the sixth hour to ask whether the room wanted shutting and read nothing at all on her way past, and she is not in this matter and has not been in it, and she has been in that room for the whole of that time and has asked about four hundred questions in it and not one of them has been about the shelf.

---

The book went to the hearing room on the fourth day of the second week and came back on the same day, and it is on the shelf, and it is the same book, and the line is the same line, and nothing about it has been touched.

**She cannot know whether it was read.** A clerk carried it in and a clerk brought it out and the clerk who carried it does not read it and could not, and there is no form that requires him to say whether he looked, and there is no person she could ask without making a party of that clerk, and being a party is a thing she cannot undo.

And there are about nine hundred of those sheets a year coming in under a heading in about nine hundred buildings, and every one of them is correct, and she has administered every one of them correctly, and she has never once been asked whether a box ought to have been filled, and there is still no form anywhere in this empire for asking her.

**And the second hand at the foot of every one of them is the only one of the three that can be asked, and nobody has ever asked the second hand, and she has just found out why.** The why is that a question is the only thing in this empire that makes a person answerable, and she has made herself one, in a book, on her own motion, with the day on it, and there is nobody in this empire who can ask her whether she meant it.
