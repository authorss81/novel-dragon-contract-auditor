# Chapter 267: A Rail About Four Feet High, A Rack Of Letters For One District, And A Woman Of Thirty-Two Who Has Carried About Nine Hundred Of Them And Has Never Been Asked What Any Of Them Was For

A rail about four feet high runs across the hearing room at the other end of a passage that smells of cold stone whatever the season is, and the shelf with the eleven books on it is in a room on the ground floor behind you, and the window in that room is about two feet wide and has a pane out of it.

She is about thirty-two and she carries letters, and she has carried them for about six years, and she is paid ninepence at a lock, and nobody has ever told her what she is carrying and nobody has ever asked her.

It is the fourth day of the first week of the fourth month of the year after the year after next, and it is about the third hour, and the passage is colder than the yard it comes out of and always is.

---

The lock is at the bottom of the steps and it is ninepence to open it and the ninepence is on a list and not on a form, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that says what a carrier is paid, and there is no form that says what a carrier has to know.

She has carried for about six years. Before that she carried for a man who kept a draper's shop in a street two off this one, and before that she carried for a woman who had a kitchen and four children and no money, and she has been eleven, and fourteen, and about nineteen, and about twenty-nine.

She can remember the shape of a rack and the weight of a full one and where the cold is on that passage, and she cannot remember the name of a single person who has ever signed for anything she has carried, because a signature is on the far side of a counter and she is on this side of it.

She came up the passage with a rack on her hip and nine of the things on it were for this room and the rest of the rack was for four rooms on the ground floor, and she has made that walk about nine times a week for six years and has never once been up it with anybody.

**A hearing room with a rail in it is not a room a person waits in, and a letter under a rail is not a letter anybody is waiting for**, and both of those are in the same building and about four hundred yards from each other and there is no form anywhere in this empire that joins them.

---

The woman of about twenty-six was at the table at the end of the room and she is one of three clerks in this building and she has been in this room for about four years and she is not in this matter and has not been in it.

She took the rack off her hip and looked down the nine.

"Four of these are the office and five of those are a company called a thing she could not pronounce and did not try to." She put the five on the table and squared them. "**Sign for the five.**"

"Where do I put the four."

"Anywhere. The rack goes back."

---

**And that is the whole of the difference and it took the woman of about thirty-two about eleven years to see it** and she saw it in about four seconds in a passage.

And the reason the five go in and the four do not is not a decision anybody made. It is that a company is a thing you can sign for, because a company is a thing that goes on being after everybody in it is gone, and a person is a thing that has to be handed it.

That is the whole of the machinery and it is about four lines long and it is the same in every room in this empire, and nobody in this building has ever written it down, and there is no form anywhere that would let her.

The five went into a book. The woman of about twenty-six wrote the day against them and put a mark at the foot of the page, and the mark was hers and it said that the thing had arrived and been received and could be produced afterwards against whoever the five were addressed to.

The four went into the rack, and the rack went back to a shelf in a room with a pane out of the window, and the four had not arrived anywhere and had not been received by anybody and could not be produced against anybody at all, and there was nothing anybody had to do about them, and nobody was going to be reminded.

One of those four is a letter. A man wrote it and paid for it to be carried and it has come about nine miles and it is on a shelf in a rack in a room and it is not late, and nothing is wrong, and it will not be late, and it will never be wrong, and it will be taken off that shelf in a year and put on another one.

She has carried about nine hundred of those a year for six years and she cannot read a paragraph faster than anybody else and there is nothing on the outside of any of them that she could not have told anybody at a door.

---

"What is the office one?"

"I do not know and neither does she." The woman of about twenty-six did not look up. "There is no name on it. There is a heading and there is a seal and there is nothing under the seal, and it comes about twice a year and it goes back and nobody has ever read one out and I am not going to be the first."

"Then where does the four go?"

"The four goes on the shelf in the rack. It will be in that rack in about sixteen weeks and in about four years it will be in a room in a different building in a town about four hundred and thirty miles up this river, and it will be a thing that happened, and it will not have been received by anybody."

---

The woman of thirty-two put the rack down on the edge of the table, and she did it for about four seconds and then she picked it up again, and the woman of about twenty-six did not look up and did not notice and was not asked to.

"I have been carrying these six years."

"You have."

"Ninepence at a lock."

"That is what the list says and I have never once asked you about it and neither has anybody else."

"You have never asked me what I am carrying."

"Nobody has ever asked you what you are carrying," said the woman of about twenty-six, "and I have been in this room four years and I have thought about that about nine times and I have never asked, because I have worked out that if I ask then I am a person who has asked, and a person who has asked is a person who is in it, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that will get me back out of it."

---

And she has thought, standing on that step on about nine working days in six years, about what it would be like to put the rack down on the table and go out of the building and not come back up it, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that would stop her, and she has worked out exactly why she has not done it.

Not the money. Ninepence at a lock.

The four. She would put five letters on a table and four back on a shelf and go down a cold passage and out into a yard, and about four people would be a line in a thing in a town about four hundred and thirty miles up this river saying they had not received anything. Nobody would ever know. Nobody would ever know that a woman had walked out of a building.

**That is the whole of what would happen and it is nothing. That is the answer, and she has had it six years, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that a person can hand to a person who has just worked that out.**

And the woman of thirty-two went back down the cold passage with the rack on her hip and stopped on the fourth step, which is the step where the light from the ground floor window comes up and catches the edge of everything, and she stood there for about four minutes.

She is a person and this empire can only send papers to her. Not a company. Not a thing wages can be held against, or letters given to, or taken to law, and she has known that since she was about nineteen and has never once been on the receiving end of anything except a wage and a list.

**The whole difference between a person and a company is a heading**, and she has been walking up and down about four hundred yards of cold stone for six years carrying a heading from one building to another about nine times a week, and nobody has ever told her that is what she has been doing.

And the thing that made her put the rack down for about four seconds is this, and she has it in her own mouth and nowhere else, and she is not going to say it to anybody:

**The five that got signed are the only ones that exist.** They are four people written down as not having received anything, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that can put a person back into a thing they are written down as not having received, and nobody knows they are written down at all.

---

**And she has been carried herself, and she found that out on the step**, and it took about four seconds and it is the whole of what she took away from a cold passage.

She has been carried on about nine of them a year for six years, and she has never been asked to be, and there is no form on which she could refuse, and there is no form anywhere in this empire that says a person who is paid to carry a thing is to be told what the thing is, and there is no other kind of work in this city that she could get that does not have one of those two things in it.

She is a thing it can only send papers to, and it has been sending her to about four rooms for six years, and the reason it can do that is not that it is rude to her.

**It is that a person cannot be held to anything she has not agreed to**, and it does not have to explain itself to her, and it does not have to give her a reason, and there is a rack for it.

---

The woman of about twenty-six called down the passage about four minutes later to say that the rack was wanted back on the shelf and not on the table, and she took it, and she said nothing else.

Nobody thanked her. There is not one form anywhere in this empire that requires a person to be thanked for carrying a thing up a cold passage, and there is not going to be one, and she has never once expected it and has thought about it more than she has said.

And the rail is still in that room, about four feet high, and the office is a heading and not a person, and about nine hundred entries a year come in under one in about nine hundred buildings, and a single set of them goes to one district, and this empire has about nine hundred districts, and the woman of thirty-two has never once been in a room where anybody knew that, and neither has anybody else.

She is one of about nine hundred a year. She is on a list. She has never been asked what a list is for, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that would let a person ask a carrier that, and she is going to be carrying letters on this passage next week.
