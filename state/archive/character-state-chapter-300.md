## The man of about thirty-four with a fair hand

He is thirty-four or thereabouts and has been writing out other men's sums so that they come out even for about eleven years, four days a season on somebody's list, in a room over a chandler's shop taken by the week with four steps and a landing, and he has a house with a door on it and a wife at the bottom of those steps. **He has now done the thing twice.** The first time was the fourth day of the fourth week of the eleventh month of the year after next, in a bay with nine men in it, forty-eight weeks before Chapter 0298, and he said in the doorway on his way out that he was not going to do that again. The second time was the fourth day of the third week of the eleventh month of the year after the year after next, in a room with a rail about four feet high in it, in about nine minutes, to a clerk of about fifty-five, and the question was whether she meant it, and she said four words and they were not the who. **He walked about nine miles each way twice in ten weeks, on days he was not paid for, and nobody sent him and the road was not walked.** He told his wife six weeks before it, on purpose, without asking her first, and there is no form in this empire for asking a person whether they will mind being told something, and he paid the second one on the same terms and was thanked by nobody. **He is now findable, by a room, by a woman of about forty who carries a scuttle up a cold passage, by a woman of about thirty-five next door who saw a man standing at a step, and by a stair four miles off where a man came up wanting nothing. ## The foreman off a dye end, about forty, a furnace town four hundred and thirty miles up this river

**He is the only person in that city who can pick up or put down the whole of a woman's money and he has said out loud that he cannot say what a wage is held against, and he has said it four times to men who were working and once to a boy of about nine with a slate, and the boy is the fifth.** He worked out in a rented room in a city four hundred and thirty miles down this river that he is one of about four people in this matter who cannot be asked, and was told it by somebody. **On the second day of the first week of the eleventh month of the year after next he said the volume's own question out loud in his own mouth, four times over about half an hour to about nine men in a dye end: if that office was meant to be temporary, then somebody changed it into an inheritable one, and I would like to know who, and I have asked it in a shed in a different city and nobody answered, and I am asking it again in my own mouth and nobody is going to answer it here either.** He did it on purpose and said the cost out loud, and a man of about fifty-five with a chain came back at him: **a man who cannot read a paragraph is the only person in this empire you can say anything to and have it not come back.** A man of about forty says there was nobody to change; a man of about forty-four says it has not been improved on in about four weeks; a man of about twenty-six says the question is about four hundred and thirty miles away and not about a vat. **A boy of about nine wrote it on a slate with no name on it, and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that will take a slate off a boy, and the foreman said out loud that if there were one she would not fill it in, and that was the first thing she had said that anybody believed.** **Owed and unpaid:** who changed a temporary office into an inheritable one; a wage held with no name against it; and a boy nobody has thanked.

## The woman of about twenty-six at the end of the cold passage

**She is about twenty-six, has been in that building about four years, and is not in this matter and has never been in it. She has counted, in her head and without touching the rack, the racks that go down that passage and come back light by one, and the figure has gone up since the seventh month, and she has not said by how much and is not going to.** She has three questions for the man of about sixty-one and has asked none of them and is not going to. In the second week of the eleventh month she worked out, in about four minutes, that the figure is the only one this empire keeps on how many people are sitting inside a thing nobody can be asked about, **and that a number in a book is a column and a column wants a heading and a heading over that figure is a heading over about nine hundred rooms, and she is not going to give it a heading and is not going to hand it on.** She went down that lane with a rack in the first week of the eleventh month and was not in the building when the asking happened, does not know it, and has not been told. **She is the only other person in the matter who knows what happened in that room, and there is no form anywhere that will let anybody ask her what she heard. She was not thanked and nobody thanked her. Owed and unpaid:** the figure she will not give and will not hand on; three questions she will not ask; and the third word on the slate, which exists and has no name on it.

## The others, and every debt this volume left standing on them, and nothing here is resolved

**About four men in a bay who cannot read a paragraph**, of whom one is a man of about fifty-five with a chain who held four in his head, had nothing written, said out loud that he is the only person on that bank that findability does not work on, that he had known it for about four years, that he had never once used it, and that he wanted it said that he knew what he was doing — **and who says out loud that he is not one of the four, has not been asked one thing in nine years, and that writing him down as one of them would be a way for a list of four to go wrong.** **THE FOUR HAVE NEVER BEEN ASKED ONE QUESTION ABOUT A NUMBER, in any chapter of fifty, and there is a finding about them in a ledger four hundred yards up that road in a book-keeper's own hand which is not his.** A man of about thirty from a second gang, a party from the day he put a word out loud in that bay, who cannot be taken back out of it, whose decision not to have it taken back was refused in writing, and who in the second week of the twelfth month asked a man of thirty-eight what his being in the room left him with. A man of about thirty with fourpence in his hand who did not spend it and has a wife. A man of about forty-four who has not slept since the first time a question was said in his end and has told his wife nothing. A man of about thirty-four who came four miles in the rain and could not finish a question, and who did not come back. A woman of about thirty-five who keeps a day-book in a lodging house, who came about nine miles twice, was not let in, and wrote none of it down. A man of about thirty who is findable and not going to be asked about a rate twice. A woman of about thirty who has money and offered some of it. **A boy of about nine with a slate, now with two slates in about four years, the second one with the office question on it and no name on it, and nobody has thanked him and nobody ever has.** **AND A READER OF SEVENTEEN, on the page across this volume, unnamed, not the point of view, not the person who is asked anything, not spoken to about the season, not thanked, not sent for, and not going to start. She is on a written engagement at the rate the list is set at, and she would say yes and has never been given the chance to say no, and a chapter that thanks her breaks this volume and no chapter of it did.** A woman of about twenty about nine miles off a building off the old river road, in a box since the fifth month of the year after, with sixteen books, who said no out loud in a room with the day on it, and who was not asked anything in this volume and was not put in a chapter that walks nine miles. A woman of about forty who carries a scuttle up a cold passage twice a week, not one of the three clerks, not in this matter, and the only other person who knows what happened in that room. The man of about thirty-four's wife, unnamed. A clerk of thirty-four at the top of that counter, a person nobody needs on purpose, told not one word of any of it. A man of about thirty-eight in a bay in some weeks and not in others whom nobody has asked to be. A man of about sixty-one who worked out a thing at a rail in the third week of the sixth month and has told one person half of it. **Bess Tarrant, sixty-three, of this city, and the only person in this matter who has chosen findability on purpose and for no procedural reason: she put a man's name into a letter in the fourth week of the third month of the year after next and asked in her own hand that it not be written down, and saying a name in a street is not the same as being found, and that has not changed and she chose it anyway, three times, to nobody, and it has never once worked in her favour; a day in a minute book has her name at the foot of it and she has decided not to find out about it, and her only protection is that in about four years she will be sixty-seven and will be able to say she did not know. Owed and unpaid: the date four years off, unlooked into and unprinted; and a surname that is not a door and never was one.** **Ivet Sarn, thirty-eight, a clerk at a window, nine years at it: her fourth column is still empty and still a waste and still has no heading over it, and the door a couple of inches open halfway along her corridor is still neither shut nor entered; she refused a seventh column in a form, with a reason and no why, and distinguished a refusal from a declination to answer, because a refusal in one form on three days is a document and a declination would leave it open for somebody to ask the same question in about four years in a different room; she identified a living man's hand by comparison on a delivery stub in about nine minutes and is a party and is therefore told everything. Owed and unpaid: the file of the sixth year, shut between two others; an undertaking that is hers in a stranger's hand and has been used four times without her; the question of who wrote thirty-one letters that she entered herself as not knowing; and a witness status that can be restored, which she will not be the one to ask for.**
