# VOLUME 12, CHAPTERS 0581 TO 0590, BATCH 0004, MOVEMENT FOUR, THE INSTRUMENT ACQUIRES A CONSTITUENCY — TEN CHAPTER SUMMARIES

*Written by the phase that wrote Chapters 0581 to 0590. **A batch and not a repair, not a close, not a review and not a second reading.** These ten summaries say what each chapter did and they do not say what it means; the meaning is in `state/continuity.md` and the measurements are in `state/batch-summary.md`, and neither is restated here.*

**0581. A Girl Of Nineteen Standing At The End Of That Sill Four Mornings In A Fortnight, And An Older Woman Going Straight Past Her.** The room with the sill in it, entered from the lane. A girl of nineteen has stopped at the far end of a four-foot sill four times in a fortnight and this morning is the fifth. A man of about sixty-one is at the near end of that sill with a tin of two identical pens and has been putting his other hand into it for about two months. **The door at the back of that floor opens for the fifth time in about a year and the woman of fifty-three comes as far as the middle of that corridor and will not come the last four foot, and says so in four lines, and goes back.** The girl then does not go into the room at the back of that floor at all for the rest of the month, and the second copy of the week's minutes goes in on the older woman's own hand.

**0582. The Room At The Back Of That Floor, And A Woman Who Is Not Going To Stand At The End Of That Sill Again.** The same room, a week later, from the woman of fifty-three. Her question is on a shelf under a window with no pane in it and it does not finish and her own name is underneath it, and it has been there about eleven years. She has said a true thing at the end of that sill four times in about a year and been answered not once, and she has worked out that the sill is the worst place in that building to be and she is not going to stand at the end of it again. **The girl of nineteen comes to the door and stops four foot inside the threshold and says one thing, and the woman of fifty-three says it back to her as not being hers to say, and tells her that the copy is going to be done once a week now by both of them and neither of them is going to say a word about it.**

**0583. A Room Taken By The Week, And A Man Carrying A Glass To That Table Four Times In A Fortnight.** The room he takes by the week, alone. A small looking-glass in a wooden frame with the top left corner gone came with the room and has been on the table at the end of that bed for about two years and he has never looked into it, and this morning he puts it face up and does not pick it up again for an hour. **He works out that a person who stands in a place cannot afterwards be shown to have been anywhere else, and that he has had that for about ten years by never once standing still, and that he stood in the middle of that floor for the length of a coin going under a door and cannot name what he was doing.** A boot comes on the fourth step at the first hour and one of them says he has had a fortnight of not saying anything, and he does not say a true thing. **At the second hour he looks into the glass and there is a man of thirty-eight in it and it is not a bad-looking one, and he leaves the glass face up on the table for the rest of that day.**

**0584. The Sill At The Near End Of That Corridor, And A Pot Moved Four Inches Along It In A Fortnight.** The same room as 0581, from the man at the sill. **He has moved the tin four inches along that stone towards the near end in a fortnight and cannot say why and nobody has put it to him, and about three feet of that sill has been empty since.** A girl of nineteen is at the empty end of it with an armful on her hip and stays there, and he signs fifteen sheets and does not look four foot along the stone. Nothing is said by either of them. **The man at the sill has worked out that there is no second copy of a thing in this empire that has as much in it as a tin of two pens and a man with two hands, and that if a person stands at the empty end of it that is a thing about the person and not about the tin.**

**0585. The Front Of A Ground Floor Started At The Other End, And A Woman Who Is Not Going To Be Seen Standing At It.** The ground floor of a rented house. A woman who has been at the foot of that stair about twenty years has started the front of that ground floor at the far end instead of the near one, so that the whole of that passage can be seen from the step at the turn and there is nobody in it. **A woman of about thirty-five comes down that lane for the twenty-fifth week running and stands on the step below the turn and the two of them say six lines about a floor and about doing it on purpose, and neither of them says one word about the eleven weeks or the ten minutes.**

**0586. The Room At The Top Of A Stair, And A Satchel Set Down At The Far End Of Those Boards.** The counter four flights up, from the courier. He has been handed a thing every week for eleven years and has got nothing back and has never once been told what is in it, and in the last fortnight he has set the satchel down at the far end of those boards twice and this morning a third time. **A woman of thirty-four at that end tells him he has never once asked what is in it, and he says he has not, and she goes on with what she is at.** He stands nine foot off the satchel and waits while a sheet gets squared, and nobody comes, and he puts the strap over his own shoulder and takes it down four flights.

**0587. A Landing Four Foot By Six, And A Man Behind Four Inches Of Wood Who Has Not Stood On It.** The room he takes by the week, and the landing outside its door. A woman has been coming up that stair at about the fourth hour for about eight weeks. **He stood in the middle of that floor for the length of a coin going under a door and knows what he was doing and cannot name it.** She comes up at the first hour and says she has not knocked and is not going to and nobody in that house is going to be thanked for that, and he says that is not the arrangement, and she says that is the arrangement and it is the only one there is. She comes up again at the fourth hour and they say nothing at all and the gap under that door does not change.

**0588. A Wall Board Behind Those Boards With Four Items On It, And A Woman Who Put One Of Them Up Herself.** The room at the top of a stair, from the woman of thirty-four. The fourth item on that wall board is in her own hand and has been there about four years and not one of the four items on that board is time. **In the last fortnight she has gone past that board twice a day instead of once and stopped at it neither time.** A woman of twenty-four at the near end of those boards tells her that nothing has ever been asked of her about that board and asks her nothing, and says she put that one up herself, and is told she is not going to take it down. **The chapter prices the man of thirty-eight's absence in a room he is not in: a man came up those four flights six times in his life and came down with nothing in his hands on every one of them, and she has kept the count and nobody in that building is going to be told that she has.**

**0589. A Girl Of Nineteen At The Far End Of That Sill At The Third Hour, And A Door That Stayed Shut.** The room with the sill in it, six weeks after 0581. A girl of nineteen has been at that empty end of that sill at the third hour every morning for about seven weeks, and the third hour is the hour the woman of fifty-three used to come out of that room, and the door at the back of that floor has not been opened for a fortnight. **The man at the sill says the first thing either of them has ever said to the other, which is that she has not set it down at that end before and that she used to go past, and signs a sheet and says nothing else.** She has worked out that a habit is a thing a person can be shown having and a place is a thing a person cannot be shown having or not having, and that she has just been given the first and came for the second.

**0590. The Room With The Sill In It, And Two People Four Feet Apart And Not One Word Between Them.** The same room, a week later, from the man at the sill. He has stopped keeping track of which of his two hands goes into that tin and this morning could not have said afterwards which one had gone in. **The girl of nineteen comes along that corridor at the third hour and stops at the far end of that sill, and he says four lines to her, and twenty words pass between them in four years, and neither of them can be shown to have asked the other for anything.** The door at the back of that floor opens for about as long as a sheet takes to be squared and nothing comes through it, and about nine hundred sheets go out of that building that year and not one of them says a word about a tin moved four inches or a girl at the other end of it or a man who could not say afterwards which of his own hands had been in it.
