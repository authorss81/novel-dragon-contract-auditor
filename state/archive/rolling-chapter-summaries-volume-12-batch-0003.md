# VOLUME 12, CHAPTERS 0571 TO 0580, BATCH 0003, MOVEMENT THREE, WHAT A REFUSAL COSTS WHEN IT IS IN THE WORLD — TEN CHAPTER SUMMARIES

*Written by the phase that wrote the ten chapters. **These are summaries and not the chapters, and where a summary and the page disagree the page wins and neither is edited to fit the other.** The measurements are in `state/batch-summary.md` once and no figure is repeated here.*

**0571, the room taken by the week, and a lamp. Point of view: the protagonist, in person, and his want is visible.** He has had a lamp on that floor for about two years and has never lit it, and in the last fortnight he has carried it up to the table and back three times, and it is in his hand at about the first hour on this morning. **The lamp came with the room, and in about two years the only thing that has come through that door is a coin that goes flat under it and stops, and so there is nobody that lamp belongs to but the floor it stands on.** A boot comes on the second tread. **Four lines: *it is dark in there*, *it is dark in there*, *it is not dark out here and it has never been dark out here*, *there is a window in it*, and then *I have put a coin under it for two years. I know what is in it*.** She steps on the corner of the mat, gets the coin under, and says *the corner will be down by the time I come back*. He does not open the door and she does not knock. **He works out that a man who lights a lamp is a man who has said out loud, to a room, that he is going to be in it after dark, and there is no form in this empire that takes a man for that, and he puts the lamp down and squares it to the wall.** The light goes at about the fourth hour and earlier than the day before, and he does that hour in the dark at the back of a chair that faces four feet of plaster. **What the dark gives him is this: he cannot tell whether there is a person in the room behind that wall or a room with a person gone out of it, and there is no way in this city of settling that which does not begin with a light being put on.**

**0572, the ground floor of a rented house, and a passage with nothing in it. Point of view: the woman of about thirty-five.** She has said one true thing out loud in that passage in the third week of the seventh month and has decided on the step below the turn to do it again every week after that, and for a fortnight running she has done it to no room at all, and this is the first week she has come down that lane to try. **The boards at the front of that ground floor have not been done for five weeks.** The top step at the turn is bare and her own paper is not on it, because she has had it under her arm since the third week of the seventh month. She comes down at about the third hour, goes along the passage, and stands on the step below the turn with her arms down for about as long as it takes to square a sheet and square it back. **The passage is about as long as a table and she can see the whole of it from the step at the turn, and there was nothing in it.** She could have said it to the tin and did not, and the reason is that a sentence said to a tin with a lid shut straight on it is a sentence said to a lid. **At about the fifth hour the woman at the bottom of that stair comes along the back of that ground floor with the cloth and does not come into the passage at all and goes out at the front door, and the front boards have a grey bloom on them and the last marks on them are five weeks old.** Nobody is thanked and nobody is asked, and the tin is going to be on that step when neither of them is in that house.

**0573, the shed on the Slade, and a true thing said out loud in a bay. Point of view: the foreman of fifty-one, and the movement's smallest thing is in this chapter and is not said in a mouth.** She had worked out that she had put the bundle at the far end of that bench to be looked at and had not wanted a girl of seventeen to look at it, and this morning she wants something worse and wants it out loud. She is fifty-one and the back of that bay is where a class left her eleven years ago and she has not walked to the front of it in about six weeks. **What she wanted was for the girl to turn round, and she got it another way and has not worked out since whether she chose it or it arrived. She says it at the ordinary volume, in the ordinary way, to the boards: *that girl has not been asked one thing in about a year and she has never once had the chance to turn anything down, and there is nowhere in this city to put the fact if she gets it.*** The girl is at the end by the door with a sheet in both hands, finishes what she is doing, puts the sheet on the top of the pile so that the top of it goes under about a foot of another sheet, squares it with the heel of her hand, picks up the next one, and does not turn round. **A boy of seventeen is at the far end where the count is made, nine foot off her, and that bay is about nine foot by eleven, and nobody in that shed has ever wanted one single thing out of him.** Cost, in her own interiority: **she wanted a thing put where the girl could put it back, and what she has instead found is the one instrument there is for saying a true thing where a person is working, and it works, and she did not choose it, and she has made a girl of seventeen the second pair of ears for a sentence about herself, and the girl was not asked and is never going to be asked and is never going to find out what she was standing in the air of.** The paper on the board by that door has not been filled in and she has not taken a pen to it in eleven years.

**0574, the ground floor of a rented house, and seven weeks of a bloom on the front boards. Point of view: the woman at the bottom of a stranger's stair.** She has not been to the front of that ground floor for seven weeks and has worked out in the first of them that the boards there are the only record in that house of a thing she has ever changed her mind about. The third tread has never had a boot of hers on it. **A rent comes out of a hand once a week and goes into a tin and has not been on a piece of paper since before she came, and she has put a receipt into nobody's hand in the whole of it, and what that house has to say about anybody sleeping in it is a number cut into the back of a key.** The marks on the front are about two inches apart and there have been no new ones in seven weeks. The woman of about thirty-five comes down that lane at about the fourth hour and says *that front has not been done*, and is told *it has not been done*, and says *you do the front*, and is told *I have not done the front for seven weeks*, and the other one comes through from the back with the cloth and says **I am going along this passage. That is the whole of what I do in it**, and goes out at the front door. **In her own interiority: the front of that ground floor can be seen from the step at the turn, and there is one person alive who comes down that lane and has been in that passage about thirteen weeks, and a person who is standing in a room when a thing is said in it cannot afterwards be produced for any room she was not in, and she has got to the front of that ground floor and cannot go back to it.** She leaves the cloth on top of the boiler and does the boards back there out of the sight of the turn, and her hand does not go after the tin behind that boiler, and the top step is bare and the tin is on it and there is nothing else on that step.

**0575, the room taken by the week, and two coins on a table. Point of view: the protagonist, in person, and his want is visible.** He picked the coin up off the boards on the first morning and it has been on that table since, and on the second morning another one came under the door and he has two. **He gave what he had for a mat at the end of a lane about three months ago and there is not enough in that room to have a lamp lit or a plate washed every day of a week, and both have been going on about four days out of seven for about two years.** The mat is about three months old, it belongs to the landing, and its near corner has stood up at the joint for about two months. She comes up at the first hour. **Five lines: *there is one of those every week*, *I have it*, *you have not opened that door*, *No*, and then *I know it is down*, and he has never said one word to her about the mat and he says one now through about four inches of wood.** Interiority: **a person who cannot be shown having received a thing cannot be shown having wanted one either, and that has been the arrangement for about ten years and it has cost him nothing, and a coin came under that door and he picked it up and there is no putting that back, and the only thing there is to do with a coin you have been given is to have a second one and give it back.** He waits until her boot has gone down past the second tread, turns the rail, opens his own door about a foot, gets down on one knee with the coin flat on the boards and pushes it, and the two of them lie against each other. **Then he stands in his own doorway for about as long as it takes a coin to travel under a door, and the stair goes down to the ground floor and does not come back up, and he does not go down it.**

**0576, the shed on the Slade, and an armful set down at the far end of that bench. Point of view: the reader of seventeen.** He had carried every armful past the end of that bench for about two months so that a girl standing there would never have it put down in front of her, and she has not been standing there for about a week, and he has gone on carrying them. **Three weeks earlier the woman who puts work at that end came down the ninety foot and said a true thing out loud about the girl at the end by the door, and he was at the far end where the count is made, and that bay is about nine foot by eleven, and there was nobody else in it.** Interiority: **he heard it, she was working, there is no form anywhere in this city that takes him as the one who heard it and there is not going to be one, and a person who has heard a thing cannot afterwards be shown to have been somewhere they were not, or to have agreed with it, or to have refused it, and the girl at the other end of that bench does not know he was there and is not going to be told.** He gets in about ten minutes after the lane wakes and he is the only person in that shed who could tell that she has stopped coming in a quarter of an hour early, and he has said nothing and is not going to. **So he sets the armful down on his own end, at the back of the bay, for the first time in about three months, and she comes along the bench at about the fifth hour and does not stop at it and does not look down at it and does not touch it and is not going to, and the count is not done for about an hour.** He wanted to be able to give her one. That is what the armfuls were for, and there are three things a person can do with a thing put where she can get at it, and the third one is that she walks past it and leaves it standing there, and nobody in this empire has a name for that.

**0577, the ground floor of a rented house, and ten minutes of a cloth on the front boards. Point of view: the woman of about thirty-five.** She has come down that lane for five weeks running and stood on the step below the turn in an empty passage, and this week she is standing in the middle of it with a whole sentence ready. **The boards at the front of that ground floor have not been done for ten weeks and the top step has the tin on it and the lid flat down on it.** The woman at the bottom of that stair comes through from the back with the cloth and stops, and says **you are in the way of the boards**, and is told *I am not doing anything* and *you are standing in the middle of them*, and **goes to the front of that ground floor and puts the cloth on the boards and works them at the rate she does everything, and the marks go on about two inches apart with a sound on them that the house has not had in ten weeks, and she does not say one word while she does it.** Interiority: **there was a person working in that passage for ten minutes and I had a true thing ready about a column I have not ruled in nine years, and I said nothing, and the reason I said nothing is that I got there, and a thing said out loud where a person is working takes that person's standing there away from them, and she is twenty years at the foot of that stair with a number cut into the back of a key and I was going to take the only thing she has.** She stands on the step below the turn and lets it be done, and afterwards there is nothing on that front to be a record of anything, and **there is another room and there has been for about a year, and she has been up that stair once and stood at the top of it, and she is not going to think about that room on a step at a turn in another woman's house.** She goes up the lane at the pace she came down it and is not going to say one word about it to anybody for as long as she lives.

**0578, the shed on the Slade, and a bundle at the far end of that bench. Point of view: the girl of seventeen.** She gave up the quarter of an hour about three weeks ago, and there is a bundle at the end of that bench on this morning and she is not there early enough to see who put it. She is seventeen, and in about a year nobody in that shed has wanted anything out of her at all, and **there has never been a single thing put in her reach for her to turn down, and so there is nothing in her that anybody could produce as a refusal.** She was standing at the far end of that bench about a quarter of an hour before the lane woke, four mornings running, to stand where a thing could be put that she could put back. **Then the woman came down ninety foot and said a true thing out loud about her, and she was at the end by the door with a sheet in both hands and she put it down on the pile and squared it and picked up the next one and did not turn round.** Interiority: **what she has worked out since is about a quarter of an hour and nothing else, because a person who is in a room before the room is awake is a person who can be shown having been in it, and that bay is about nine foot by eleven, and she had been standing at the far end of that bench in it a quarter of an hour before anybody came in, four mornings, and the two of those are a quarter of an hour apart and nothing at all, and what it has cost is the only thing she ever wanted in that shed, which was to be standing somewhere a thing could be put that she could turn down.** She looks at the bundle for about as long as it takes a square a sheet, and goes to her own end. **He comes in about ten minutes after her, stops about four inches short of it, works round it, does not move it and does not read the top of it, and goes on with what he was on the whole of that morning.** She has let it stand, because carrying two of them on is being shown to have wanted both, and she wanted one and has never found out which. **It stood at the far end of that bench until the sixth hour and went out with the rest of that floor, and the count was short again.**

**0579, a landing, and a door she stood in front of to see whether it was lit. Point of view: the woman at the bottom of a stranger's stair.** She has begun going up past the second stair at about the fourth hour, and has been doing it for a fortnight, and she did not decide to start and is not going to stop. The first hour is the hour the coin goes up and no other hour of any day has ever had her on that flight. **She does the boards at the back of that ground floor now and has done for about twelve weeks, and there is no lamp in that back room and the light comes up out of the well, and from the back of that ground floor you cannot see the window of the room on the other side of it, and from the landing you can see under the door.** She stands about a yard off that door. The light goes off that landing while she is standing there, earlier than the week before, and the gap under the door does not change, because there was nothing in that room to change. **Three things through the wood: *there is a window in it*, *I know there is a window in it*, *I have a lamp*, and then *you have not lit it*.** He does not open the door and she does not knock. Interiority: **she went up those stairs to find out whether there is a light in that room and she has found out that there is not one, and that is no information at all, because a room with a man in it doing the last of an hour in the dark and a room with nobody in it are the same dark, and she has come down with the one thing she came up for and it is the thing she had before she started, and she has given up the only thing she ever had, which is that nobody can produce anything about her.** She goes down that stair at the rate she comes down it and does not stop on the third tread, and the light in that room was the same as the light on that landing in the end.

**0580, the shed on the Slade, and ninety foot walked down and back with nothing on the bench. Point of view: the girl of seventeen, and the movement closes on her and on nothing being resolved.** She has not come in a quarter of an hour early for about a month and has stopped standing at the far end of that bench at all, and the woman comes down the ninety foot and puts nothing anywhere. There have been two things put at the far end of that bench in about two months. **She carried the first one the nine foot to her own end. The second one is a fortnight gone and she has never found out who put it there, and the woman who puts work at that end has stayed at the back of that bay since the morning she said it.** At about the fourth hour the woman comes down all ninety foot at the rate she walks, does not stop at the front of that bench, does not put her hand on the wood, comes along it past the far end and on to the end by the door where the girl works, and stops there about as long as a sheet takes to be squared. The girl has a sheet in both hands and does not stop and does not turn round, and nothing was put on that bench that morning. **Interiority: she came all that way and stopped at her end and said nothing, and there are only two ways anybody in that shed has ever got a thing put in her reach, which are to stand at the front of that bench for a quarter of an hour first and then put it down so it looks like ordinary work, and to say something out loud in that bay while a person is in it. She did the first of those once. She did the second of those once, and it was about the girl, and she is not going to be given one at the end of that bench by anybody in that shed as long as she is in it.** The count has been short twice a week for about a month, and **she is going to be at that end of that bench at the hour, every working morning, in the open, where anybody coming into that shed can see her standing there and say she was never anywhere else, and she has put herself back on purpose, and this morning she put her hand flat on the wood and left it there about as long as it takes a sheet to be squared.**

## The three figures in these ten entries that were wrong on the page when the chapter was written, and what the lattice makes, and the standing

**The bloom on the front boards of that ground floor is the one figure that fought the calendar and it fought it in four files.** It is five weeks old in `chapter-0572.md`, which is where the count starts, and the three files after it had it at five, twelve, twelve and twelve. The date lines are the lattice and the count is not, and every one of the four is now **five, seven, ten, and referred**, against a lattice of one week per chapter. **The number of weeks a woman has been in that passage was eleven in `chapter-0574.md` and the lattice makes thirteen, because Chapter 0570 is the tenth week of that lane.** One weekday name was written into `chapter-0578.md` in the first draft and is not on the page. *The date lines of all ten are byte-identical to the ones the batch derived from `chapter-0570.md`'s own line five, and no date line was moved by any of the corrections above.*

## The one coinage in these ten entries that is this repository's word and not the page's, and is named rather than rewritten

**Four of the entries above use the word *instrument* to describe the second ear, or the absence of one, and it is the word `state/batch-summary.md` and this project's outlines use and the word the page does not.** The two entries written by the batch before carry the same coinage and were left in place by that batch's own repair, and this batch has followed the standing rather than improved on it: **the word is left in these entries and named here, and no chapter of the ten uses it.**
