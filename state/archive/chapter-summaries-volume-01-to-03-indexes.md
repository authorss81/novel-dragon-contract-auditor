### Chapter 0151 — The Number of a Run

A clerk of thirty-four at the fourth counter of the Notaries' Table in Auremar is given a four-figure number by a man who has copied it off a covering slip four hundred and thirty miles away, and tells him at the counter that she is not going to be a door for him and that a register on a high shelf is the only place in the city the numbers exist. She looks the run up on her own time over two days and reports that about thirty of the rows in that run are not scale instruments at all but a striking-out of a name out of a roll, because an impression takes a number like anything else, and that the column a name goes in is headed *by whose authority*, which is a heading and not a charge, and that a name under it is an office discharged and not an admission. She refuses to read the day aloud in a public room and gives the two prices of not having it, one of which is free and has been paid. The chapter ends on the fee board, which has four things on it and none of them is what is in the book, and that this is not a fault in the board.
