# AFTER VOLUME 12 BATCH 0003, THE SAME PEOPLE, AND WHAT TEN MORE WEEKS DID TO EACH OF THEM

*Written by the phase that wrote Chapters 0571 to 0580. **A batch and not a repair, not a close and not a second reading.** It wrote no entry for anybody new and invented no person, and **no person below is re-entered: who each of them is, and what each is owed, refused and not asked, is in the roster above, under *THE CHARACTER STATE AT CHAPTER 0500*, and this section says only what ten more weeks did to them.** That is a different object and it is kept apart for the reason the file's own head gives: a cast is a working surface and a working surface holds a person once. **Volume 11's five per-batch records are all in `state/archive/` already and nothing was moved by this batch.**

**The rule this section is here to enforce, because a batch is where a person is invented: every person who appears in Chapters 0571 to 0580 is a person already on the page, named by what distinguishes them, and this batch adds no new named figure and no new person of any kind.** The new objects in the whole batch are a lamp, two coins, a grey bloom on a floor, ten minutes of a cloth, and a bundle at the far end of a bench, and none of them is a person and none of them is a copy of anything.

## What the ten weeks did, person by person, in the present tense, because all of it is still true

**The man of thirty-eight. In person in two of the ten chapters and absent with his absence priced in a third.** In `chapter-0571.md` he is in the room taken by the week for the whole chapter and **his want is visible by a new object: a lamp, which came with the room, which has never been lit in about two years, and which he has carried up to the table and put back three times in a fortnight.** He has it in his hand at about the first hour on the morning a boot comes on the second tread, and he puts it down, and he works out that a man who lights a lamp is a man who has said out loud to a room that he is going to be in it after dark, and he does the last hour of the light in the dark. In `chapter-0575.md` he is in that room for the whole chapter, **a coin has been on his table for a day and a night and a second one came under the door, and he waits for her boot to go down past the second tread, opens his own door about a foot, pushes a coin back under it and stands in his own doorway and does not go down that stair.** In `chapter-0579.md`, in a room he is not in, **his absence is priced: a woman has come up that stair at the fourth hour for the first time in twenty years to find out whether his room has a light in it, and the light on the landing went while she was standing there and the gap under his door did not change, and she came down with nothing.** *Owed, refused and unanswered are unchanged and are in the roster above.* **He did not say out loud the sentence about a name coming back, did not arrive at a sentence and deliver it, did not say anything out loud that he has already said, did not go up the four flights, did not come down that stair, did not buy anything, did not look in the coat, did not take either paper out, did not cut anything, did not send for anybody, and nobody told him anything in ten weeks.** The mat is spent as an object and the second plate is spent as a want and neither is used again as one.

**The woman of about thirty-five with a day-book of her own hand. She is the point of view of two of the ten chapters and is in two more.** The turn is not repeated and is not revoiced. **She has not said a second true thing out loud in that passage or in any other room in ten weeks, and in `chapter-0577.md` she had one ready in a passage with a woman working in it for ten minutes and did not say it, and the reason is in her own interiority: saying a thing where a person is working takes that person's standing there away from them for good, and that woman has twenty years of being a person nobody could produce anything about and she was not going to take it off her for a sentence about a column she has not ruled in nine years.** She has stood in an empty passage for five weeks in `chapter-0572.md` and has come down the lane for the fifteenth week in `chapter-0577.md` and has thought about a sixth room at the top of a stair, where a woman comes four times a week and has been handed a thing every week for eleven years, and **has referred it to herself in her own interiority and has not gone up that stair and is not going to this month.** Whether she takes a colleague's judgment on a thing like this is unmade and was not made in this batch in either direction and no row of this section may be read as having made it.

**The woman at the bottom of a stranger's stair. She is the point of view of two of the ten chapters and is in two more.** **She has not done the boards at the front of that ground floor in seven weeks and does them for ten minutes in `chapter-0577.md` for no reason anybody is ever going to hear stated, and the reason is on the page in her own interiority and is that the front of that ground floor can be seen from the step at the turn and a person who comes down that lane has been in that passage about thirteen weeks.** She has refused four things this month and each was an act: she will not come into the passage when the other one is standing in it, she will not say a word about the ten minutes, she will not be asked about the mat or the tin or the boards, and **in `chapter-0579.md` she goes up past the second stair at the fourth hour for the first time in twenty years, which she has done about a fortnight of mornings running, and she has not decided to start and is not going to stop.** About nine years of one man's rent is in that tin and it is still not on paper and she has still given nobody a receipt for anything in twenty years. **She has never once been asked about any of it and is not going to be, and she still has not said a true thing out loud in that room in twenty years and did not say one this month either, and she still does not know that anybody said one there, and nothing in these ten chapters comes one inch nearer to her knowing it.**

**The foreman of fifty-one. She is the point of view of `chapter-0573.md`.** **She has said one true thing out loud in that bay, in the ordinary way, at the ordinary volume, to the boards, on an ordinary morning, and a girl of seventeen was at the end of that bench with her back to her and did not turn round.** She wanted the girl to turn round and did not get it. **She has now found the one instrument this empire has for saying a true thing where a person is working, and it works, and she did not choose it, and she chose everything else about that shed for eleven years, and the cost of it lands on the girl and cannot be entered and is not hers to pay.** In `chapter-0580.md` she comes down ninety foot of that floor and stops at the end by the door where the girl works and puts nothing anywhere and goes back up. **She has priced both ways there are of getting a thing put in a girl's reach, which is to stand at the front of that bench for a quarter of an hour first and then put it down so it looks like ordinary work, and to say something out loud in that bay while a person is in it, and she has done each of them once and is not going to do either again.** Her sentence, begun in a doorway about two years ago, has still not arrived and this batch did not arrive it. The paper on the board by that door has not been filled in.

not going to be, and nobody in that shed is going to be told that she did it and she is not going to be thanked for it and she is not going to be sent for.** The reader of seventeen is the point of Not once, in any of the ten chapters, not in a way that could be softened into a hint, and not by the narrator.** What is permitted happened: a boot came on the second tread; a tin was carried round a turn; ten minutes of a cloth went over a floor with nobody in the passage; a woman stood in the middle of a passage; a foreman said a true thing to the boards; a girl put a sheet down on a pile; a boy set an armful down at his own end; a bundle stood at the far end of a bench until the sixth hour; and a man put a coin back under a door. **And the decision about whether a woman of twenty-four takes a colleague's judgment on a thing like this is still unmade, still not on the page, she is in no chapter of these ten, and no row of this section may be read as having made it.** The pull to resolve it is the correct sign that the volume is working.
