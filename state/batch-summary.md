### Volume 01, Batch 0001 (Chapters 1–10) — The First Correction

- **Goal and outcome:** The batch set out to make the Venn alteration a human problem with a winter deadline, prove it was already in a second Mosswake household, and force Marek to choose public accountability over a quiet fix. The Venn case was won on the twelfth of the month: Tavi's personal debt is void, the field and the remaining term are preserved, Sivra Oris's gate and navigation duty survives, and the bank works are taken off the Venn family by the district water office. Corvin Dray is arrested after his ledger is copied, and a nine-paragraph audit request is in Examinations custody with its scope undecided. The public-necessity sentence was read at two o'clock and the Dunn household was broken on it in the same minute, eighty minutes before the five terms were ruled, which is the intended reversal and is slightly worse than a later batch might assume.
- **New canon:** (1) The Venn summary carried a third witness notch that was never pressed into the original, and the filed correction bore no attached authority; the Reed Cut flood memorandum was dated the eighth of the eighth month and the correction was received on the ninth. (2) The back of the correction leaf carries a flat, even certificate impression and a broker's fee entry naming Candlewright Lane in Auremar, which Marek has on the page in Chapter 4 and cannot use until Chapter 7. (3) The source leaf stays in the Notaries' Table's cabinet in Auremar, and a comparison cannot be had by a clerk who is not in the room when the sleeve comes out; the leaf reaches Mosswake only because Ivet Sarn sent her own rider on the district court's custody slip on the night of the tenth. (4) The ruling of the twelfth has **five** operative terms: the void debt, the preserved field, Sivra Oris's unaffected duty, the new-signature rule (always "the fourth term"), and the district works with the household's release from field-side upkeep (always "the fifth term"). The fifth term is Tavi's own ask, made on the step of the Venn house on the evening of the tenth, and Rell put it last on purpose. (5) The first Roll-Answer was earned by exercising the limited Venn audit at the Chapter 8 hearing and was taken against the operative filed record after the ruling, so it names the present holder of the field, not Tavi Venn. (6) The public-necessity sentence, read once in full at the hearing, is now the first portable institutional wording of the inheritance echo, and the Mosswake registrar filed the previously unfiled Dunn summary on its authority in the same minute the sentence was read at two o'clock, which is before the five terms were ruled at twenty past three. (7) Corvin Dray's business is a sold mark and a sold date, not written sentences: sixty-one copied certificates and ninety-eight witness sequences at four shillings each, with entries for Venn, Dunn, and two provincial files at Glasswater and Emberwatch. (8) A sealed First Seat document is a printed four-paragraph administrative form, *Continuity Filings, Flood Seasons, Categorisation*, carrying a series number and a print run, which requires flood-season continuity filings to be entered in the routine column and not itemised or heard individually except on quarterly review. (9) An unexplained faded red running stitch with a sequence of small marks appears in the margin of a leaf at the bottom of a lane-man's mother's bundle, and one of the twenty-six public copies was written on that leaf because it was the paper to hand; a thread of the same colour is caught in the fold of the sealed form. (10) Ivet Sarn stated in Chapter 3 that it took her four days to open a file in the fifth year and that on the fourth day the relief rolls could not be certified; which file and which household are still unspent. (11) Minor new named figures bound for reuse: Garren Pyle, Corvin's attending certifier; Ivo Harker, the old lane-man who keeps the lane's papers in a tin; Brammel, one of Corvin's waiting clients; Hallis Dren, who testified as the water-office clerk who signed the memorandum; Wend and Pella Osk, the two court working-staff Tamsin names in open court; and Doctor Perrin Ashe of the Mosswake river landing.
- **Costs and consequences:** Marek paid for the first reading in fatigue, a persistent headache, an ash taste, and a temporary inability to lie about one named obligation, still active at the end of the batch. He spent his supervisor's goodwill, his private comparison advantage, and his habit of deciding what other people could safely know; Nell and Tamsin both made him pay for it in conditions, and Tamsin's trust is conditional rather than repaired. Tavi's field is safe until the second month, when the seven-year term expires, and not a day longer, and Tavi is refused a guild seat until the Dunn question is decided. Orra Dunn has accepted a hearing on the twenty-third that can only expose her whole household. The seed-barn summary was already in the machine before Marek proved anything, and the machinery has a name now: a form with a run on it, four hundred miles off.
- **Carried forward:** Open: who ordered the corrections, who writes the four sentences Corvin will not name, what the First Seat form is and who prints it, what the red stitch is, and why the Dunn summary predates the Venn correction by four months. Sarn's refusal must still be explained in Chapters 11–18. Nell's exit clause has been signed by Nell and the wage schedule but not yet accepted by the guild, which is Vare's Chapters 11–18 problem. The audit request sits in Examinations with no scope and no named office answer. The next batch inherits one dated question: on the twenty-third, a separate sitting decides whether fifteen-year-old Nessa Dunn can be joined as guarantor on a summary she never signed, and nobody in the room yet knows that a person who is owed must first be heard.

### Volume 01, Batch 0002 (Chapters 11–20) — What a Ruling Costs

- **Goal and outcome:** The batch took the local win and charged it. The fifth term's works actually get done, under Dren's own flood authority, on a sheet nailed to a gate rail, and the lane immediately reads Tavi's five months of unpaid ditch-cutting as acceptance — which is the price of the girl's own ask, named out loud on the tenth. Orra gets her sitting on the twenty-third and gets a postponement with a date and no bar, which Rell says out loud is worse for that household than a refusal; the court's own limitation is entered, dated the twenty-third, that it will not apply that sentence again on its own motion without a separate hearing and a signature. Sarn's four days are put on the record with a reason and a named household, and the Sallow wording turns out to be the Venn wording four *years* older, which makes the whole thing a practice rather than a fraud. Nell's exit clause is amended and the craft exemption survives, and the amendment costs a levy that lands hardest on one woman at the Caudle house. Tamsin sends the First Seat form to thirteen named people without publishing it and without asking. The two letters arrive, two provincial files are confirmed, and the batch climax finds the mechanism: a printed model circular, **MC/FS**, sealed by the **Standing Office of Provincial Continuities**, whose amendment sheet of the twenty-second of the ninth month carries the Mosswake sentence as approved model wording in a run of **nine hundred and forty**. Examinations offers a bounded certification against the closure of the request. Marek has not answered.
- **New canon:** (1) A duty **pending full review** is a duty: a creditor may name it, hold security against it, and put a number on it, and the number does not wait for the review. (2) The Mosswake court window keeps a **public register of pending duties** in Sarn's hand — a date, a household, a duty, the next review, a shilling a copy — and it protects nobody by itself. (3) Ivet Sarn's four days: an upper-district barn term file in the fourth month of the **fifth year**, Rennie Sallow, eleven pounds of seed bought over the price, the Sallow summary carrying the same four lines. The wording in that file is word-for-word the wording in the Venn correction, four years older, prepared in the Mosswake court building. (4) Nell's amendment: tool debts named to the departing worker, a sum set out, eight weeks, balance and recovery carried by the **Greyfen Dyers' Tool Fund**; four houses on the lane raise **nine pounds a quarter** between them and the Caudle share is **two pounds and five a quarter**. (5) A district court's **clerk may admit an exhibit on her own authority**, without the magistrate; exhibits are copied to the parties on the day of admission; a copy that has gone to a party cannot be recalled. (6) The **Forms Received rack** in the passage behind the third gallery, unlocked and indexed by sending office, holds the printed series. (7) **MC/FS**, *Model Endorsements for Continuity Filings (Flood Seasons)*, Standing Office of Provincial Continuities, distributed to all district courts and landing offices holding a flood docket and to be filed with the standing instructions; base issue of the second month of this year, run **five hundred and sixty**; amendment sheet of the **twenty-second of the ninth month**, run **nine hundred and forty**, carrying the Mosswake sentence verbatim with a note reading *Adopted from a provincial court, the ninth month.* (8) The First Seat form is a **quarterly return**, not a rule, and it works by making the honest answer small rather than by making it a lie. (9) Two provincial files: Glasswater (a delta landing, a three-year holder, a sixteen-year-old in the surety line, a miller writing to Osric Lave in Greyfen) and Emberwatch (a **dyers' house** whose second instrument names a person, so the exemption is exposed). Both **itemised**. (10) The ledger's mark: a filled square with a bar and a nick, against **thirty-eight** lines in the *carry* column, in **three** districts, naming a counting house on a row by the Kiln sheds and no person. (11) Marek signed a **narrowing** confining the request to Auremar with the two provincial entries reserved and not abandoned. (12) The reading's cost lapsed on the page on the thirtieth of the tenth and is described as a lapse, not a cure; the headache went on the twenty-sixth. (13) The reading table is now a **lane institution with a rota of four** — Ivo Harker, the seed-barn copyist entered as a role, Coll Fenner, Wenna Sallow — with a chalk rule and no clerk and no number. (14) The audit request is answered for the first time and offered closure against an **empire-wide** six-month certification of the Mosswake practice with a review in the fourth month; Marek filed a two-page **supplementary request** naming MC/FS, both issues, both runs, the distribution line, and the signer.
- **Costs and consequences:** The Venn family gets a winter and a version of itself in four mouths it cannot call back. The Dunn family gets a date instead of a safety, and a factor in Greyfen holding Tale's paper for the second month. The dye-house gets a clause and a levy and an exposure, and Crenn signs a form she does not believe because a form that has been read will be read again. Four of the eleven Candlewright Lane clients shut, three cannot work because the protection order covers paper and not competence, and a chandler loses his custom to a bar on his door. Bryn Mawer gets a line in a file and no wages. Rennie Sallow pays eleven pounds and gets an apology in his own window. The certified offer is real and would protect the current supply, and it is the price of the thing Marek actually wants. The letter to Magistrate Rell is written and not sent, and Tamsin has not yet told Marek what Pell Vey said in the Chancery yard.
- **Carried forward:** Marek must answer the certification by the **fourth month**, and answering no means several households are pressed in the meantime. The two provincial files are in four buildings and no court has ruled on them. Perry Ostrow is in a copyist's room in Auremar with nowhere to go until the end of the week and a court to go back to. The Emberwatch letter is unsigned and its sender is unknown. The counting house on the Kiln row has a mark and no name. The circular's issuer, the amendment's signer, and the first issue of the series are all unestablished. The second sealed page is still shut and now has a refusal against Marek's name in a form in Examinations.

### Notes for the next batch

- **Length, measured.** Chapters 11–20 are **50,028 prose words** (prose only, headings and scene-break rules excluded), an average of about 5,000 a chapter. Batch 0001 is 43,815 on the same count, and the manuscript stands at 93,843 words across twenty chapters. The two longest chapters are **Chapter 10 at 7,944** and **Chapter 20 at 7,007**; the shortest is Chapter 2 at 3,095. The guide's 2,200–3,200 is a soft range and these are not inside it. Do not read the two batches as a licence to keep lengthening: the runs that are actually long are the ones carrying a hearing, an inventory, or a batch climax, and those are the ones that were long because a scene would not come apart.
- **Pacing exposure, recorded deliberately.** At the current average the volume lands near 235,000 words over fifty chapters, which is long for a Volume 1 and longer than `outline/volume-01.md` implies. This is not a reason to compress Chapters 1–20, which are finished and which the cards earned. It is a reason for **Batch 0003 and later to land nearer 3,200–4,000 a chapter**: give Chapters 21–30 one room and one decision each instead of two, put a card's second beat into the following chapter instead of doubling the chapter, and resist opening a second location after the turn. Ten chapters at 3,600 is a better third batch than ten at 5,000. Never pad, and never split a finished scene to hit a number.
- **Calendar.** Batch 0001 sat inside the ninth month, the seventh to the eighteenth. Batch 0002 ran from the **nineteenth of the ninth** to the **ninth of the eleventh**: Ch 11 the 19th–20th, Ch 12 the 23rd, Ch 13 the 24th–27th of the ninth, Ch 14 the 30th of the ninth to the 4th of the tenth, Ch 15 the 9th–10th of the tenth, Ch 16 the 1st–11th of the tenth, Ch 17 the 18th–19th of the tenth, Ch 18 the 23rd–30th of the tenth, Ch 19 the 3rd–4th of the eleventh, Ch 20 the 8th–9th of the eleventh. **Weekday names appear in the prose of Batch 0002, but only as recurring local schedules and appointments** — Dren's crew on a Monday, the assessors' man on a Tuesday with the rack, the seed bench swept every fourth day on a Thursday, Brammel at the office on a Thursday — and never as a way of fixing a date in the chain. The day-by-date calendar here and in `state/continuity.md` is what governs, and a weekday name must never be used to derive or to check a date.
- **Two documents, not one, and they must not be merged.** The **circular** carries the words and is sealed by the Standing Office of Provincial Continuities. The **First Seat form** is the quarterly return that classifies and counts, is sealed with the fire-and-water device, and contains not one word of the wording. There is no document connecting them, and nobody in either office is on the page. The mechanism is a practice, not a conspiracy.
- **The four lines and the sentence have different ages.** The four lines (the substituted *present holder* wording) are in the Sallow file in the fifth year and are in twenty-two of thirty-one MC/FS sheets in the rack, the oldest of them older than the ninth year before last. The public-necessity sentence is new: stated on the twelfth of the ninth, printed on the twenty-second. No chapter may put the sentence in an older file.
- **The Venn ruling has five terms and the numbering is canon.** "The fourth term" is the new-signature rule and is the phrase the lane now uses by itself. "The fifth term" is the district works. In Chapter 18 the fourth term is used in the lane by people nobody trained, which is the batch's proof that the ruling escaped.
- The word **exception**, counter-seal, and true name do not appear in this batch and must not appear in the next. No reading, no Seam-Sight, no Stage 2. The first Seam-Sight is spent.
- **Pella Osk is still owed a chapter.** She was named in open court in Chapter 7 as a person who will say what the copying room was like on the ninth and has not yet been asked. Wend the lamp-trimmer is bound in the same way.
- The **Reed Cut quarterly return** Hallis Dren answered honestly on the eleventh of the tenth — one, and yes — went four hundred miles in a sealed sleeve, and **no character in the story knows this yet.** It is a live charge and it is dormant.
- The **red stitch** and the **thread in the form's fold** are not mentioned anywhere in Batch 0002 and are not joined. The prohibition Marek entered in Chapter 10 stands. Ivo Harker's tin no longer holds the stitched leaf; Tamsin took it on the night of the sixteenth of the ninth.

## Format for each entry

`### Volume NN, Batch NNNN (Chapters X–Y) — <short title>`

Then, in this order:

- **Goal and outcome:** what the batch set out to do and what actually happened, in two or three sentences.
- **New canon:** only facts that change the world, the power rules, or a character's standing. Do not restate the cards.
- **Costs and consequences:** what Marek paid physically, materially, or relationally, and what remains unpaid.
- **Carried forward:** the open threads this batch advanced, and the exact question the next batch inherits.

Do not put scene description, prose excerpts, or chapter-by-chapter retellings here. Per-chapter detail belongs in `state/chapter-summaries.md`.
