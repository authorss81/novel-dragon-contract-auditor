**THE FOUR DEBTS THIS VOLUME OPENED ON A PERSON, AND THEY ARE FLAGGED IN THEIR OWN ENTRIES BELOW AND ARE NOT LOST ANYWHERE. One, Halla Wray: about nine men now know she is in a box, and there will not be another season in which she could ask one of the four who cannot read a paragraph, and nobody decided that and nothing was resolved. Two, the man of about thirty-four: he is a party and is findable by his own question, there is no form in this empire for asking a person whether they will mind being told something, and he has decided not to ask a second question. Three, the clerk of about fifty-five: she is the only person in this matter who is answerable in writing for a question, on her own motion, and there is no instrument anywhere that will let her be asked whether she meant it. Four, Tamsin Rook: the decision about whether she trusts his judgment, which is the volume's first debt, unmade, not on the page, and not made for her.** No romance has begun anywhere in this volume and none is implied, and the relationship lock held in all five movements: two instruments that were never merged, two boxes on a page ruled by hand that are not merged with either instrument, a rule used once and a correct non-use entered, an undertaking live and unretired and unamended and unexercised, and a fourth envelope untouched, unsent and unopened with the noun not used in the prose.

- **Marek Kest, thirty-eight.** **He chose it, and the choosing was the removal of the only person in this empire who could have refused him.** On the fourth day of the fourth week of the eighth month of the year after next, at about the fifth hour of the morning, he filled the twelfth line of a book with no office on it — empty since the third week of the ninth month of the year after — read it back once and read it back again with the second reading slower, and then chose a labour he can be refused, and said out loud that this is a worse position than the other one and that he has known it since the third week of the fourth month of the year after next, when a woman of twenty asked him whether anybody could protect him. **A man who cannot be found cannot be made answerable, and a person who asks a question in a room is a party from that day on, and the whole of the cost of a fifth asking is the price of being findable, and he cannot pay it twice.** The day comes out of about forty-four pounds a year; there is no fund and no form anywhere in this empire for a fund; **the road was walked zero times across all fifty chapters of this volume and nobody was sent.** **He has torn up the only document he has ever had** — a sheet of clean paper nobody in this empire has ever issued him, kept about nine weeks in the drawer of his table — and gave the reason out loud, which is that a person with a clean sheet in a drawer in a rented room is a person waiting to be asked something and therefore a person with a document, and that about four years is how long anything about him can be found for. He has opened the door to his bread and said nothing to the step. He answered a man of about thirty-four on a landing about the cost of the choice, gave the price of the day, refused the cost of the choice in the same breath, said he had said it once and was not going to say it twice, said the man did not hear it, and said he was not going to pretend to him that he did. **He has no certification, has held none at any point in five volumes, and is never shown withdrawing one; his bar came off on the twelfth of the ninth month of last year on an application of one line which he wrote himself, and that is the canon sentence word for word wherever it is carried.** The line about two inches long on the inside of his left wrist reads nothing, decides nothing, imposes no tether, and does not know where the ford is. **Owed and unpaid:** a labour nobody can instruct and cannot pay and cannot thank; about four years of findability; a day of his own money; a missing half of a day of a man's memory and the day is not coming back and there is no form anywhere in this empire for a person to have a day back; a woman of about thirty-five with a day-book in a lodging house who came about nine miles twice and is findable and he is not, and he has had since the third week of the fourth month of the year after next to find a way round what she said and there is not one; and the second half of the reason for staying, which he still has not said and is not going to say in a doorway twice.

- **Tamsin Rook, twenty-four, a clerk at the fourth counter for two years, two boxes on a page ruled by hand in her own book with the same name in both.** **THE FOURTH DEBT THIS VOLUME OPENED ON A PERSON IS HERS AND IT IS THE VOLUME'S FIRST DEBT: the decision about whether she trusts his judgment is not made, is not made in a room, and is not on the page, and no batch of this volume moved it.** She said in the fourth movement that two pieces of evidence is not a decision and three is not either, and that she has said it in about nine other rooms in other words, and that she is not going to say it in this one because she has said it enough. **In the closing movement she said out loud that she has wanted to be asked every working day since about the seventh month of next year, that a person who is not asked has not been given a duty, and that she had known that since about the seventh month of next year and had never once known that it did not apply to her until about four minutes ago — which was the first time in two years that anybody had asked her one question at all.** She also said out loud that she has been carrying a fact about a person of seventeen since the fourth day of the fourth week of the seventh month of the year after next, that it is in nine mouths and in no book, and that she cannot do one single thing with it. She used the rule of the twelfth month of last year once, in writing with a date, and answered it in a room in words with a date — the second half no, the first half yes, on the day you have written — and refused to tell him which day, and said out loud in the closing movement that she has not been asked one question by anybody since the second day of the first week of the first month of the year after next. **The man who came up her stairs came in the last week of the ninth month of the year after, he is not one of the four, he said a price she quoted twice because he had not caught it the first time, he has not come back, and she says out loud that she does not know whether what he asked her counted and that she has got no book that says and that there is nobody she could ask without asking a person to look themselves up.** **Owed and unpaid:** the decision; a fourth box, if there is one, and she will not say; the undertaking of the fourth of the first month, live, unretired, unamended and unexercised; the fourth envelope, in a drawer with three others, unsent, unopened, and the noun is not used in the prose; the price of being a party, said out loud ten times and never once thanked, which she said in advance nobody would be, and which nobody has; and a fact about a girl of seventeen that she cannot do one single thing with.

- **Halla Wray, fifty-one, foreman of the Slade Cut for nine years, in a box since the first week of the seventh month of the year after, entered by a class, never asked.** **THE FIRST OF THE FOUR DEBTS THIS VOLUME OPENED ON A PERSON IS HERS.** She told about nine men, in a bay and not in a doorway, on the second day of the second week of the ninth month of the year after next, that she has been entered in a box by a class and has never been asked one thing about it — on purpose, and the reason she gives is that being the only person who can say out loud what an entry in a box does to a life is a door and she would like to be found. **About nine men know she is in a box and not one of them has said a word about it since. There will not be another season in which a foreman of fifty-one could ask one of the four who cannot read a paragraph a question, and nobody decided that and nothing was resolved.** She refused to say what the box was for until a man of about thirty-four asked her, in a bay, in about nine men, on the fourth day of the fourth week of the eleventh month of the year after next, **what the word *representative* means**, and she answered: the form was not asking her whether she would represent anybody, it was asking who she represents, and there is a column for the answer and there is no column for a refusal, and that is why a no she said out loud in a room with the day on it did not work. Not because a clerk did anything wrong. Because there was nowhere to put it. She said out loud that what the man had just done had not changed one word of the position of the four and was never going to. She said out loud in the same batch that she has three bad reasons for not asking the reader of seventeen and no good one, and is not going to be told a fourth by anybody in that room who is not one of the four, and that is an admission and not a reason. **Her three standing decisions all hold and none was re-argued or re-staged: that she is not going to make herself easier to ask, that she is going to stop being glad of a thing nobody can check, and that she does not know the right reason about the reader of seventeen.** She has read a bay with her ears since she was forty-two and there is not one form anywhere in this empire that pays a woman for knowing where a man is standing, and she has never once told one of the four and is not going to. She walked up four hundred yards to a book-keeper's door and said one true thing to a person and not to a bay, and the chapter flags the cost on the page. **Owed and unpaid:** about four people in a bay who cannot read a paragraph and have never been asked anything; the half of a second sentence she has owed since the third month of the year after, which is unasked for and unasked about and cannot be got back, and the seventh month's arrangement is unbroken; about four hundred yards of ditch and a scour that were on nobody's list and are now on one with no figure against them; and the box, which has not got her out and is not going to.

- **Rennick Adley, thirty-eight, book-keeper of the Slade Cut Company, who has kept the Company's books since the second month of next year.** **His wages are held against a roof he put there on purpose and the document he made on purpose is the thing standing between himself and a wage he is owed.** He is not on the list of works and has never been on it, because a list of works is a list of works and a book-keeper does not do works, and he built that column with no names in it in the second month of next year **because a name in a column is a thing a person can be served at**, and a list of forty-one names goes into a company book about four hundred and thirty miles down this river in nine hands over about sixty years, and the newest hand in that book is his. The Company's whole money is four shillings a year and all of it is the rent of a hired stone store, taken out four times a year at a shilling a quarter, entered by him in one line in the second month of next year and read back twice. **The days he spends on the numbers of other people are not paid and cannot be, and he found that out at about the fourth hour of the sixth month of the year after next, and nobody ever told him, and a foreman of fifty-one on his own bank has known since the sixth year of a guild's blank list.** He read a man's own paper to him in a yard twice with a mark in chalk on a wall at the height of the third link of a chain, on a wall nobody looks at, and **the chain is on the man and the cross means nothing and is nobody's, and the reason it is there is that in about four years a person with a copy of the right page could put the cross and the wall and the yard together and ask a question about them and he would be the only person who could answer it.** He was offered the chance to enter a decision on his own account and refused to write it down. He read about nineteen reasons out of a column in a bay and did not stop a man standing up with his own. He has worked out, after about four days, that the reason he could not account for the entry about the reader of seventeen is structural: a reason under an entry about a person needs a day, a day about a person needs a name, and there is no name in that column. **He is one of about four people in this matter who cannot be asked anything and he is not one of the four who cannot read a paragraph, and the two are not the same and a chapter that merges them breaks the matter.** **Owed and unpaid:** his own wage; the reason for the entry with no reason under it, which he has now and has not written down; the half of a sentence a foreman owes him, which he has said he wants and is not going to ask for; and the about nine hundred and forty names in a company book that has four hundred dead people in it, which is why he is not going to put two words in it.

- **Nell Kest, twenty, a dye-house worker, sixteen books, a furnace town about four hundred and thirty miles up this river.** **She has decided to go and tell four women that she is not paid, and she has told one, and nobody has thanked her and there is not going to be a form for it.** The decision falls in the fourth week of the ninth month of the year after next. She refused money from a woman of about thirty who has some — **the first time in about three years — and gave the reason out loud, which is that if she takes it then she is a trade and a trade can be told what to do, and the woman cannot be told what to do, and that is the only thing she has got.** The woman said *then you will not have it when I am gone*, and she answered that a person who keeps another person's numbers for nothing is keeping them for the four years and not for the person, and that she chose the four years on purpose out of sixteen books and not out of one. Fifteen of the sixteen are other people's, none has an office on it, one has her own name at the head of it, and **if somebody came up those stairs with a sheet and a seal she could not show a soul the fourth of them.** Her wage has been held since the second week of the third month of the year after by a man who cannot say what it is held against, and the money for nineteen days is in a weigh-house ledger with her wage in it and not her name under it. A foreman off a dye end has come up two stairs four times, said the same three things each time, and held a wage-day notice in a coat without sending it; she has told him what to do instead — write it, keep it, do not send it — and has said the reason is one she made up in about four seconds and would not be thanked for. She is not going up the river this season and has not said she is never going to, and she is the only person who knows there is a difference between the two. **Owed and unpaid:** three of the four women untold and no fund for the telling; the sixteenth book; a wage held since the second week of the third month of the year after; a room where nobody is to be told; and a letter of hers to a man of about thirty-eight that he has not answered and is not going to be able to.

- **The man of about thirty-four with a trade, a house with a door on it and a wife, and a bedroom at the back of it, who asked the fifth question.** **THE SECOND OF THE FOUR DEBTS THIS VOLUME OPENED ON A PERSON IS HIS.** He is not a clerk and not a foreman and not a man of thirty-eight and nobody sent him, and he is the sixth person in the manuscript to ask a question on purpose and the second to do it for no reason a foreman gave him. - **The clerk of about fifty-five in the office of a seat, who is on the page by name in four chapters of this volume as Orla Denning and is not named in the chapter where she entered the question.** **THE THIRD OF THE FOUR DEBTS THIS VOLUME OPENED ON A PERSON IS HERS.** Nineteen years of eleven minute books on a shelf in a room with a window about two feet wide and a pane out of it, about four thousand entries in those books and no name in any one of them, and that is not carelessness, it is the only shape a minute book has got. - **Marn Ottery, thirty-four, eleven years at the fourth counter, about four hundred bought and unfilled blanks in a box under it.** **She asked a colleague one question out loud for the first time in eleven years, and it was not a question about a word on a form, and she said the shape of it out loud before she asked it** — that a board is not a form and a line of writing on a board is not a word on a form — and that she had thought about it for about four days and was going to ask it anyway and wanted it noticed that she thought about it. She told a woman of about forty who came up the stairs for a copy and asked about a name and not about the paper, **that a name in a column is a thing a person can be served at**, which she has said at that counter about four hundred times to rooms and once to a person, and she put the coin back on the counter rather than taking it, which is not a thing a clerk does. - **The foreman off a dye end, about forty, a furnace town about four hundred and thirty miles up this river, one of about four people in this matter who cannot be asked, holding a wage-day notice in a coat.** **He is the only person in that city who can pick up or put down the whole of a woman's money and he has said out loud that he cannot say what a wage is held against, and he has said it four times to men who were working and once to a boy of about nine with a slate, and the boy is the fifth.** He worked it out in a rented room in a city four hundred and thirty miles down this river that he is one of about four people in this matter who cannot be asked, and he was told it by somebody and has said so. **On the second day of the first week of the eleventh month of the year after next he said the volume's own question out loud in his own mouth, four times over about half an hour to about nine men in a dye end: if that office was meant to be temporary, then somebody changed it into an inheritable one, and I would like to know who, and I have asked it in a shed in a different city in the fifth month of the year after next and nobody answered, and I am asking it again in my own mouth and nobody is going to answer it here either.** He did it on purpose and said the cost out loud, and a man of about fifty-five with a chain's reason came back at him: **a man who cannot read a paragraph is the only person in this empire you can say anything to and have it not come back.** A man of about forty says there was nobody to change; He paid fourpence for a copy in each of two other rooms and is in neither. He is owed four things and has collected none: the bill for being findable, which is not his and stands on somebody else's account and whose owner he has not looked up; the thing nobody has thanked him for; a form for asking a person whether they will mind being told a thing before they are told it, which he has looked for about two years; and a woman at the foot of about four steps about four miles off who is not thanked either and has said out loud that she is not going to ask him about his week. **He has had a question ready for about two years, it takes about four seconds to say, he is not going to say it, he is not going to be the eighth, and the next asking is not his to make.** In Chapter 0359 the same man is at the bottom of four steps of his own house with a fire gone out at about the fourth hour: six weeks before he did the last thing of that kind he told her about it without asking her first and gave the reason that if he had asked she would have said no or said yes and been frightened, and this time he is not telling her at all, and the not telling is the same absence as the not asking. **No romance begins, none is implied, and nothing in either chapter decides whether anybody trusts anybody's judgment. The bill for being findable is named twice in his own mouth and the figure nineteen pounds three and fourpence is named zero times in the batch.**

- **Nell Kest, twenty, in a counting room at the back of a dye end about four hundred and thirty miles up this river (Chapter 0352).** Sixteen books, fifteen of them other people's and none with an office on the cover, the sixteenth with her own name at the head of it and no question ever put against it, and a room about eleven feet by fourteen with a bench, a window with a broken light in it, about forty books on shelves built for taller books and a stove behind the bench that whoever gets in first lights and lets go out. **The second week of the third month of the year after is the week her wage stopped arriving, and the man sitting on it cannot say what it is sitting against, and she is not going to ask him, and the decision is hers and was made before this week and did not move this week.** She has told one woman of four that she is not paid and has said the whole of a rule to a second of the four in about four minutes; **the two she cannot reach are the same two she could not reach before she said anything.** A foreman off a dye end has a notice in a coat that says a sum and not what it is against and he has named no day for sending it. The eleven houses off the dyers' lane came in on the first of the two days and eleven numbers went out on the second and the numbers are the same eleven numbers. **Nobody came up those stairs for the sixteenth book this week and nobody has ever sent for it, and she is not going up this river this season, and that is not the same as never and nobody has asked her either thing.** The new fact of the chapter is ordinary and is not a turn: an ordinary day of numbers in and numbers out is what a practice looks like from inside it.

- **Marn Ottery, thirty-four, at the fourth counter of a Table in Auremar (Chapter 0353).** Eleven years at that counter and not a lawyer, at the top of a stair with no other way up, open the second hour to the sixth, about nine people a day coming up in a week when the weather is against them, a fee board of four items and not one of them time. **She is the reason she is still standing at that counter and has never said so out loud to anybody and nobody has thanked her for it.** She told the border law out loud this week, the shape before the thing: a column with a heading over it is a machine and a machine can be asked things all day; a column with no heading over it is a person, and a person is only ever asked one way, out loud in a room by somebody who wants something. A thing put to a border household at no charge has to be answered and the answer entered as a form and not a letter, and it got an answer in about three weeks where a House's own table took about eleven and then said no and cost a hundred and forty years, **and the reason is that the law does not need a person, because a border household is not a person either.** The box of about four hundred bought and unfilled blanks has been under that counter eleven years, no form was ever drawn to account for it, nobody has ever asked her what it is for, and the box is not a post and is not an offer. **The fourth item on the fee board is the one three clerks cannot account for, she wrote it there in her own hand about four years ago and was not in the room when she wrote it or does not think she was, she has said that out loud twice and nobody thanked her either time, and nobody at that counter has put one question to another about it out loud in about four years.** She asked nothing this week and was asked nothing.

- **Tamsin Rook, twenty-four, at the other end of that counter (Chapter 0354), and the fact that she is asked nothing in this batch is entered as a choice and not as a fault.** She has been at that counter about two years. **Nobody has asked her anything in about two years and she has wanted to be asked every working day since about the seventh month of next year**, and the inherited phrase stands once in its own words: *a person who is not asked has not been given a duty*. One man came up that stair once, in the second week of the tenth month, put one question into the air, did not wait for the answer and got none; that is the last time anybody has put a question to her, and she has not decided since whether she wishes she had answered it. **Her undertaking of the fourth of the first month has not been retired, has not been amended and has never been exercised, about two years, and the reason she gives this time is not a variation of the three she gave before: nobody asked her, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for asking a person whether they will mind being told a thing before they are told it, and she has looked in about nine places for one and found a column with a heading over it in every one of them, and a machine is the thing that never asks anybody anything.** The line in her own book about the woman at the other end of the counter is a window and not a wall, and it is a wall the moment she is asked whether she minds its being there. **The four things in the drawer are unopened, the box of blanks is not hers, and the decision about whether she trusts a man's judgment is unmade, was not made in Volume 07, is not made here, and is not going to be made in a dead hour at the end of a counter.**

- **Marek Kest, thirty-eight, in a rented room two streets off a street that goes down to the river road (Chapter 0355), and what this chapter establishes is two refusals and two non-uses and all four are paid for.** He does not attempt the reading, and the reason is his own and he gave it out loud in the room, to himself and to about four feet of wall: it has been done to him twice, each time it took a whole day out of his mother's own history, he was not in the room for that day and was watching her at a table in a house in a lane in this town, he has the address of it and the length of it, and he is thirty-eight and cannot tell whether he came out of that house or stood in the doorway of it and waited. **The third one is not a thing he is going to spend on a man at a rail.** He does not do the other thing either, the one he is able to do, which would take about four minutes and a clean sheet and has been done once and worked; **a correct non-use is entered as one, and the reason is nine words long and it is on a page in another year and this volume is not going to say it a second time.** He is not at a stage and is not going to arrive at one, and nothing in this empire would put a man in a rented room and examine him about one. **The mark is about three inches long on the inside of his left wrist and it reads nothing and it decides nothing and it does not know where the ford is; the wage is the price and the mark is what he has instead of a receipt, and there is no form anywhere that gives a man a receipt for what he has paid.** A bar of his own came off him on the twelfth of the ninth month of last year on an application of one line which he wrote himself. He holds no paper, no office, no fund and no list, and he chose a labour a man is free to refuse out of his own hand and it is not being re-decided. **A woman about four miles off is not told that he said the reason out loud to about nine men in a bay, and nobody is going to tell her.**

- **Halla Wray, fifty-one, foreman, in a box nobody has asked her about (Chapter 0356).** The bay at the top of a lane off the Slade is about nine foot by eleven with a bench longer than the bay is wide and a lamp that is not lit until about the seventh hour. The job came to her because nine families agreed between themselves in a shed behind the second bay, about nine years ago, in about an hour and a half. **She has been inside that box since the first week of the seventh month of the year after, entered by a class, and nobody has asked her about it and nobody is going to, and being in a box and not being asked about it are the same arrangement and the arrangement was made in that shed by nine families and not by her, and she has had about two years to find a way of saying it as though it were two things and there is not going to be one.** The entries were about a class; the class is a heading; a heading is how a person turns into a company; a company is a thing this empire can hold wages against and hand letters to and take to law. She said the rest of it once, in a room with about nine people in it, in the closed part of this: the form did not ask her whether she would represent anybody, it asked who she represents, there is a column for the answer and no column for a refusal, and a person who writes nothing in it has not refused and has not agreed and is still in the class. **She is not going to say it again and nobody thanked her for saying it once.** At the back of that bay the two of seventeen are unspoken to, unthanked, unsent for and not going to start, and there is no answer in that bay about why and not one of the men in it has one, and no paper anywhere asks a person of seventeen whether she would rather not, and the girl has never been given the chance to say no. **About four men in that bay cannot make sense of a paragraph and not one question about a number has ever been put to any of them.** The man of about fifty-five with the chain is not one of the four and says so out loud about once every few months to nobody in particular, and he has known for about four years that findability does not work on him, has never used it, and has never told anybody. **The man who keeps the books of that Company is four hundred yards up the same lane and has wanted the second half of a sentence of hers since the third month of the year after, and he has not asked and is not going to, and the day he asks is the day she stops saying the half she has got.** The cross in the chalk is at the height of the third link of that chain, it means nothing, it is nobody's, it is about four years off being anybody's evidence, and nothing in that lane is going to turn either one of them into anything.

- **The man of about sixty-one, the address, who signs at the foot of every sheet (Chapter 0357).** The second hand since he was forty-two, in a room on the ground floor of a building off the old river road with a shelf of eleven books, a table, a chair, and a window about two feet wide that has been without a pane for about four years. **He cannot read a paragraph: he can read a figure, a name and most of the short words that hold a sheet together, and everything else is shape, and he has been working out which is which for nineteen years.** He reads what he can and signs the rest, about nine hundred times a year, and he signs for people who cannot write and for people who are not in the room. **About nine of those sheets a year are not what the person above him wrote, in about nineteen years, and he signed every one of them because he was not asked and was given nothing to sign against, and he is the only person alive who knows which ones they were and he cannot write one of them down and has never told anybody.** There is no form for believing a thing you cannot read and there is nowhere in this empire for a man to put his mark beside a sum and call it a disagreement, and it is not an oversight in the making of the page. **What he wants is about four minutes: no to about half of the ones of a year he can remember and yes to the rest, and the yeses matter as much, and there is nobody to ask.** On every one of those sheets there are three things that can be asked and two of them are a clerk and an office and neither of those is a person anybody can put a question to, and that leaves him, and he is the one nobody has asked anything in about nineteen years. **He is not in a bay and he is not one of the four men who cannot make sense of a paragraph; he is nine miles off in a different building, and a list with him in it alongside those four would be a list of five, and there is no such list and there is not going to be one.** He has a figure in his head about how many times he has come to that room in nineteen years and he has never written it down and is not going to, and the distance is about nine miles and he knows it to about a yard. Nobody has ever thanked him, he has never asked to be, and **he knows both halves of that and has never put them in one sentence.** A carrier is paid ninepence at the lock, is on a list, and in about six years nobody has asked her what she carries, and he has not asked her either.

- **The woman of about twenty-six at the end of the cold passage (Chapter 0358), and she is not in this matter and never has been.** She has been in that building about four years, is not one of the three clerks, and stands at a lock at one end of about four hundred yards of cold flags with a room and a rail about four feet off the floor at the far end that is not on the passage's work. **She keeps the count in her head and has never once put a hand on a rack, and her figure has gone up since the seventh month of the year after and she has not said by how much and is not going to; she worked that out in about four minutes standing at a lock, one time, and the figure is the only one this empire keeps on how many people are sitting inside a thing nobody can be asked about, and hers is a count of persons under nothing.** A number in a book is a column and a column wants a heading and a heading over that figure would be a heading over about nine hundred rooms at once, and a person cannot be under a heading like that and stay a person, and she is not going to be the one who puts it there. **Nothing that comes down those flags goes into that room and nothing that comes out of it comes up them, there is no list that reaches it and no rack that stops at it and no person whose work takes her to that door, and that is the whole of the reason a thing said out loud in that room stays in that room, and it is not because anybody is careful.** She has three questions for the man of about sixty-one and has asked none of them, and the fact that he came at all gave her no fourth. She is the only other person in this matter who knows what was said in that room, she was not in the room and has never been in it, and there is no form anywhere that would let anybody put to her the question of what she heard.

- **The clerk of about fifty-five (Chapter 0360), nineteen years in one room, who writes the minutes, advises nobody and asks nothing.** The room is the one off the old river road with the shelf of eleven books under the window with the pane out of it, about four thousand entries and not one name in any of them. **The arrangement that has kept about nine hundred sheets a year out of every book in this empire for nineteen years is the arrangement that has kept anybody from asking the man at the foot of them one question, and those are one fact and not two; she got to that in about four minutes spread over about six years, with a pen on the table and nobody to say it to, and it is not a discovery anybody could act on.** A question put in that building is a question put into a record and the record is what the sheet goes in, and the last time a thing of that shape happened to about four hundred people their sheet went back to them and nobody was punished. **A minute with an answer in it is an order, an instruction has to be carried out by somebody, the somebody would have a name on it and a name on it is a party, so she has not entered one in nineteen years, and not entering one is the price of the only thing anybody has ever asked her to do, which is to be right.** Her own finding of about six years of her own motion is on the shelf under the window with the day and the hour on it and her own name at the foot, and it is the only thing on that shelf with a name at the foot, and it is still a question, has not been turned into a sentence and has no full stop on it. **She is the only person in this matter anybody could serve a question on in writing and she is paid nothing for standing ready to have it served.** A quarter of it was answered out loud in a room at the far end of her own passage in the second month by a man who walked nine miles to get there on his own two feet, and she entered none of that either, and nobody is going to ask her about it a second time, and there is no form anywhere in this empire for a person to ask themselves whether they would still have written it. **She is not told anything about what was said in that room and she wrote none of it down and nobody is going to ask her why she wrote none of it, and there is nothing in this empire that would let a clerk of an office be told that a person is coming or that a person is not, and that is the price of the standing ready and nobody laid it out and she has never had the chance to find out whether she would do it again.**

- **The people who are not spoken to and not thanked, and none of them moved.** The girl of seventeen and the reader of seventeen at the back of the bay; **the reader is paid forty-five pence a day and four days a week and a day of that is anywhere between four pence and a shilling according to what gets set on the bench on the day.** About four men in that bay who cannot make sense of a paragraph. The man of about fifty-five with a chain, who is not one of them and never is, and who used the one thing he has had for about four years once, at a shut gate, and told nobody. A woman of about forty with a scuttle, who is not in this batch. A woman of about thirty-five next door, who came out on her own step twice and said nothing and was spoken to by nobody. A foreman off a dye end with a notice in a coat that is not sent. A carrier paid ninepence at the lock who is on a list. A man of about forty who came up four steps for a copy and does not know the name of the man who copied it. About nineteen people behind a bank, and about sixty children inside a guarantee that is not printed in its own words.

- **What the second batch is handed.** The shape and not the instrument: a practice in two people about four hundred and thirty miles apart with no list, no form, no heading and no fund; about nine hundred entries a year still coming in under a heading with a second hand at the foot of every one of them; about nine of the nine hundred a year not as the person above him wrote; and nobody having worked out how a thing is asked in about nine hundred buildings at once by people who cannot be instructed to ask it, which is the answer the last volume gave in the negative, in a room, in about nine minutes, and it took two years and it is worth a room. **The next asking is nobody's yet. The second half of this matter's question is not asked and is not restated in any form a writer could put in a mouth. Nobody in the next ten chapters may ask anybody anything out loud, and nothing may be thanked, forgiven, sent for or resolved, and the two people in their boxes stay in them.**
