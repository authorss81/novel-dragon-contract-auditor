## Tamsin Rook

She told him about the drawer on the twenty-fourth of the fifth and then, three months late, told him what was in it. She built a referral, put his name **second** in a caption instead of first, and watched the registrar do in a minute what he had failed to do in eight months.

What she got back is a name she will never know the source of: a woman in Greyfen with four children and no candle who did not answer a letter from a Chancery in eight months and answered a shop window, and paid four pence for a sheet, and told nobody. **The sixth column, *why not*, is still empty because she will not invent it.**

Then, of the Cinder Court's record, **twenty-nine say yes to keeping a copy and twelve say no**, and the twelve are the finding. - Unresolved want: **the reply from the district of Glasswater**, which the handover list says in terms is *hers* and not his. **The fourth envelope**, addressed to the standing assessor of a furnace town, written in the fourth week of the first month, still in the drawer, and it is the one that is going to matter in nine years. And a copy of a decision being a weapon and a target, which she worked out before anybody told her.

## Nell Kest

- Position: **dye-house worker, twenty, and the person who turns up.** She put four questions on a board, and the board is now public property. **In Batch 0001 she found out that the fifth question has an answer in her own lane and the answer is not a door: it is four pounds ten each way, or eleven if you go on your own, two days lost at four shillings a day, a bench bond of eleven pounds, and four years before a return without a second bond.** What a house does when the assessor says no is that it moves, and a house of four people on a lane does not have four pounds. She wrote a fifth question of her own — *and what does it cost, in pounds, to leave, and who has paid it, and what did you have on the day you went that you did not have on the day before* — and posted it to four trades on the twenty-sixth of the eleventh, one of which she has never seen, with the price in the body of the letter and nobody's name on it. **A fourth trade answered before it was asked and put a trade's name at the top of two lines: their assessor is a bench, and if the holder stops the bench is the holder of the paper.**
- **What she gained in the batch:** she read the guild's three-and-a-quarter-page finding out in a lane in front of about a hundred and forty people and then read Roke's other half, and she **put the four houses' nine children in the room**, because the number is a wage and a number nobody has heard of is a number that gets made true by whoever is standing in the room when it is read out. At the eleventh door, **Ottoline Bray, fifty-two**, has a mill bench paper about a son of seventeen with the boy's own name on it, spelled right, in the boy's own hand, that was never read to her — and **the boy read it to her himself**, and said there is nothing in it about after. **It is the only paper in eleven months the four questions have made better rather than frightened, and Nell said so out loud, and then said the other half about her own cloth of eleven shillings and her own four unsent envelopes.**
- **What it cost and what is new:** the levy is gone for the last time in writing, the four houses have paid a year, and **the next four are the ones who will be out**; the professional copyist on the Greyfen road has nine names on a waiting list and four of them are trades; a woman from a fifth trade came sixty miles to ask four questions at a counter, was not in, waited two days and left them on the step with her own name on it, and it is in a drawer. **And on the twenty-eighth of the eleventh Nell Kest wrote to her brother for the first time since the twenty-ninth of the first month of last year — four lines, off the vats, with the lamp off, no line of it put into her book first — and it says she has not forgiven the ninth month and is not going to, and will say it to his face in about four hours, and it mentions the ninth month only to say that it is not mentioned.**
- What she gained: **twenty-three doors walked in four hours a day.** The fourth question asked of a woman who goes round with the quarter's sheet finds a different answer than it found at Millbrook Reach, because here the person who read the paper **is** paid for the reading and says so first, before being asked the second half. She put the fourth question in the open at a trestle in front of a hundred and four people and **sixty said yes they are paid to read and not paid for the answer, ten said I do not know, and four said nobody read it to me**, and thirty in the back half were not asked and she wrote that number on the board herself in chalk with her name under it. Then she asked the assessor of eleven houses a question nobody had asked — whether a girl of nineteen on a bench since Michaelmas is a person in the file — and the answer was that the book has eleven houses and no column for a name and never has, and the assessor **refused to put one in** and asked for that to be written down. **Nell's own line about it, written in her book that night: *a person can say no to me in a room of eleven and be thanked for it. That is a thing I have not got questions for.***
- What she lost: **the levy is lost on paper**, three and a quarter pages against the four houses that signed, and Roke's other half was said out loud in a lane in front of ninety people and written down. She published a number about four neighbours. She spent two days of work and six months of saving on a coach fare. She has no trade standing, no bench and no money, and the practice that came out of her trip is **in four buildings in three districts and does not belong to her.**
- What she did: **four hundred and forty miles, made to go through the assessor because going round it would prove nothing**, nine days in a furnace town advising nobody, two hundred and some papers read out belonging to about forty houses, **eleven in the model form, nine with a house in the box and two with a person in it.** She read the box last and did not say whether it was right. She asked Bettin Rahl the fifth question nobody has asked — *what a house does when the assessor says no* — and he could not answer it and wrote it down under his own rule at the second leaf of his trade's book with his own name beside it. She did not write to Marek. **Her brother wrote to her on the sixteenth of the eighth, in four lines, about the levy, and she has not answered and is entitled to.**
- Unresolved want: the fourth question, which has never once been asked of a dragon. The levy's fight, which she has said in advance will be lost. And the fact that the thing that travels is not a form and not a ruling and not a question, and is a person who turns up.

- What she did in the closing batch: **she ruled off a line on her own board for the Cinder Court's record on the fourth of the sixth, thirteen days before the court sat, before anybody had thought of asking her to**, and the record arrived on the twenty-ninth and went into it. A copy is a shilling. She wrote the head of it in her own hand and by her own instruction, not because anybody told her to, and told a wall to read the first order, the second and the fourth, and that the third is about a man's certificates and you will not understand it. She then wrote **seven numbered paragraphs of what her district has to do about it, which is almost nothing, and said so on the wall**, including that the first order is now true in forty-five other places and this court is glad of it and claims none of it, and that the sentence is **not withdrawn here, because this court is the only body that can withdraw its own practice**, and that the district has not decided whether to run a process next season and she has not asked the magistrate and will not be asked. A man in a good coat read all three things on that wall standing up, in about eleven minutes, before he reached the sill, and bought two things.
- The numbers on the wall, unchanged and still true: **thirty-four hearings**, a hundred and two hours of a six-hour window, nine shillings and fourpence of the eleven. **Four** people said *I do not understand this* and are entered as having said it, which is a complete answer and not a refusal. **Eleven** of the other thirty signed something else inside a month, and both facts sit in the same box.
- What she wrote in the first person about herself, which she has never done in nine years: *I would rather be frightening and indispensable at a window with a nail in it, in a district of four hundred and sixty households where a man in a good coat has to stand and read everything before he reaches my sill. That is the whole of what I have got and it took nine years and a trial that cost nine shillings and fourpence and four people saying the words I do not understand this in a register, and I have nobody to hand it to, and there is a sentence on this wall about it, and the sentence is the only inheritance I have ever been left.*
- What she is carrying: **eleven shillings in a cloth she did not spend a penny of**, and a copy of a register line about Rennie Sallow's own house that has been in a drawer in her room since the fourth of the tenth. She has decided to walk up the upper lane on the fourteenth of the seventh, on his way home from the works, and say four words — *from the window, no charge* — and go, and if he asks her in, she has a shilling to take back to a man with a tray.
- Unresolved want: she has told a wall that if you come to this window with a paper with a duty on it she will read it to you and will not tell you what to do about it, and will enter that you came, **because the register above her head is a register of duties and does not do that and never will.** Nobody's job is to open the window.
