# Character State

Standing of each principal character at the close of Volume 01, Batch 0004 (Chapters 31–40). This file answers "where is everyone now," not "who are they"; identity, voice, and motivation live in `bible/characters.md`. Keep it compact enough to read whole.

## Marek Kest

- Stage: still 0, Clerk's Eye, plus the unstable first Seam-Sight earned on the page in Chapter 8. **He performs exactly one reading in this batch, in Chapter 40**, under the limited Venn audit of the twelfth of the ninth month, which has never been discharged because closing it would have required somebody to write a conclusion. It reports that the record now carries nothing because the term expired on the nineteenth of the second, **it decides nothing**, and it costs him ash and iron, a headache behind the left eye, a nosebleed on a gate rail in the rain, and two days of not being able to look at print. He does not go to Doctor Perrin Ashe and gives the reason: there is nothing a penny buys and there is no medicine for this.
- Position: junior clerk of the Inheritance Desk, and the man whose name is now under **two recorded refusals and one reply** in this matter — the certification of the twentieth of the eleventh, the promotion of the twenty-ninth of the third, and Pell Vey's signature on all three. He is also the only person in the batch with nothing to hand anybody.
- What he gained: a **limited sitting** that produced Sivra Oris's sworn statement; a scoping note that says in writing that his best available outcome is one sentence in a form that cannot be reissued; the knowledge that a letter could have been found in the corridor; the knowledge that the fact nobody wanted him to know is that he was relieved; and a closing record, four lines long, filed.
- What he lost: the **promotion** — a standing examiner of provincial filings, a room, a drawer, and the standing to which forty-six districts would answer — refused in a room with a witness on the twenty-ninth of the third, with its terms entered and his signature on the record. The letter to Mara Venn is written and read out and it says the file cannot be made complete. Two days of print. And the standing, which is a permanent loss: he is a junior clerk with no more authority than in the ninth month.
- Standing: Pell Vey has his name on two recorded refusals and knows there will be a third conversation about the yard. Tamsin Rook has told him four times about the piece of paper she put in his coat and has not once collected it, and has told him she knows exactly what he did in Chapter 36 and will not reward it by pretending it was different. **He told her four hours after deciding, having already got it witnessed, and it is the third and last time he does that.**
- Unresolved want: to find out what he actually wants, which he has written down he will do in the fifth month and not tonight and not out loud; the ninety words in Nevin Trost's hands, which he will not move because the rule he made to protect him now protects Pell; and the Caudle notice with a man's name in a box, which he is not the right person for and has not touched.

## Tamsin Rook

- Position: junior roll-keeper, and the person who has **stopped waiting to be the person who is asked**. On the ninth of the fourth month she filed four lines over her own signature as a roll-keeper of the third gallery, in her own name, naming the district of Glasswater rather than the standing office, and the filing is unanswered. It is in no way protected and she is entirely exposed.
- What she gained: the **Emberwatch answer** — Bettin Rahl's name and address, the four who did not answer being one house, and the trade's own rule; the truth about why the answer is unanswerable (the man who taught her the sentence is not the man who sent it); her own **objection back in her own book**, on a page about a cooper's gauge, with her reason written under it for not moving it in the fourth month; and a second trip up the water on her own money, out of two weeks' wages, for no reason except that Iselle Oster asked her to be in the room.
- What she lost: the second of the two documents stays out of every reply, and the cost of that is now hers personally and not the file's. She told Marek one thing four times and not another. She has a rule written down with a date on it that he must honour before he writes anything else about a dragon seat's paperwork, and she has said she will check it in a year.
- The copy list: forty-one names, thirty-eight addresses, **thirty-two answers and nine who have not answered** after a man in Greyfen came back on the fourth of the third. Four of the nine are the Emberwatch house, and the fourth column now carries **the surname Ferren and a landmark and no first names**, which is all Bettin Rahl's rider of the twenty-fourth of the second could give her; **Osa Ferren is a person who has written to a lane, not a name on the list**, and the two must not be merged. Four letters addressed to a lane with no numbers are in a drawer in her alcove and have not been sent, and she told Marek about the letter and not about the drawer.
- Unresolved want: the reply from the district of Glasswater, which will name a clerk and not a seal; the four unsent envelopes; and a woman in a furnace town who asked what a person does who is owed an answer.

## Nell Kest

- Position: dye-house worker, the person who put the four questions on a board and who has now been asked by a **trade** to publish the arithmetic about her own lane in chalk. She did it on the seventh of the third month, at a table, with nine people, and wrote underneath which house asked to be named first and which three did not, and apologised in chalk.
- What she gained: Nery Caudle's permission and Roke's sentence about a farthing, which is that a farthing in this trade is not money but a thing a house says about itself; the finding that nine pounds a quarter is going into a Tool Fund holding four pounds eleven and fourpence that has never been empty; the assessor's own sentence in a room of eleven houses — *paid to read, not paid for the answer* — and the eleven women who heard it; and a second paper out from under the cloth at the Caudle house, which is a notice with **Mike Caudle's name in a box** and a thumb over the line that says he owes nothing.
- What she lost: the second paper is out, which means Mike Caudle's bench is named in a lane and cannot be un-named. She published a number about four neighbours. Marl Crenn has told her she will lose the levy argument within a year. She has twenty-three houses to walk and no coach and four hours a day. And Wenna Sallow's name is in chalk on the board and she has not gone to the gate, and has written down the decision and the reason.
- What she did not do: she did not write to Marek, again, and the letter from Emberwatch went to a trestle table and not to her brother, and she is going up the road to find out whether there are four hours in the day.
- Unresolved want: the four days after the sixth hour, twenty-three doors, and the assessment on the twenty-third; and a second association in the next province that has just cost eleven houses their certification because of the fifth line on the back of the form.

## Pell Vey

- Position: head of the Notaries' Table, still holding the cabinet key, and now **the most exposed man in this matter by his own signature**: a reply of the thirteenth of the twelfth, a recorded refusal of a certification, and a recorded refusal of a promotion.
- What he offered and what it was: a **standing examiner of provincial filings**, which is not a title but a letter — a district registrar's answer is given to it and taken by it, and every instruction issues to forty-six places on the ordinary distribution. In exchange, the Venn correction is a routine filing defect and no district's answer is framed by reference to a series. He framed it as ordinary and he was not making a threat, and he told Marek in advance that he was going to say the condition out loud because saying it out loud is cheaper than the other way.
- What he gave, unpaid: the notice trap. If the Standing Office is entered for notice on a sitting that cannot touch its series, a clerk opening the minute book in six months puts a note on the file saying the matter concerns a series, and in two years somebody audits the file about the series, and it is not Marek's audit. He warned Marek, and Marek took the office off the list.
- What he is owed and has not been given: the **ninety-word corridor conversation** of the ninth of the eleventh month, which he has asked for twice. He has known since the sixth of the twelfth that Tamsin has a dated record of the yard of the fourth of the tenth and has let her keep it, and he calls it the single worst supervisory act of his career and says he would do it again.
- Unresolved want: to know what his own office said, and to keep his own hands out of a file he has now signed three times, which is no longer available to him.

## Ivet Sarn

- Position: clerk of the Mosswake Roll Court, and **the person who runs the thing**. The trial's accounting is on a public wall between the two registers, in her own hand and in the first person, with the day on it, and it states that the six-week trial was run with the certification window shut on purpose, that she asked the roll-keeper the question so the answer would be in somebody else's hand, and that no household in the district waited for a seed parcel because of it.
- The numbers on the wall: thirty-four hearings, a hundred and two hours of a six-hour window, nine shillings and fourpence of the eleven. **Four** people said *I do not understand this* and are entered as having said it, which is a complete answer and not a refusal. **Eleven** of the other thirty signed something else inside a month, and both facts sit in the same box. Twenty-two nights the room was lit past the seventh hour, and the door was shut on all twenty-two.
- What she did that nobody has done: she asked **Wend the lamp-trimmer** at last, on the twenty-seventh of the third, at the ninth hour, and wrote the question down with a line number **before** the answer existed, because he told her that a clerk who asks a question and does not write the answer down is checking whether he will give one twice.
- What she ruled: the review on the fourth of the fourth is **not a hearing of any application**, and anybody who wants to say something about the process itself must say it in the room, out loud, on the fourth. A factor's man asked for a hearing to be added and bought two copies of the wall at a shilling each and learned nothing, which is the only income the trial ever had.
- What she sent: a **witness order** through Bettin Rahl to a house in Emberwatch, out of her own drawer, a shilling and fourpence, because the district works fund buys the running of the window and the paper and nothing else and she said so herself in November. It came back **signed** on the second of the fourth, the first witness order that has ever left that district and come back signed, with Rahl's line that he did not ask them to be brave. **Leda Rell's name is at the foot of the third paragraph.**
- Unresolved want: a docket that finishes. It finished on the sixth of the fourth, and she has not decided what she does on the seventh.
