# Character State

Standing of each principal character **at the close of Volume 01** (Chapters 41–50). This file answers "where is everyone now," not "who are they"; identity, voice, and motivation live in `bible/characters.md`. Keep it compact enough to read whole.

## Marek Kest

- Stage: **still 0, Clerk's Eye**, plus the unstable first Seam-Sight earned on the page in Chapter 8. **He performs no magic anywhere in Chapters 41–50.** The one reading of the volume was taken in Chapter 40, has had its cost paid in full, and does not recur. The inability to lie lapsed on the page in Chapter 18 and does not return. **No valid exception, counter-seal, mixed warrant, true-name reading, Roll Assembly or Stage 2 ability exists anywhere in Volume 1, and the word "exception" appears nowhere in Chapters 41–50.**
- Position: **a clerk who has been transferred, with a date, off the Inheritance Desk and into the Received Room of the Roll Hall** — the room with a barred door, a stove and eleven feet of shelf, which is where things go when nobody wants to be the one who has them. His successor is **Halvard Riet**, nine years at the Desk, chosen by the Registrar in about four minutes on the twelfth of the eighth because he asked for the Venn box and not the Kest one and was given the Kest one as well, and because Marek had no standing to object and did not try. **Marek ends the volume with no standing, no promotion, and no decision of his own name at the top of anything.**
- What he gained: **a Cinder Court record with four orders in it** — no guarantor duty without a hearing and a signature, the printed sentence recorded as model wording and not a decision of any court, Corvin Dray's sixty-one certificates suspended, and the sealed instruction escalated to a full sitting with jurisdiction. **He won the case he opened and lost control of the larger one in the same afternoon.** He also got a thing he had not expected: a registrar who refused three of his questions in a form with a name at the foot of it, because he had read four months of the file and understood that this was the only shape of refusal anybody in the matter had ever got a use out of.
- What he lost: **the escalation he spent eight months building toward.** He objected in the room — not to the escalation, but to its being placed on a list for a year with a date and no name, which is the exact thing he wrote a rule about in the fourth month — and Sela Vint entered the objection and decided against him and told him why: *a thing that is not on a list is in a drawer.* **He argued the case for four hours when there was no case and Tamsin Rook said so on a stair.** He handed over a file he cannot close, with a six-item list in his own hand and no heading on it, item six being the second sealed page and the line *I will not read it.* He agreed, in a room, with a clerk as his only witness, that **from the twelfth of the eighth every stranger who wants to know about his mother is a person he did not decide to tell.**
- Standing: **a name at the bottom of four hundred and forty lines of public record and not one line of it that says he was right.** Pell Vey's name is on four documents. Tamsin Rook has told him four times about the piece of paper and has not collected it, and has now told him on a stair that he argued badly, and did not pretend she liked watching.
- Unresolved want: **the Cinder Court's full sitting**, on a list with a date and nobody's name on it, which is the thing he spent eight months learning does not count. The ninety words in Nevin Trost's hands, which he will not move. **A bundle marked *damaged, four items, uncollated* on the second shelf of the room he has just been moved into, with a red working stitch through two leaves, which he has decided not to open.** And four lines to a sister, sent on the sixteenth of the eighth, with no answer and no second letter.

## Tamsin Rook

- Position: **roll-keeper of the third gallery, having no office, entered in a Cinder Court minute book as attending for the applicant.** She is a person whose findings now have her name on them, which is the one thing she spent four months refusing and cannot now go back on.
- What she gained: **three index cards copied standing up out of an unlocked rack, without removing anything**, and the batch's best narrowing: **a number on a document is a fact about the book the document was entered in and not about the document.** The Caudle notice is not from the Venn series, carries no substituted wording and no public-necessity sentence, and comes out of an assessors' instruction **four years and four months older than the Venn correction and about tool debts in a dyers' trade.** She **sent nothing to Marek** and wrote down why. She told him about the drawer on the twenty-fourth of the fifth and then, three months late, told him what was in it. She built a referral, put his name **second** in a caption instead of first, and watched the registrar do in a minute what he had failed to do in eight months.
- What she lost: **being the person who knows.** She handed the list over — four pages, four hands, Bryn Mawer at a chandler's counter, Sarn, Sayer and Nell — and the four pages do not agree and the disagreement is written on all four and nobody tidied it. What she got back is a name she will never know the source of: a woman in Greyfen with four children and no candle who did not answer a letter from a Chancery in eight months and answered a shop window, and paid four pence for a sheet, and told nobody. **The sixth column, *why not*, is still empty because she will not invent it.**
- The copy list: forty-one names, thirty-two answers and nine non-answers as at the tenth of the fourth. Then, of the Cinder Court's record, **twenty-nine say yes to keeping a copy and twelve say no**, and the twelve are the finding. She wrote the reasons down in full because reasons are the thing copyists are for, including a woman who burned hers and a woman with eleven hundred pounds of debt who thinks keeping a copy is a thing you do if you are going to be proved right.
- Unresolved want: **the reply from the district of Glasswater**, which the handover list says in terms is *hers* and not his. **The fourth envelope**, addressed to the standing assessor of a furnace town, written in the fourth week of the first month, still in the drawer, and it is the one that is going to matter in nine years. And a copy of a decision being a weapon and a target, which she worked out before anybody told her.

## Nell Kest

- Position: **dye-house worker, twenty, and the person who turns up.** She put four questions on a board, and the board is now public property: anybody may take the questions off it and use them, and two people have, a man off the Reed Cut bank and a woman of seventy-one at the top of the lane.
- What she gained: **twenty-three doors walked in four hours a day.** The fourth question asked of a woman who goes round with the quarter's sheet finds a different answer than it found at Millbrook Reach, because here the person who read the paper **is** paid for the reading and says so first, before being asked the second half. She put the fourth question in the open at a trestle in front of a hundred and four people and **sixty said yes they are paid to read and not paid for the answer, ten said I do not know, and four said nobody read it to me**, and thirty in the back half were not asked and she wrote that number on the board herself in chalk with her name under it. Then she asked the assessor of eleven houses a question nobody had asked — whether a girl of nineteen on a bench since Michaelmas is a person in the file — and the answer was that the book has eleven houses and no column for a name and never has, and the assessor **refused to put one in** and asked for that to be written down. **Nell's own line about it, written in her book that night: *a person can say no to me in a room of eleven and be thanked for it. That is a thing I have not got questions for.***
- What she lost: **the levy is lost on paper**, three and a quarter pages against the four houses that signed, and Roke's other half was said out loud in a lane in front of ninety people and written down. She published a number about four neighbours. She spent two days of work and six months of saving on a coach fare. She has no trade standing, no bench and no money, and the practice that came out of her trip is **in four buildings in three districts and does not belong to her.**
- What she did: **four hundred and forty miles, made to go through the assessor because going round it would prove nothing**, nine days in a furnace town advising nobody, two hundred and some papers read out belonging to about forty houses, **eleven in the model form, nine with a house in the box and two with a person in it.** She read the box last and did not say whether it was right. She asked Bettin Rahl the fifth question nobody has asked — *what a house does when the assessor says no* — and he could not answer it and wrote it down under his own rule at the second leaf of his trade's book with his own name beside it. She did not write to Marek. **Her brother wrote to her on the sixteenth of the eighth, in four lines, about the levy, and she has not answered and is entitled to.**
- Unresolved want: the fourth question, which has never once been asked of a dragon. The levy's fight, which she has said in advance will be lost. And the fact that the thing that travels is not a form and not a ruling and not a question, and is a person who turns up.

## Pell Vey

- Position: head of the Notaries' Table, still holding the cabinet key, and **the most exposed man in this matter by his own signature: four documents** — the reply of the thirteenth of the twelfth, the recorded refusal of the certification, the recorded refusal of the promotion, and now the referral.
- What he did in the closing batch: **he won the caption and lost nothing else, and the winning was the whole point.** He refused to argue the merits of a thing his own court had not read, and argued instead that a document with a clerk's name at the top of it is a document about the clerk, and that it would go into a file where a man reviewing it in four years would say that a clerk had a view about a series. He put Marek's name **in the second line instead of the first**, and the difference between those two things is, in his words, *the difference between a matter and a campaign, and a campaign is what a man in a good coat files against you with.* He signed, said the arithmetic out loud to himself and could not make it come out lower, and **wrote down that Marek did not ask him to.**
- What he is owed and has not been given: the **ninety-word corridor conversation** of the ninth of the eleventh month, which he has asked for twice. He has known since the sixth of the twelfth that Tamsin has a dated record of the yard of the fourth of the tenth and has let her keep it, and he calls it the single worst supervisory act of his career and says he would do it again. **He has still not asked about the yard and Marek has noticed that he did not ask.**
- Unresolved want: to know what his own office said, and to keep his own hands out of a file he has now signed four times, which is no longer available to him.

## Ivet Sarn

- Position: clerk of the Mosswake Roll Court, thirty-eight, and **the person who runs the thing, and has stopped minding.**
- What she did in the closing batch: **she ruled off a line on her own board for the Cinder Court's record on the fourth of the sixth, thirteen days before the court sat, before anybody had thought of asking her to**, and the record arrived on the twenty-ninth and went into it. A copy is a shilling. She wrote the head of it in her own hand and by her own instruction, not because anybody told her to, and told a wall to read the first order, the second and the fourth, and that the third is about a man's certificates and you will not understand it. She then wrote **seven numbered paragraphs of what her district has to do about it, which is almost nothing, and said so on the wall**, including that the first order is now true in forty-five other places and this court is glad of it and claims none of it, and that the sentence is **not withdrawn here, because this court is the only body that can withdraw its own practice**, and that the district has not decided whether to run a process next season and she has not asked the magistrate and will not be asked. A man in a good coat read all three things on that wall standing up, in about eleven minutes, before he reached the sill, and bought two things.
- The numbers on the wall, unchanged and still true: **thirty-four hearings**, a hundred and two hours of a six-hour window, nine shillings and fourpence of the eleven. **Four** people said *I do not understand this* and are entered as having said it, which is a complete answer and not a refusal. **Eleven** of the other thirty signed something else inside a month, and both facts sit in the same box.
- What she wrote in the first person about herself, which she has never done in nine years: *I would rather be frightening and indispensable at a window with a nail in it, in a district of four hundred and sixty households where a man in a good coat has to stand and read everything before he reaches my sill. That is the whole of what I have got and it took nine years and a trial that cost nine shillings and fourpence and four people saying the words I do not understand this in a register, and I have nobody to hand it to, and there is a sentence on this wall about it, and the sentence is the only inheritance I have ever been left.*
- What she is carrying: **eleven shillings in a cloth she did not spend a penny of**, and a copy of a register line about Rennie Sallow's own house that has been in a drawer in her room since the fourth of the tenth. She has decided to walk up the upper lane on the fourteenth of the seventh, on his way home from the works, and say four words — *from the window, no charge* — and go, and if he asks her in, she has a shilling to take back to a man with a tray.
- Unresolved want: she has told a wall that if you come to this window with a paper with a duty on it she will read it to you and will not tell you what to do about it, and will enter that you came, **because the register above her head is a register of duties and does not do that and never will.** Nobody's job is to open the window.

## The others

- **Leda Rell**, magistrate, Mosswake Roll Court: **entered five numbered findings on the twenty-first of the fifth with her name at the foot of the minute**, refused Orra Dunn's early-hearing request in a room on the tenth of the fourth with a date, and in the closing hearing **admitted out loud, for the first time in writing anywhere, that this court applied the public-necessity sentence in letters and without a hearing to about sixty households in this district in the sixth year** and cannot get their names in time, and that they are not before it. She also entered that the district will have to work out for itself what it does in a flood when it cannot use the sentence, and named that cost so nobody could say afterwards it was not known, and answered Sarn's question on the board in the court's name and not the clerk's. She is the volume's most expensive witness against her own practice.
- **Sela Vint**, presiding, room four, Cinder Court: read out four orders, refused three of Marek's four questions in a form with a name at the foot of it, **took the fourth**, recorded the public-necessity sentence as model wording and not a decision of any court, let the registrar suspend sixty-one certificates on his own authority, **escalated the sealed instruction to a full sitting on her own motion over the applicant's objection**, told him he had lost, entered his objection verbatim, and then told him in a room why she had done it anyway: *a thing that is not on a list is in a drawer, and a drawer in this building is where things go in the ninth month of a year and are not opened again.* She also states, with her own name at the foot of the minute, that **this court does not know and cannot compel, and knows of no person at the head of the office whose standing authority sets the sheet of the twenty-second of the ninth month in type.**
- **The registrar of the Cinder Court**, unnamed, about fifty, a heavy file that has been heavy for years: read the caption twice, read the whole of an entry-by-device form aloud in seventeen minutes, refused three questions in a shape that would be useful, took the fourth, read Sivra Oris's statement into the court **as a document and not as a citation**, read forty minutes of file and stopped once, and then did the volume's central act in a registrar's note: **a pattern of five offices, no two within four hundred miles, each of which did exactly what it was required to do, four of which can no longer do harm, and one of which can do it again tomorrow and has nobody in it.**
- **Orra Dunn**, thirty-six: her early-hearing request was refused on the tenth of the fourth with a date, and on the twenty-first of the fifth the household was heard, the girl was heard, and the creditor's paper was **read into the hearing in full at the fifth hour at the household's request and not the court's own motion.** She asked for the opposite of what she wanted and said out loud, twice, that she did not know why it was the right thing. **She is not protected from the second paper**, which is on the barn term, was not served, and was not going to be served before the hearing because of three words in a lawyer's letter: *before the hearing.*
- **Nessa Dunn**, fifteen: **was heard**, is entered as heard, and is on the page having rewritten two of the four questions in a hand that is her mother's with her own name at the top of it — the first from *is there work in it* to *is there work in it, and is the work mine, or the work of whoever is in the room when it is done*, and the second to *whose name is on it, and is mine one of the names on it, and is mine the first one* — because in that file she is the last name and the last one pays. She kept the other two exactly as they are, on the record, because they are the two she cannot answer and she would not have her mother's handwriting make them sound better than they are.
- **Ilsabet Kell**, sixty-one: came to the room on the seventeenth of the fifth, three months later than she had meant to, on her son's arm, with a box in her own hand, and **asked that her note and the hearing be in the same hand in the same book on the same day**, and said first that she was sixty-one and that she lent the four lines to a house of forty years standing and not to a girl of fifteen. Her son asked her twice in the yard whether she was sure and was told to stop asking, and **she asked for it to be noticed that not asking was a thing he chose and not a thing she got.**
- **Tavi Venn**, seventeen, and **Mara Venn**, forty-three: **Tavi was examined for canal work on the twenty-sixth of the fifth by Odile Fenn and passed**, in reading, writing, arithmetic, ditching on the level and the setting of a gate linkage to a maker's gauge, and may now be taken on by a master on a **written** engagement and not on a verbal one. The fee was **two shillings and sixpence** out of a Reed Cut works fund under the head *labour, examinations, engaged*, entered by Wendt Sayer on the **sixth of the fourth — seven weeks before the examination** — as a recruitment cost and not a kindness. The household still has a house, four quarters of the upper holding, two rooms over a shop and a gate, and **Aldren's file cannot be made complete and is not going to be**, and all five terms of the ruling are now posted in two places with the date.
- **Wendt Sayer**, Reed Cut district works foreman: nine men, a boy and a wet rope. Paid the examination fee out of his own fund under a head a stranger will read in a year and have to be argued with, **seven weeks before it was needed**, and now holds one of the four pages of the copy list in his works book. He still has not been thanked and has not asked to be.
- **Bettin Rahl**, about sixty, the standing assessor of the Emberwatch Dyers' Combination, the only door a house in that trade may knock on for thirty-one years: **would not open the door to an uninvited woman of twenty**, made her answer the fourth question from the step before he would let her up, and then gave her the whole of it, including the sentence that the rule is there so that a house has a witness and **he is the only witness in four hundred and forty miles and he is not going to be turned into a stranger's errand boy by a lane he has never visited.** He gave her a chair and nine days and no advice. He then **wrote the fifth question and his own answer — that there is none — under his own thirty-one-year-old rule at the second leaf of the Combination's book, in his own hand, with Nell Kest's name beside it**, on a page nobody has read since the rule was written. The rule is still in force and is still the only door.
- **Marl Crenn**, fifty-one: read her own association's instruction for the first time in eleven years, on the ninth of the sixth, **and sat down**; wrote to two of her eleven houses and not the other nine and wrote down why; has not told the guild and is not going to; and has a decision in her own association's favour on the page that costs Greyfen's four houses thirty-six pounds a year and that she said in advance would be fought and lost.
- **Nery Caudle**, thirty-nine, and **Mike Caudle**: the notice is out from under the cloth, and **Mike has known since the fifteenth of the fourth and told nobody for a month**, and the reason is not dignity — Nery knew on the sixteenth and did not tell him because she has been the house everybody is kind to since the first month and did not want to be the house that got a man in a good coat to come and be told things by her husband. **Nobody is going to ask him whether he objects, because the guild's second instrument does not require anybody to ask him.** His whole remedy is four words: read the line out before he puts his thumb on it.
- **Bryn Mawer**, seventeen: copying in the counting house on the Kiln row, not on the bench at the inventory, not among the eleven protected, a line in a file and no wages, paid **a penny a sheet at a chandler's counter in Candlewright Lane**. She wrote to the third gallery on the seventh of the seventh, over her own signature, asking to be examined in the copying again, in her own words, and not recorded as frightened. Her reason is the chapter's argument: **a copy of a decision is a weapon and it is a target**, the households that kept it are the households that can be shown to have had notice, and in four months a man with a tray will go down that lane asking who has got a copy, and the answer will be her, and she is seventeen with no trade and no assessment and nobody who will stand in a room with her. **She holds one of the four pages of the list.**
- **Nevin Trost**, thirty-two: wrote the nine scoping lines that made the limited sitting possible, marked the seventh as his own, granted the thirty-day extension, tried to keep Bryn Mawer's line in the file, and made the record of the sealed page's refusal. **Marek still has not written to him**, and has now written down the reason: the rule he made to protect himself is protecting Trost, and a rule that only ever works in one direction is not a rule and is a preference.
- **Hallis Dren**, thirty: sent the counterparty notification to a dragon on his office's own paper, found the instrument in the water office's book, stamped the copy that stays, and wrote the page number on it unasked. Wrote two hundred and eighty-four at the foot of the rate book on the sixth of the fourth and it never happened. **He has still not told anybody that he answered a quarterly return on the eleventh of the tenth**, and nobody in the story knows, and Marek has now written in his own book that he has not mentioned it to one human being in eleven months.
- **Iselle Oster**, forty-one, Millbrook Reach, three hundred miles down the water: **still short the master's hoop gauge**, which went on the fourth of the twelfth and cannot be got back, and the trade cannot be examined without it and the season's hoop work comes back in. She put **forty-one pages of the Cinder Court's record into a book that says on its first page that nothing in it is evidence**, and wrote the first order in her own words as *no person may be put in for something they did not hear and did not sign*, which is not what the court said and is the sentence she wanted, and **that is the one anybody in that town will read in nine years.** **Tace Oster**, sixteen, asked to be asked and got asked.
- **Sabra Ollett**, forty-one, nine years at the Millbrook Reach landing office: stamped a creditor's filled-in model into the routine column in about four seconds, cannot produce a document, told the truth about the stamp in front of two witnesses, and has still not been given anything to feel better about. She is named on the Cinder Court's roll-call of people willing to stand in a room.
- **Sabra Ollett's stamping is now the fourth of the five offices** in the registrar's note, and the fact that it is named in a public minute book does not make her culpable and does not alter one thing in that landing office except the number of people who know what she did.
- **Odile Fenn**, the Mosswake guild witness: **examined Tavi Venn on the twenty-sixth of the fifth and entered her in the guild's book as a canal worker.** Her docket that month held the Dunn review on the twenty-first, which is why the examination could not be in the fourth month.
- **The unnamed woman who goes round the Greyfen lane with the quarter's sheet**: paid **four shillings a day** to be in a room with a sheet, paid by the quarter, and she said yes **before** she was asked the second half, and asked for two things written down that are not in her wage: that she said yes first, and that she read the amended clause in all eleven houses including the seven that did not sign. She did not give Nell Kest her name.
- **The assessor of the eleven houses**, unnamed, the only door in her own trade as well: came sixty miles in a cart to a lane, brought a house with her, **refused to put a column for names in her own association's book**, said so out loud, and asked for it to be written down that she was asked, because the next assessor will be a different person in about six years and will not have been in the room. She has not been asked again and has said she will not answer anything else.
- **The man of sixty-one** in Chapter 45: was asked for his name twice, **said no twice**, and asked for that to be written down, and said that a person being asked twice is the whole of what he had heard about and did not want to be it. **His refusal is on the board** and is the fourth entry, and it counts.
- **The four women of the fourth house past the weigh-house in Emberwatch**: sent the first letter that trade has ever sent to a court, got four first names into a copyist's book and a clerk's page by the third of the seventh, and were in the room on the second day of a nine-day visit. They are named in Chapter 50 as four women with a surname and a landmark and no first names in one hand and four first names in four other hands.
- **The woman of twenty-two** in Emberwatch: put her own name in the box headed *the person who holds* on her own house's amendment because the man who brought it told her the box takes whoever is on the bench, and there is no line under the box saying he is not in it, and she has wanted to say it out loud in a room with somebody in it since the second week of the second month and there has been no room in that trade for thirty-one years. **She has now said it out loud in a room with somebody in it.**
- **The professional copyist on the Greyfen road**: six free copies in the ninth month of last year that were anger and not charity, **four hundred and eleven sheets at four pence in the fifth month of this year**, and a waiting list inside the window. She has gone from being a woman with anger and a window to being a woman with a queue, and she says that somebody else's anger went out of a door and became her week. She is **not** a holder of the copy list and the pages are four.
