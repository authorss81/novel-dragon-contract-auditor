# Character State

Standing of each principal character at the close of Volume 01, Batch 0002 (Chapters 11–20). This file answers "where is everyone now," not "who are they"; identity, voice, and motivation live in `bible/characters.md`. Keep it compact enough to read whole.

## Marek Kest

- Stage: still 0, Clerk's Eye, plus the first unstable Seam-Sight spent in Chapter 8. He performs no magic in Chapters 11–20 and the inability to lie about the guarantor **lapsed on the page** in Chapter 18, at the fourth hour, to a nineteen-year-old in a lane. The headache went on the twenty-sixth of the tenth. Both are described as a lapse and not a cure, and he is explicit that he will not pay Perrin Ashe a penny to be told the word *well*, because a thing that has stopped is not a thing that has been treated.
- Position: junior clerk of the Inheritance Desk with unstable standing. In Examinations custody: the nine-paragraph request of the eighteenth of the ninth, **narrowed by his own signature** on the nineteenth of the tenth to the sale of marks and matrices in the district of Auremar, with Glasswater and Emberwatch "reserved and not abandoned"; a thirty-day extension on the file; a **supplementary request of the eighth of the eleventh** naming MC/FS, both issues, both runs, the distribution line and the signer; a supplementary sheet of the eighteenth of the tenth for Bryn Mawer, left in on purpose; and a recorded **refusal to produce the second sealed page**, which is the price on the file in his own name.
- What he gained: the mechanism. A printed model circular carrying the substituted wording and, since the twenty-second of the ninth, the Mosswake sentence itself, in a run of nine hundred and forty, from a standing office of this Chancery — and a separate First Seat quarterly return that makes the honest answer small instead of making it a lie. What he lost: a district's private settlement that would have restored the Venn file completely; two provinces of scope in his own request; Bryn Mawer's wages; four of the eleven clients' trades; and the right to tell anybody in a room that nothing further will touch them.
- He is a man who is now the person the lane comes to, and Chapter 18 is the chapter in which he discovers that and cannot fix it. His two live failures are named: the letter to Magistrate Rell, written on the eighth of the eleventh and not sent, and the four sentences he wrote in a book about himself that Tamsin has not used against him.
- Unresolved want: to answer the certification offer, which is due by the fourth month, without turning the sentence into a rule and without leaving the sentence with nobody under it.

## Tamsin Rook

- Position: junior roll-keeper, the copyist of record for the Mosswake matter, and the person the two provincial files went to. She has an independent book with a time written beside Marek's signature, the answer Tamsin gave in Mara's yard, the four days under the date, and Pell Vey's yard conversation recorded in full on the fourth day of the tenth with a line under it: *He says he did not know and did not act. That is one sentence too many for a man who had not read it. Ask him one day, not today.* **She has still not told Marek any of this.**
- Standing: she sent the First Seat form to all thirteen named protected people with a plain warning and a date, did not publish it, did not ask Marek, and told him afterwards in one sentence. She opened the chain in front of a constable and a boy to get the existence of the second sealed page entered without opening it. She wrote to Mosswake on the second, enclosed a provincial file without asking, told him on the third, and asked for the time on it. She admitted the two exhibits into the Venn matter on the clerk's own authority and the copies went out before anybody thought about it. She then said out loud that she is frightened, because the thing is larger than a widow and she is a copyist with a stool.
- Relationship to Marek: professional, and now reciprocal. He signs what is made in his own name; she strikes out of it what will not do. The second letter she did not send, and the narrowing he signed, are both the same lesson arriving from two directions.
- Cost: she is on a register of her own, has made a decision she admits was wrong on purpose, and is holding a witness in a copyist's room with nowhere to go.
- Unresolved want: to know who set MC/FS, and to know what the red stitch is, without being told.

## Nell Kest

- Position: dye-house worker at four and six the week to Michaelmas, and the person who runs the reading table at the Venn lane. She has a rota of four on a levy sheet nailed to the table leg, a chalk rule, and no clerk and no number; and a paid job copying the amended guild clause into the other houses' schedules.
- Gains: an exit clause that names her, a sum, eight weeks, and a fund rather than the next woman; and the first paid work she has had that is not vats.
- Costs: the levy, which costs a woman at the Caudle house two pounds and five a quarter out of a single wage, and which Nell had to go and ask for in person. And the amendment is a **form with a number in it** that Crenn warned her will travel to four houses on a lane and be signed by frightened people.
- Relationship to Marek: she has now been told about their mother — the Chancery roll with a blank where the name should be and a word meaning *struck*, a copy in Bren's tin since Marek was seventeen, a disputed file that is neither enforceable nor free, and a sealed page he will not open. She has **not forgiven him for the ninth month** and said so, and she named the price: a vicar and a lie about the levy, in her own words. Her standing sentence is that someday he will be in a room and she will be behind him and will need to have been told first, in a room with nobody in it, while he still could — and she gave it to him twice and he did not ask either time.
- Unresolved want: the four other houses' signatures, and a trade that does not depend on a form somebody else can read with somebody else's instrument.

## Pell Vey

- Position: supervisor of the Notaries' Table, still holding the cabinet key. In the yard behind the Chancery on the fourth day of the tenth he asked Tamsin how many of thirteen protected people she had it, was refused, then said one true thing: he did not know the form existed, found out three weeks earlier from a boy with a tray, and would not have acted on it in the eighth month. He then gave her his own worry — a form with a run on it is a form about a business, and somebody in it will eventually need to know which thirteen names are with a copyist.
- Function: mentor-shaped antagonist, more exposed and no more guilty. Nothing he has said is contradicted and nothing he has said is corroborated. Tamsin has written it all down and has not told Marek, which is the first thing she has withheld from him since the Chapter 5 draft and which she intends to use at a time of her choosing.
- Unresolved want: to keep his own hands out of it, which is no longer available to him.

## Corvin Dray

- Position: in the Auremar district house. **His plea is refused on the record** and a trial is set in the next sitting of the district court, on forging a witness sequence and selling a pressed certificate, to be proved on the ledger and on a box with forty-one leaves in it against a docket of forty-three. He will be convicted on that and it will not be about the four lines.
- What he gave on the nineteenth of the tenth: a statement taken properly, in a cell, with a magistrate, a clerk and the roll-keeper, of **eleven pages containing no person's name at all** and one description of a place — scale matrices bought from a counting house on a row by the Kiln sheds, identified in his own book by a mark, a filled square with a bar and a nick, against thirty-eight lines in the *carry* column in three districts, one of them Emberwatch. He has never been up the stairs, and he made his own not-looking into evidence in his own hand because a man who looked and got nothing would be worse than a man who never looked.
- What he still has not given: the four clients who write the sentences.
- Unresolved want: to be the man who said it first, and to be believed about the four without being believed about everything.

## The Venn household

- Tavi Venn, seventeen: the fifth term works. Dren's sheet went up on the rail, the crew did the ditch and the linkage, and she stood clear and gave her bar back at noon in front of thirty witnesses. The lane has decided she took the ditch and said so kindly, and Marek could not correct it. She is still refused a guild seat until the fourth month, and Odile Fenn's examination is still dated to the fourth month's docket.
- Mara Venn, forty-three: has until the second month, twenty-six public copies, and no security. The wax tablet of household figures is now entered in the record as the household accounts and goes down with the ruling and not with Marek. She is owed a letter from Marek telling her that a settlement which would have restored her husband's file completely was refused, and he has not written it yet.

## The Dunn household

- Orra Dunn, thirty-six: has a postponement pending full review with a date in the fourth month, a public entry in Sarn's register she asked for against everything she said in court, and a floor that Grice will hold to the first week of the fourth month only if Nessa is taken off the paper in front of him. She has a neighbour's four pounds of tilling seed she cannot refuse, and a paper sold for a hundred and six that will be served on her daughter's forty quarters of rye in the second month.
- Nessa Dunn, fifteen: heard, answered, read back, entered, and copied to her mother. She has signed nothing, she has said she would rather be asked than safe, and she wants the seed-trade examination that is gated on the fourth month's review. She is also the person the register points other children to.

## The others

- Ivet Sarn, thirty-eight: her four days are on the record with the reason and the cost together, attached to a named household. She told Rennie Sallow herself, in her own words, at the top of the upper lane, and initialled his copy of the tally. She began the public register of pending duties because it costs a shilling and nothing else and because she has been wrong once. She admitted the two provincial exhibits on her own authority on the fourth of the eleventh, before telling the magistrate, and put a paper on Rell's bench about it.
- Hallis Dren, thirty: has posted the fifth term's schedule at the gate under the office's own flood authority with the cost on it, and put his name and his mother's canal house on a nail. On the eleventh of the tenth he answered a quarterly return honestly — one, and yes — and sent it four hundred miles without knowing what it was.
- Ruhl Dunnet, fifty-two: nineteen years a constable, and the record has changed what is written down four times and what happened twice. He carried the district's settlement offer, wrote Marek's refusal down and made him read it back, entered the refused plea, and told him he must tell Mara himself. He has stated that a protection order does not extend to a person's competence.
- Ostyn Vare, forty-five: signed the amendment for the house knowing the audit of his seat, and told Nell out loud that he would rather lose the exemption trying than keep it on a clause he read to her and used against her. He sent her round the lane alone because a foreman asking would be the guild speaking.
- Marl Crenn, fifty-one: signed for this house, this year, on the foot of the amendment, and said in front of both of them that she was signing because a form that has been read will be read again and she would rather it be read the second time with her name on it. She gave Nell the card with the number on it and warned her that the clause is a form and forms travel.
- Rennie Sallow: paid eleven pounds over the price for seed a clerk did not certify, has the figure in his own hand, and asked that it be recorded as his and not the court's. He is on the rota's list through his daughter and is the first person the rota explained itself to.
- Nery Caudle, thirty-nine: signed the amendment in her own yard with two pounds and five a quarter of her own wage in it, on condition that the terms be written on the back in her own words, and had them read back twice.
- Wenna Sallow, nineteen: on the rota, and the person who asked, out loud in a lane, whether a duty stands against her own family, and got the true answer instead of a comfortable one.
- Coll Fenner, twenty-eight: gave his name to a rota because a rota cannot run on a man who has not got one.
- Bryn Mawer, seventeen: Dray's copying girl, not among the thirteen, named in a supplementary request on the eighteenth of the tenth, told by her own request that she was not recorded as frightened. She wants to be examined in the copying and has no wages.
- Brammel, sixty: has his papers back in a leather case with a receipt and the district's inventory still in the district store, and could not get his name out of item nine. He asked for the box to be destroyed and was refused, and read the return before signing it.
- Ilsabet Roon, fifty-five: writes wills, marriage settlements, and sea protests for the dock labourers, and cannot work because a man in a good coat told her people to find another writer. The order protects her paper and not her competence.
- Perry Ostrow, thirty-five: a copyist in the copying room of a Glasswater river court, in a copyist's room in Auremar with a spare blanket and nowhere to go until the end of the week. She entered the endorsement into the filing book on the third of the tenth, told nobody at home, posted the sheets to the lane on the twenty-ninth, and came up the river herself on the first. Her husband is blind since the spring.
- Nevin Trost, thirty-two: an examiner of Examinations, six years in the job, not an enemy. He granted a thirty-day extension and wrote the reason in the box, tried to keep Bryn Mawer's line in the file, was made to record the sealed page's refusal, and handed over an unsigned offer he is not the man to make, and told Marek to choose in the fourth month with the arithmetic rather than in a fortnight in a panic.
- Doctor Perrin Ashe: not revisited, and offered nothing. Marek has decided not to pay him.
- Odile Fenn, thirty-four: still only a fourth-month promise. She does not appear in this batch.
- Wend and Pella Osk, Mosswake court working staff: not yet asked. Pella is still owed a chapter.
- Sivra Oris: not called, not present, not merged with Seryn Oris, who is not named anywhere in this batch.
