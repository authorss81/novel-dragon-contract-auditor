# Character State

Standing of each principal character at the close of Volume 01, Batch 0003 (Chapters 21–30). This file answers "where is everyone now," not "who are they"; identity, voice, and motivation live in `bible/characters.md`. Keep it compact enough to read whole.

## Marek Kest

- Stage: still 0, Clerk's Eye. **He performs no magic anywhere in Chapters 21–30.** No reading, no Seam-Sight, no exception, no counter-seal. The inability to lie lapsed in Chapter 18 and does not return, and the batch makes a private use of the fact: in Chapter 27 he can lie to Nell, in Chapter 25 he lies to Tamsin, and the cost of the reading is that nothing in his own body will now stop him.
- Position: junior clerk of the Inheritance Desk, and the man whose name is under a **refusal signed on the back of a certification offer** in a file in Examinations, entered in a room with a witness at the second hour of the afternoon of the twentieth of the eleventh month. The offer is withdrawn. The request of the eighteenth of the ninth and the supplement of the eighth of the eleventh are still alive and unanswered.
- What he gained: the **review** as the answer to the offer, a named process he did not write, and the midpoint finding that the **Standing Office of Provincial Continuities has no person at the top of it** — the authority for the circular is the drawer the circular is in. He also has the standing instructions, in his own notes, quoting *no further authority is required*.
- What he lost: four households the process will not cover, named out loud in the alcove on the seventeenth of the eleventh before he accepted anything; the letter to Magistrate Rell, which he carried in his coat for a month and posted on the thirteenth of the twelfth; and nine days of not telling Tamsin about Pell's request for the corridor conversation, which is the same failure as the fifth of the ninth and is the last one.
- Standing: Pell Vey's name is on the reply, not his, and Pell has said out loud that this was a spectacular reason to be on it and not a good one. Tamsin has put her written objection to his decision about the quarterly return into his coat pocket and told him she will collect it.
- Unresolved want: the scope decision before the fourth month, from a person with a name on it; the ninety words in the corridor; and the four families his own process does not cover.

## Tamsin Rook

- Position: junior roll-keeper, and the person who has been **handing a process to other people since the twenty-ninth of the eleventh month**. She wrote the four-box form, the witness order, the hearing slate, and the cost ceiling; she carried Dren's list up the river and made Sarn count the cost out loud; she argued the exempt form's fifth line out of a room in Greyfen; and she built a list of forty-one copy-holders out of her own wages.
- Standing: she now knows three things about the case that no office does — that the certification was genuinely good, that the process costs two hundred and eighty-four relief rolls, and that a district which obeys a form perfectly is quiet rather than safe. She has said out loud that the process is only safe while somebody is standing at the window.
- The register: the Mosswake **hearing slate**, in Sarn's hand, on the same board as the register of pending duties, at a shilling a copy. **Nineteen names by the end of the first month, four of whom said the words *I do not understand this*.** Sarn will write the day the window stops on the board and in the book.
- What she lost: two shillings and ninepence and eleven nights; the offer of a quire of Chancery paper, which she refused because an office's paper in a district is a list with an office's name on it; and the argument she did not win, about keeping the First Seat quarterly return out of the reply of the thirteenth. That objection is in Marek's coat pocket in her hand and she has told him she intends to collect it.
- Unresolved want: a district that obeyed the form, a household with no document, and the sentence nobody has been able to put in a way that can be answered — who in the world is helped by quiet.

## Nell Kest

- Position: dye-house worker and the person who made the reading table a piece of lane furniture. She handed the table over on the sixth of the twelfth: the rota sheet is off the leg, there is a chalk board with the **four questions** on it, and there is a list of names that anybody may add to and nobody may take off. **It runs without her.** She was four days in bed in the second week of the twelfth and found it running, and four days in Greyfen in the first month and found it running again, and somebody washed the top of the trestle. What she found the second time was not that the table worked but that **a name had come off the board while she was away and nobody had noticed for three weeks**, and that noticing it is a job that was hers and was never written down as one.
- What she gained: the four questions, which came out of a lane and are now in a letter to eleven houses in the next province in somebody else's hand with the source left out; a paid job copying the amended clause into the schedules of houses that have not signed; the levy book in which four houses' nine pounds a quarter is recorded house by house, with Nery Caudle's two pounds and five and a quarter dated the ninth of the twelfth and her **signature on the clause itself dated the twenty-ninth of the tenth**.
- What she lost: the second paper at the Caudle house, which she now knows about and cannot get to twenty-three houses in the fourth month; the levy fight, which Marl Crenn has told her in advance she will lose; and the part of the table she misses, which is a frightened person arriving and finding her standing there.
- What she did not do: she did not write to Marek. She composed the letter twice on the road and twice at the lane mouth and put it in her coat both times, and the reason she examined and did not believe is that the work was going on without her. The true reason is the workroom in Greyfen in the eighth of the tenth, and she is not going to say so to him, ever, and she knows why.
- Unresolved want: the fourth month's assessment, the two pounds and five and a quarter coming out of one wage, and the twenty-three houses she cannot walk.

## Pell Vey

- Position: supervisor of the Notaries' Table, still holding the cabinet key, and for the first time in two months **his name is on a document in this matter**. He signed the reply of the thirteenth of the twelfth, which states that an office offered a young clerk something and the office put him in a position where he had to, and he said out loud that the reason is a spectacular one rather than a good one and that he has been counting on somebody noticing and being annoyed.
- What he gave: the finding that the Standing Office of Provincial Continuities has no person at the top of it, that its sheets are *issued* and not signed, and the exact wording the reply should use, which is *the office states that its sheets are issued under standing authority and are not signed by a person, and the applicant is referred to the standing instructions of his own district, which are the authority.* He also said that he has signed district standing instructions nine times without asking who the authority was, and that a supervisor who asks is not a supervisor.
- What he has asked for: the ninety-word corridor conversation of the ninth of the eleventh, which is unfiled, in Nevin Trost's hands, in a room with no witness. He is entitled to it because he signed the reply and is exposed to it, and he has not been given it.
- Unresolved want: to know what his own office said, and to keep his own hands out of it, which is no longer available to him.

## Ivet Sarn

- Position: clerk of the Mosswake Roll Court, and now the person who runs two registers. The **public register of hearings** hangs on the same board as the register of pending duties, in her own hand, at a shilling a copy, with a line number against every entry and nothing else.
- What she agreed to and at what price: the process runs six weeks; the cash cost is eleven shillings found by the district works; the real cost is a hundred and fifty-six hours of her own window, which is twenty-six days out of the forty-two that run from the twenty-fifth of the second month to the sixth of the fourth, which would leave sixteen certifying days and therefore **two hundred and eighty-four relief rolls not certified before the rate is struck on the sixth of the fourth month** — a price quoted in advance, not a bill incurred, because the funded trial is deliberately being run with her window shut. She agreed to that only after Tamsin said the number out loud in her window, and she wrote on the wall of the room that a process which costs money it cannot pay in money stops quietly on a Friday in the fifth week and that there is no person to complain to.
- What she holds: the list of people who have agreed to witness at that window, four names in the first week and a promise of eleven by the fourth month; her own set of the three documents in a drawer under the board; a form with no number and no series, written out two hundred and forty times by Pella Osk; and a standing rule that if the sum is spent the window stops and the book says on its face the day it stopped and why.
- What she refused: to put the Dunn household in the hearing register, in eleven seconds, on the ground that a hearing list is a record that you were heard.
- Unresolved want: a docket that finishes, and the sixth of the fourth month.

## The others

- **Hallis Dren**, thirty: the first person in the case to say a certification out loud as a working convenience, on his own letterhead, to the court, to Upper Cut, to Millrace and to Examinations, with his name and his mother's canal house on it. He has compiled the district's own arithmetic in four columns and sent it to four places. Unresolved want: a copy of his own four columns in his mother's house, and a hearing about a return he answered honestly in October that nobody has yet told anybody about.
- **Wendt Sayer**, foreman of the Reed Cut district works: nine men, a boy, and a wet rope he did not need. He priced a hearing at three hours and a shilling and sixpence for a witness, refused to make the arithmetic a good number, and is the reason the cost ceiling is a real figure. Unnamed in the book as a witness and used for nothing else.
- **Rennie Sallow** and **Wenna Sallow**, nineteen: Wenna has taken her own name off the lane's chalk board and given the reason, which is that a thing which only works when you are not the one in trouble is not a thing for people in trouble, and that she had asked once in open lane and been told *I do not know* and would not be told the same answer in front of people twice. Rennie has still not been told anything and has not asked. Nell will not lie to him about it.
- **The Venn household:** the term expired in the second month, on the nineteenth, with nothing decided. Mara Venn has a house, four quarters of the upper holding, two rooms over a shop, and a gate, and has said that a gate is not a field. Nell sat on the step on the evening of the eighteenth and asked nothing. Odile Fenn's canal examination is still dated to the fourth month's docket and still gated on the Dunn review.
- **Orra Dunn**, thirty-six: asked to be entered in the register of hearings and refused; asked instead for the fourth month's review to be called in the second month; had the request entered on the hearing slate with *asked, not granted* under it; bought four weeks by being visible; lost forty quarters of winter rye to a serving on the twenty-ninth of the first month and got the rye thirty-one miles up a canal before the sale was priced. **Ilsabet Kell, sixty-one, unasked, has offered to stand in the second month with her own note in her hand**, at a cost Orra cannot protect her from, on a sentence Orra had to write in the old woman's words. Nessa Dunn, fifteen, has said she is coming to the fourth month and is not staying in the loft and being right about it afterwards.
- **Nery Caudle**, thirty-nine: signed the amended clause in her own yard on the **twenty-ninth of the tenth**, with an assessor reading the paper out of it, in a line in her own words at the bottom that nobody has asked to see; paid the two pounds and five and a quarter on the **ninth of the twelfth**, out of a cloth she had had ready since October, written out with the quarter on it and read back twice. She is the person who has a **second copy of the amended clause under a cloth**, brought by a man on a rack in the second week of the twelfth who was paid by the assessors and did not ask her anything, and who has told nobody for a month.
- **Marl Crenn**, fifty-one: read out the fifth line on the back of the exemption form in a room with a foreman and an assessor from a second association, and agreed to recalculate the levy onto the four signing houses on paper, and said in advance that it will be overruled within a year and that Nell should expect to lose it. Her own seat is exposed because the exemption has been read with another form.
- **Ostyn Vare**, forty-five: audited on the **seventh of the twelfth**, and said out loud that a foreman who has been audited stops being the person anybody brings a paper to. He walked the amended clause into four workrooms on his own two days. He has stopped answering Tamsin's letters and has not been chased.
- **Nevin Trost**, thirty-two: recorded the refusal, wrote the covering paragraph that sent the reply to forty-six districts, wrote in his own hand and signed that the office is under an obligation to state the terms, and is holding an unsigned ninety-word instruction from a corridor in his own building that he deliberately did not file. He has not been written to and has said he is not the man who offers it and is not the man who holds it.
- **Perry Ostrow**, thirty-five: still in the copying alcove, on loan and paid, with her husband's stick, and she has not been written to at home. She would not go to Millbrook Reach and gave Tamsin the four instructions that made the fortnight possible, including *do not take a paper in, because a paper means a decision has already been taken.*
- **Pella Osk:** asked, at last, in Chapter 23, what the copying room was like on the ninth of the ninth, and answered, and it is in the record. She wrote out two hundred and forty of the four-box forms by hand. Discharged.
- **Wend** the lamp-trimmer: still named in open court and still unasked. Available.
- **Iselle Oster**, forty-one, of Millbrook Reach, a cooper's widow doing her own trade: her shop is called Oster and Son with the *and Son* painted over and not painted out. She lost the master's gauge on the fourth of the twelfth, has a daughter of sixteen in a surety line, and has signed, in her own hand, what a woman who said four times that she was nobody wrote down. **Tace Oster**, sixteen, gave the fourth question twice and gave it differently the second time.
- **Sabra Ollett**, forty-one, clerk of the Millbrook Reach landing office, nine years: she stamped a creditor's filled-in model endorsement into the routine column in the tenth month in about four seconds, she cannot produce a number for it, she has thought about those four seconds every day since the ninth of the first month, and she has said that the four questions a wheelwright copied onto a bond receipt in her own row of counting houses are the first thing since January that has made her feel as though the form is not the worst thing in her building.
- **Doctor Perrin Ashe:** not revisited and not paid. **Ruhl Dunnet:** not needed and not in the batch. **Ilsabet Roon, Brammel, Bryn Mawer, Rennie Sallow, Coll Fenner, Ivo Harker:** all placed, all holding, none advanced in this batch. **Odile Fenn:** still only a fourth-month promise. **Sivra Oris:** not called, not present, not merged with Seryn Oris, who is not named anywhere in this batch.
