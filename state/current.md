## Who is alive, who wants what, and what each of them is refused

**Marek Kest, thirty-eight, the protagonist.** Junior inheritance clerk, then a man who chose a labour he was free to refuse. He holds nothing: no office, no fund, no list, no leave, and no paper anywhere that says he has leave to do what he does. He refuses the reading, which has been done to him twice and each time cost a whole day out of his mother's own history. He does not exercise the thing he is able to do. He is not at a stage and is not going to arrive at one. **He has a mark about three inches long on the inside of his left wrist that reads nothing and decides nothing, and it is a symptom and not a price. He wants the erased name of his mother, Ilyra Kest, and the disputed family claim, and he has neither.** He is thanked by nobody, sent for by nobody, and asked by nobody.

**Ilyra Kest, dead on the roll, not dead on the page.** Marek's mother, a Chancery copyist, gone twelve years in the Ashfall Archive fire. The roll declares her dead and removes her name from a disputed family record. She made a secret exception that delayed her younger daughter's conscription and left the claim legally disputed, and it cost her the record and cost her children the claim. **Her name comes back in Volume 12 and her decision is published with it, and she is not turned into an infallible martyr when it does.**

**Nell Kest, twenty, Marek's sister, a dye-house worker in a furnace town about four hundred and thirty miles up this river.** Sixteen books in a counting room at the back of a dye end, fifteen of them on loan, the sixteenth carrying her own name at the head of it. **Her wage has been held since the second week of the third month of the year after. Nineteen days of a crew's money, in a coat, four miles off, against a sum nobody has named. She is not going to ask what it is held against, and that decision is hers and it does not move.** She has told a rule out loud, in about four minutes, to a second of four women. It went nowhere. The other two she has never reached. **She wants a trade of her own, an income that does not depend on a dragon's approval, and the right to reject a contract without being called ungrateful.** She is not going up this river this season, and that is not the same as never.

**Bren Kest, alive, Marek's father, a locksmith in Greyfen, grieving and angry.** He does not understand dragon law and refuses to pretend that he does. He offers ordinary work, honest meals, and the example of repairing a thing rather than declaring it whole.

**Tamsin Rook, twenty-four, at the fourth counter at the top of a stair in Auremar.** Two years at that counter and nobody has asked her anything in two years, and that is a choice and not a fault. She has a live undertaking, unretired, unamended, unexercised, and a decision about a man's judgment that is unmade. **She wants to be asked, and wanting is not owing and owing is not asking, and there is no form for the wanting.** She keeps her own ruled book with two boxes in it carrying the same name, unmerged.

**Marn Ottery, thirty-four, at the other end of the same counter, eleven years, not a lawyer.** About four hundred bought and unfilled blanks under her counter, eleven years, and no form ever drawn to account for them. **Halla Wray, fifty-one, a foreman in a bay at the top of a lane off the Slade.** Entered there by a class, not by herself, and the class is a heading, and being in a box and not being asked about it are the same arrangement. **The man of about thirty-four with the fair hand.** Copies other men's figures until two sets agree, four days a season, on somebody's list. **He is the only person in this matter anybody could put a question to and he is the one nobody has.** He has a number in his head he has never written down and never said, and handing it over would make a heading, and a heading would stand over him too. **The man of about sixty-one, the second hand since he was forty-two.** Signs at the foot of about nine hundred sheets a year and cannot read a paragraph. **About nine of them a year are not what the person above him wrote, and he signed every one, and he is the only person alive who knows which ones they were and he cannot write one of them down.** He wants about four minutes to say no to about half and yes to the rest. **The clerk of about fifty-five, nineteen years in one room off the old river road.** Writes the minutes, advises nobody, asks nothing. **The woman of about twenty-six at the end of four hundred yards of cold flags.** Keeps a count of how many people are sitting inside a thing nobody can be asked about, in her head, and it has gone up, and it is not being handed on and does not get a heading.
