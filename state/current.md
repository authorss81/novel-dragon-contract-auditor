## Volume 01, closed.

**The question the volume asked**, quoted from the next-volume question in `outline/volume-01.md`: *Why does the same substituted language appear in three provinces, Corvin's records, and Ilyra Kest's private file—and what authority is behind the sealed First Seat instruction?*

**The answer the volume gave** is deliberately narrower than anybody wanted. Nobody names anybody. Two courts have now recorded in writing that they do not know and cannot compel, each with a name at the foot of the sentence — a magistrate at Mosswake on the third of the fourth, and Sela Vint at the Cinder Court on the seventeenth of the sixth. The public-necessity sentence is model wording and not a decision of any court, and the district that stated it has not withdrawn it, because only that court can withdraw its own practice. What the registrar of the Cinder Court found instead, out of a document and in a minute, is **a list of five offices four hundred miles apart, each of which did exactly what it was required to do**. Four of them can no longer do harm. The third can do it again tomorrow and there is nobody in it. **A list of offices is a route and it is not an answer**, and it is worse than what Marek asked for and the only thing there is.

**The smaller question the volume actually answered** is the one that turned out to be the volume: *who is helped by quiet?* It is answered in the negative, in a furnace town, by a trade. A district that obeyed a form perfectly produced no document, no number, and nobody who could be asked a question, and the absence of a document turned out not to be a defect in the system but the system working exactly as designed. A document nobody can produce is not a failure of the machinery. It is the machinery.

**The sentence the volume is a book about** is the one on the last page, in Marek Kest's own hand, at the foot of a roll-call of the people who stood in a room under their own names and what each of them paid: *there is not one line of it that says I was right.*

**The volume's last image is that roll-call.** The red thread is the route the volume ends on: a copyist's working stitch through two leaves of a burned archive wrapper from twelve years ago, found on a shelf in a received room, unexplained, and joined in a sentence to nothing else on the page. **The two are not the same thing.** The divergence between the Batch 0005 goal and the Chapter 50 card is already recorded in `state/continuity.md`: the card was followed because a card is the chapter's plan, and Volume 02 should treat the roll-call as the volume's closing image and the stitch as the route it ends on. **Volume 02's first batch has put the stitch four feet from where the man who found it works, and has not opened it, and has written down a new reason.**
