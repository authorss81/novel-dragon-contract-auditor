# VOLUME 09, CHAPTERS 0401 TO 0410, BATCH 0001, MOVEMENT ONE — "THE SEED, AND THE THING NOBODY IS BUYING"

*Written by the phase that took `workspace/volume-09/batch-0001/PROMPT.md`. Ten entries, one per chapter, and this is a batch and not a review, not a close and not a repair. The file was empty of entries by the handoff's consolidation and fills again with the first batch of the first volume that has an outline of record. **Each entry carries the right claim and names the wrong one beside it where a cold reader would otherwise inherit it, and none of the three corrections is a thread and none of them is closed here.***

**0401 — Four Items On A Fee Board, About Four Hundred Blanks Under The Boards, And The Plainest Sentence She Has Said In Eleven Years.** Marn Ottery, thirty-four, eleven years at those boards, not a lawyer. A courier comes up the four flights with a satchel, is told one word and no sentence, and is never told what anything is for. The clerk of about twenty-nine resists her: *your hands are on the boards, I have watched you do that four times in eleven years and three of them were followed by a sentence.* Then the clerk says the fourth of the four items on that board is the one none of the three of them can account for, that she has said its number out loud to two people in eleven years, and that the not asking was not a courtesy, because there is no form anywhere in which a person may be told what a thing on a board is going to be used for. **Marn Ottery says the plainest sentence anybody has said on that floor in eleven years — a blank is a piece of paper with nothing printed on it, it is the only paper in this empire that cannot be laid against anybody, about four hundred of them a year go out of that counter in the hand of a person who is not doing anything anybody asked for, and not one has come back with a heading printed over what was written on it — and nobody thanks her, and she says in terms that she is not going to be thanked.** The clerk refuses to pass it on to the man who comes in on the second of the two days: *you do not get to give a thing to a person who has not asked you for it.* The box is not opened, the drawer is not opened, nothing is ruled. The count of things asked did not move and is printed in no chapter.

**0402 — Twenty Minutes Before The Fourth Hour On A Floor Nobody Has Needed Yet, And A Woman Who Does Not Know What They Are For.** Tamsin Rook, twenty-four, two years at that counter. Nobody has ever put a question to her and nobody has ever thanked her; her undertaking is live, unretired, unexercised, and its date is not said out loud here or in any state record. She does eleven things in twenty minutes in a fixed order and then has ten minutes of nothing. **Marn Ottery resists her and says a thing that is not about the twenty minutes: a person who is in a room before the room is needed is a person who has made herself available in a way nobody can undo, and nobody in that building has done one thing about it, and the not doing anything about it is the whole of the arrangement.** Tamsin Rook calls that the cheapest thing either of them has paid and says she will not say it again. She says out loud, once, that she does not know what the twenty minutes are for, that she will not retire or exercise anything, and that she does not know whether she is being careful or getting ready and will not decide it in a room with Marn Ottery in it. The decision about whether she trusts a colleague's judgment is not made. The twenty minutes are kept, and they are not a decision and are not on anything.

**0403 — The Same True Thing Twice In Front Of Two People, A Man Who Has Loaded Here Nineteen Years, And A Box Three Inches Forward.** The young man from the shipping floor, who in 0391 told Marn Ottery out loud that a clerk who moves a box has made a statement, and that she was right the statement was hers. **A man who has loaded on that floor for nineteen years resists him and says, without a question, that he has made the same statement twice in front of two people, that a statement made twice in front of two people has two people attached to it, and that he did not think about that when he said she was right.** He does not take it back and he does not say it a third time. He goes up the four flights to leave a sheet, stands at the near end of the boards, does not touch the box and does not go near the end where it is, and neither woman at that counter says a word about where he stands. **A courier is on the bottom step with a satchel and cannot be told what is in it and asks nothing, and the two of them go in opposite directions and neither knows the other's name.** The box is where it was. The statement stands with two people attached to it and he has no room to be served in.

**0404 — Four Feet Of Bare Wall, One Nail In It From A Man Nobody Has Asked About, And A Lamp Brought Up That Was Not Wanted.** **Marek Kest, thirty-eight, in person, first of the three.** The room taken by the week, about four feet of bare wall beside a window, a nail driven in crooked that nothing has ever hung on, no fire and not going to be one. **The woman who keeps the ground floor, who has never had his name and has never given him hers, comes up with a lamp she was not asked to bring and tells him the smallest true thing anybody has said to him in eight volumes: a nail is the only thing in this matter that somebody put up and then never took down, and he has been in that room long enough that the nail is older than he is in it.** She gives him the shape of the man who was there before him and refuses the rest, and he does not ask. He does not go and look, does not write anything down, hangs nothing on the nail, and holds nothing at the end that he did not hold at the start. The name on the leaf is not printed and the name of the hand that struck the line is not printed, and the reason he gave for not reaching a stage is referred to and is not paid a second time.

**0405 — Four Lines At The Top Of A Column, A Sentence That Stopped In A Doorway, And A Man At The Head Of Those Tables.** **Rennick Adley, thirty-eight, the Company's book-keeper, four hundred yards up the lane off the Slade. This chapter is his and it is not the protagonist's and the two men are not in one sentence.** He writes the four lines out for the twelfth time and they come out the same. **The clerk of about twenty-nine resists him and says out loud that the second half of that sentence is not going to arrive, because a sentence which stops halfway in a doorway was a sentence with two people in it, and because the man who has been at the top of those two joined tables for eleven years has never been down that lane and has not mentioned it, and a thing nobody mentions is not a thing anybody is waiting for.** He asks for nothing. **He turns the sheet so the four lines sit at the foot of it, under the last ruled line, where a book-keeper's own labour goes and where anybody reads — the clerk points out that that is the part of a sheet anybody reads, and that there is no form anywhere for taking a thing back up a column once it has been down it.** A courier sets a satchel on the counter and is not examinable and asks nothing. The four hundred yards is not walked, no blank is carried up it, the half sentence does not arrive, and nobody is thanked.

**0406 — Twenty Years At The Bottom Of Those Stairs, A Man At The Door Who Wants A Name, And A Rent Taken Anyway.** The woman who keeps the ground floor of a rented house, about twenty years at the bottom of those stairs, never once above the second one, takes a rent, and has never had his name and has never given him hers. **A man of about thirty-one with a book comes to the door about the freeholder's wish to let the second floor to somebody who can be served, and says out loud that a person who takes a rent for twenty years under no name cannot be served with anything, and that this is not a protection, because the reason it is not a protection is that nobody would want to.** She does not give the name and says it in the doorway rather than inside, and tells him the difference between the two things is the whole of what she has and she is the only one in that house who knows which side of it she is on. He says he will come back on the same day next week and she will be in the doorway again and they will both be right about their own half of it. **The rent is taken out of a hand into a hand, nothing is written anywhere, nobody comes down those stairs, and the clerk of about fifty-five is not in that house and has never been and is not going to be; the two women are never in one room and no distance between them is stated or denied.**

**0407 — The Width Of The Space At The Foot Of A Sheet, A Woman Who Has Been On That Floor Two Years, And A Man Who Has Been Up Those Flights Once Before.** **Marek Kest, thirty-eight, in person, second of the three, and his want visible in it.** He comes up the four flights for the second time in his life, at the sixth hour, and the counter shuts at the sixth hour. A woman of about forty-four puts fourpence on the boards, is handed a piece of paper with nothing printed on it, is asked nothing, says nothing, folds it in three and goes down the stairs. Marn Ottery gives him the second hand's figures without being asked: about nine hundred sheets a year out of a building nine miles off, every one signed at the foot by a man of about sixty-one who cannot read a paragraph, about nine of them a year not what the person above him wrote. **Tamsin Rook comes round the end of the counter and says the sentence the chapter is built on: a space at the foot of a sheet is the exact width of a name, nothing else has ever been that size, and a person who has a name that fits in a space that size and no paper to put it on is not a person whose thing is missing, and a person whose thing is missing has a shape you can point at, and he has a shape and no thing.** **Marn Ottery says nothing, for the first time in eleven years, and notices that she has said nothing.** He does not write the name down and does not go and look. The case the blanks come out of is not the box, and no form says where the case is filled. The nine miles is not walked and the man of about sixty-one is asked nothing.

**0408 — The Lock End Of Four Hundred Yards Of Cold Flags, A Rack That Comes Up Light, And The Going Back Up That Nobody Has Ever Asked About.** The woman of about twenty-six, four years at the lock, never one of the three clerks, never sent for, thanked or handed a sheet. **She is at the lock and the room with the rail is at the other end of the same four hundred yards and is not hers, and it has nothing on it.** Her figure is on no paper, has gone up, and does not get a heading. **The man in the felt apron resists her and says the one true thing he has carried for four years: a sheet came down that passage once in four years and went back up, and nobody has ever asked her about the going back up, and the going back up is the half that has a person in it, and there is no form in which they could.** He adds, once, that a blank is the only paper in this empire with nothing printed over it, that there is no rule on that passage about one, and that the reason there is no rule is that no rule was ever needed. She does not ask where it went back up to. **Nothing with a heading on it comes down that week, the not coming is not a withholding, a week in which nothing arrives is not a week in which anything has been withheld, and she loses most of a month in between the two while standing at a lock.** The four hundred yards is not walked and the woman of about forty with a scuttle is not in that passage.

**0409 — The Corner Of A Window With No Pane In It, A Woman Who Came Up Those Stairs Once This Week, And A Man Who Was Not Sent Anything.** **Marek Kest, thirty-eight, in person, third of the three.** The room taken by the week, referred to and not described again; the window corner where the cold is worst, the four feet of wall still bare, the nail still in it. **Nothing has been sent to him and nothing has ever been sent to him, and he has not asked anybody for anything and nobody has asked him anything.** The woman who keeps the ground floor comes up once with nothing in her hands and gives him the plainest statement of his position anybody has made to him: **there is no form in this empire for a man to be given a piece of paper, not because the paper does not exist but because being given a thing is being made a party to it, and the reason nothing has ever been sent to him is that nothing has ever been sent to anybody, and a man nobody sends anything to is a man nobody can be shown anything about, which he already knew and has now been told.** He does not ask her anything. He moves the chair out of the middle of that floor to under the window corner and sits down in it. No blank arrives, the volume's smallest thing does not happen, and the arrangement is still standing.

**0410 — The Fourth Of Four Items On A Fee Board, A Sentence Shaped Like A Question, And The Counter Shutting At The Sixth Hour.** Marn Ottery, thirty-four, and this is the last chapter of the batch and it is not a close. The fourth of the four items on the board has been there since the third hour and has not been asked about. Three people buy a blank at fourpence in that morning and are asked nothing and say nothing. **Marn Ottery says out loud that the box is not the supply and that she has never been able to find out where the case is filled and has stopped trying.** **Tamsin Rook says the thing a person says when she has been early for two years for a reason she has not been told, and it is a question-shaped sentence with no mark at the end of it: and in eleven years you have not once told me what the fourth of those four is for. Marn Ottery does not treat it as a question and does not answer it, and tells her instead that a person who is early for a reason is a person who has already been used and has not been told, that the not doing anything about it was the using and not a courtesy to her, and that the not being told is the part that cannot be put back.** Tamsin Rook says she is now the fourth item on that board and has been for two years. Marn Ottery says she has done the one thing in that room in eleven years she had promised not to do, at the fourth hour on an ordinary morning, with a witness, and is not sorry. **The counter shuts at the sixth hour. Nobody was thanked, nobody was forgiven, nobody was sent for, nothing was resolved, and the count of things asked is the same as it was on the first morning of the batch. The box is not opened, the drawer is not opened, and the arrangement is still standing.**
