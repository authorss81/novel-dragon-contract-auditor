A room over a chandler's shop with a table, a chair, a window about a foot wide, a nail in about four feet of bare wall and nothing on it, the second day of the second week of the twelfth month, the last chapter of the volume, and the man of about thirty-four, who did a thing four weeks ago and said a figure out loud once, two and a half weeks ago, and is not going to say it again. **He heard about the store four hundred yards off a bay on the third day of this week at second hand and then at second hand's second hand — a man with a chain who cannot read a paragraph, then a man of about twenty-eight in a bay, then a man of about thirty at a gate — and he did not go and look, because a man who goes and finds out what came of a thing he has heard about is a man who has been findable by the hearing of it.** And then he got to the part that took the rest of the afternoon: **there is a practice, and a practice is a count of things done and not of people, and there are two of it in now, about four hundred and thirty miles apart, with no list and no form and no heading and no fund, and nobody in either building knows that the other one exists, and neither has said the other's name out loud.** One of them is a man of about thirty-four with a fair hand; the other is a man of thirty-eight with a labour a gate can refuse and a mark about three inches long that reads nothing and decides nothing and does not know where the ford is, and the wage is the price. **And about nine hundred entries a year still come in under a heading, a single return goes to one district, this empire has about nine hundred districts, and nobody has worked out how a thing is asked in about nine hundred buildings at once by people who cannot be instructed to ask it.** The two people in the boxes are in the boxes; the question in the second of the eleven books is still a question and is not an answer and is not an order; nobody was thanked, including the woman of about forty with a scuttle and the man of about thirty from the second gang and his wife. About nineteen people are still behind four hundred and forty foot of bank and a guarantee is still standing offered and unanswered with about sixty children under sixteen inside it and no child in it has a name anybody has said. **A bill at Lowcross stands at nineteen pounds three and fourpence and is unpaid and nobody is liable, and he did not pay it and he is not the reason it is unpaid.** The four who cannot read a paragraph are unasked. The two documents stay two documents and neither is named. The office of a seat is a document and not an office and its holder is not named. **Nobody has worked out who it is, and the number is seven, and a count that counted people would be six, and that is the whole of the difference.** The lamp is not lit and he does not light it.
