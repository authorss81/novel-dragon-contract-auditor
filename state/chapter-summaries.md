**Chapter 344 (1,205) — the counting room at the back of a dye end, a stove let go out, sixteen books, and a wage she is not going to ask the reason for.** Nell Kest, twenty, alone in the room all day. **The new beat in this chapter is that she is not going to ask the man who holds her wage what it is held against, and the reason is the volume's own: asking him would make the two of them parties, and a party is the only person a thing can be told what it is going to be used for, and the answer would be a record.** She is one of the people nobody has asked and there is nobody she can put one to. The two women she cannot reach are still two she cannot reach. The sixteenth book is not given to anybody and nobody has offered her a key. The notice in a coat is not sent. She is not going up this river this season and that is not the same as never, and she is the only person who knows it.

**Chapter 345 (1,117) — a house with a door on it four miles off, four steps, a woman at the bottom of them, and a man who is not going to tell her where he is going.** The man of about thirty-four is going to walk about nine miles on a day he is not paid for to a room at the end of a cold passage, and he has been in that room once, and he is not going to tell her. **The six weeks comes back in its own words and is not re-derived: the last time he did a thing of that kind he told her six weeks before he did it, out loud, on purpose, without asking her first, and the reason he gave her is still the truest thing he has ever said to her.** This time he is not telling her at all, and the not telling is the same absence as the not asking and is not a better way of doing anything. A woman of about thirty-five next door is unspoken to for about four lines and is a different person from every other woman of about thirty-five on the page. The bill for being findable is not his and stands on somebody else's account.

**Chapter 346 (1,425) — the room at the far end of the passage, a rail four feet off the floor, and nine minutes with a coat open and a hand that never went to the rail.** The second visit and not the third, on his own two feet, on a day he is not paid for. He says the reason out loud, standing, in about nine sentences, to the man of sixty-one and to a clerk of about fifty-five and to about four other people, and it is the finding: a question at that rail goes onto the shelf the sheets go on, a question anywhere else has to be carried to him, there are about nine of about nine hundred a year that are not as the person above wrote and he would tell the truth if anybody asked him, there is nowhere for a man to mark a disagreement, a question is a party, a party is a person, a person is one, about nine hundred of these go out of that room a year, what is available at that size is a class, a class is a heading, a heading is a company, and therefore a question cannot be asked in about nine hundred buildings at once by people who cannot be instructed to ask it. **The man of sixty-one does not answer and is not asked and is not thanked. The woman with the scuttle says nothing. The clerk writes none of it. The room does not have it afterwards and he does not tell anybody what was said in it.**

**Chapter 347 (1,089) — the ground-floor room at the top end of the same passage, a carrier up the flags, and four things entered correctly in a hand that does not vary.** Four days after the nine minutes. The question on her shelf is still a question and carries no period.

**Chapter 348 (1,221) — the top of a stair in Auremar with no other way up, a fee board of four items, and a box of about four hundred bought and unfilled blanks under the counter.** Tamsin Rook, twenty-four, two years at the fourth counter. **Chapter 349 (1,153) — the room over a chandler's shop, four steps, a table with nothing on it, and a lamp not lit at about the fifth hour.** Three days after the nine minutes. **He does not tell her what was said in that room and she is at the bottom of the four steps and does not ask him how it went, and there is no form for refusing to be told a thing and none for being entitled to insist on it.** He has a number in his head that is made up of people and not of sheets, and he cannot give it away because the giving of it would be a list and a list of persons is a company. He puts his hand flat on the table and the table has nothing on it and the lamp is not lit until about the seventh hour and is lit then by somebody else.
