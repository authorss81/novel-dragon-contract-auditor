# Chapter Summaries

Per-chapter detail for the rolling summary. Two to five sentences each. Newest batch last.

## Volume 01, Batch 0001 (Chapters 1–10) — The First Correction

### Chapter 0001 — The Summary

Late-afternoon inheritance docket in the Auremar Roll Hall. Marek finds a third witness notch on the Venn clerk's summary that does not exist on the witness copy, and a filed correction with no authority attached that substitutes "the present holder" for Aldren Venn's name. He refuses to certify from the summary, asks Tamsin Rook to make a private comparison copy, and reaches Pell for a countersignature on a slip withholding the summary as the courier leaves. Stage 0 only; he gains a preserved copy and a question, not authority.

### Chapter 0002 — The Work That Is Named

At the Reed Cut channel and the Venn farm, Tavi Venn works the gate linkage and refuses to discuss inheritance in the lane. Mara Venn, from the step and not the kitchen, gives her account: she witnessed the original, she never signed a continuation, and a young clerk brought a paper to the field in the sixth month that she refused. Sivra Oris states publicly at the gate that the field-side upkeep was Aldren's and the gate and navigation duty is hers, refusing both readings. Tamsin goes ahead to the court; the deadline is fixed: gates inspected on the eleventh, winter-seed notices issued on the fourteenth.

### Chapter 0003 — The Clerk's Window

Tamsin's POV at the Mosswake Roll Court. Ivet Sarn refuses to reopen settled precedent and gives her real reason, recorded by Tamsin with date, time, witnesses, and the two inches of hand, pinned up publicly. Tamsin indexes the received tray and finds an unfiled fourth-month Dunn summary carrying the same four lines, with Nessa Dunn, fifteen, in the surety line. Nessa has signed nothing and has been told at the gate that she is going to owe it. The Venn packet has already gone north with the summary and a withheld certification.

### Chapter 0004 — The Cabinet

Pell Vey at the Notaries' Table. He concedes the correction is probably bad and argues that reopening a flood-season file costs the district eleven hundred households a late winter; he offers to certify this week and compare in the ninth month. Tamsin puts the Dunn copy on the table openly rather than in a drawer, and Pell reads it three times. Marek leaves a written comparison request on the desk; a board notice then records an administrative hold on the Venn file under a sealed First Seat reference, not to be enforced, removed, or assigned.

### Chapter 0005 — The Next Household Worker

Nell's POV in the Greyfen dye-house. Ostyn Vare reads the continuation clause aloud, honestly, and the clause makes a departing worker's tool debt answerable by "the next household worker." Marek arrives intending to remove the document and is refused. Nell finds the Venn summary in his satchel, forces him to state what he knows, and demands the draft he has been hiding: an invalid proposed exception that recites a comparison he never made. Tamsin admits she found it in the wastebasket and refuses to work beside him until he discloses everything. Nell's terms: he shows her anything he writes before he writes it.

### Chapter 0006 — The Emergency Stair

Marek learns what the hold costs: it saves the field from seizure and forbids the Venns from assigning the crop, so the family cannot feed itself, and the field-side duty is being done unpaid by Tavi because no one else will touch it. Magistrate Leda Rell offers a closed order that would preserve the grain and release seed tonight, founded on the corrected file and without the family in the room. Marek asks for a public rehearing on the twelfth instead and accepts the cost. He hands Tamsin the whole file, including the draft, and asks Mara and Tavi to decide whether to testify; Tavi testifies about the linkage, Mara about the paper offered on the field.

### Chapter 0007 — The Order on the Board

Tamsin's POV on the morning of the hearing. She stops the court before the applicant opens by showing that the posted witness order is a third issue the register does not have, with the widow's name struck through in raking lamplight and the broker's certificate put in front of her. Pell objects that a copyist's memory is not evidence. Ivet Sarn produces the source leaf in a sealed sleeve under a court direction, and Rell enters the whole exhibit. Garren Pyle's certificate is shown to be a cold press mark, not a true seal, and to carry the copied third notch again. Rell issues a limited audit of the Venn file, named and witnessed, and warns that whatever she says this afternoon will be copied by clerks who do not know Marek's name.

### Chapter 0008 — The Sentence the Court Wrote

Marek argues from the leaf that the instrument names a person and requires a signature from anyone assuming a continuing duty. Rell refuses to narrow the sentence, because a signature asked for in February is not a signature, and rules at twenty past three: Tavi's personal debt void, the field preserved for the remaining three years and one flood season, Sivra Oris's gate and navigation duty preserved, a new signature required of any successor, and the bank works done under the water office's own authority. She also records the public-necessity sentence. Exercising the audit, Marek receives the first Roll-Answer, which is accurate about the record the court has just made and names the present holder of the field rather than the girl whose debt was voided; he pays in ash taste, counterpressure, a headache that persists, and a temporary inability to lie about one named obligation. Within the hour the registrar files the long-unfiled Dunn summary on the authority of the new sentence, and Nessa Dunn asks the bench whether she has just been made a debtor by another name.

### Chapter 0009 — What the Lane Made of It

Tamsin's POV over four days of public readings at a trestle table. The ruling mutates in the lane into "the holder pays," which is the opposite of the fourth term, and Tamsin corrects it in public and tells the lane to keep the copies in drawers rather than wait for her. Mara asks about the spring and gets an honest answer: three years, not security. Orra Dunn is afraid a hearing will expose the whole household, and receives the date of the twenty-third and a separate sitting requested in the register. Nell reads her own clause aloud, signs a new wage schedule with a visible exit clause that the guild has not yet accepted, and sits on the third stool. Odile Fenn refuses to examine Tavi while a guarantor claim is open. Tamsin finds a faded red running stitch with a sequence of small marks in the margin of one of the public copies, taken from a tin under an old lane-man's bed, and states plainly that she does not know what it is. A court runner summons them: the exhibit's chain is being opened, and a sealed First Seat page with a thread in its fold has been slipped in behind the memorandum.

### Chapter 00010 — The Form

Marek arrives in Auremar five days after the reading with the headache still in his skull and the ash taste still in his mouth, and finds that he can no longer lie about the guarantor, which he proves twice on a quay. Under a joint order, Ruhl Dunnet states that he may take a person but not a shop, and only once the paper is safe, so Tamsin inventories Corvin Dray's office over two days with the eleven waiting clients sent out to the yard. The ledger is a price list: sixty-one copied certificates and ninety-eight sold witness sequences at four shillings, across Venn, Dunn, and two provincial files at Glasswater and Emberwatch. Corvin admits selling a mark and a date, refuses to name the four clients who write the sentences, and offers to restore the Venn file if the comparison is closed; Marek refuses. The chain reveals a four-paragraph printed form with a series number and a print run requiring flood-season continuity filings to be entered in the routine column and not itemised. Dunnet arrests Corvin only after the ledger is copied. Marek files a hand-signed nine-paragraph audit request naming the form and asking for twenty years of source files to be preserved, and Examinations takes custody of it without deciding scope; the intake clerk writes the file number on Tamsin's copy in ink and initials it.
