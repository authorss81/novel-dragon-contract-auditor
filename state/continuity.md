## One, the movement's own smallest thing, and it is a refusal in ordinary use that somebody has to live inside

**The refusal is in ordinary use in five rooms and it is refused in practice by somebody who has to live with having refused it, and there is no form anywhere in this empire in which any of it can be entered.** It is on the page in ten small acts, in ten rooms, by ten people, and it is said out loud in nine mouths and it is a right in none of them, and that is the whole of the difference between what the sixth movement spent and what the seventh movement is made of.

**One, the ground floor of a rented house.** A coin put on a tread in the fiftieth week is back in an apron in the fifty-first, because a place that stays a place for a week is a place a person can be shown standing on twice, and a woman of about thirty-five has put a sheet of paper down on the top step beside a tin of nine years of a man's rent and gone out at the front door, **and the woman at the foot of that stair has twenty years of being at the foot of it and is not going to spend them on four foot of passage, so the sheet is going to sit on that top step for the rest of the time she is there and she is going to look at it every morning and is not going to touch it.**

**Two, the counter at the top of four flights.** A line entered a year and more ago has been copied, and the copy has no name at the top of it and no date and no heading, **because a name at the top of a copy puts a person in it and a person in a copy is a person who can be pointed at**, and it is being carried down four flights in the front of an apron to a person in a room the woman of twenty-four has never been in who will not be able to find out who made it.

**Three, the building off the old river road.** A man of about sixty-one who signs about nine hundred sheets a year, and has been signing with his other hand since a spring that is gone and cannot say why, **has this month refused to put that other hand on the second of a week's minutes**, and the second goes down that lane with nothing at the foot of it, and a woman of fifty-three walked the whole length of that corridor to say one sentence: that it goes in her hand or it does not go at all.

**Four, the lock end of about four hundred yards of cold flags.** A tin of oil that two people have each moved has been carried back nine foot by the man who moved it the first time and set down square to the edge, **because a thing that has been moved once is an accident and a thing that has been moved twice is a place, and a person can be shown standing in a place, and what is there instead is a thing two people can be shown having picked up and put back, which is worse.**

**Five, the shed at the end of the lane off the Slade.** A count is two out and there is a space at the foot of the sheet for the name of the person who counted, **and a foreman of fifty-one has put the number in and left the space empty on purpose, because a name in a column is a party, and the only way to find out how many is to walk ninety foot of her own floor and stand at the end of the bench where she said a true thing about a girl, and she will not do it, and so the number goes down the lane wrong with her name not on it.**

**And the movement's smallest completed act, said once and not restated: a person who refuses an ordinary thing in an ordinary room is a person who cannot be shown to have refused it and cannot be shown not to have refused it, and the price is that she has to be in that room the next morning, and there is nothing in nine hundred buildings that lets her be anywhere else.**
