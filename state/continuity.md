## Contract Rules

- Ordinary plain and scale contracts lose force when terms, assent, witness, record, or continuation is missing; a defect does not automatically void every ordinary contract.
- A foundational compact binds only named ratifying powers to publicly stated duties. It cannot create a perpetual office, inherited debt, or beneficiary for an unnamed future population.
- The Cinder Clause is an unlawful later proxy practice, not a new compact or a sentient mechanism. Its use can be preserved as evidence and may ground restitution for harm, but it cannot enlarge the Compact or create a new inherited duty by itself.

## Rules Established On The Page In Batch 0001

These are recorded because Chapters 1–10 turned planning into text, not because they are new mechanics.

- **The summary is the door.** A summary is what a later file copies from. Compressing a packet is ordinary practice and is where an alteration survives; a court acting on a summary acts on the copy, not on the source leaf. The Chapter 8 reading is the proof of this in the reader's hands.
- **A witness mark is a device with a copy in it.** A notch in the left margin records who was standing in the witness line. Two means two were heard. A third is cut at a different angle because the third person stood differently. A notch that is cleaner than the original, or at the wrong height, is a copied notch. Third notches were sold as a service, at a fixed height, for a fixed fee.
- **A true seal is laid, not pressed.** A true seal is made from a scale impression, spoken assent, and a witness record; it takes about eleven seconds and the holder is present throughout. Its rim is uneven. A cold press mark in a workshop with a screw press is a copied seal, and a copied image is not a true seal. This is how Corvin's certificate is broken.
- **A general paper can shelter a particular one.** A district flood memorandum that authorizes bank works and names no parcel is a real document, and anything filed under it in the following day inherits its standing. The memorandum dated the eighth of the eighth month and the correction received on the ninth of the eighth month are a day and a night apart, and the memorandum is the schedule, not the authority.
- **A form with a run on it is not a person.** *Continuity Filings, Flood Seasons, Categorisation* requires a flood-season continuity filing to be entered in the routine column and not itemised or heard individually except on quarterly review. It does nothing and creates nothing. It arranges that a class of correction arrives already inside a rule. Its print run implies every court that files a flood docket, which is the Volume 01 volume-level question and is not answered in this batch.
- **A public-necessity sentence travels by being quoted.** A court that needs to act fast in a flood will say a general thing, and clerks in a hurry will copy it. The sentence is the first portable institutional wording of the inheritance echo; the copied Dunn summary shows the vehicle before the court supplies the authority; the authority then makes it operative. This is the mechanism Marek's narrow win turns into a second household.
- **A reading is only evidence once a recognized body admits the record.** The Chapter 8 reading was taken against the exhibit as the court had just amended it, with the ruling in the same sleeve, and it was entered in the minute book and copied into the widow's hand by Sarn's direction on Marek's request.
- **Ordinary competence defeats a forger.** The witness order was exposed by memory plus a register that lacked a third issue plus a raking lamp. The broker's missing leaves were exposed by a docket count and a boy's habit. The file number was preserved by a young intake clerk writing it in ink and initialling it. None of this is magic and all of it wins cases.
- **A duty pending full review is a duty.** A postponement is enforceable enough for a creditor to name, to hold security against, and to put a number in a demand on, and the demand does not wait for the review. This is why a postponement can be worse for a household than a refusal, and why Rell says so in open court on the twenty-third.
- **A public register of pending duties is a real and cheap protection.** Kept at the Mosswake clerk's window in Sarn's hand, with a date, a household, a duty, the next review, and a shilling for a copy. It protects nobody by itself and it puts every named household where creditors, neighbours, and other clerks can read.
- **A public reading table becomes a lane institution, and the lane uses the ruling without being taught.** The rota is four named readers on a levy sheet nailed to the table leg, with a chalk rule that a paper may be brought there and read out loud and no advice given. In Chapter 18 a child of seven is sent to ask for the fourth term to be read at a house before a paper is signed.
- **A clerk may admit an exhibit on her own authority, and a copy cannot be recalled.** A district court's clerk does not need the magistrate to admit an exhibit in a matter of record; exhibits are copied to the parties on the day of admission; a copy that has gone to a party cannot be un-copied, and an order striking the exhibit is an order about the *use* and not an erasure. This is the mechanism Tamsin uses in Chapter 19.
- **A small truthful number is more effective than a big lie.** The First Seat form is a quarterly *return* with four boxes. It does not say what the answers are and it does not tell anybody to write a zero. It works because the first paragraph puts flood-season continuity filings in a routine column, so the honest answer is nearly always a very small number. Hallis Dren answered one and yes, honestly, on the eleventh of the tenth month, and sent it four hundred miles in a sealed sleeve, and does not know what he did. **No character in the story knows this yet.**
- **A model circular is a form, and a form travels without anybody wanting it to.** MC/FS is the proof: a good clause drafted for one household becomes a numbered form that is signed by frightened people in four houses on a lane and then travels to four hundred and forty miles of furnace towns. Marl Crenn says it on the page in Chapter 15.


## Succession and the Cinder Proxy

- The original First Custodian is a human, temporary public office, limited to seven years, with no enforceable inherited asset or right at appointment. Halden Ro is the sitting lawful Custodian in the final crisis; his term expires during the convocation.
- The office is chosen at a public succession convocation by at least five of nine human provinces, five of nine dragon Seats, and three of six independent civic assemblies. The First Seat may call a convocation but cannot appoint alone.
- During a vacancy, the Emberroot Interim Keepers are two human record keepers, one dragon notary, and one civic witness selected by the affected local court, recognized dragon seat, and nearest civic assembly. They may maintain existing duties and immediate relief for no more than forty-five days, but they cannot create or transfer a perpetual duty, appoint a successor, or invoke the Cinder proxy route. The First Seat alone cannot select all of them or fill the office.
- The Cinder route pairs a First Seat seal with a human who has a recorded inherited claim, names the human a proxy guarantor, and claims control behind the proxy. Seryn Oris cannot hold the original human office; she seeks permanent First-Seat control behind a controlled proxy.
- After Halden's term expires, the First Custodian is vacant. Only a new 5/5/3-of-6 convocation could appoint a temporary successor; the final Covenant records that none will be appointed and transfers the maintenance function to rotating Roll Assemblies and a bounded Interim Continuity Council.

- The later correction substitutes “the present holder” for the named signer and routes Aldren's winter-seed debt to the field's current occupant. The substituted sentence on the page reads, in part, that the field-side upkeep and the winter-seed repayment, “being a work of the ground and not of a person,” shall be held against the field, the present holder answering for the same “until full review, the named signer having ceased.”
- The original leaf, witness marks, ink, seal pressure, and filing sequence prove the correction was added later. Tamsin preserves a comparison copy. Established on the page: the leaf carries Aldren's double-reed shorthand, two ragged notches, an embossed signature, and the requirement that anyone assuming a continuing duty sign a new obligation. The back of the correction leaf carries a certificate impression pressed flat and even and a fee entry naming a broker at Candlewright Lane in Auremar, which Marek has on the page in Chapter 4 and cannot yet use.

(2) The channel and lower field are preserved to Aldren's household until the second month, when seven years from the signing are up, against any creditor, tenant, surety, or assignee. (3) Sivra Oris's gate and navigation duty is unaffected. (4) **"The fourth term" is the new-signature rule:** any person who thereafter assumes a continuing field-side duty at that place must sign a new obligation for it, the original instrument requiring it. (5) **"The fifth term" is the district works:** the district water office must perform the bank works and the gate reset under its own flood authority and not under the instrument, and the household is not required to perform the field-side upkeep for the purposes of the order. Later batches must not call these three terms and four terms; there are five, and the lane already reads them aloud by number.

She told Marek not to be thanked for it in front of the lane, because a magistrate who is said to have been fetched by a girl with an iron bar will be waited for and not found. This is the beat that Chapter 11 must collect: Tavi foresees, out loud, that a girl who has cut a ditch unpaid for five months will be read as having accepted the duty the fourth term requires a signature for.
- The court's broad public-necessity sentence is applied to Orra and Nessa Dunn: “Where immediate public necessity requires a recorded continuation to prevent loss of food or shelter, the present holder of the burdened property may be joined as guarantor until full review.” The Dunn summary is the vehicle; the sentence supplies the authority that makes it operative. This is the Volume 1 mini-arc reversal and the first visible inheritance echo. The formal volume midpoint arrives in Chapters 19–25, when the sentence is traced to a higher administrative template. **On the page:** the sentence is read aloud at two o'clock, and at two o'clock, in the same minute, the Mosswake registrar enters and files the previously unfiled Dunn summary on the authority of it. The five operative terms are not ruled until twenty past three. The reversal is therefore slightly worse than a later batch might assume: the summary was made operative by the sentence **before** the court finished ruling on the file the sentence was written for.
- The Venn instrument was signed in the **second month of the year four years back** and runs seven years from that month, so it expires in the second month. It entered its fourth year when Aldren died, which left three years and one flood season of balance at his death; the batch falls in the ninth month, so rather less than that remains by the ruling. **The ruling does not state a duration. It preserves the field until the second month, when seven years from the signing are up.** Later batches must use the date, not a counted balance, and must not say that the Venns have three years in front of them. “For the fixed term” is therefore a real limit, not a formality.
- **The Sallow finding.** Ivet Sarn's four days are now a dated, named, documented fact and not a boast. The file was an upper-district barn term in the fourth month of the **fifth year**; its summary carried the substituted wording **word for word**; a minor of the household was in the surety line; Rennie Sallow came to the window twice on the fourth day and once on the fifth and was told the docket was not closed and not why; Sarn bought the seed at eleven pounds over the price; and Sarn told him herself, in her own words, on the thirtieth of the ninth month, at the top of the upper lane, with Marek Kest standing at the bottom of the lane for five hours and not permitted up. The record carries the reason and the consequence together, entered on the **fourth of the tenth month**. The wording is four *years* older than the Venn correction, which is the volume's standing finding: this is a practice, not a fraud, and there is no wrong party in the room for a minute book to rule on.
- **The ruling's stated limits, which later batches must respect:** the ruling decides the Venn file and nothing else. It does not void the correction, does not open other files, and does not name who wrote the sentence. Its reach is exactly terms four and five — the new signature and the district works — and neither of those is a general rule for other files.

## The Two Documents: The Mechanism, Established In Chapters 16 And 20

Canon. Later batches must not merge them, must not make one the child of the other, and must not find a document connecting them that does not exist on the page.

**Document one, the circular — the words.**
*Model Endorsements for Continuity Filings (Flood Seasons).* Series **MC/FS**. Sealed by the **Standing Office of Provincial Continuities**, a standing office of this Chancery whose authority is not established anywhere on the page. Distribution line: *to all district courts and landing offices holding a flood docket, to be filed with the district's standing instructions and not with its papers.* Base issue of the **second month of this year**, run **five hundred and sixty**. Amendment sheet dated the **twenty-second of the ninth month**, run **nine hundred and forty**, which is ten days after the Mosswake ruling and which carries a form of words in which the third line is the public-necessity sentence **verbatim**, under a note reading *Adopted from a provincial court, the ninth month. A court may adopt this wording or any other.* The model endorsement carries blanks for the district, the instrument, the ground and the person. It lives in the **Forms Received rack** in the passage behind the third gallery, which is unlocked and indexed by the sending office rather than by what the things are. Tamsin has pulled thirty-one sheets; twenty-two carry the four lines; the oldest is older than the ninth year before last; the first issue is not in the building.

**Document two, the First Seat form — the counting.**
*Continuity Filings, Flood Seasons, Categorisation.* Four paragraphs, sealed with the old fire-and-water device of the First Seat, no office name, issued this year. Paragraph 1: a flood-season continuity filing shall be entered in the routine column and not itemised, for the purpose of the quarterly return. Paragraph 2: it shall not be itemised, scheduled, or heard individually except on quarterly review. Paragraph 3: the court is not obliged to examine such filings individually, and no request by an affected person for individual hearing shall of itself require departure. Paragraph 4: it is not a direction as to validity and confers no authority and takes none away. **It is a permission, not a crime, and its correct use is administrative.** It contains no model wording and does not need to.

**How the two work together without touching.** The circular supplies the sentence in a form a clerk can fill in. The form supplies the reason not to look at the filing. A small truthful number on a quarterly return is more useful to the machine than a big lie, because a lie gets caught. What joins them is a practice across two offices in two buildings four hundred miles apart, and there is **no document anywhere in the empire that says the First Seat issued the form because the office printed the circular**, and no character knows that both exist except Marek Kest and Tamsin Rook.

**Ages, which must not be confused.** The four substituted lines (the *present holder* wording) are in the Sallow file in the fourth month of the **fifth year** and are in the series long before that. The public-necessity sentence was stated on the **twelfth of the ninth month** and printed on the **twenty-second of the ninth**. No chapter may put the sentence in an older file, and no chapter may put the four lines in a newer form than the one already traced.

## Current Characters and Power

- Marek opened at Stage 0. He is still Stage 0 plus an unstable first Seam-Sight, earned on the page in Chapter 8. **He performs no magic anywhere in Chapters 11–20.** The cost of the one reading has been paid in full: the headache went on the twenty-sixth of the tenth month, and the temporary inability to lie about one named obligation **lapsed on the page** in Chapter 18 at the fourth hour, in a lane, to Wenna Sallow, and is described as a lapse and not a cure. It was used against him at least three times before it lapsed — the woman with the soup pot at the gate rail on the twentieth, Hallow Parrow in the corridor on the twenty-third, and Tamsin in the alcove in the eleventh month of the ninth — and the last of those was used as a substitution rather than a silence. He does not revisit Perrin Ashe, and the reason given is that there is nothing to pay a penny for. He minds the loss of the shield more than the loss of the headache, and says so. That inability is on the page three times. The first is in Chapter 9, in the Venn yard: Mara asks whether anything left in the file can come back for them, he opens his mouth to give her the sentence that would let her sleep, and it does not come, and Tamsin answers in his place and writes the unanswered question in her own book. The second is on the Auremar quay on the seventeenth, where he opens his mouth to say it to a stranger and nothing comes. The third is on the quay on the eighteenth, where there are four things he would like to say and may say only the one he is permitted to, so the limitation is a substitution rather than a silence. It is not a collapse and it is not free. No counter-seal and no Stage 2 ability appears in Volume 1.
- The first Seam-Sight is shallow. Marek can see one active chain and its immediate beneficiary. He cannot identify who first introduced it, and he has said so out loud rather than guessing. As established in Chapter 10, the red stitch and the thread in the form's fold are two facts of the same colour and are explicitly not joined in any sentence, in the record or in speech, until there is evidence.
- A reading can be accurate and still wrong: it reports the present legal effect of the record in front of the auditor, so an altered or incomplete record produces a correct answer about a false document. Volume 1 opened on exactly this gap and Chapter 8 closed it in the other direction, which is the point.
- The Chapter 8 panel reads: `Named: the present holder of the Venn field. Promised: the present holder may be joined as guarantor until full review. Carried after death: the record carries a guarantor duty to whoever occupies the field unless a new hearing ends it.` It does not name Tavi Venn, whose personal debt the same ruling voids. The reversal depends on that: the file now reaches whoever occupies the field, and the same sentence travels to the Dunn summary.
- The reading was entered in the Mosswake minute book and copied into the widow's hand by Sarn's direction, on Marek's request in open court. Tamsin holds a copy. It is a public record, not a private fact, and Marek insisted on that at the time.
- A reading reports the record, not the world. The case in Volume 1 is still carried by material comparison, witness order, ink, seal pressure, and custody.
- Court orders are not Marek's exception. Magistrate Rell and the Mosswake court issued their own interim, closed-door, and final orders in Volume 1, including the closed order preserving the Venn crop and releasing seed that she offered on the tenth and never issued, the five operative terms, and the public-necessity sentence; those place no burden on Marek's body and are never his reading. What does appear in the Roll-Answer is not a court order but the **filed record as it stands after** the court acts on it, and the public-necessity sentence is part of that record, which is exactly why the reading reports the guarantor promise the sentence supplies. That is the whole of the rule: a reading reports the operative record, and the court's own gloss is in the operative record. Marek writes no valid exception, counter-seal, or mixed warrant anywhere in Chapters 1–50. The word "exception" in Batch 0001 belongs only to the invalid draft in Chapter 5, which cannot be used: it was written by a clerk with no such power, it recites a comparison the clerk never made, and it names a party who was never heard.
- Tamsin is a roll-keeper whose ordinary copying, counting, and memory preserve evidence; she is not a magical auditor, has no power of any kind, and has independent professional judgment that has twice overruled Marek's.
- Nell is a dye-house worker who wants paid craft independence and refuses to be protected by concealment. Her exit clause is amended, named, and accepted in Chapter 15, and the amendment is a numbered form that four houses on her lane have signed or will sign under fear. She now runs the reading table and has been told about Ilyra.
- Pell is a mentor-shaped institutional antagonist, not the final enemy. In the yard behind the Chancery on the fourth day of the tenth month he asked Tamsin how many of thirteen protected people had the form, was refused, said he did not know the form existed and would not have acted on it, and gave her his own worry about a print run. Tamsin wrote it all down, underlined one sentence of it, and has still not told Marek. Corvin is the first concrete criminal and a valuable witness, and he remains accountable for his own crimes.
- Tamsin is the only slow-burn romantic relationship; no romance begins in Volume 1. In this batch she moves from the colleague who discloses by agreement to the colleague who decides and reports, and then to a reciprocal arrangement in which Marek signs what is made in his own name and she strikes out of it what will not do.


## Ending Lock

Seryn Oris (distinct from Sivra Oris, the Volume 1 water keeper) remains the final antagonist and survives constrained and accountable. Ilyra remains dead; her record and consequences are restored. Halden Ro's First Custodian term expires, the office remains vacant, and no temporary successor is appointed after the transition. The final image is an ordinary local hearing with two public copies of a record. No new cosmic threat, secret dragon heir, or replacement final enemy is introduced.
