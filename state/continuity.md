## Contract Rules

- Ordinary plain and scale contracts lose force when terms, assent, witness, record, or continuation is missing; a defect does not automatically void every ordinary contract.
- A foundational compact binds only named ratifying powers to publicly stated duties. It cannot create a perpetual office, inherited debt, or beneficiary for an unnamed future population.
- The Cinder Clause is an unlawful later proxy practice, not a new compact or a sentient mechanism. Its use can be preserved as evidence and may ground restitution for harm, but it cannot enlarge the Compact or create a new inherited duty by itself.

## Rules Established On The Page In Batch 0003

Recorded because Chapters 21–30 turned the midpoint into institutions rather than findings, not because they are new mechanics.

- **A review date with no named person is not a review.** This is the volume's midpoint answer in one line, and it is what Marek accepts in place of a certified rule. A rule is survivable if a person is accountable for running the hearings under it and a date is fixed for looking at it again. A sentence with nobody under it is not a rule and is not safe; it is forty-six clerks each deciding alone.
- **A refusal can be a public act.** What makes it one is that the terms offered, the person who offered them, the person who declined, the date, and a witness are all entered, and the decliner signs the record of the refusal. The first version of Marek's reply — *the applicant does not accept and has no further observation* — was three sentences of sulking and would have read as a clerk who would not answer.
- **A standing office can have a seal, a series and a run and no person.** The **Standing Office of Provincial Continuities** is such an office. Its sheets are *issued*, not signed. The authority for the series is a line at the head of a district's flood standing instructions: *Issued under the standing authority of the Standing Office of Provincial Continuities… Printed forms of this series are issued under the same authority and no further authority is required for their use in a district holding a flood docket.* The authority for the circular is the drawer the circular is in, because the distribution line says the series is filed with the standing instructions and not with the papers. This is the volume's answer to whether the local precedent was invented or **issued**: issued, traceable to a series and a date and not to a man, and unanswerable to question three because there is nobody to name.
- **A procedure with no person standing at it is not a procedure.** The finding is that the new process is safe only while a named clerk is at a window; if the window shuts for the middle of the day a person's ask evaporates rather than failing, and she will not come back because nobody wrote to her.
- **The absence of a number is the entry.** A filing entered in the routine column gets no number, and a thing that gets no number cannot be found by anybody who has not got the book in front of them. In Chapter 30 the instrument that removed a cooper's gauge from a shop bench is a creditor's filled-in copy of the printed model, stamped by the landing office into the routine column in the tenth month, and the only record of it is a line in a clerk's book reading *guarantee, shop goods, bond, and the name of a district office.* The stamp is the whole crime and it took about four seconds.
- **A form with a run on it is a model; a form with a room on it is an instruction.** The Mosswake four-box form has **no number, no series, no seal and no office at the head of it.** The *entry in the book* is numbered, not the form. This is the deliberate structural opposite of MC/FS and it is why it can be used the next day and cannot be issued empire-wide.
- **A question asked of a paper is a tool, and tools travel.** The **four questions** in the lane are: *Is there work in it? Whose name is on it — yours, or a word that walks about, like the holder? If you stop, whose name answers for it? Who read this to you, and do they get paid if you sign?* The fourth is the one that decides whether the other three can be trusted, and it came from a woman of about seventy at the seed barns who would not give her name. Within three weeks of it going onto a chalk board it was in a letter from a second association in the next province and on the back of a bond receipt in a third district. The only difference from a printed form is that anybody may be asked about it.
- **A register of hearings is worth more than a register of duties, because it shows who came.** Both hang on the same board at the Mosswake clerk's window, in Sarn's two handwritings, at a shilling a copy.
- **A levy that buys a signature is a tax on doing nothing wrong.** The Greyfen craft levy of nine pounds a quarter falls hardest on the houses that took the amendment, and a house of forty years standing that has never had a dispute is being charged for a clause about a girl who leaves. Recalculated onto the four signing houses on paper, and the assessor who agreed it says in advance that it will be overruled within a year.

## Rules Established On The Page In Batch 0005

Recorded because Chapters 41–50 closed the volume's local promise and produced its route. These are the rules of the closing batch and a later batch may not soften them.

- **A person must be heard in a room and must sign before a guarantor duty attaches.** The Mosswake Roll Court said it on the twenty-first of the fifth, and the Cinder Court said it on the seventeenth of the sixth in a different form and binding on itself and on any court that takes a recognition from it: **no court in this empire may join a person as guarantor on the strength of a printed model of general wording without that person being heard and signing.**
- **A printed sentence is a record of a document and is not a record of a law.** The sentence of the twelfth of the ninth, as printed in the MC/FS amendment sheet of the twenty-second, is **model wording of general application and is not a decision of any court**, and may not be the recorded reason for a decision in any district without a hearing of the person it names. It is not a precedent, no district is bound by it, and no district is permitted to use it as a reason. **The district that stated it does not withdraw it, because only that district can withdraw its own practice.**
- **A seat is not a party.** Where a document bears the seal of a seat and no name, the thing that would answer it is entered by its device, and **no person of or belonging to that seat is party, cited or examinable, and no inference of any kind may be drawn in that proceeding from the fact that a person has not been named.** The form was drawn in the reign of the third of the Ashfall line, is used about twice in a decade, and its operative line was read aloud in a hearing four times in nine years, three of them by a person who was relieved. **A court that declines to name a person is not thereby wrong, and this is the only rule in the compact's own procedure that does any work on a record of nothing but names.**
- **A sworn statement made once may be read into a higher court as a document without being cited**, because the rule that forbids it is against being put to the same question twice *in another matter*, and a referral on the same record is the same matter. What enters the record is its existence, date, court, form, two boxes, the blank second box entered as complete so, and the fact that the swearer spoke only to her own duty and named nobody. **That is a fact about the record and not about the oath.**
- **A court may name its own failure and pay for it.** The Mosswake court entered that between the third month of the sixth year and the sixth year it applied that sentence in letters and without a hearing to **about sixty households in this district**, that it does not know their names and cannot get them in time, and that they are not before it and are not represented and have not asked to be. It also entered that **it will not order the district to do anything in place of the sentence, cannot, and that the district will have to work out in the sixth month what it does in a flood when it cannot use it** — a cost named so that nobody can say afterwards it was not known. **Those sixty households are owed a thing in Volume 02 or later and nobody in this volume has yet asked them.**
- **A number on a document is a fact about the book the document was entered in.** If two documents carry the same number, one of them is in the wrong book, and the person who can say which book is the person who keeps the books. **The Caudle notice is not from the Venn series, carries no substituted wording and no public-necessity sentence, and nothing in the Forms Received rack connects any two documents.** It comes out of **AS/2, a craft assessors' instruction on entering a second instrument, second year of issue, being the third year of the sixth year back, run four hundred** — four years and four months older than the Venn correction, about tool debts in a dyers' trade, and the reason a box headed *the person who holds* exists at all. **This is a fact about a number and a date. It is not a finding about a seam and must not become one.**
- **A distributed record is not a controlled record.** Of forty-one names, **twenty-nine say yes to keeping a copy of the Cinder Court's record and twelve say no**, and the twelve are the finding: four with a creditor or landlord or factor's man in the house, three who cannot read well enough to know what they are agreeing to keep, two with a damp room, one who burned hers and said why in full, one who has had a man with a tray at her door twice this year, and one who has eleven hundred pounds of debt behind her and thinks keeping a copy is a thing you do if you are going to be proved right. **A copy of a decision is a weapon and it is a target**, because the households that kept it are the households that can be shown to have had notice.

The holders are **Bryn Mawer at a chandler's counter in Candlewright Lane, Ivet Sarn, Wendt Sayer in a Reed Cut works book, and Nell Kest.** There is still no fifth holder and the Greyfen road copyist is not one of them.
- **The transmission mechanism of the whole case is a person who turns up.** A trade with one door, told by a woman with a bad hat and a carriage clock that the door is optional, will find out in about a year that it has four doors; it will cost nothing, leave no series and no run and no office, and in nine years nobody will be able to say which of the four doors was opened by her. The Emberwatch rule is **still in force and is still at the second leaf**, and Bettin Rahl has written **the fifth question and his own answer under his own rule**, in his own hand, on a page nobody has read for thirty-one years. The fifth question is *what a house does when the assessor says no.*

## The Flood-Season Continuity Process, As Established On The Page

This is an institution created in Chapters 22–23. It is the court's, the water office's and a register's, and it places no burden on any body.

- A **flood-season continuity filing** in a district that adopts the process is not categorised away and is heard **individually on request**, at a window, in the person's own words.
- The ask is written down, and the hearing is entered in a **public register of hearings** kept at the Mosswake clerk's window, in Ivet Sarn's hand, on the same board as the register of pending duties, at a shilling a copy.
- The **witness is not the applicant** and is not owed anything in the matter. A household that cannot name one has one named for it from a list of people who have agreed in advance to witness, and that person is told in advance in writing, may refuse, and is not held to it for refusing. A five-line **witness order** exists for this.
- The **cost ceiling is eleven shillings for six weeks**, found by the district works out of the works fund, and it buys the running of the window and the paper and nothing else. Exceeding it **stops the process** rather than continuing it unpaid, and the register records the day it stopped and why. Sarn has stated in writing that a process which costs money it cannot pay in money stops quietly and that there is no person to complain to.
- The **review is the fourth of the fourth month.** The flood docket closes and the **rate is struck on the sixth of the fourth month**, two days later, and the last relief roll must be certified on the sixth because that is the day it is struck. The review is the fourth; the rate is the sixth. Do not merge them.
- **The trial's accounting, as established in Chapter 34 and entered on a public wall.** The trial ran **the first of the twelfth month to the twelfth of the first month** with the certification window deliberately shut, so it cost the district nothing in rolls. It produced **thirty-four hearings**, **a hundred and two hours** of a six-hour window, and **nine shillings and fourpence** of the eleven stated. **Four** people said the words *I do not understand this* and are entered as having said it, which is a complete answer and not a refusal; **eleven** of the other thirty signed something else within a month, and both facts sit in the same box. Twenty-nine hearings were inside the window, three were at the seventh hour, and **two were at the ninth hour at night for the same woman, who works the channel**, and the witness slip for them was filled in by the lamp-trimmer because there was nobody else in the building. Sarn states on the wall, in the first person and with the day on it, that she asked the roll-keeper the question about running it outside the window **so that the answer would be in somebody else's hand and not hers**, and that no household in the district waited for a seed parcel because of the six weeks.
- **The review, as held on the fourth of the fourth month.** The magistrate read the wall aloud in her own words and read the third paragraph twice. It created **no precedent**, is **not binding on any district including this one**, **compelled nothing**, and the district may or may not run the process next season and will decide that itself. It is over. The **only** thing it produced is that nine people in the district now know where a hearing can be asked for and every one of them can prove it happened.
- **The rate was struck whole on the sixth of the fourth month.** Four hundred and sixty relief rolls, four hundred and sixty parcels, four hundred and sixty households, all certified. Hallis Dren wrote **two hundred and eighty-four** at the foot of the rate book in his own hand on that day. The figure was a price and it was never incurred.
- The cost, priced by Hallis Dren and counted aloud by Tamsin Rook at Sarn's window: one hearing is **three hours** and a shilling and sixpence for a witness; the district has **fifty-two** such filings in the season; fifty-two hearings is **a hundred and fifty-six hours**, which is **twenty-six days** of a six-hour window. The window is **the last forty-two days before the rate is struck — the twenty-fifth of the second month to the sixth of the fourth** — at about **eleven** relief rolls a day, which is four hundred and sixty-two against a season of **four hundred and sixty**: two rolls of slack in the whole season. Dren has had the list of which two hundred and eighty-four in his head since the eighth month and has never shown it to anybody.

The arithmetic is therefore a price quoted in advance for a district that adopts the process and runs it inside a season, and every figure in it can be checked. **No chapter may state or imply that the two hundred and eighty-four households have already been delayed by the trial.**

## Rules Established On The Page In Batch 0001

These are recorded because Chapters 1–10 turned planning into text, not because they are new mechanics.

- **The summary is the door.** A summary is what a later file copies from. Compressing a packet is ordinary practice and is where an alteration survives; a court acting on a summary acts on the copy, not on the source leaf. The Chapter 8 reading is the proof of this in the reader's hands.
- **A witness mark is a device with a copy in it.** A notch in the left margin records who was standing in the witness line. Two means two were heard. A third is cut at a different angle because the third person stood differently. A notch that is cleaner than the original, or at the wrong height, is a copied notch. Third notches were sold as a service, at a fixed height, for a fixed fee.
- **A true seal is laid, not pressed.** A true seal is made from a scale impression, spoken assent, and a witness record; it takes about eleven seconds and the holder is present throughout. Its rim is uneven. A cold press mark in a workshop with a screw press is a copied seal, and a copied image is not a true seal. This is how Corvin's certificate is broken.
- **A general paper can shelter a particular one.** A district flood memorandum that authorizes bank works and names no parcel is a real document, and anything filed under it in the following day inherits its standing. The memorandum dated the eighth of the eighth month and the correction received on the ninth of the eighth month are a day and a night apart, and the memorandum is the schedule, not the authority.
- **A form with a run on it is not a person.** *Continuity Filings, Flood Seasons, Categorisation* requires a flood-season continuity filing to be entered in the routine column and not itemised or heard individually except on quarterly review. It does nothing and creates nothing. It arranges that a class of correction arrives already inside a rule. Its print run implies every court that files a flood docket, which is the Volume 01 volume-level question and is not answered in this batch.
- **A public-necessity sentence travels by being quoted.** A court that needs to act fast in a flood will say a general thing, and clerks in a hurry will copy it. The sentence is the first portable institutional wording of the inheritance echo; the copied Dunn summary shows the vehicle before the court supplies the authority; the authority then makes it operative. This is the mechanism Marek's narrow win turns into a second household.
- **A reading is only evidence once a recognized body admits the record.** The Chapter 8 reading was taken against the exhibit as the court had just amended it, with the ruling in the same sleeve, and it was entered in the minute book and copied into the widow's hand by Sarn's direction on Marek's request.
- **Ordinary competence defeats a forger.** The witness order was exposed by memory plus a register that lacked a third issue plus a raking lamp. The broker's missing leaves were exposed by a docket count and a boy's habit. The file number was preserved by a young intake clerk writing it in ink and initialling it. None of this is magic and all of it wins cases.
- **A duty pending full review is a duty.** A postponement is enforceable enough for a creditor to name, to hold security against, and to put a number in a demand on, and the demand does not wait for the review. This is why a postponement can be worse for a household than a refusal, and why Rell says so in open court on the twenty-third.
- **A public register of pending duties is a real and cheap protection.** Kept at the Mosswake clerk's window in Sarn's hand, with a date, a household, a duty, the next review, and a shilling for a copy. It protects nobody by itself and it puts every named household where creditors, neighbours, and other clerks can read.
- **A public reading table becomes a lane institution, and the lane uses the ruling without being taught.** The rota is four named readers on a levy sheet nailed to the table leg, with a chalk rule that a paper may be brought there and read out loud and no advice given. In Chapter 18 a child of seven is sent to ask for the fourth term to be read at a house before a paper is signed.
- **A clerk may admit an exhibit on her own authority, and a copy cannot be recalled.** A district court's clerk does not need the magistrate to admit an exhibit in a matter of record; exhibits are copied to the parties on the day of admission; a copy that has gone to a party cannot be un-copied, and an order striking the exhibit is an order about the *use* and not an erasure. This is the mechanism Tamsin uses in Chapter 19.
- **A small truthful number is more effective than a big lie.** The First Seat form is a quarterly *return* with four boxes. It does not say what the answers are and it does not tell anybody to write a zero. It works because the first paragraph puts flood-season continuity filings in a routine column, so the honest answer is nearly always a very small number. Hallis Dren answered one and yes, honestly, on the eleventh of the tenth month, and sent it four hundred miles in a sealed sleeve, and does not know what he did. **No character in the story knows this yet.**
- **A model circular is a form, and a form travels without anybody wanting it to.** MC/FS is the proof: a good clause drafted for one household becomes a numbered form that is signed by frightened people in four houses on a lane and then travels to four hundred and forty miles of furnace towns. Marl Crenn says it on the page in Chapter 15.

## The Two Documents: The Mechanism, Established In Chapters 16 And 20

Canon. Later batches must not merge them, must not make one the child of the other, and must not find a document connecting them that does not exist on the page.

**Document one, the circular — the words.**
*Model Endorsements for Continuity Filings (Flood Seasons).* Series **MC/FS**. Sealed by the **Standing Office of Provincial Continuities**, a standing office of this Chancery whose authority is not established anywhere on the page. Distribution line: *to all district courts and landing offices holding a flood docket, to be filed with the district's standing instructions and not with its papers.* Base issue of the **second month of this year**, run **five hundred and sixty**. Amendment sheet dated the **twenty-second of the ninth month**, run **nine hundred and forty**, which is ten days after the Mosswake ruling and which carries a form of words in which the third line is the public-necessity sentence **verbatim**, under a note reading *Adopted from a provincial court, the ninth month. A court may adopt this wording or any other.* The model endorsement carries blanks for the district, the instrument, the ground and the person. It lives in the **Forms Received rack** in the passage behind the third gallery, which is unlocked and indexed by the sending office rather than by what the things are. Tamsin has pulled thirty-one sheets; twenty-two carry the four lines; the oldest is older than the ninth year before last; the first issue is not in the building.

**Document two, the First Seat form — the counting.**
*Continuity Filings, Flood Seasons, Categorisation.* Four paragraphs, sealed with the old fire-and-water device of the First Seat, no office name, issued this year. Paragraph 1: a flood-season continuity filing shall be entered in the routine column and not itemised, for the purpose of the quarterly return. Paragraph 2: it shall not be itemised, scheduled, or heard individually except on quarterly review. Paragraph 3: the court is not obliged to examine such filings individually, and no request by an affected person for individual hearing shall of itself require departure. Paragraph 4: it is not a direction as to validity and confers no authority and takes none away. **It is a permission, not a crime, and its correct use is administrative.** It contains no model wording and does not need to.

**How the two work together without touching.** The circular supplies the sentence in a form a clerk can fill in. The form supplies the reason not to look at the filing. A small truthful number on a quarterly return is more useful to the machine than a big lie, because a lie gets caught. What joins them is a practice across two offices in two buildings four hundred miles apart, and there is **no document anywhere in the empire that says the First Seat issued the form because the office printed the circular**, and no character knows that both exist except Marek Kest and Tamsin Rook.

**Ages, which must not be confused.** The four substituted lines (the *present holder* wording) are in the Sallow file in the fourth month of the **fifth year** and are in the series long before that. The public-necessity sentence was stated on the **twelfth of the ninth month** and printed on the **twenty-second of the ninth**. No chapter may put the sentence in an older file, and no chapter may put the four lines in a newer form than the one already traced.

## Custody And Document Chain After Batch 0001

- **Where the source leaf lives.** The Venn source leaf is in the locked cabinet of the Notaries' Table at Auremar, along the north wall of the third gallery, and the key is on a chain around the neck of whichever supervisor is on duty. A certified packet sent to a district court carries the witness copy, not the leaf. This is canon and must not be contradicted: a district court has no power to open that cabinet, and a junior clerk's written comparison request cannot produce a leaf, because a comparison cannot be had by a man who is not standing in the room when the sleeve comes out of the cabinet. **The only thing that gets the leaf to Mosswake is Ivet Sarn's own rider, sent on the evening of the tenth on the district court's custody slip, with the applicant's name at the head of it.** The leaf comes back on the eleventh and is held sealed in the Mosswake strong box from that afternoon, and is produced in open court at ten on the twelfth, out of a Table sleeve with the Table's seal on the wax, on the magistrate's own direction and not on anybody's request, and is entered in the minute book as received into the custody of this court, with the rider's receipt and the date it left the building entered beside it.
- **The provincial hold** on the Venn file is sealed and its reference carries no office name. The old fire-and-water device of the First Seat is on the seal. It is named for the first time on the page by Tamsin to Marek on the channel bank at night on the tenth, in a private conversation, and for the first time **in open court** by Magistrate Rell in Chapter 7. No character may call it a First Seat instruction before Chapter 6, and no character may name it aloud in a room before Chapter 7.
- **Where the summary and the slip are.** The Venn summary was kept out of the courier's satchel on the evening of the seventh and is in Marek's inside coat; it would go north on the next courier if a supervisor ever signed the slip. The slip is in the same coat and is **unsigned** — Pell Vey refused to countersign it on an evening docket, and he retracts that promise himself in open court on the twelfth. The comparison request is a different sheet: Marek left the original on the Table's tray on the ninth, and it is still there at the end of the batch, unsigned. Do not put any of these three on a tray and in a coat at the same time.
- **How to read an hour.** The chapters use ordinal hours ("the seventh hour", "the second hour") and the sense shifts with the scene, so a later batch must not assume a single convention. What is fixed and matters: the courier leaves Auremar at dusk on the seventh; Sarn's rider leaves Mosswake at the seventh hour of the evening of the tenth and is at the Table at the second hour of the morning of the eleventh; the witness order goes up on the board at the eighth hour of the morning of the eleventh; the leaf is produced at ten on the twelfth; the ruling is made at twenty past three on the twelfth; and in Chapter 10, all on the eighteenth, in this order: the offer to have the three documents returned out of the inventory is made and refused at the eighth hour, the ledger is produced at the ninth hour of the morning and copied until the second hour of the afternoon, the chain opens at the fourth hour of the afternoon, the arrest is at the sixth hour of the afternoon, and the request is stamped into Examinations custody and the two of them come out at the ninth hour of the evening.
- **The exhibit sleeve** holds the source leaf, the witness copy, the filed correction, the Reed Cut memorandum, Pyle's certificate, and Tamsin's comparison copy, sealed and carried to the Mosswake registry and to no other office **except by a joint order of the Mosswake Roll Court and the Chancery district of Auremar made for the purpose of copying it**, which is how the sleeve lawfully reaches the room above the chandler's shop in Chapters 9 and 10. No copy of the six documents may be made except by the court or by a person the court names in open session; the twenty-six public copies are of the court's ruling and its five terms, which are not among the six.
- **The chain** also holds two sealed pages folded in behind the memorandum: the First Seat form, and a second sealed page whose contents have not been read. A thread of old red is caught in the fold. The chain was opened **twice**: once at the Mosswake courtroom on the night of the sixteenth, to receive and enter the joint order coming down from Auremar with a constable's name at the foot of it, which is the opening Chapter 9 sends the roll-keeper to, and once in Auremar at the fourth hour of the afternoon of the eighteenth, which is the opening that produces the form. **The joint order is also what puts a constable in the broker's office at all**: the room above the chandler in Candlewright Lane is where the copies are made and the record kept, and the office itself is worked in front of the constable for the two days. The second page's contents are a live open thread and must not be resolved in Batch 0002.

- **The audit request** is a hand-signed nine-paragraph document dated the eighteenth, stamped *Received*, custody *Examinations*, with a file number written in ink on Tamsin's copy by the intake clerk and initialled. It is not before the Table, not before the Inheritance Desk, and its scope is undecided. **On the nineteenth of the tenth it was narrowed by the applicant's own signature** to the sale of copied witness marks and the supply of pressed matrices within the district of Auremar, the entries at Glasswater and Emberwatch being **reserved and not abandoned**, with a clerk's time written beside the signature by Tamsin. **On the eighth of the eleventh a two-page supplementary request was filed**, naming MC/FS, the base issue, the amendment sheet, both runs, the distribution line, and the signer, and it took a second number and a second *Custody: Examinations* stamp without the applicant having to ask. ### 2. The Cinder Court's four orders, and exactly what each binds

Made on the **seventeenth of the sixth month**, in **room four**, **Sela Vint presiding**, the registrar unnamed and about fifty.

1. **No guarantor duty attaches to a person who has not been heard in a room and has not signed, and no court in this empire may join a person as guarantor on the strength of a printed model of general wording.** It **binds that court and any court that takes a recognition from it. It does not reach back to a district decision already made in a district that has not been heard by that court** — and the Mosswake Roll Court's own decision of the twenty-first of the fifth month **stands**, because it was made in a room.
2. **The sentence stated by the Mosswake Roll Court on the twelfth of the ninth month, as printed in the amendment sheet of the twenty-second, is recorded by the Cinder Court as model wording of general application and is not a decision of any court.** It **may not be the recorded reason for a decision in any district without a hearing of the person it names.** The record of the sentence is a record of a document and is **not** a record of a law, is not a precedent, and no district is permitted to use it as a reason. **It does not withdraw the sentence anywhere, because only the district that stated it can withdraw its own practice, and that district has not withdrawn it and has not been asked to.** It is still in nine hundred and forty printed copies on the ordinary distribution to forty-six addresses.
3. **The seal certificates of Corvin Dray are suspended — sixty-one of them** — on the **registrar's own authority**, with no argument heard. ### 3. The entry by device, and its operative line

A court with four hundred years of practice has assumed a document has a person at the top of it. Where one does not, the Cinder Court enters the device. The form was drawn in the reign of the third of the Ashfall line for a case nobody can now find, is used about twice in a decade, and the registrar read the whole of it aloud in seventeen minutes.

**Operative line, in substance: where a document is produced whose face bears the seal of a seat and no name, the seat is entered as a respondent by its device; no person of or belonging to that seat is party, cited or examinable; and no inference of any kind may be drawn in that proceeding from the fact that a person has not been named.** The entry is made in the open minute book with the device struck in wax in the presence of the clerk at the back of the room.

The registrar's own gloss, which is canon: the line has been read aloud in a hearing **four times in nine years, and three of those were read by a person who was relieved.** It does not say a person was not named; it says the court will not draw a conclusion about a person out of the shape of a document. **It is the only rule in the compact's own procedure that does any work on a record of nothing but names.** Nineteen of them were in the room's file; six of the people were in the room; **none of them was at the top of anything.**

**A court that declines to name a person is not thereby wrong**, and Volume 02 may not treat the entry by device as a trick, an evasion, or a defect to be got round.

### 7. The calendar, so that Volume 02 does not re-derive it

**The volume runs from the seventh of the ninth month of the ninth year to the twenty-second of the eighth month of the year after it**, in five batches of ten.

- Batch 0001: the seventh to the eighteenth of the ninth month, all ten chapters inside one month.
- Batch 0002: the nineteenth of the ninth month to the ninth of the eleventh.
- Batch 0003: the eleventh of the eleventh to the twenty-second of the second month of the year after.
- Batch 0004: the second week of the second month to the tenth of the fourth.
- Batch 0005: the third week of the fifth month to the twenty-second of the eighth.

**All five day-by-day calendars are already in this file** — one under each batch heading in the chronology section, with every fixed date and every card reading that had to be interpreted. **Volume 02 does not re-derive any of them.** The Batch 0005 calendar is the one that matters for the hand-over, and it is the last calendar in the file.

**Weekday names are not usable as anchors.** Exactly one is on the page — the ninth of the fourth month is a Monday, because Chapter 38 has the sealed scope decision arriving on Monday morning — and the lengths of the fourth, fifth, sixth and seventh months are nowhere fixed, so **Volume 02 uses no weekday name at all.** Chapters 41–50 use no real month name either; months are numbered on the page throughout the closing batch. Date the day and let the reader do the arithmetic.

**Four repair passes are recorded**: one on Batch 0003, two on Batch 0004, one on Batch 0005. Three of the four are summarised at the head of this file and all four are set out in the per-batch notes in `state/batch-summary.md`. Batches 0001 and 0002 have had none. ### 8. The two rules most likely to be broken by accident

- **The two documents must not be merged.** The MC/FS circular is a Chancery standing office's paper; the First Seat quarterly return is a dragon seat's paper. They are not connected by any document, one is not the child of the other, and the circular was not before the Cinder Court and is not examined there. **Only Marek Kest and Tamsin Rook know both exist.** A Volume 02 chapter that puts them in one sentence, one file, one reply, or one argument has broken the volume's spine.
- **The four lines and the sentence have different ages and must never be merged.** The four substituted lines are in the Sallow file in the **fourth month of the fifth year** and are in twenty-two of thirty-one MC/FS sheets in the rack. The public-necessity sentence was **stated on the twelfth of the ninth month and printed on the twenty-second.** No chapter may put the sentence in an older file, and no chapter may put the four lines in a newer form than the one already traced. **Chapter 44 turned the Caudle notice's resemblance to the fourth term into a fact about a number and a date, and it is a fact about a number and a date; it must not be turned back into a seam.**
