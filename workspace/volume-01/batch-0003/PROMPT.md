## Canon locks for this batch

These are fixed. If anything disagrees with them, this section wins and the discrepancy is recorded in `state/continuity.md`.

- **Marek performs no magic in Chapters 21–30.** No Roll-Answer appears and the first Seam-Sight does not recur. He is a Stage 0 clerk whose standing is unstable, and the instability is the pressure.
- **No valid exception, counter-seal, mixed warrant, true-name reading, Roll Assembly, or Stage 2 ability appears.** The word "exception" does not appear in this batch at all. The only draft that used it is dead and cannot be used. A court may issue its own order and a guild may issue its own form; neither is Marek's power.
- **The reading's cost has been paid.** The headache went on the twenty-sixth of the tenth month and the temporary inability to lie about one named obligation lapsed on the page in Chapter 18, at the fourth hour, in a lane, to a nineteen-year-old, and was described as a lapse and not a cure. It does not return, it does not get better by rest, by a physician's fee, or by a decision to try harder, and Doctor Perrin Ashe of the Mosswake river landing is an ordinary doctor who cannot end it. A later chapter may use the fact that Marek can lie again as a pressure on him. Do not stage the lapse a second time.
- **The ruling has five operative terms, and the numbering is canon.** (1) Tavi's personal debt void. (2) The field preserved until the second month, when seven years from the signing are up — a date, not a counted balance. (3) Sivra Oris's gate and navigation duty unaffected. (4) "The fourth term" is the new-signature rule: anyone assuming a continuing field-side duty must sign a new obligation for it. (5) "The fifth term" is the district works: the district water office must do the bank works and the gate reset under its own flood authority, and the household is not required to perform the field-side upkeep for the purposes of the order. Never describe the ruling as a four-term ruling.
- **The source leaf does not travel on request.** It lives in the Notaries' Table's cabinet in Auremar, and a comparison cannot be had by a clerk who is not standing in the room when the sleeve comes out. Sarn has no Table key and never had one. Any chapter that moves the leaf needs a written application, a court direction, and a recorded custody entry. The provincial hold's reference carries no office name; the First Seat's device was named aloud in open court for the first time on the twelfth of the ninth month, so nothing may retro-name the hold.
- **Two documents, not one, and they must not be merged.** The **MC/FS circular** carries the words and is sealed by the **Standing Office of Provincial Continuities**: base issue of the second month of this year, run five hundred and sixty; amendment sheet of the twenty-second of the ninth month, run **nine hundred and forty**, carrying the Mosswake public-necessity sentence verbatim with the note *Adopted from a provincial court, the ninth month. A court may adopt this wording or any other.* The **First Seat form** is a quarterly return that makes the honest answer small and says nothing about validity. There is no document connecting them, and only Marek and Tamsin know both exist.
- **The four lines and the sentence have different ages.** The four substituted lines (the *present holder* wording) are in the Sallow file in the fourth month of the **fifth year** and in twenty-two of thirty-one sheets in the rack. The public-necessity sentence was stated on the **twelfth of the ninth month** and printed on the **twenty-second of the ninth**. No chapter may put the sentence in an older file.
- **The second sealed page stays shut through this batch.** Nobody opens it. Anyone who comes close to it must be witnessed and must pay for looking. Examinations has already recorded one refusal against Marek's name; do not stage a second opening.
- **The red stitch and the thread in the form's fold are not the same thing.** No chapter may assert, imply, hint at, or let a character assert that they are connected. Marek's prohibition is on the record from Chapter 10 and it stands. The stitched leaf is no longer in Ivo Harker's tin; Tamsin took it on the night of the sixteenth of the ninth month.

## What this batch must do

- **Chapters 21–25: finish the volume midpoint.** Marek refuses a bounded, empire-wide certification that would genuinely protect the current food supply, and accepts instead a **named, reviewable emergency process** with a form, a witness who is not the applicant, a stated cost ceiling, a public register of hearings, and a review in the fourth month. The refusal must cost something real and must be a public act rather than a silence. The process must protect current food supplies without pretending the copied rule is safe, and the batch must make the reader feel why that is the honest answer rather than the clean one.
- **Chapters 26–30: open the coalition-under-material-pressure arc.** Tamsin begins building independent copies across Mosswake and Greyfen, and the copies must fail on money before they fail on courage. A household in a third district loses a tool before the notice reaches them. The volume outline's own escalation for Chapters 26–33 is "a separate household in another district loses a tool before the notice reaches them," and that is Chapter 30's job.
- **Every win has a named cost.** The refusal protects the rolls and costs four families their certainty. The process protects the filings and costs the district hours it does not have. The amendment protects the exemption and costs a levy. Nobody in this batch gets a clean sheet.
- **Ordinary competence keeps winning.** A posted schedule, a register, a form with four boxes, a cost ceiling, a chalk board with four questions, a list of who holds a copy, a witness order, a docket. The people who decide cases are clerks, a foreman, a water-office clerk, an assessor, a seed keeper, a woman at a court window, and a district works foreman.
- **Do not spend a chapter on a document being read.** Chapter 21 is Hallis Dren's arithmetic, Chapter 22 is a refusal made in a room, Chapter 24 is four questions in a lane, Chapter 27 is a form read aloud to a stranger, Chapter 30 is a copyist with a book in a house three hundred miles away. Only Chapter 25 may lean on paper, and it must lean on a person signing it.
