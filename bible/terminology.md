# Terminology

This is the canonical vocabulary for planning and prose. Use the terms consistently and do not introduce alternate synonyms for the same mechanic unless the difference is intentional.

## Proper Nouns

- **Cindral Empire:** The human-and-dragon polity that contains the story's main civilization.
- **Cindral Concord:** The broader political settlement formed by dragon Houses, human provinces, guilds, and civic assemblies. It is a living constitution, not a single throne.
- **Auremar:** The capital city and seat of the Crown Kiln and Roll Hall.
- **Crown Kiln:** The sealed furnace and notary complex where major dragon seals are fired. It is infrastructure, not a throne.
- **Emberroot Compact:** The public treaty that ended the War of Nine Bridges. It is the oldest foundational compact still recognized by the Empire.
- **Nine Seats:** The nine recognized dragon Houses and their territorial/legal seats. The First Seat is one of the nine, not a supreme throne.
- **First Seat:** The seat with the old authority to call a succession convocation. Seryn Oris holds it during the final succession crisis.
- **House Oris:** The dragon House that holds the First Seat and much of the old fire-and-water authority.
- **Chancery of the Roll:** The human civil institution that records contracts and legal events. “The Chancery” is the short form.
- **Notaries' Table:** The Chancery division that certifies filed packets, holds the source-leaf cabinet, and is headed by Pell Vey.
- **Inheritance Desk:** The Roll Hall desk where Marek works, responsible for inheritance packets and their summaries.
- **Packet:** The bundle of source leaf, witness copy, filed corrections, and clerk's summary that travels as one case.
- **Summary:** The compressed statement of a packet that later files copy from. A summary is evidence of what a clerk believed, not of what was signed.
- **Source leaf:** The retained original page held in the locked cabinet. It cannot be taken from the gallery without a supervisor's written authorization.
- **Mosswake Roll Court:** The provincial court on the Mosswake flood terrace, under Magistrate Leda Rell's administration.
- **Reed Cut:** The channel and gate district where the Venn field sits and where the district water office issues flood memoranda.
- **Public reading table:** A trestle table set up in a yard or workshop so a ruling can be read aloud to the people it affects.
- **Living Roll:** The distributed public record of contracts, witnesses, seals, marriages, births, work, debt, and inheritance. It is incomplete and politically contested.
- **Roll Hall:** The public reading and copying hall in Auremar.
- **Roll Court:** A provincial human court that hears ordinary civil and inheritance disputes.
- **Cinder Court:** The high court for foundational compacts and major scale disputes. It has authority but is bound by the public rule of a clear hearing.
- **Scalehold:** A dragon-held territory with its own settlements, resources, and local agreements.
- **Mosswake:** The river province and opening-case location.
- **Greyfen:** The commoner ring around Auremar, including Bren Kest's workshop.
- **Salt Teeth:** The mining and furnace region where guild succession becomes a major conflict.
- **Cinder Marches:** The contested human-dragon borderland.
- **Ashfall Archive:** The archive branch destroyed twelve years before the opening; Ilyra Kest dies there.
- **War of Nine Bridges:** The historical war that preceded the Emberroot Compact.
- **Quiet Reign:** The period in which dragon succession and foundational law became increasingly centralized and secret.

## Legal Terms

- **Dragon contract:** The common term for a contract involving a dragon party, a dragon-held territory, or a dragon resource. It does not mean every dragon contract is valid or harmful.
- **Scale contract:** The formal category for an instrument involving a dragon party or a dragon-held resource.
- **Plain instrument:** A human civil contract without a dragon party.
- **Continuation clause:** A clause that carries a specified duty to an heir or successor. Validity depends on clear terms, hearing, witness, and record.
- **Foundational ratification:** The public act by which named provinces, Seats, and civic bodies accept specified duties in a foundational compact. It cannot bind an unnamed future population or create a perpetual office or debt by private inheritance. The shorthand **5/5/3-of-6** means five of nine human provinces, five of nine dragon Seats, and three of six independent civic assemblies.
- **Cinder Clause:** The hidden unlawful addition to the Emberroot Compact that attempts to turn a temporary human office into a First-Seat-controlled proxy succession. It is the central mechanism behind the inheritance-echo plot.
- **Cinder proxy route:** The later practice of pairing a First Seat seal with a human who has a recorded inherited claim, naming the human a proxy guarantor, and claiming that control passes behind that person. It does not confer the original office on a dragon and is not recognized public ratification.
- **Inheritance echo:** The spread of a continuation clause into ordinary law after courts and institutions accept it as precedent. It changes what people believe they owe, not only what a single document says.
- **Seam:** A mismatch or hidden join between recorded terms, witnesses, seals, precedents, and current legal effect. A seam is evidence of a problem, not proof of who caused it.
- **Counter-seal:** The written record of a valid exception that places one specified consequence on a named party or record. It remains active until the exception is resolved or withdrawn; it does not require the writer to hold a Continuity Auditor title and is not a permanent change to the original contract.
- **Counter-mark:** The physical line that appears on Marek's wrist when a deep reading or a valid counter-seal makes him answerable for a term. It is a symptom of the legal burden, not a separate power. A reading-born mark is shallow and temporary; a counter-seal that places the consequence on Marek can deepen and extend it until the duty is resolved. Its cost must be named and does not create an unspecified geographic tether.
- **Exception:** A formal, publicly heard restriction or transfer of a contract's consequence. An exception can narrow, suspend, or reassign a term only within its jurisdiction and consent rules.
- **Clear hearing:** A public proceeding in which the relevant parties hear the actual term in a usable language, can question it, and either accept or refuse it. Coercion can invalidate a purported assent; a hearing cannot force honesty.
- **True seal:** A dragon's authenticated legal mark, made from scale impression, spoken assent, and a witness record. A copied image is not a true seal.
- **True name:** A dragon's legal self, not a secret spell. Speaking it in a binding gives the speaker a reciprocal duty; using it does not grant unlimited power.
- **Witness:** A person or recognized civic recorder responsible for remembering and reporting what was agreed. A witness may be challenged but not simply replaced by a more convenient record.
- **Roll:** A record of a legal event or contract. A roll is evidence and a public act, not a mind-reading device.
- **Plain reading:** A normal comparison of paper, ink, seals, witness lists, dates, and filing history.
- **Audit:** A formal review of whether the recorded terms match their present legal effect. An audit is authorized by a recognized office or civic body.
- **Continuity Auditor:** A trained legal official who can identify and challenge inheritance seams. Certification is rare, politically sensitive, and does not grant sovereign authority.
- **Sole foundational office:** A single-person or single-bloodline office that claims all foundational duties for an empire. The Covenant prohibits it. Marek's final cost is surrendering eligibility for such an office; the term does not mean the ordinary First Custodian office by itself.
- **Public-necessity sentence:** The exact generic ruling introduced in the Mosswake case: “Where immediate public necessity requires a recorded continuation to prevent loss of food or shelter, the present holder of the burdened property may be joined as guarantor until full review.” It is the first portable institutional wording of the inheritance echo; the copied Dunn summary shows the mechanism before a court accepts it, and the sentence may not be reused without a separate hearing and signature.
- **Mixed warrant:** A revocable public instrument issued by a recognized Roll Assembly together with a dragon notary. Before formal Roll Assemblies exist, a recognized civic body with a public roll and hearing procedure may issue the provisional form. It lets a named auditor perform a specified hearing, including a witnessed true-name reading, while creating a reciprocal duty. It grants no office, ownership, permanent seal, sole access, or continuing authority after the named hearing.
- **First Custodian:** The original temporary human office that maintains the Emberroot Compact when a throne, dragon House, or recognized government fails. A candidate serves no more than seven years, has no enforceable inherited asset or right at appointment, and is chosen at a public mixed convocation. Halden Ro is the sitting human Custodian during the final crisis; no person or dragon bloodline inherits the office.
- **Emberroot Interim Keepers:** The original vacancy body of two human record keepers, one dragon notary, and one civic witness, selected by the affected local court, recognized dragon seat, and nearest civic assembly. They may maintain existing duties, records, and immediate relief for forty-five days, but cannot create a perpetual duty, transfer the office, or invoke the Cinder proxy route. The short form is **the Keepers**.
- **Interim Continuity Council:** The Covenant-era body that maintains existing duties during a vacancy, failed quorum, or transition. It has two human province representatives, two dragon Seat representatives, and one civic or guild witness selected by the last recognized mixed quorum, serves no more than ninety days, may be renewed once, and must call a full convocation.
- **Succession convocation:** A formal gathering called to determine the First Custodian or the continuity of a foundational compact. The final succession crisis centers on this event.
- **Roll Assembly:** A local or regional body that maintains copies, hears an exception, and participates in a distributed legal quorum. Roll Assemblies are the institutional successor to a single custodian.
- **Restitution:** A public duty to repair a proven loss caused by a broken contract or failed hearing. It may involve land, wages, records, care, or political authority, not merely money.
- **Recognition:** A jurisdiction's willingness to treat a seal, ruling, or compact as valid within its borders. Recognition can be withdrawn temporarily without destroying the underlying document.
- **Flood-season continuity process:** The named, reviewable, six-week process agreed in Volume 01 Chapter 22 and built in Chapter 23 at the Mosswake Roll Court. A flood-season continuity filing in a district that adopts it is not categorised away, may be heard individually on request at a window, is entered in a public register of hearings, is witnessed by a person who is not the applicant and not a creditor, carries a stated cost ceiling, and is reviewed on a fixed date. Exceeding the stated cost **stops the process** rather than continuing it unpaid. It is not a rule, not a precedent, and applies to no file but the flood-season filings of a district that adopts it. It is the court's, the water office's and a register's, and is never Marek's.
- **Four-box form:** The Mosswake form for a request to be heard, with a box for the hearing, a box for the witness, a box for the cost, and a box for the next review. It has no number, no series, no seal and no office at the head of it, because a form with a run on it is a model. The *entry in the book* is numbered; the form is not.
- **Witness order:** A five-line slip telling a person they have been named to witness a hearing, that they are not the applicant and are not owed anything in the matter, what they will do, and that they may say no and are not held to it for saying no.
- **The four questions:** Four plain questions a person may ask any paper, in this order: *Is there work in it? Whose name is on it — yours, or a word that walks about, like the holder? If you stop, whose name answers for it? Who read this to you, and do they get paid if you sign?* They are not a law, not advice, and cannot be made into either. The fourth is the one that decides whether the other three can be trusted. They travel, because they are short and cost nothing.
- **Hearing slate:** The Mosswake public register of hearings, kept at the clerk's window in Ivet Sarn's hand on the same board as the register of pending duties, at a shilling a copy, with a line number against every entry. A register of duties shows what is owed; a register of hearings shows who came. A district practice, not an institution, and not a Roll Assembly.
- **Copy list:** The private list of who holds a copy of a document, in whose hand, of what, dated, and whether the holder has agreed to read it to anybody who comes to the house with a paper. It is not distributed, has no heading and no promise, and is the only record of where the proof of a case physically is. It is not a network, not a seal, and not an office.
- **Issued, not invented:** The finding of Volume 01 that a local precedent which appears in unrelated districts was **issued** by a standing office as approved model wording in a numbered series with a print run, and is therefore traceable to a series and a date and not to a person. A standing office can hold a seal, a series and a run and have no person at the top of it, and the authority for its sheets is a line in a district's own standing instructions that no further authority is required.
- **Plain law:** Ordinary human inheritance and civil law, including blood-right and earned-right. It is not opposed to scale law by definition; the conflict is over which rule controls a particular consequence.
- **Blood-right:** A claim recognized because of documented family relationship or descent.
- **Earned-right:** A claim recognized because of paid work, completed service, or a transfer made during the claimant's life.
- **Carried-right:** A claim or duty that a contract attempts to pass to a successor. It is valid only when the continuation is clear, heard, witnessed, and recorded.

## Volume 02 Terms

- **Docket of Powers:** a small office of the Roll Hall, three rooms off the corridor behind the third gallery, with a card in a window and no sign on the door, that keeps **the register of persons recorded as having performed a scale reading under a recognised authority**. One page to a year, four lines to an entry: name, date, instrument, authority. The record is a permission and the permission is what a court relies on, so the record is made **beforehand** and not after the fact. About fourteen entries a year, because a person only agrees to have one when there is no other way of getting at the record. **The Docket keeps a register of persons and not a register of records**, and that is why it is safe: it has no power to say what a reading did to a file.
- **Unauthorised scale reading:** a reading of a scale instrument by a person not entered in the Docket's register at the time of the reading. **It is not void.** Nobody is to pretend it is void, and the record in which it was entered is a record of a court and not of the Docket.
- **Limited administrative inquiry:** the shape into which a request about a numbered series is converted when the office receiving it has no officer for series. It fixes a scope on its own page, is conducted by the applicant in his own time at his own wage, **and can compel nothing** — no document, no office, no witness, no return — and does not touch any series, any standing instruction, or any return under the fire-and-water device. **Granting it is not a small version of the thing asked for; it is a different thing wearing the same word.**
- **Entry by register, on a clerk's own initiative:** a person entering another person's act into a public register, in her own hand, with a paragraph on the face of the entry saying that she did it on her own initiative, that she has not asked the person named, and that it is not a finding. **A register with a gap in it is a shape with a hole in it, and a person does not have to lie about a shape.**
- **Custody of public copies during an inquiry:** a four-line form with two boxes, one headed **the office of the receiving person, if any**. **None is not nothing: None is a person who cannot be instructed.** The independent copy of anything held in a book of one's own with no office on it is outside the reach of the Docket, of the Chancery, and of every office in this book.
- **Continuance application:** a request that a court treat a matter it is already in as a matter it must go on hearing, so that an office which is not a party and cannot be compelled has to come and be heard. **A hearing is not a fund**, and a continuance can be refused correctly with a date, and the closure that follows can be the cost of a ruling that is entirely right.
- **Press-mark:** the word a returning officer writes in the corner of a returned envelope, with the officer's initial beside it. Thirty-one press-marks with a name and a door against them, eleven with a word and no name, twelve returns with the post stamp and nothing else. **No clerk in nine years had ever gone and looked at an outgoing rack**, and the whole of a court's unacknowledged harm was in a drawer eight feet from the window.
- **The present holder:** the substituted phrase. **A word that does worse damage to a public duty than to a private one, because a body can be dissolved and a household cannot.** It stands in a district works return of the fourth month of the sixth year, in a form that is not in any series, in an office that is not on the forty-six addresses, written by a clerk with a hundred and forty returns to do that month. It is a **practice and not a chain**: four appearances, four instances, no instrument joining any two of them.

- **Delivery stub:** the four-line cardboard stub the Notaries' Table keeps for every sheet that leaves the building, being *enclosed; to whom and where; on whose instance it is sent; by whose authority it is sent*. The Table numbers everything it sends, and everything it makes for a thing that is going to be sent, out of one run at one counter in one hand for about sixty years, so a number cut into the edge of a plate is a number of that run. The stubs are kept in a **press room off the Crown Kiln stair** and not in a gallery, because a stub is not a record. **A stub is a fact about a book and not about a document and not about a person**, and nothing that goes out of that room is ever a case and has no file number. **There is no stub for an instruction handed to a man in a corridor.**
- **Officer's return:** the paper an officer at a door produces after a sitting, with a column of figures for the sheets accounted for in and out. It carries an office at the head of it and **a clerk's name at the foot of it**, which is what makes it a document about that clerk.
- **Limited plea:** a plea that admits the counts in a schedule and neither admits nor denies any matter touching any person or office not named, and in which the defendant's appearance to be tried is not an admission touching any such person or office. **It is a new act and not a re-refusal, and it is not absolution and it is not a pardon.**
- **A mark is not a signature:** a mark says *this is one of ours* to somebody who already knows what ours is. A signature says who did it; a mark carries a **direction**, and a direction is the one thing in the world that does not need anybody's permission to be followed. It is a separate fact from **the counting-house mark** and from **any person's own signature mark**, and no two of them may be joined in a sentence.
- **Examination, in the Docket of Powers:** an examination is not a hearing. There are no rules for one, because the office that holds a bar has never had to enforce anything. The master of the Docket says so in advance, sits in the room, and is not one of the two examiners, **because a man who is being examined should not be examined on the side of the man examining him.**
- **A bar is a register entry:** the Docket's second of three powers is to bar a person from any audit in any jurisdiction until examined. What it amounts to in practice is that the Docket's register has a name on a page with a disposition against it, and **every court which takes a recognition from the Docket has to answer for that page.** The only procedure for being entered is a written application to the Docket stating the authority under which the person reads, and it is one line long.
- **A reading of no legal effect, and a record that stands:** the Cinder Court's answer of the eighth of the third month of Volume 02, in the worst of both available forms. The two unauthorised scale readings were **of no legal effect whatever**; the record they were entered in **stands**, is not altered, and every act done in reliance on it stands, and no person who acted on it in good faith is to be questioned. **A reading of no legal effect is better than a permitted one and worse than a disowned one:** it reaches nothing, and it makes the record permanent. It does not discharge a bar.
- **A limited audit in the form of the eleventh of the third month:** a grant with a size and not a shape. One room in one city, fourteen days, the two entries in one register and nothing else, a document produced of a person's own motion and nothing compelled, **no finding usable in any court but the granting court**, and **an express line that it may not be carried out in any district**. **Granting it is not a small version of the thing asked for; it is a different thing wearing the same word**, and the person who has to say so is the one person in the world who wanted the thing.
- **A notice of a seat:** an instrument on which the device is at the head and **no person is named, party, cited or examinable, and no inference of any kind may be drawn from the fact that a person has not been named**. It is a way of being in a room without being a person, and it is the form the Cinder Court itself published in the reign of the third of the Ashfall line, and a court that declines one has to write down why and there is no why. **A court that cannot name a party cannot be asked what it decided, and a matter held by one of these can be held until somebody loses interest in it.**
- **A human clerk's instrument:** *a document with a person's name at the top of it is a document about that person.* It is the volume's best instrument and it is a **human** one. It works on a district works, a provincial court and a man in a good coat with a tray. **It does not cross a species, and a person who signs for a seat is discharging an office, and an office cannot be examined.**
- **The sheet with two boxes:** the second instrument, made by hand in the Received Room in the last week of the fourth month of Volume 02 and **not sent to anybody**. One side, two ruled boxes and three lines of rule, **no number, no series, no seal and no office at the head of it**, because a form with a run on it is a model waiting for a district. Box one, *what the person was told, in the person's own words.* Box two, *the office of the receiving person, if any*, and **`None` when there is none.** The three lines: *you are not asked anything and nothing is asked of you; you are not to be given advice; write the day and your initial on the other side and hand this back.* The other side carries one rule: *the day. Your initial.* And a column at a counter headed with one word, **Given**, which is the only heading and the whole of the design. **Four pence a sheet** and the price is on no sheet and is in a copyist's book in a hand that is her own.
- **What the sheet is for:** a document can only be beaten by being there first, and the cheapest fact in the empire is a date and an initial. It **does not stop a document, does not put a household back in a field, and does not open a door**, and it is beaten by the one case it was made for, because a person who has been warned about a document is already afraid of it. **It fixes the next thing, and the next thing is the one where nobody warned anybody.** A person who cannot read a paragraph can put four words in a box, be handed a piece of paper back with a day on it, and go; that is the whole of what it is for, and the reason is the word `None`, which is the reason a copyist can be protected absolutely and is the reason a plate-cutter in a lane can do the same thing on her own account in the fifth month with no form and no idea where it came from.
- **The year in the run:** the one fact a plate-cutter of twenty-two years holds and no office can be written to about. A sending number is a number of the run and not a number of the plate, the run does not stop, and there is one **year** about halfway through it at which a run changes because a counter changes or a hand changes. **The person who can say which numbers are hers is a ledger**, and a ledger is the thing the seven plates could not be and the thing the run needs most.
- **A temporary office:** the First Custodian, whose defining term is that it is temporary. A temporary office is the only office in this empire that is honest about when it stops, and it is the only one nobody in the Roll Hall has ever had to administer. **The word is in the operative words and every office that has widened that office has done it by not putting the word in the sentence.**

## Character-Specific Terms

- **Red-thread copy:** Ilyra Kest's coded set of copied pages and marginal marks. It is incomplete evidence, not a prophecy or complete answer.
- **The Kest claim:** The Kest family's disputed property, inheritance, and debt record tied to Ilyra's erased name. A temporary hold lapsed without valid claimant assent or settlement, so it is neither Marek's enforceable asset nor free of historical obligations. It is both a personal goal and a legal test case.
- **The blank-heir rule:** The original Compact's safeguard for public office, applied in three distinct stages. **Original eligibility** requires a human candidate to have no enforceable inherited asset or right at appointment; Marek already satisfies that test. The **Cinder proxy test** is the later, unlawful use of any recorded inherited route, including a disputed claim, to identify a First-Seat-controlled human guarantor; Marek's disputed Kest file unintentionally satisfies it. **Final release** means opening the claim in public, settling the affected parties' independent rights, and voluntarily releasing both Marek's prospective share and the recorded route as a proxy. It preserves the debt's history and restitution duty while removing the prepared succession path.
- **Clear hearing network:** A distributed set of public readings and local assemblies used in the final volume to establish consent and common evidence.

## Prose Usage Rules

- Prefer **contract**, **clause**, **roll**, **seal**, **witness**, **hearing**, and **exception** over vague terms such as “magic rule” or “power system.”
- Use **inheritance echo** for the legal-social spread. Do not call the same phenomenon “blood magic,” “dragon mind control,” or “curse.”
- Use **Cinder Clause** only for the hidden foundational mechanism. Do not use it as a synonym for every continuation clause.
- Use **First Custodian** for the original human office, **Cinder proxy route** for Seryn's unlawful method of controlling it, and **Seryn Oris** for the person who seeks permanent control behind a proxy. Never imply that a dragon is eligible for the original office or call Seryn the clause itself.
- Use **plain law** and **scale law** as overlapping categories. Most disputes are conflicts of jurisdiction or consequence, not a clean human-versus-dragon split.
- A Roll-Answer display is at most three lines: **Named**, **Promised**, **Carried after death**. It never displays numbers, skills, experience, or an objective moral verdict.

### How the Law Should Sound

- Quote operative words **once, in full, in plain language**, and paraphrase them everywhere else. The reader should know exactly what was ruled without rereading the chapter.
- A clerk names nouns before feelings: dates, seals, witness counts, filing order, and who was in the room. Emotional content arrives through what a person chooses to say about those nouns.
- A ruling has three parts in this world: the term as recorded, the term as heard, and the term as carried forward. Let scenes show the difference rather than narrating it.
- Never write that a law “has always existed,” that a clause “means” something on its own, or that a court “felt” a result. Courts decide; clerks record; families absorb.
- Avoid **mana**, **level**, **skill tree**, **cooldown**, **buff**, **quest**, **system**, **stats**, and **XP**. If a document or a dragon is "reading" something, name the record.
- Avoid calling dragons **evil**, **ancient evil**, **chthon**, **eldritch**, or **beasts of the deep**. They are signatories, neighbours, employers, and politicians.
- Avoid **-ly** overuse as filler (he said quietly, she answered softly). Let a few strong adverbs carry weight and let most dialogue stand on its nouns.
- Do not write **the System said** or **a voice told him**. The Roll-Answer is a legal reading of a record, and the story says so in those terms.
- One Roll-Answer at most per chapter, and only after permission and cost have been shown in the scene.
- Read a chapter once for these rules before it is submitted. A single vivid sentence with a banned term is enough to break the world's voice.
