## Section one — the finding: the batch's river runs both ways, and the two ways are eleven lines apart in one chapter

**This is a canon contradiction in a range that three prior passes certified as having none.** Item 270C closes with *"No out-of-world breach and no canon contradiction in this range."* **There is one, it is structural, it is on eleven lines across six of the ten chapters, and it is invisible to every instrument in this repository because it is made of a direction word and not of a number.**

### One. `chapter-0237.md:107` and `chapter-0237.md:109`, eleven lines apart, both measured from the furnace town, both with the identical phrase, landing on two places the volume elsewhere separates by four hundred and thirty miles

> `chapter-0237.md:107` — *"There is a **man in a city about four hundred and thirty miles down this river** that a woman of twenty walked about nine miles to … He is a man of thirty-eight with no office and no fund and a bar of his own that came off him on the twelfth of the ninth month of last year…"*
>
> `chapter-0237.md:109` — *"And **about four hundred and thirty miles down this river** there is a **bank about four hundred and forty foot long** with a guarantee standing on it that nobody has answered and about sixty children under sixteen inside it…"*

Both sentences are in the same chapter, at the same point of view, in the furnace town, eleven lines apart, and both carry the same figure and the same direction. **They cannot both be true, because the man of thirty-eight with no office and no fund and that bar is Marek Kest, and the volume separates him from that bank by four hundred and thirty miles in the same batch.** `chapter-0234.md:13`: *"The four lines ask him to come and look at a thing on a bank four hundred and thirty miles down this river"* — from Marek Kest's own rented room. `chapter-0240.md:93`: *"About four hundred and thirty miles down the river there is a bank about four hundred and forty foot long and a foreman of fifty-one…"* — from Marek Kest again. **The eleven lines apart make one point on the river; the rest of the batch makes it two, four hundred and thirty miles apart.**

### Four. Why nothing in this repository could have found it, and it is the standing item 170 has been carrying for five dispatches

Item 170's standing: *this project measures sentence length, emphasis, word counts and lifted phrases, and has never once measured an interval against a calendar.* Item 285D added the calendar instance. **This is the geography instance and it is the same shape one level over: every distance figure in the batch is the figure four hundred and thirty, at all twenty-nine of its occurrences, except the two four-hundred-and-forty-foot bank lengths.** A re-print window, a lift scan, a sliding-window duplicate test, an emphasis measure, a calendar parse and a duplicate-paragraph test are all comparisons of *token sequences*, and this fault is a disagreement about **which way a token set travels** — the same tokens in the same order in both families, differing only in the preposition between them. `grep -c "four hundred and thirty"` returns 47 and it is 47 whichever way the river runs. **Item 266B's shape was a figure that contradicts a figure in another file; this is a figure that agrees with itself and is attached to two incompatible orientations, which is strictly harder, and it took a reader rather than a counter.** And there is no geography instrument: `python3 tools/measure.py calendar --volume 05` parses **1 chapter in 50** (item 285D) and there is no distance or bearing check of any kind in `tools/`.
