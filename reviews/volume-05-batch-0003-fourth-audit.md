## Section one — one prose defect no pass on this range has reported

**`chapter-0226.md:60` places a man's knowledge of a fact 317 days before the fact happened.**

> **`chapter-0226.md:60`**, the foreman, in speech: *"That is all I have got and I have had it since **the first week of the fourth month of last year** and I did not come up, and I want to say why I did not come up, and the reason is that I could not think of one thing to tell you that would not be a reason to keep holding it."*

Against three lines in the same chapter that date the holding:

> **`chapter-0226.md:5`**, narration: *"Her wage stopped in **the second week of the third month of the year after** and it is stopped now and the word for it is *held*."*
> **`chapter-0226.md:52`**, narration: *"…and the second time was **the second week of the third month of the year after**, when he came and told her that her wage was held."*
> **`chapter-0226.md:98`**, narration: *"…a wage held since **the second week of the third month of the year after**…"*

**Method, and every input printed.** The convention under test is `outline/batches/volume-05-batch-0003.md` line 69, card item 28, verbatim: *"**THE CONVENTION, STATED HERE AND NOT LEFT TO BE INFERRED. *This year* is the year after. *Last year* is the year before the year after. *Next year* is the year after next.**"* The volume counts days in weeks of seven and months of four weeks, so a year is twelve months of twenty-eight days and a day is `(month−1)×28 + (week−1)×7 + day + yearoffset×336`. On that arithmetic:

| Quantity | Line | Phrase | Day |
|---|---|---|---|
| the wage is held | `0226:5`, `0226:52`, `0226:98` | second week of the third month **of the year after** | **−269** |
| the foreman has known it | `0226:60` | first week of the fourth month **of last year** | **−586** |
| the chapter's own date | `0226:7` | second day of the second week of the fourth month **of the year after next** | **+93** |

**So the knowledge stands at −586 and the holding at −269: the knowledge precedes the fact it is knowledge of by 317 days, which is 45 weeks and 2 days, and eleven months and nine days of the volume's own calendar.** On the day of the scene he has known for 679 days that the wage is still held; the wage was first held 362 days before the scene. **The sentence does not merely overstate a duration. It places the knowing before the cause.** Re-run the arithmetic with the year offset moved and the result does not improve: the only assignment that puts `:60` after `:5` is *last year* = *the year after*, and the discussion in section two is why that assignment is not available.

**Why no instrument in this repository can see it.** It is not a repetition: the two phrases share no run beyond `the … week of the … month of`. It is not a lift. It is not a sweep form. It is not a wrong canon figure — nineteen pounds and six weeks are not canon for anything here. **It is a year-term resolved against a date line, which is the one class `python3 tools/measure.py calendar` was built for and does not reach: that command returns *files read: 50; chapters with a parsed date line: 1* on this volume, because `m.DATE_LINE` admits only `It is the` and `The date is the`, and every one of these ten chapters dates itself in its own prose. It is item 170's standing stated for a specific line — this project measures sentence length, emphasis, word counts and lifted phrases, and has never once resolved a relative year-term against a day table.**

**It needs prose authority this run does not have and did not take, because Volume 05 is closed.** A fix is a substitution against base at `chapter-0226.md:60`. **Left, recorded.** A line number in a file no phase has edited is a citation that resolves, which is item 267's standing and which this whole section rests on.

## Section two — a correction, and it moves the year-term fault onto a line inside this range

**The third audit's marginal at item 283 named the wrong year-term, and its conclusion does not survive.** It recorded that `chapter-0229.md:5` and `:98` place a past event in *next year*, that `chapter-0226.md:23` puts a past event in *the first week of the second month of this year*, and concluded that *"the volume uses **this year** for the current year, and next year at 0229 is a year ahead of it."* **The inference is unsound and the conclusion is false, and the term it should have named is `last year`, which is where section one is.**

**On `this year`, the page agrees with the card and the marginal's test proves nothing.** The deciding pair is outside this range and both lines are quotable:

> **`chapter-0209.md:17`**, a blockquoted entry: *"On the fourth day of the third week of the fifth month of **this year**. **And the box was for the person, institution or thing engaged in the matter…**"*
> **`chapter-0216.md:105`**, narration: *"A person of twenty had been put into a box on the fourth day of the third week of the fifth month of **the year after**…"*

**One event, one full date, two year-phrases: so `this year` is `the year after`, which is exactly what card item 28 states.** The marginal offered `chapter-0226.md:23`'s *"the first week of the second month of this year"* as evidence that `this year` means the current year, but that line discriminates nothing: `chapter-0226` is the fourth day of the second week of the fourth month of the year after next, so the first week of the second month reads as a past event on **both** assignments — at day −306 if `this year` is the year after, at day +57 if it is the year after next. **A test that returns the same answer on both hypotheses is not a test.**

**On `last year`, the card is right and one line outside this range proves it.**

> **`chapter-0207.md:29`**, speech, in a chapter whose own line 3 dates it *"on the second day of the third week of the tenth month of **the year after**"*: *"**And the rule of the twelfth month of last year** has not been used today and I am entering the not-using as…"*

Under card item 28 the rule is dated in the twelfth month of the year *before* the year after, about ten months earlier, and the sentence is true. **Under the alternative that saves `chapter-0226.md:60` — *last year* = *the year after* — the same sentence dates a rule to the twelfth month of a year the chapter is in the middle of, two months in the future, and the sentence is impossible.** The corroboration is volume-wide: the identical phrase *the rule of the twelfth month of last year* is carried at `chapter-0203.md:95`, `chapter-0207.md:29`, `chapter-0216.md:11`, `chapter-0222.md:49` and `chapter-0236.md:20` — five holders, not six — in chapters dated from the ninth month of the year after to the seventh month of the year after next, and every one of them treats the rule as already existing. `grep -rni` and not `grep -rn`: the phrase opens capitalised at `0222:49` and `0236:20` and a case-sensitive search returns three of the five. And *"the sixth month of last year"* at `chapter-0204.md:101`, in a chapter dated the ninth month of the year after, points before the volume opens under item 28 and forward under the alternative.

**The standing this produces, and it is the whole of section two: a relative year-term is a term, and it has to be pinned to a dated event rather than to an opinion about which year the narrator is standing in.** The page pins `this year` and `last year` twice, by two events it dates both ways, and card item 28 is right on both clauses. **What is left of the marginal is one real fact and one wrong attribution: the year-relative naming in Volume 05 is a volume-wide property that cannot be closed from a ten-chapter range, and it is not `this year` and not `next year`.** Item 283's file stands unamended — a duplicate dispatch amends nothing paid — and this correction is recorded here and nowhere else. **A finding that survives four audits can still be half wrong, and the half that is wrong was the half nobody re-derived, because it sat in a paragraph headed *Marginal*.**
